## 4. Status with respect to the Clay Millennium Prize

### Takeaway
The Clay Mathematics Institute has said nothing about this claim; its Riemann Hypothesis page still reads "Unsolved". Under the rules (dated 26 September 2018) a prize needs publication in a refereed "Qualifying Outlet", at least two years after that publication, and general acceptance by the mathematics community; the rules are silent on AI and formal verification, and a Lean archive on a personal website does not start the clock.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 4: CMI may award a prize "if and only if: a. the Proposed Solution has been published by a Qualifying Outlet…; b. at least two (2) years have elapsed since publication of the Proposed Solution in a Qualifying Outlet; c. the Proposed Solution has achieved general acceptance in the global mathematics community, as determined in the sole discretion of CMI; and d. the Proposed Solution has satisfactorily answered the questions raised by the Problem's official description". — [CMI Millennium Prize Rules (PDF, 26 Sept 2018)](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/millennium_prize_rules_0.pdf)
- Rule 6(a): a Qualifying Outlet is "a refereed mathematics publication of worldwide repute" or "a publication meeting a relaxed set of conditions approved by the BOD following a recommendation from the SAB"; 6(e) requires a named editorial board, "a published refereeing process that … ensures that a submitted paper is reviewed and verified by appropriate experts", and MathSciNet listing; 6(f) lets CMI relax these "if it has received advice from experts in the field of the Problem, chosen by CMI, that a published solution is likely to be correct." — [CMI Rules](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/millennium_prize_rules_0.pdf)
- Rule 6(d): "CMI will not consider supplementary material submitted by the author of a Proposed Solution." Rule 5(e): "CMI will not accept Proposed Solutions submitted directly to CMI". — [CMI Rules](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/millennium_prize_rules_0.pdf)
- Rule 7(a)(i): the solution "must survive rigorous examination by the global mathematics community for a minimum of two (2) years"; acceptance may be judged by independent publications, conferences, awards and "any other factors". Rule 7(a)(ii): CMI then convenes "a special advisory committee … of no fewer than three (3) members, at least two of which are experts in the area of the Problem". — [CMI Rules](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/millennium_prize_rules_0.pdf)
- Rule 8: a prize may go "to one person" or be "divided among multiple solvers of a Problem or their heirs"; CMI "will pay special attention to the question of whether a Prize solution depends crucially on insights published prior"; "Any person who is not a disqualified person … may be eligible". Rule 3: CMI has "sole authority … to modify or interpret these Rules". The rules contain no reference to computers, AI or formal proof. — [CMI Rules](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/millennium_prize_rules_0.pdf)
- CMI's Riemann Hypothesis page is labelled "Unsolved" and carries no note on this claim; CMI's most recent news item is dated 11 September 2026. — [CMI RH page](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/riemann-hypothesis/); [CMI news](https://www.claymath.org/news/)
- **Precedent for an AI-originated claim.** CMI's "Navier-Stokes Announcement", 11 Sept 2026 (three days after OpenAI's claim): "Today, CMI shares in the excitement of the global mathematical community as we contemplate the announcement that the Navier-Stokes problem has apparently been settled. We hope to see waves of new human understanding unleashed as the innovations behind this work are analysed and interrogated." and "The rules governing the prizes describe the process for evaluating what has been achieved and for assigning credit. The process is deliberately unhurried, but we will provide updates." — [CMI, 11 Sept 2026](https://www.claymath.org/news/navier-stokes-announcement/)

### Inferences
- Nothing released today is a "Proposed Solution" in CMI's sense: there is no paper, let alone one in a refereed outlet. Even if a paper were submitted immediately, refereeing plus the two-year minimum puts any award no earlier than about 2029.
- Rule 6(d) and the "refereed publication" requirement suggest a Lean formalization would count as supporting evidence for "general acceptance" rather than as the qualifying publication; this is a reading of the text, not a CMI statement.
- The rules speak of "persons" and "solvers … or their heirs", so an AI system is not a contemplated recipient; how credit would be split between Alpöge and prior human work the proof relies on falls under rule 8(b) and CMI's discretion.
- The Clay problem is RH for the Riemann zeta function; GRH/ERH, which the author also claims or expects, are outside the prize statement.
- CMI's Navier–Stokes wording ("human understanding", "deliberately unhurried") indicates it applied the existing process to an AI-originated claim rather than creating a new one.

## 5. Track record that makes mathematicians cautious

### Takeaway
Every earlier claimed proof of RH by a credentialed mathematician has failed or gone unaccepted, usually within days, and several 2025–2026 AI-mathematics announcements were later narrowed; but in 2026 some AI-produced, Lean-checked results (including one Millennium-problem claim) have so far survived formal checks, so precedent cuts both ways.

### Cited Findings
**Earlier RH claims**
- Michael Atiyah presented a claimed "simple proof" at the Heidelberg Laureate Forum on 24 September 2018, with a five-page write-up relying on another unpublished paper; mathematicians were sceptical, and Wikipedia's summary is "Again, the proof did not hold up". Atiyah's remark as reported at the time: "Nobody believes any proof of the Riemann hypothesis, let alone a proof by someone who's 90" (quote and write-up details surfaced in search-result summaries of 2018 coverage; the originating article was not individually opened). — [AMS Blog on Math Blogs, 1 Oct 2018](https://blogs.ams.org/blogonmathblogs/2018/10/01/on-michael-atiyah-and-the-riemann-hypothesis/); [The Aperiodical](https://aperiodical.com/2018/09/atiyah-riemann-hypothesis-proof-final-thoughts/); [Wikipedia: Michael Atiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Atiyah)
- Louis de Branges announced a proof in June 2004 (124-page web preprint), replaced it in December 2007, and posted further versions to 2016 that do "not conclude with a proof"; Conrey and Li (published 2000) gave numerical counterexamples to positivity conditions his approach needed; "Mathematicians remain skeptical, and neither proof has been subjected to a serious analysis." His 1984 proof of the Bieberbach conjecture "was initially met with skepticism" and then verified. — [Wikipedia: Louis de Branges de Bourcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Branges_de_Bourcia)
- Xian-Jin Li posted "A proof of the Riemann hypothesis" to arXiv on 1 July 2008; version 4 of 6 July 2008 carries the comment "withdrawn by author, due to mistake on page 29". The record shows new versions under the same title from October 2024 to October 2025 (v5–v10). — [arXiv:0807.0090v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.0090v4); [arXiv:0807.0090](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.0090)
- Opeyemi Enoch (Nigeria), November 2015: British outlets reported he had solved RH after a talk at a Vienna conference; Clay did not confirm, the problem stayed listed as unsolved, and a paper circulating under his name was found to be plagiarised. — [Gizmodo, Nov 2015](https://gizmodo.com/sorry-the-riemann-hypothesis-has-almost-certainly-not-1743014788); [Quartz](https://qz.com/africa/552327/the-nigerian-mathematics-genius-who-fooled-the-british-media)

**Earlier AI-and-mathematics claims that were qualified**
- October 2025: OpenAI's Kevin Weil posted that GPT-5 had found solutions to 10 previously unsolved Erdős problems and made progress on 11 more; Thomas Bloom (who runs erdosproblems.com) called this "a dramatic misrepresentation" because "GPT-5 found references, which solved these problems, that I personally was unaware of"; Demis Hassabis replied "This is embarrassing"; the post was deleted. — [TechCrunch, 19 Oct 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/19/openais-embarrassing-math/)
- August 2026: Anthropic's zeta-zeros result (41.6% → 67.2% on the critical line) was widely headlined as Riemann-hypothesis progress; Scientific American ran "No, Anthropic's AI didn't just solve math's hardest problem" (12 Aug 2026), with Maynard calling it "a genuinely interesting mathematical contribution" but with "no pathway" to RH. — [Scientific American, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/no-ai-didnt-just-solve-the-thorniest-problem-in-math/)
- October 2026: OpenAI released hundreds of AI-written papers (Quanta gives both 377 and 722). For the quasi-Riemann hypothesis, the Lean-verified version was set aside and human experts read a shorter unformalized version; Sarnak withheld the word "Theorem" pending human checking. — [Quanta, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.quantamagazine.org/ai-proved-the-quasi-riemann-hypothesis-what-happens-now-20261019/)
- Same release: for about 38 of 290 papers with Lean code, "the Lean code does not even state, let alone prove, the main theorem of the paper" (Buzzard). — [Xena, 12 Nov 2026](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/11/12/and-the-proof-is-checked-in-lean/)
- October–November 2026, Yang–Mills: Woit's post "Secret Unified Physics Slop" (26 Oct 2026), about AI-assisted Yang–Mills existence claims, refers to "the Levent Alpoge/Ethan Sussman ongoing effort to do rigorous Yang-Mills" and to "tens of thousands of pages of unchecked slop"; of a press release announcing a 4d Yang–Mills existence proof without saying whether the mass gap was covered, he wrote that "not specifying that seems to me intentionally deceptive". John Baez commented there (27 Oct): "I hope they're not so feckless as to invite the scrutiny of the world's experts based on such flimsy grounds." The post was read through an automated summary, and which group each remark targets (the Alpöge/Sussman effort or a separate group around the press release) was not verified line by line. Woit followed with "The Yang-Mills Publicity Stunt" on 11 Nov 2026 (title only; not read). — [Not Even Wrong, 26 Oct 2026](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=15948); [Not Even Wrong index](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/)

**Precedent in the other direction (2026)**
- Buzzard reports that OpenAI's Navier–Stokes resolution passes his Lean checks including multiple kernels, and that he compiled Anthropic's 13-million-line Fermat's Last Theorem formalization. — [Xena, 12 Nov 2026](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/11/12/and-the-proof-is-checked-in-lean/)

### Inferences
- The historical base rate for announced RH proofs is zero accepted, including from a Fields medallist (Atiyah) and from the prover of Bieberbach (de Branges). What differs here is a machine-checkable artifact and a 2026 context in which very large AI-written Lean developments have compiled.
- The recurring failure mode in 2026 AI releases was not false Lean proofs but a mismatch between the headline claim and what was formally stated, or a formal proof no human understood; both risks apply directly to a Lean-only, 17-million-line release.
