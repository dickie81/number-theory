## 4. What exactly was the August 2026 result?

### Takeaway
On 10 August 2026 Anthropic announced that an unreleased research version of Claude, prompted by non-mathematician Jarred Sumner to "take a real stab" at RH, failed at RH but proved unconditionally that at least 2/3 (0.6725 with an optimized window) of the nontrivial zeta zeros are simple and on the critical line, up from the previous record 5/12 (the "41.6%" in Anthropic's post), and at least 5/6 (0.8362) are distinct. The result is a lower bound only; the paper itself says the method caps near 2/3 and its inputs also hold for functions where the RH analogue is false, and Anthropic wrote: "We don't expect that the techniques Claude used will lead to proving the Riemann hypothesis."

### Cited Findings

**Anthropic's post**
- Title "Learning more about Claude's mathematical capabilities" (page title: "Claude has improved on a longstanding lower bound for the fraction of zeros of the Riemann zeta function that satisfy the Riemann hypothesis"); published 10 August 2026; changelog: "this post was updated on August 13, 2026, with an updated version of Claude's paper. This paper was revised by Claude to provide a clearer proof and additional historical context." **[primary]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta)
- "Recently, a member of staff at Anthropic gave Claude an unreasonable challenge ...: Take a real stab at the Riemann hypothesis. Claude did take a real stab, but ... it didn't succeed. Nevertheless, during its attempt, it unexpectedly made strides on a related problem." **[primary]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta)
- "An unreleased research version of Claude has improved on a longstanding lower bound for the fraction of zeros of the Riemann zeta function that satisfy the Riemann hypothesis. ... it has increased this bound from 41.6% to 67.2%." **[primary]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta)
- "Two mathematicians at Anthropic studied and validated Claude's paper, and produced an informal note for experts stating Claude's proof concisely. Claude also produced a formally verifiable proof of its result. - Process: "Jarred Sumner, an Anthropic staff member (and non-mathematician), prompted Claude to 'take a real stab' at the hypothesis itself, leaving the mathematical choices from there up to the model. Initially, Claude generated and tried 650 ideas, none of which worked. Jarred prompted Claude to try again, and it spent a day and a half coordinating about 60 Claude subagents ...: between them, they ran 2,400 shell commands and wrote hundreds of Python scripts. ... Jarred's input was mostly limited to sending Claude messages of encouragement (mostly variants of 'keep going' or 'believe in yourself')." Two sessions in Claude Code, 31 million output tokens; subagents downloaded 54 arXiv papers to check novelty; of 60 subagents, 2 developed the key ideas, 13 contributed ideas, 30 failed, 13 validated, 2 drafted. **[primary]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta)
- "Levent Alpöge and Ralph Furman, two of Anthropic's own mathematicians, examined Claude's work to understand the new results and how they related to the prior work ... In parallel, Claude worked with another member of staff, Eric Easley, to produce a Lean formalization of the result, which passes the standard validation tool comparator." **[primary]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta)
- Method in Anthropic's words: Claude combined results of Aryan and of Baluyot, Goldston, Suriajaya and Turnage-Butterbaugh (making Montgomery's 1973 techniques unconditional) with a 2000 paper of Bombieri; "Claude simply writes down an inequality on the rank of a quadratic form in terms of first- and second-moment information." **[primary]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta)
- Linked artifacts: Claude's paper, an informal note, an appendix on process, detailed transcripts, and the Lean repository. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta); [Lean repo](https://github.com/anthropics/zeta-23-lean)

- Listing title "More than two thirds of the zeta zeros are simple and on the critical line"; authors Levent Alpöge, Ralph Furman; math.NT; MSC 11M06, 11M26, 15A42; v1 Thu 13 Aug 2026 17:44:42 UTC (24 KB); v2 Wed 19 Aug 2026 18:17:52 UTC (31 KB); submitted by Ralph Furman; comments: "21 pages. - Title-page statement: "The mathematical argument in this paper was discovered and written by Claude, an AI developed by Anthropic. Footnote: "Jarred Sumner posed the problem and guided the investigation; the accompanying Lean 4 formalisation was orchestrated by Eric Easley." The v2 PDF carries the arXiv stamp "19 Aug 2026" but an internal "Date: August 21, 2026" (unexplained discrepancy). **[primary]** — [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13637)
- Abstract: "We prove unconditionally that at least two thirds of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function, counted with multiplicity, are simple and lie on the critical line, and that at least five sixths are distinct; the previous unconditional records are 5/12 and 0.6603. With the Montgomery–Taylor window the constants become 0.6725 and 0.8362. ... the Riemann hypothesis, classically needed to read the zero side as a positive sum over real ordinates, is replaced by a rank–trace inequality applied to a finite compression of Weil's Hermitian form, with Sylvester's law of inertia handling off-line pairs. ... The results extend to primitive Dirichlet L-functions and are formally verified in Lean 4." **[primary]** — [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13637)
- Theorem A: as T→∞, N_0^s(T,2T) ≥ (2/3 − o(1))·N(T,2T) and N_d(T,2T) ≥ (5/6 − o(1))·N(T,2T); with the Montgomery–Taylor window the constants are 0.67250… and 0.83625…. Theorem B: the same for L(s,χ), χ any fixed primitive Dirichlet character. Previous unconditional records: 5/12 for simple on-line zeros [PRZZ20] and 0.6603 for distinct zeros [Wu15]. **[primary]** — [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13637)
- Further statements in the paper: at least 85.8% of zeros of ξ′ are simple and on the line unconditionally (previously 79.874%, Conrey 1989); averaging over primitive characters gives 0.811 simple-on-line and 0.905 distinct. "No mollifier, zero-density estimate, or zero-free region is used." **[primary]** — [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13637)
- Limits, §1.4 "What the results are not": "The theorems are lower bounds only: the remaining third of the zeros are not shown to be off the line, merely not reached by the certificate. The inputs are insensitive to o(N) off-line zeros and hold for Davenport–Heilbronn and Epstein zeta functions, for which the analogue of RH is false. ... improving on 2/3 by this route would require pair-correlation information beyond Fourier support 1." The ceiling over all bandwidth-one certificates "is approximately 0.682". **[primary]** — [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13637)
- §1.5: "The author of this paper is a large language model developed by Anthropic; the argument was found over two interactive sessions and checked by repeated adversarial review by independent model instances." **[primary]** — [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13637)

## 5. Was Alpöge involved in August, and how do sources relate August to November?

### Takeaway
Alpöge was involved in August only after the fact: he and Ralph Furman verified, condensed and communicated a proof that Claude found while prompted by Jarred Sumner, and they are the arXiv authors of record. No source yet explicitly relates the two events; Alpöge's own November statement never cites the August paper and describes a different, far heavier approach (a "descendant of Stepanov's" argument using multiplicity via chained theta lifts and automorphic machinery), begun on 6 October 2026.

### Cited Findings
- August role: "Furman and Alpöge read the argument in detail, checked it independently, condensed it to the form in which it appears here"; they "have taken charge of its communication ... and they take responsibility for any errors that remain." The human operator was Sumner. **[primary]** — [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13637)
- Anthropic, August: "We don't expect that the techniques Claude used will lead to proving the Riemann hypothesis." **[primary]** — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta)
- Scientific American's 20 Nov story does not mention the August result at all. **[press]** — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/anthropic-mathematician-claims-to-prove-riemann-hypothesis-most-famous-problem-in-all-of-math/)
- November method, in Alpöge's words: "In Stepanov's argument resolving Riemann over function fields one uses a divisor involving multiplicity. This argument is a descendant of Stepanov's, but multiplicity is hard to find over the integers ... The simple, new, Fourier-analytic ideas in the toy setting led us to examine chaining theta lifts if we were to have the same tricks recur but work in a natively automorphic manner. The line: for the von Mangoldt sum of length X, Claude's suggested length of chain of theta lifts, in view of each mimicking multiplicity, was √X." **[primary]** — [alpo.ge/remark.pdf](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)
- His timeline lists ingredients absent from the August paper: Stepanov determinant; CM curves; iterated theta lifts between totally definite unitary groups; GGP/Bessel periods and a seesaw (Rallis); Chowla–Selberg, Weyl and Siegel mass formulas; an Iwasawa tower "specifically using Behrend"; Bost slope inequality; relative trace formula; "a significant weakening of Vandiver"; final entry "11/16/26: GRH." **[primary]** — [alpo.ge/remark.pdf](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)
- One entry echoes the August barrier in a new setting: "11/15/26: precise computation of the 'cost' of each lift, showing that one would stop at 2/3 a priori, attempt to get a main term and transfer constant to a function field or kill via twisting, need for 'division'." The statement does not connect this to the August paper. **[primary]** — [alpo.ge/remark.pdf](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)
- Entries such as "W(1_S) ≥ 0 via a determinant, interpret as Stepanov with multiplicity one again" and "Guinand-Weil had the sign" use a functional W and explicit-formula positivity; the August paper likewise starts from "Weil's Hermitian form", whose positivity "is equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis". **[primary]** — [alpo.ge/remark.pdf](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf); [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13637)
- He names the trigger as other labs' results, not the August paper: OpenAI's "quiet inclusion of the Siegel argument ... I immediately recognized it as Stepanov without multiplicity, though it was only clear to me during this rather harebrained tornado through the whole of number theory that this problem would fall ... when I had the qN = Mk + R sign-only proof of Weil and Deligne." **[primary]** — [alpo.ge/remark.pdf](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)
- He frames the proof as a long-held personal goal: "It was my secret in my student days that my plans were for this problem in decades", and admits limits: "I personally have little mastery of automorphic forms on higher rank groups"; "I may have baked lots of strange suboptimal choices into the argument, and expect short arguments to follow fairly quickly". **[primary]** — [alpo.ge/remark.pdf](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf)
- People common to both: Jarred Sumner and Ralph Furman are thanked in the November statement and were central in August. **[primary]** — [alpo.ge/remark.pdf](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf); [arXiv PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13637)

### Inferences
- The November claim should be described as a different approach, not the August method extended: August is pair-correlation plus linear algebra on a finite compression of Weil's form, capped near 2/3 by its own authors' account; November is described as a transplant of Stepanov's function-field method to number fields through automorphic constructions. The shared element is, at most, Weil-type positivity as a starting viewpoint.
- The roles differ sharply: in August Alpöge was a verifier of an autonomous model result; in November he is the claimant and strategic guide, with Claude credited for decisive technical steps.
- The earliest timeline entries (6 Oct 2026) coincide with coverage of OpenAI's 5–6 Oct release that included a no-Siegel-zeros result, consistent with his remark that the "Siegel argument" prompted the Stepanov idea. This dating link is an inference.
- Anthropic's August sentence about not expecting "the techniques Claude used" to lead to RH is not contradicted by the November claim, since the techniques are different; but no Anthropic source has said this.

### Gaps
- No source (Anthropic, Alpöge, or press) explicitly states how the November argument relates to the August theorem; the above is a reading of two primary documents.
- The mathematical content of the timeline entries is, by Alpöge's own admission, partly a paraphrase of a model-written summary that "might make zero sense"; none of it has been independently assessed in sources found.
- Whether Furman, Sumner, Easley or other August participants took part in the November formalization is not stated beyond the thanks list.
