# The Linnik paper's certificate tools

These scripts compute the Linnik sieve's certificates outside Lean and generate the Lean files that the kernel
checks. They need Python 3 only. The paper's theorem at `43/20` (`Linnik21500.linnik_21500`) is `hint_cert.py`'s, on
the searches of `tree_cert.py`, `rev_cert.py`, `fill_cert.py` and `flip_cert.py`, with the curve and region of
`gen_zone.py`. The scripts from `route_limits.py` to `type2_model.py` in the table are those of the window route, of
the library's theorems at `11/5`, `219/100` and `218/100`; in their rows "the paper" is the paper's version at `11/5`,
in the git history at commit `49dc981`.

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `route_limits.py` | Computes, for a density curve, an exponent `L` and a grid size `n`: the Halász–Montgomery window, its corners in exact rationals, and the depth-4 certificate's total (`replay`: the first certificate, before its two deeper levels, its kept parts and its sharper cell bounds, kept as a cross-check of the window and the grid's conditions). It reads the curves from `lib/pnt`. Its docstring lists its commands. |
| `route_limits.jsonl` | The rows `route_limits.py run` recorded: a curve, `L`, `n`, the window, the corners and the total. |
| `refine_k2_probe.py` | Replays the depth-4 refined certificate (before the deeper levels) on the same grids (`replay2`); the refined certificate's `total2` is `mirror2.py`'s. |
| `mirror2.py` | Evaluates `total` and `total2` of the refined certificate of `SieveCertDefs.lean` and `SieveCert2Defs.lean`, the earlier theorem's at `278/125` (depths up to 6, with the coarse grid of `n/4` cells of four for `p₅` and `p₆`, the kept parts, Buchstab's `ω` on `[2, 3]` with its table `lnT`, and the cells' trapezoid and exact prime sums) cell by cell in exact integers, in parallel, and writes each cell's cost and kernel work to JSON. It prints the totals and the boxes tested at depths three to six below the decomposed cells. With `--depth4` no cell of depth four is decomposed: the decomposition stopped at depth four, whose `total` on the paper's grid is the paper's `1.45721𝒦`. With `--ib a/b` it is the certificate under the bilinear Type I rule of `SieveRuleDefs.lean` at `L = a/b` (`ibRule`, `total2R`), and each cell also records the rule's `ibOK` tests. The kernel is the check of the Lean; this is the cut's source. |
| `mirror8.py` | Evaluates `totalD` of `SieveDeepDefs.lean`, the certificate of the decomposition to depth 8 under the bilinear Type I rule (the cells of depth 4 dropped or decomposed over the coarse cells of `p₅` and `p₆`, those of depth 6 dropped or decomposed over `p₇` and `p₈`, the masks of the surely evaluable boxes, the block test `okD` and the memoised bounds by index sums), cell by cell in exact integers, in parallel, and writes each candidate cell's cost and the kernel work it makes (the boxes `ok3D` tests, the counted cells of depths 3 and 4, those of depth 4 in the pure and the masked modes, the terms of depths 5 and 6). About a minute on four cores at `n = 272`. |
| `gen_cert8.py` | Cuts `mirror8.py`'s certificate into parts by the kernel's predicted time (a least-squares fit of the kernel's time on the cells' work, at most 30 seconds a part) and writes `SieveGrid22000E.lean`, `SieveCert22000EP<k>.lean` (114 files of at most 10 parts) and `SieveCert22000E.lean` (`totalD < K`). |
| `gen_cgl_curve.py` | Writes `LinnikCGLSteps.lean` (Chen–Gupta–Li's Proposition 3.1 on 30 steps of `[5/7, 131/168]`, fourteen of width `1/1680` and then sixteen of `1/280`, with `ParC` parameters computed here and every field of `ParC.Good` checked in exact rationals, and Ingham's last step at `ε = 1/400000`), `SieveCurve7D.lean` (the curve `g7d`, `g7d_le_one`, `g7d_half`, `g7d_le_lin` and its counts; the window and Type II estimate at `L = 278/125` are in the git history), and, for the decomposition to depth 8, `SieveTypeIIU22000D.lean` (the window and Type II estimate of `g7d` at `L = 11/5`) and `Linnik22000E.lean` (the theorem at `L = 11/5`, with no hypothesis); and `SieveTypeIIU21900D.lean` and `SieveTypeIIU21800D.lean` (the windows and Type II estimates of `g7d` at `L = 219/100` and `L = 218/100`, for the theorems of `tree_cert.py` and `rev_cert.py`). `--check` compares them with `lib/pnt`; `--table` prints the steps. |
| `density_payoff.py` | What a lower density curve would buy: for hypothetical curves made from `g7d` (a cap on its exponent, Chen–Gupta–Li's bound without the term behind its `7/3`, Heath-Brown's exponent `2` from `15/19`, with or without Ingham's estimate below `4/7`; and `classical`, without Chen–Gupta–Li, with Ingham's and Huxley's exponents in steps of `1/1680` on `[5/7, 131/168]`, which starts from `23/10` and gave the earlier version's `2.2685`), the window, `mirror2.py`'s `total2` at `n = 340`, and the lowest `L` passed with `total2 < 0.999K`. For each curve it computes a window admissible at `L` (the sampled scan, then the exact corners in rationals, moving an end inward by `10⁻⁵` while a corner fails), evaluates `total2` on it in exact integers, starts from `278/125` (which must pass), lowers `L` by `1/1000` while the total stays below `0.999K`, then tests once the value `1/2000` below the last one passed, and reports the lowest `L` passed. Computations, not theorems; all variants take about an hour. With `--deep` it replays the certificate at `11/5` instead, `mirror8.py`'s `totalD` at `n = 272` under the bilinear rule at `L`, on grids that satisfy the Lean's conditions (a narrow window fails `4H + 4HC ≤ V`), starting from `11/5`, and from `56/25` for `classical`, which gives the paper's `2239/1000` (its Section 8 and its supplement's §S6); a few minutes for each `L`. |
| `choices.py` | The certificate's choices at depths two and four, from `mirror2.py`'s definitions: the candidate cells and the ones the sharp rule allows, whether each would be cheaper decomposed (so that the sharp rule makes every choice), the counted cells of depth four below the decomposed ones, the ones decomposed and what the others would save decomposed, and the cost of the dropped cells that reach the line `α₁ + α₂ = 1 − U₁/D`. The figures of the earlier version of the paper, at `278/125`; about a minute. |
| `fig_cells.py` | Draws the Figure 1 of the paper's version at `11/5` (in the git history), the cells of depth two of the certificate at `11/5` by kind (decomposed by the split at `X′`, decomposed whole by the bilinear test, dropped, evaluated, corner, the window's strip), as a TikZ picture, by default into `papers/linnik-exponent/fig-cells.tex` (which the paper at `43/20` no longer includes), from `mirror8.py`'s definitions, and prints the counts and costs of the three kinds of candidate cell, their total, and the dropped cells' cost on either side of the line `α₁ + α₂ = 1 − U₁/D`; about two minutes on four cores. |
| `breakdown8.py` | The certificate's total at `11/5` (`mirror8.py`'s `totalD`, to the integer) split by depth and kind, on the paper's grid by default: at depth two the decomposed cells (split at `X′` or taken whole by the bilinear test) and the dropped ones by reason (the rule fails on the cell's box, or on a box of depth three below it, or the cell is cheaper dropped) and by side of the line `α₁ + α₂ = 1 − U₁/D`; below the decomposed cells, the boxes of depths three to six that are counted, masked, decomposed or dropped, with their costs. Its variants `depth6`, `depth4`, `lim` and `lim6` stop the decomposition at depth six or four, or take (R1) alone as the rule. The counts, costs and variant totals of the paper's Section 5 and its supplement's §S6; about three minutes on four cores. |
| `two_factor.py` | Replays `mirror8.py`'s certificate with a second Type II estimate, in two variables, as one more way to evaluate a box of depth at most four: the box's primes and its cofactor split into two parts, each a variable with arbitrary coefficients, and a rest that is a product of primes, with the exponents of the two parts in the region where Cauchy–Schwarz between the two variables (orthogonality or the Halász–Montgomery inequality for each, the paper's bound for the prime sums, the paper's margins) gives the estimate. `region` prints the region, `total` the certificate's total on a grid, `scan` the totals for a list of exponents with the window chosen afresh; `--stops` adds the estimate's stops to the rule, `--bil` takes the bilinear Type I rule without the condition that `d₂` be shorter than the cofactor, and `--lean` takes for the region the one proved in Lean (`SieveTwoZone7D.Zone7d`), in exact integers and on the rectangle of the two parts' exponents, as the Lean lemmas ask. At `11/5` the total is `0.76216𝒦` (`0.93214𝒦` without the estimate; `0.77375𝒦` with `--lean`), and the last exponent that passes is `2.193` (`2.199` without; `2.193` with `--lean`, at `0.98606𝒦`). A computation, not a certificate: the region is sampled in floating point (`gen_zone.py` writes the Lean proof of the estimate on an explicit region inside it), and the lemmas that bring the estimate to the sieve's sums are not written. Its docstring has the statement, what is assumed and the numbers. About a minute for a total at `n = 272`; needs numpy. |
| `type2_model.py` | A model, in floating point, of which Type II sums over the characters the large-value tools bound: the profiles of sizes of the factors, the number of characters with a profile by orthogonality, Halász–Montgomery, the fourth moment and, where the modulus can be cut, Huxley's large values, and the density curve for a prime-factored factor. `window` recovers the paper's window at `11/5`; `harman` recovers the region of Baker, Harman and Pintz's Lemma 9 with Huxley's theorem on the prime-factored factor, and gives the smaller region without it; `every` adds the paper's inputs. Its docstring has the table. A few minutes; needs numpy. |
| `harman_sieve.py`, `harman_sieve.c` | A decomposition that does not lean on the window, in floating point: the floor is chosen for each term, a box is evaluated by the window or by the estimate in two variables at every depth, a term is decomposed when Harman's comparison can stop its tree by the estimate, and a term may be treated through any of its primes (role reversal, nested). `cells` takes every test and every cost conservatively on boxes of cells, the boundary terms (the products `n = r` that a term also counts) among them, and so bounds the loss from above; `points` replaces each cell by its midpoint, the limit of fine cells; `parity` runs with the linear sieve's bounds and no Type II information, where the loss must stay above `1`. `--rev`, `--no-first`, `--fill` and `--direct` restrict the reversal (which terms, through which prime, with which cofactor, as an identity or an inequality, and what a reversed tree opens), the stops (`--fill`: in the form that `SieveFillComp.prop6_fill` proves) and the ways to evaluate a box, to see what a tree needs. The docstring states the decomposition and what it assumes, and `docs/research-opportunities.md` §3.1 has the values. Needs numpy and a C compiler with OpenMP (the library is built in the temporary directory); with cells of side `0.002`, four to seven minutes for an exponent on four cores. |

With `RES_CURVE=g7d` or `RES_GRID=n` the script measures the test with the curve `g7d` in place of `ghx`, or another grid (the Lean has `ghx`'s test on `2^-12` only). Python 3 only. |
| `hint_probe.py` | Measurements on the plan of `hint_cert.py`, not a certificate: where the plan's cost is (dropped boxes by their number of primes, reversed boxes and the dropped boxes of their chains), what the cells cost (the tests taken at the midpoints of the cells, by kind of test and of cell), and what variants of the search would give in exact terms: cuts at the ends of the part that a configuration evaluates, boxes of the sign `+` reversed, a second level of the reversal (a prototype, with the roles' sums read from two slabs and two cofactors), a reversal through another prime than the largest, and a planner that chooses each step by its cost plus a price for the kernel's time. `docs/research-opportunities.md` §3.1 has the numbers of 12 November 2026. Python 3 only. |
| `check_certificate.py` | Regenerates the certificates' Lean files and compares them with `lib/pnt` byte for byte: for the theorem at `11/5` (`22000E`), `mirror8.py` and `gen_cert8.py` in a temporary directory; for the tree at `219/100` (`21900`), `tree_cert.py --check`; for the search with role reversal at `218/100` (`21800`), `rev_cert.py --check`; for the search with no window at `2165/1000` (`21650`), `fill_cert.py --check`. |

To check that the committed certificate is the one these scripts generate:

```sh
python3 tools/linnik/check_certificate.py           # `22000E`, `21900`, `21800` and `21650`, about ten minutes
python3 tools/linnik/check_certificate.py --inputs  # the files it reads
```

`--inputs` lists, as glob patterns, the scripts that the check runs and those they import, and the files of
`lib/pnt` whose names hold a certificate's tag, among them every file it compares. A change to no other file can
change its outcome, so CI runs it only when a change touches one of them or the workflow (the job `changes` of
`.github/workflows/ci.yml`), and on a manual run.

It prints the totals, the cross-checks, the number of parts and the comparison, and ends with
`CERTIFICATE <tag> PASS` when all its files are identical:

| Tag | `L` | Window | Curve | `totalD/K` | Parts | Files |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `22000E` | `11/5` | `[43457, 46959]/100000` | `g7d` | `0.9321382` | 1136 | 114 part files + grid + assembly |

`gen_cgl_curve.py --check` regenerates the six files of the curve, its windows and the theorem (about a minute, for the windows' scans).

The kernel's check of a certificate is the build itself: at `11/5` each part file proves its parts by
`decide +kernel`, and `SieveCert22000E.lean` assembles them into `SieveDeep.totalD_lt_22000E`. The kernel's check of the 114 part files took
6.2 hours of one core in all (`gen_cert8.py` predicts 8.2), 1.6 hours on four cores, with at most 2.8 GiB for any one
file.

The curve `g7d` (`SieveCurve7D.g7d`) is the window route's (the theorems at `11/5`, `219/100` and `218/100`; the paper's estimate in two variables uses `ghx`, above): the steps of `LinnikCGLSteps.lean` on
`[5/7, 131/168]` in place of the plateau `7/3 + 1/2000` on `[5/7, 16/21]` and its first five Huxley steps, and Ingham's
last step at `ε = 1/400000` in the Lean lemma's convention (its exponent is `2 + 4c + 4ε`, so the slack is
`4ε = 1/100000` in place of `1/2000`). `route_limits.jsonl` has its row at `278/125` (`g7d`, `n = 340`, window
`[41556, 47531]`; its total is the first, depth-4 certificate's, `1.49216K`). Between `11161/5000` and `89/40` the curve
took Huxley's bound on the three steps of `[1307/1680, 131/168]` (`LinnikHuxJunction.lean`, in the git history): at
`11161/5000` the window's lower end was set near `7/9`. From `89/40` on both ends are set on the first Chen–Gupta–Li step
and those steps do not change the window, so they are Chen–Gupta–Li's again. The first fourteen steps are of width
`1/1680`; from `5/7 + 14/1680` on, steps of `1/280` leave the window as it is (the least slack of their corners is
`4.4·10⁻⁵` at `278/125` and `6.8·10⁻⁴` at `11/5`), and at `278/125` the curve has thirty Chen–Gupta–Li steps in place of the earlier `110` of `1/1680`.

The theorem at `278/125` (`Linnik22240R`, in the git history with its certificate `22240R`) used the refined
certificate of `SieveCert2Defs.lean` at `n = 340`: the two deeper levels of the Buchstab decomposition, the coarse grid
of `85` cells of four, the kept parts, and the cells' bounds (Buchstab's `ω` itself on `[2, 3]`, `omKw`, with the table
`lnT` of `log((16 + k)/16)` and `log y ≤ (y − 1/y)/2`; `0.565073` above `3`; the trapezoid bound of each cell's

parts) brought its `total2/K` to `0.99754`. The theorem at `11/5` (`Linnik22000E`) replaced it: its certificate
(`SieveDeepDefs.lean`) adds the bilinear Type I rule and two more levels of the decomposition, and totals `0.9321382K`
at `n = 272`, without the kept parts. Both theorems have no hypothesis.
