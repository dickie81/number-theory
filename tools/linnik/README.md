# The Linnik paper's certificate tools

These scripts compute the Linnik sieve's certificates outside Lean and generate the Lean files that the kernel
checks. They need Python 3 only, where a row says nothing else. The paper's Theorem 1.2, at `43/20`
(`Linnik21500.linnik_21500`), is `hint_cert.py`'s, on the searches of `tree_cert.py`, `rev_cert.py`, `fill_cert.py`
and `flip_cert.py`, with the curve and region of `gen_zone.py`. Its Theorem 1.1, at `21/10`
(`Linnik21000.linnik_21000`), is `hint_cert.py`'s too, with the leaves' region of `gen_leafzone.py` and the resonant
window's test of `res_test.py`. The scripts from `route_limits.py` to `type2_model.py` in the table are
measurements of the window route; in their rows "the paper" is the paper's version at `11/5`. The certificates at
weaker exponents, with the windows of `g7d` and the parts of these scripts that wrote them, are no longer in the
tree; they are in the git history (the paper's version at `11/5` at commit `49dc981`; the certificates at `11/5`,
`219/100`, `218/100` and `2115/1000` at commit `76abe3c`).

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `route_limits.py` | Computes, for a density curve, an exponent `L` and a grid size `n`: the Halász–Montgomery window, its corners in exact rationals, and the depth-4 certificate's total (`replay`: the first certificate, before its two deeper levels, its kept parts and its sharper cell bounds, kept as a cross-check of the window and the grid's conditions). It reads the curves from `lib/pnt`. Its docstring lists its commands. |
| `route_limits.jsonl` | The rows `route_limits.py run` recorded: a curve, `L`, `n`, the window, the corners and the total. |
| `mirror2.py` | Evaluates `total` and `total2` of the refined certificate at `278/125` (depths up to 6, with the coarse grid of `n/4` cells of four for `p₅` and `p₆`, the kept parts, Buchstab's `ω` on `[2, 3]` with its table `lnT`, and the cells' trapezoid and exact prime sums) cell by cell in exact integers, in parallel, and writes each cell's cost and kernel work to JSON. It prints the totals and the boxes tested at depths three to six below the decomposed cells. With `--depth4` no cell of depth four is decomposed: the decomposition stopped at depth four, whose `total` on the paper's grid is the paper's `1.45721𝒦`. With `--ib a/b` it is the certificate under the bilinear Type I rule at `L = a/b`, and each cell also records the rule's `ibOK` tests. |
| `typeib_payoff.py` | What a bilinear Type I estimate would buy the window route (issue #39): `mirror2.py`'s `total2` replayed with a wider sharp rule, a term decomposed also when the estimate (`parseval`, `trap`, `sharp` or `weil`, stated in the docstring) compares the leaves of its iteration; `total` at a grid and an exponent, `scan` the lowest exponent passed. `parseval` is the estimate of `lib/pnt/SieveTypeIB.lean`; the rule is the script's `ok`, `tstar`, `ib_leaf` and `ib`. Computations under the model, not theorems; one to four minutes a total on four cores. |
| `mirror8.py` | Evaluates `totalD`, the certificate of the decomposition to depth 8 under the bilinear Type I rule (the cells of depth 4 dropped or decomposed over the coarse cells of `p₅` and `p₆`, those of depth 6 dropped or decomposed over `p₇` and `p₈`, the masks of the surely evaluable boxes, the block test `okD` and the memoised bounds by index sums), cell by cell in exact integers, in parallel, and writes each candidate cell's cost and the work of its evaluation (the boxes `ok3D` tests, the counted cells of depths 3 and 4, those of depth 4 in the pure and the masked modes, the terms of depths 5 and 6). About a minute on four cores at `n = 272`. Buchstab's bound `omKw` is that of `lib/pnt/SieveCertDefs.lean`; `tree_cert.py` and `cplan.py` take it from here, and `density_payoff.py --deep` and `two_factor.py` replay the certificate at `11/5`, on the grid `43457 46959 272`. |
| `density_payoff.py` | What a lower density curve would buy: for hypothetical curves made from `g7d` (a cap on its exponent, Chen–Gupta–Li's bound without the term behind its `7/3`, Heath-Brown's exponent `2` from `15/19`, with or without Ingham's estimate below `4/7`; and `classical`, without Chen–Gupta–Li, with Ingham's and Huxley's exponents in steps of `1/1680` on `[5/7, 131/168]`, which starts from `23/10` and gave the earlier version's `2.2685`), the window, `mirror2.py`'s `total2` at `n = 340`, and the lowest `L` passed with `total2 < 0.999K`. Computations, not theorems; all variants take about an hour. With `--deep` it replays the certificate at `11/5` instead, `mirror8.py`'s `totalD` at `n = 272` under the bilinear rule at `L`, on grids that satisfy the Lean's conditions (a narrow window fails `4H + 4HC ≤ V`), starting from `11/5`, and from `56/25` for `classical`, which gives the paper's `2239/1000` (its Section 8 and its supplement's §S6); a few minutes for each `L`. |
| `two_factor.py` | Replays `mirror8.py`'s certificate with a second Type II estimate, in two variables, as one more way to evaluate a box of depth at most four: the box's primes and its cofactor split into two parts, each a variable with arbitrary coefficients, and a rest that is a product of primes, with the exponents of the two parts in the region where Cauchy–Schwarz between the two variables (orthogonality or the Halász–Montgomery inequality for each, the paper's bound for the prime sums, the paper's margins) gives the estimate. `region` prints the region, `total` the certificate's total on a grid, `scan` the totals for a list of exponents with the window chosen afresh; `--stops` adds the estimate's stops to the rule, `--bil` takes the bilinear Type I rule without the condition that `d₂` be shorter than the cofactor, and `--lean` takes for the region the one proved in Lean (`SieveTwoZone7D.Zone7d`), in exact integers and on the rectangle of the two parts' exponents, as the Lean lemmas ask. Its docstring has the statement, what is assumed and the numbers. About a minute for a total at `n = 272`; needs numpy. |
| `type2_model.py` | A model, in floating point, of which Type II sums over the characters to one modulus the large-value tools bound: two factors with arbitrary coefficients and a prime-factored rest, split by the sizes of the factors and of their sub-products (profiles), with orthogonality, the Halász–Montgomery inequality, Huxley's large values estimate (which holds for one modulus: `LinnikReflect.huxley_large_values`), Chen, Gupta and Li's Proposition 3.1, the density curve and the half-plane's cap; the largest contribution is a linear programme over the sizes, solved exactly by its dual's vertices. `harman05` sets Harman's region of 2005 (Baker, Harman and Pintz's `γ_g`) beside the model's: Chen, Gupta and Li's estimate on the powers of the rest, where his proof uses Huxley's, gives his region and no more, and Huxley's estimate and theirs on every sub-product widen it a little; `widen` shows that the half-plane's saving does not move it; `regions` prints the regions side by side, among them the current paper's region of its Definition 3.1; `zone SET L` writes a region as the table that `harman_sieve.py --zone-file` reads; `check` compares the model's two searches. Its docstring has the tables. Two minutes for `harman05` or `widen`, under a minute for a zone; needs numpy. |
| `harman_sieve.py`, `harman_sieve.c` | A decomposition that does not lean on the window, in floating point: the floor is chosen for each term, a box is evaluated by the window or by the estimate in two variables at every depth, a term is decomposed when Harman's comparison can stop its tree by the estimate, and a term may be treated through any of its primes (role reversal, nested). `cells` takes every test and every cost conservatively on boxes of cells, the boundary terms (the products `n = r` that a term also counts) among them, and so bounds the loss from above; `points` replaces each cell by its midpoint, the limit of fine cells; `parity` runs with the linear sieve's bounds and no Type II information, where the loss must stay above `1`. `--rev`, `--no-first`, `--fill` and `--direct` restrict the reversal (which terms, through which prime, with which cofactor, as an identity or an inequality, and what a reversed tree opens), the stops (`--fill`: in the form that `SieveFillComp.prop6_fill` proves) and the ways to evaluate a box, to see what a tree needs. The docstring states the decomposition and what it assumes, and `docs/research-opportunities.md` §3.1 has the values. Needs numpy and a C compiler with OpenMP (the library is built in the temporary directory); with cells of side `0.002`, four to seven minutes for an exponent on four cores. Its what-if switches (`--curve`, `--theta0`, `--kernel`, `--kshift`, `--ext`) run it with a hypothetical density curve, zero-free half-plane, Halász–Montgomery kernel or Type I reach: `docs/research-opportunities.md` §3.1 has what each moves. `--res` adds the resonant window, a Type II estimate that takes nothing from the density curve (the characters sorted by the sizes of the rest's own sums, each counted at its own length: by the mean values of its powers, the Halász–Montgomery inequality and Chen, Gupta and Li's Proposition 3.1 for them; stated in `docs/research-opportunities.md` §3.1, not in Lean as a rule of the sieve): with it `points` passes `1` at `2.068`, where it has `2.101` (`2.085` with `--res hm,sifted`, the mean values and the zeros' count for the sifted parts). `--res` also takes `cofp` (a cofactor that is prime counted as one more prime of the rest), `hux` (Huxley's estimate for the powers) and `nozs` (a sifted part counted by its own sums only). `--region`, `--zone-file` (a table of `type2_model.py zone`), `--zone-mode`, `--type1 hkl`, `--four` and `--up` give it Harman's own information: his region of 2005, and Harman, Kumchev and Lewis's Type I reach, estimate on four variables and Buchstab's identity in reverse. With these, Chen, Gupta and Li's estimate in his lemmas leaves his sieve above `21/10` (about `2.108` to `2.111` on his route), and the paper's estimate in one variable is what puts its rules ahead; the docstring has the table and the commands, and the Linnik paper's Section 12 states them. |
| `tree_cert.py` | The search for a tree of Buchstab steps in Python, in exact integers, with the window's tests and those of the estimate in two variables on `Zone7d`: the decomposition of `harman_sieve.py` without role reversal. To check that the committed certificate is the one these scripts generate:

```sh
python3 tools/linnik/check_certificate.py           # `21500`, about a minute and a half (a C compiler with __int128)
python3 tools/linnik/check_certificate.py --inputs  # the files it reads
```

`--inputs` lists, as glob patterns, the scripts that the check runs and those they import, the C planner's sources,
the two files of `lib/pnt` that the scripts read when they start (`SieveCurveHx.lean`, `SieveLeafZoneXDefs.lean`),
and the files of `lib/pnt` whose names hold the certificate's tag, among them every file it compares. A change to no
other file can change its outcome, so CI runs it only when a change touches one of them or the workflow (the job
`changes` of `.github/workflows/ci.yml`), and on a manual run.

It prints the search's cost, its counts, the number of parts and the comparison, and ends with
`CERTIFICATE 21500 PASS` when all its files are identical:

| Tag | `L` | Curve | Cost`/K` | Boxes searched | Parts | Files |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `21500` | `43/20` | `ghx` | `0.94847` | `3.4·10^7` | 1552 | 17 part files + `SieveHint21500.lean` + `Linnik21500.lean` |

The kernel's check of the certificate is the build itself: each part file proves its parts by `decide +kernel`, and
`Linnik21500.lean` assembles them into the theorem. The certificate at `21/10` (`hint_cert.py 21000`) is not in the
check, for its plan takes hours on several machines.

`gen_cgl_curve.py --check` regenerates the two files of the curve `g7d` (seconds). The curve `g7d`
(`SieveCurve7D.g7d`) is the curve of the leaves' region of `gen_leafzone.py`, which Theorem 1.1 uses (the estimate
in two variables uses `ghx`, above): the steps of
`LinnikCGLSteps.lean` on `[5/7, 131/168]` in place of the plateau `7/3 + 1/2000` on `[5/7, 16/21]` and its first five
Huxley steps, and Ingham's last step at `ε = 1/400000` in the Lean lemma's convention (its exponent is
`2 + 4c + 4ε`, so the slack is `4ε = 1/100000` in place of `1/2000`). `gen_leafzone.py` writes the leaves' region for
it, and `route_limits.py`, `density_payoff.py` and `two_factor.py` read it.
