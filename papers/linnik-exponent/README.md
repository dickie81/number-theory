# Linnik's exponent

`main.tex` is the paper: Linnik's theorem with exponent `11161/5000`. All eight sections are written: the introduction,
the two inputs and the density curve, the Type II estimate, Type I and Harman's sieve, the decomposition and its
certificate, the proof, the formalisation, and a comparison with what the inputs give without the sieve. The
bibliography entries were checked against the publishers', arXiv and repository pages and, for Ingham and Huxley,
against scans of the originals; Xylouris's Acta Arith. 150 paper (L = 5.18, checked against the journal's PDF and EUDML) and the Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan corrigendum (checked against the AMS contents page, Math. Comp. 86 (2017), 2551–2554) were added later; Buchstab's volume, issue and pages (1937) are still unverified, having been seen in secondary
sources only, and the entries for the OpenAI preprints, OpenAI's `lean/formalization.yaml` catalogue and the AIProofWatch record are web pages, not refereed sources. The paper claims only its own steps. The results it uses from the literature are cited to their sources and to
[`formalisations/`](../../formalisations/README.md): its two inputs, OpenAI's zero-free half-plane and Chen, Gupta
and Li's density estimate, and the classical results its proof uses, among them Ingham's and Huxley's density estimates,
the Halász–Montgomery inequality and Harman's sieve.

The theorem is `Linnik22322R.linnik_22322r` in `lib/pnt/Linnik22322R.lean`. It has no hypotheses in Lean. Its proof uses two unrefereed results that the library re-proves itself: the zero-free half-plane `Re s > 11/12` of OpenAI's preprints (the library follows the companion preprint), and Chen, Gupta and Li's arXiv preprint (density estimate), of which the library formalises Proposition 3.1 and what it rests on, and derives the 107 density steps from it; their Theorem 1.2 and the rest of their paper are not formalised. Its truth rests on Lean's kernel, Mathlib and the pinned toolchain (with the vendored upstream ports and the provenance of the build cache), not on the preprints; whether the Lean statements faithfully capture the preprints' theorems matters for attribution and interpretation, not for the truth of the theorem. Independent checking of the half-plane result is partial: OpenAI's release has its own Lean formalisation (its catalogue `lean/formalization.yaml` marks it "partial progress", review status "unchecked"), which is not independent of OpenAI; third-party reports of replaying OpenAI's Lean proof of the 7/8 result exist (they check the proof, not the mathematics, and the author has not rerun them); and the AIProofWatch record covers the Riemann zeta function only. Its exponent is not comparable with the unconditional results of Heath-Brown and Xylouris. Section 7's table names the Lean theorem
behind each claim, and `lib/check/Check.lean` prints their axioms (of the 107 Chen–Gupta–Li density steps it prints the first and the last, of the three Huxley junction steps the first and the last, and it prints Ingham's last step and Chen, Gupta and Li's Proposition 3.1; the other steps print their axioms in `LinnikCGLSteps.lean` when it is built; `lib/check/CheckAll.lean`, with `python3 scripts/check_axioms.py --all`, prints the axioms of further declarations of the library).

To build: `latexmk -pdf main.tex` (pdfLaTeX, as CI does). The preamble also lets XeTeX (for example `tectonic main.tex`) compile it; the Lean blocks then lack some glyphs of the monospace font.
