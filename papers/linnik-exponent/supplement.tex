\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:prime-sum}, and the kernel bound}\label{app:prime-sum}

\begin{proof}
Throughout, $Q \ge 4$, so that $\log Q > 1$, and $L_\chi$ stands for $L(\cdot, \chi)$, which is entire since $\chi$ is
not trivial. Put $\sigma_* = 11/12 + \delta$ and $\tau = (1 + H)^{-J}$.

\emph{The weight.} Let $f_Q(u) = f_0(u) \log Q/\log(uQ)$ where $f_0(u) \ne 0$, which needs $u \ge 1/2$ and so
$uQ \ge 2$, and $f_Q(u) = 0$ elsewhere; and let $w_y(u) = u^{-iy} f_Q(u)$. If
$f_0(n/Q) \ne 0$ then $n \ge Q/2 \ge 2$ and $f_Q(n/Q) = f_0(n/Q) \log Q/\log n$, so that
$\Lambda(n) f_Q(n/Q) = \log Q \cdot (\Lambda(n)/\log n) f_0(n/Q)$. Since $\Lambda(p)/\log p = 1$,
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w_y(n/Q) = \log Q \, \bigl(S_Q(\chi, y) + R\bigr), \\
  R = \sum_{\substack{p^k \le 2Q \\ k \ge 2}} \frac{\chi(p^k)}{k} \Bigl(\frac{p^k}{Q}\Bigr)^{-iy} f_0\Bigl(\frac{p^k}{Q}\Bigr).
\end{gather*}
Each term of $R$ is at most $C = \max |f_0|$ in absolute value, and a proper prime power $p^k \le 2Q$ has
$p \le \sqrt{2Q}$ and $k \le \log_2(2Q)$, so $|R| \le C(\sqrt{2Q} + 1)(\log_2(2Q) + 1)$. It remains to prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sw-bound}
  \Bigl|\sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w_y(n/Q)\Bigr| \le M_\delta(\theta) \, \lambda \, B \bigl(Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{\sigma_*} \tau\bigr).
\end{equation}

\emph{The Mellin transform.} Write $\mathcal M f(s) = \int_0^\infty u^{s - 1} f(u) \, du$. The substitution
$u = e^{-v}$ gives, with $c_Q = 1/\log Q \in (0, 1/\log 4]$,
\[
  \mathcal M f_Q(\sigma + it) = \int_{\mathbb R} e^{-itv} V(v) \, dv, \qquad
  V(v) = e^{-\sigma v} f_0(e^{-v}) (1 - c_Q v)^{-1}.
\]
The function $V$ vanishes for $|v| > \log 2$, and $c_Q v \le 1/2$ for $v \le \log 2$; the pole $v = \log Q \ge \log 4$ of
$(1 - c_Q v)^{-1}$ lies where $V$ vanishes, so $V$ is smooth. By Leibniz's
rule, with $|(d/dv)^i (1 - c_Q v)^{-1}| = i! \, c_Q^i |1 - c_Q v|^{-1 - i} \le i! \, 2^{i + 1}$ on $|v| \le \log 2$,
the $j$-th derivative of $V$ is bounded by a constant $V_j$ that depends only on $f_0$ and $j$, uniformly in
$0 \le \sigma \le 2$ and $Q \ge 4$. Integration by parts $j$ times gives
$|t|^j |\mathcal M f_Q(\sigma + it)| \le 2 \log 2 \cdot V_j$, and with $j = 0$ and $j = J + 3$,
\[
  (1 + |t|)^{J + 3} |\mathcal M f_Q(\sigma + it)| \le B \qquad (0 \le \sigma \le 2,\ t \in \mathbb R,\ Q \ge 4),
\]
where $B = 2^{J + 4} \log 2 \, (V_0 + V_{J + 3})$ depends only on $f_0$ and $J$. Since $f_Q$ is continuous on
$(0, \infty)$ and vanishes near $0$ and near $\infty$, $\mathcal M f_Q$ is entire. Finally
$\mathcal M w_y(s) = \mathcal M f_Q(s - iy)$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mellin-decay}
  (1 + |t - y|)^{J + 3} |\mathcal M w_y(\sigma + it)| \le B \qquad (0 \le \sigma \le 2,\ t \in \mathbb R).
\end{equation}

\emph{Perron's formula.} For $\operatorname{Re} s > 1$, $-L_\chi'/L_\chi(s) = \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) n^{-s}$.
Mellin inversion on the line $\operatorname{Re} s = 2$ at each point $n/Q$, with the exchange of sum and integral
justified by \eqref{eq:mellin-decay}, gives
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w_y(n/Q) = \frac1{2\pi} \int_{\mathbb R} F(2 + it) \, dt, \\
  F(s) = -\frac{L_\chi'}{L_\chi}(s) \, \mathcal M w_y(s) \, Q^s.
\end{gather*}

\emph{The logarithmic derivative.} For $1/2 \le \theta_0 < \theta' < 3/2$ there is $C_{\log}(\theta_0, \theta')$ with
the following property, for every $q$, every non-trivial $\chi$ mod $q$ and every $t \in \mathbb R$: if $L_\chi$ has no
zero in the open disc $D_t$ of centre $3/2 + it$ and radius $\rho = 3/2 - \theta_0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logderiv}
  \Bigl|\frac{L_\chi'}{L_\chi}(\sigma + it)\Bigr| \le C_{\log}(\theta_0, \theta') \bigl(\log(q + 2) + \log(|t| +
  4)\bigr) \qquad (\sigma \ge \theta').
\end{equation}
For $\sigma \ge 3/2$ this holds as soon as $C_{\log}(\theta_0, \theta') \ge -\zeta'/\zeta(3/2)$, by the Dirichlet series
of $L_\chi'/L_\chi$. For $\theta' \le \sigma < 3/2$ it follows from a lemma of Landau's type: if $f$ is analytic near
the closed unit disc, $f(0) = 1$, $|f| \le B_0$ on $|z| \le R$ with $B_0 > 1$, $f$ has no zero in $|z| < 1$ and
$0 < r' < r < R' < R < 1$, then
\[
  |f'/f(z)| \le \Bigl(\frac{16 r^2}{(r - r')^3} + \frac1{(R^2/R' - R') \log(R/R')}\Bigr) \log B_0 \qquad (|z| \le r').
\]
(The library uses the form of this lemma in PrimeNumberTheoremAnd \cite{pnt-plus}, \lean{FinalBound}, which combines a
Blaschke product, the Borel--Carath\'eodory inequality and Jensen's formula, and allows zeros.) Apply it to
$f(z) = L_\chi(3/2 + it + \rho z)/L_\chi(3/2 + it)$ at $z = (\sigma - 3/2)/\rho$, with $r' = (3/2 - \theta')/\rho$ and
with $r$, $R'$ and $R$ a quarter, a half and three quarters of the way from $r'$ to $1$; then
$f'/f(z) = \rho \, L_\chi'/L_\chi(\sigma + it)$. The bound $B_0$ comes from two estimates. First,
$|L_\chi(s)| \le 3(q + 1)(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 2)$ for $1/2 \le \operatorname{Re} s \le 3$, which follows from the
partial-summation bound $|L_\chi(s)| \le (q + 1)|s|/\operatorname{Re} s$ for $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$. Second,
$|L_\chi(3/2 + it)|^{-1} \le \zeta(3/2)$, from $1/L_\chi(s) = \sum \mu(n) \chi(n) n^{-s}$. Together they give
$|f| \le B_0 = 2 + 6(q + 1)\zeta(3/2)(|t| + 4)$ on $|z| \le R$, and $\log B_0 \ll \log(q + 2) + \log(|t| + 4)$. The
constant $C_{\log}(\theta_0, \theta')$ is explicit and of order $(\theta' - \theta_0)^{-3}$.

The disc $D_t$ lies in $\operatorname{Re} s > \theta_0$ and, since $\rho \le 1$, in $|\operatorname{Im} s - t| < 1$. So
\eqref{eq:logderiv} applies in two cases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] with $\theta_0 = 11/12$ and every $t$, by Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane};
\item[(ii)] with $\theta_0 = \theta$ and every $t$ with $|t - y| \le H$, by the hypothesis of the lemma.
\end{enumerate}
Since $|t| + 4 \le (|y| + 4)(1 + |t - y|)$ and $\log(1 + u) \le u$, we have
$\log(q + 2) + \log(|t| + 4) \le \lambda(1 + |t - y|)$. With \eqref{eq:mellin-decay} this gives, in either case,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-bound}
  |F(\sigma + it)| \le C_{\log}(\theta_0, \theta') \, \lambda \, B \, Q^\sigma (1 + |t - y|)^{-J - 2} \qquad (\theta'
  \le \sigma \le 2).
\end{equation}

\emph{The contour.} By Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane}, $F$ is holomorphic on $\sigma_* \le \operatorname{Re} s \le 2$, and by
\eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (i), with $\theta' = \sigma_*$, it is $O((1 + |t - y|)^{-2})$ there uniformly. So Cauchy's
theorem on the rectangles $[\sigma_*, 2] \times [-t_0, t_0]$, with $t_0 \to \infty$, moves the line of integration from $2$ to
$\sigma_*$. Split the line $\operatorname{Re} s = \sigma_*$ at the heights $y \pm H$. By the hypothesis of the lemma, $F$
is holomorphic on the rectangle $\theta + \delta \le \operatorname{Re} s \le \sigma_*$, $|\operatorname{Im} s - y| \le H$,
so by Cauchy's theorem the integral over $|t - y| \le H$ on the line $\sigma_*$ equals the integral over the same
heights on the line $\theta + \delta$ plus the integrals over the two horizontal sides at the heights $y \pm H$.

\emph{The pieces.} Put $C_1 = C_{\log}(11/12, \sigma_*)$ and $C_2 = C_{\log}(\theta, \theta + \delta)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item On the line $\sigma_*$ with $|t - y| \ge H$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (i) and
  $(1 + u)^{-J - 2} \le (1 + H)^{-J} (1 + u^2)^{-1}$ for $u \ge H$,
  $|F| \le C_1 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau/(1 + (t - y)^2)$. Each of the two tails contributes at most
  $\pi C_1 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau$.
\item On the line $\theta + \delta$ with $|t - y| \le H$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (ii),
  $|F| \le C_2 \lambda B Q^{\theta + \delta}/(1 + (t - y)^2)$, so this part contributes at most
  $\pi C_2 \lambda B Q^{\theta + \delta}$.
\item On each horizontal side, of length $11/12 - \theta \le 1$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (ii), with
  $Q^\sigma \le Q^{\sigma_*}$, $|F| \le C_2 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} (1 + H)^{-J - 2} \le C_2 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau$.
\end{itemize}
Adding the pieces and dividing by $2\pi$, the sum in \eqref{eq:Sw-bound} is at most
\[
  C_1 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau + \tfrac12 C_2 \lambda B Q^{\theta + \delta} + \tfrac1\pi C_2 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau
  \le (C_1 + C_2) \, \lambda \, B \bigl(Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{\sigma_*} \tau\bigr),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:Sw-bound} with $M_\delta(\theta) = C_{\log}(\theta, \theta + \delta) + C_{\log}(11/12, 11/12 +
\delta)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The kernel bound of Section~\ref{M-sec:two-bounds}}

Let $\delta' = \varepsilon' = 1/1000$, let $\psi$ be a non-trivial character mod $q$, and let $Y \ge 1$. By Mellin
inversion, $k_Y(\psi) = (2\pi i)^{-1} \int_{(2)} L(s, \psi) \Gamma(s) Y^s \, ds$. Since $L(\cdot, \psi)$ is entire and
$1/12 - \delta' > 0$, the line moves to $\operatorname{Re} s = 1/12 - \delta'$ without crossing a pole. There the
functional equation of the primitive character $\psi^*$ that induces $\psi$, of conductor $q^*$, gives
$|L(s, \psi^*)| \ll (q^*(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 2))^{5/12 + \delta'} |L(1 - s, \overline{\psi^*})|$, and $1 - s$ lies
on $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta'$. There Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane} gives the Lindelöf-type bound
$|L(s, \chi)|^{\pm 1} \ll ((q + 2)(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 4))^{\varepsilon'}$, uniformly in $q$ and $\chi \ne \chi_0$,
by the argument by which the Riemann hypothesis implies the Lindelöf hypothesis \cite[\S 14.2]{titchmarsh}. The lemma
of Landau's type above bounds $|L'/L(s, \chi)|$ by a multiple of $\log((q + 2)(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 4))$ on
$\operatorname{Re} s \ge 11/12 + \delta'/2$; the three-lines theorem, on rectangles of height comparable to that
logarithm, interpolates this against $|L'/L| \ll 1$ on $\operatorname{Re} s = 3/2$; and integrating $L'/L$ from
$\operatorname{Re} s = 3/2$, where $|\log |L|| \le \log \zeta(3/2)$, gives the bound for $|L|$ and $1/|L|$. The Euler
factors at the primes dividing $q$ are at most $2$ each, which is $\ll q^{\varepsilon'}$ in all, and together
$|L(s, \psi)| \ll (q(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 2))^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}$ on the new line. The decay of
$\Gamma$ makes the integral converge, and $|k_Y(\psi)| \le C Y^{1/12 - \delta'} (2q)^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}$
with $C$ independent of $q$, $\psi$ and $Y$.

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:family}}\label{app:family}

\begin{proof}
Let the family have the scale $P$, the set $S$, the coefficients $a_m$, the primes $p_1, \dots, p_K$ at the scales
$Q_i$, and the comparisons $C_1, \dots, C_J$ with forms $\ell_j$ and pivots $\mathrm{piv}(j)$. Let $\mathcal T$ be the
set of the $t = (m, p_1, \dots, p_K)$ with $m \in S$ and each $p_i$ a prime at most $2Q_i$; for $t \in \mathcal T$ put
$w(t) = a_m \prod_i f_0(p_i/Q_i)$ and $n(t) = m p_1 \cdots p_K$, and for a function $v$ on $\mathcal T$ put
\[
  \mathcal D(v) = \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} v(t) \, \Delta(n(t)), \qquad
  \Delta(n) = \mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \frac{\mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
The sum of the lemma is $\mathcal D(w \prod_j I_j)$, with $I_j(t) = \mathbf 1[C_j \text{ holds at } t]$. Let $F_0 = \max f_0$ and
$\eta = 10^{-5}$, the largest height exponent that Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2} allows.

\emph{Orthogonality.} For $a$ prime to $q$,
$\Delta(n) = \varphi(q)^{-1} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \overline\chi(a) \chi(n)$, so for every $v$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orth}
  |\mathcal D(v)| \le \frac1{\varphi(q)} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \Bigl|\sum_{t \in \mathcal T} v(t) \chi(n(t))\Bigr|.
\end{equation}

\emph{A smooth family.} Let $g_1, \dots, g_J$ be continuous and integrable, with integrable Fourier transforms
$\hat g_j(\xi) = \int g_j(u) e^{-2\pi i u \xi} \, du$, and let $T_0 \ge 1$ with
$2\pi \sum_j |e_{ji}| \, T_0 \le q^{\eta}$ for every $i$. We show that for $q$ larger than the $q_0$ of
Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2} (with $f_0$, $K_0$, $e_1$ and $\eta$),
\begin{multline}\label{eq:smooth-family}
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \Bigl|\sum_{t \in \mathcal T} w(t) \prod_j g_j(\ell_j(t)) \, \chi(n(t))\Bigr|
  \le q^{L - 1/50000} \mathcal N \prod_j \|\hat g_j\|_1 \\
  + \varphi(q) \, \mathfrak B \sum_j \int_{|\xi| > T_0} |\hat g_j| \prod_{l \ne j} \|\hat g_l\|_1,
\end{multline}
where
\[
  \mathcal N = \Bigl(\frac1P \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2\Bigr)^{1/2} \le A_0, \qquad
  \mathfrak B = \sum_m |a_m| \prod_i \sum_{p \le 2Q_i} f_0(p/Q_i).
\]
By Fourier inversion, and since the phase factorises over the variables,
\begin{gather*}
  \prod_j g_j(\ell_j) = \int_{\mathbb R^J} \prod_j \hat g_j(\xi_j) \, e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \ell_j} \, d\xi, \\
  e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \ell_j(t)} = e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \lambda_j(m)} \prod_i p_i^{2\pi i H_i}, \qquad
  H_i = \sum_j \xi_j e_{ji}.
\end{gather*}
So the inner sum in \eqref{eq:smooth-family} is
$\int \prod_j \hat g_j(\xi_j) \, W_\xi(\chi) \prod_i Q_i^{2\pi i H_i} S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i) \, d\xi$, where
$W_\xi(\chi) = \sum_m a_m e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \lambda_j(m)} \chi(m)$ and $h_i = -2\pi H_i$. Take absolute values,
sum over $\chi$, and bound the integrand for each $\xi$. If $|\xi_j| \le T_0$ for every $j$, then $|h_i| \le q^{\eta}$,
and Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2}, with the coefficients $a_m e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \lambda_j(m)}$, which have the moduli
$|a_m|$, and the heights $h_i$, gives
$\sum_\chi |W_\xi(\chi)| \prod_i |S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i)| \le q^{L - 1/50000} \mathcal N$. Otherwise some $|\xi_j| > T_0$, and the
trivial bound $\varphi(q) \mathfrak B$ holds. Integrating gives \eqref{eq:smooth-family}.

\emph{The cut-off.} Let $g$ be smooth, with $g = 0$ on $(-\infty, 0]$, $g = 1$ on $[1, \infty)$ and $0 \le g \le 1$, and
let $c_k = \int |g^{(k)}|$. For $0 < \nu \le 2\Lambda$ put $G_{\nu, \Lambda}(u) = g(u/\nu) - g((u - 2\Lambda)/\nu)$.
It is smooth, $0 \le G_{\nu, \Lambda} \le 1$, it vanishes outside $[0, 2\Lambda + \nu]$, and
$G_{\nu, \Lambda}(u) = g(u/\nu)$ for $u \le 2\Lambda$. So if $u \le \Lambda$ and a condition holds when $u > 0$ and
fails when $u < 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
  \bigl|\mathbf 1[\text{condition}] - G_{\nu, \Lambda}(u)\bigr| \le G_{\nu, \nu}(u + \nu),
\end{equation}
since the left side vanishes unless $0 \le u < \nu$, where $G_{\nu, \nu}(u + \nu) = 1$. Integration by parts
gives $(2\pi |\xi|)^k |\hat G_{\nu, \Lambda}(\xi)| \le \|G_{\nu, \Lambda}^{(k)}\|_1 \le 2\nu^{1 - k} c_k$, and
$|\hat G_{\nu, \Lambda}| \le 2\Lambda + \nu$. Hence
\begin{gather*}
  \|\hat G_{\nu, \Lambda}\|_1 \le \pi(2\Lambda + \nu) + \frac{c_2}{2\pi\nu}, \\
  \int_{|\xi| > T_0} |\hat G_{\nu, \Lambda}| \le \frac{4\pi c_k}{(2\pi)^k} \, \nu^{1 - k} T_0^{2 - k}
  \qquad (k \ge 2,\ T_0 \ge 1).
\end{gather*}

\emph{Sharp against smooth.} Let $\Lambda = \Lambda_0 \log q + 1$, so that $\ell_j \le \Lambda$ on $\mathcal T$, and put
$G_j = G_{\nu, \Lambda}(\ell_j)$ and $M_j = G_{\nu, \nu}(\ell_j + \nu)$. All these values lie in $[0, 1]$, so
by \eqref{eq:cutoff} $|\prod_j I_j - \prod_j G_j| \le \sum_j |I_j - G_j| \le \sum_j M_j$. Since
$|\Delta(n)| \le \Delta(n) + 2 \cdot \mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]/\varphi(q)$ and $f_0 \ge 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-smooth}
  \Bigl|\mathcal D\Bigl(w \prod_j I_j\Bigr)\Bigr| \le \Bigl|\mathcal D\Bigl(w \prod_j G_j\Bigr)\Bigr|
  + \sum_j \Bigl(|\mathcal D(|w| M_j)| + \frac2{\varphi(q)} \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} |w(t)| M_j(t)\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The first term and each $\mathcal D(|w| M_j)$ are smooth families: the first with $g_j = G_{\nu, \Lambda}$, the second with
the coefficients $|a_m|$ and the single function $G_{\nu, \nu}$ at the form $\ell_j + \nu$. Both are bounded by
\eqref{eq:orth} and \eqref{eq:smooth-family}. A trivial count would not do for them, since it would lose the factor $1/\varphi(q)$ that the condition
$n \equiv a$ gives. The last sum, which carries that factor already, is counted trivially, by the pivot. Fix $m$ and the primes
other than $p_{\mathrm{piv}(j)}$; then $\ell_j = c \pm \log p_{\mathrm{piv}(j)}$, and $M_j \ne 0$ forces
$-\nu \le \ell_j \le 2\nu$, which confines $p_{\mathrm{piv}(j)}$ to an interval $[Y, e^{3\nu} Y]$. At most
$(e^{3\nu} - 1) 2Q_{\mathrm{piv}(j)} + 1$ integers up to $2Q_{\mathrm{piv}(j)}$ lie in it, so
\[
  \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} |w(t)| M_j(t)
  \le F_0 \bigl((e^{3\nu} - 1) 2Q_{\mathrm{piv}(j)} + 1\bigr) \sum_m |a_m| \prod_{i \ne \mathrm{piv}(j)} \sum_{p \le
  2Q_i} f_0(p/Q_i).
\]

\emph{The parameters.} Put $\varepsilon_2 = 1/(400000(J_0 + 1))$, $\nu = q^{-\varepsilon_2}$, $T_0 = q^{1/200000}$,
$k = 1200003$ and $\delta = \min(\varepsilon_2, e_1)/2$. For large $q$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $2\pi J_0 E_0 T_0 \le q^{\eta}$, so \eqref{eq:smooth-family} applies, as $\sum_j |e_{ji}| \le J_0 E_0$;
\item $\pi(2\Lambda + 1) \le q^{\varepsilon_2}$, so $\|\hat G_{\nu, \Lambda}\|_1$ and $\|\hat G_{\nu, \nu}\|_1$
  are at most $C q^{\varepsilon_2}$ with $C = 1 + c_2/(2\pi)$, and a product of at most $J_0$ of them is at most
  $C^{J_0 + 1} q^{1/400000}$;
\item $\nu^{1 - k} T_0^{2 - k} = q^{\varepsilon_2(k - 1) - (k - 2)/200000} \le q^{-3}$, which bounds the tails.
\end{itemize}
The constants come from $\eta = 10^{-5}$: the norms lose at most $q^{\eta/4}$, $T_0 = q^{\eta/2}$ leaves room
for the factor $2\pi J_0 E_0$ below $q^{\eta}$, and $k = 1200003$ is the least $k$ with
$(k - 1)\eta/4 - (k - 2)\eta/2 \le -3$, so that the tails are at most $q^{-3}$ whatever $J_0$.
Moreover $\mathfrak B \le P A_0 (3F_0)^K \prod_i Q_i \le A_0 (3F_0 + 1)^{K_0} 2^{K_0} q^L$, since at most $3Q$ integers
lie in $[1, 2Q]$ for $Q \ge 1$ and $P \prod_i Q_i \le 2^{K_0} x$. Finally $e^{3\nu} - 1 \le 6\nu$ for
$\nu \le 1/3$ and $Q_{\mathrm{piv}(j)} \ge q^{e_1}$ give
$(12\nu Q_{\mathrm{piv}(j)} + 1) \prod_{i \ne \mathrm{piv}(j)} Q_i \le (12\nu + q^{-e_1}) \prod_i Q_i$. Putting these
into \eqref{eq:sharp-smooth}, with \eqref{eq:orth} and \eqref{eq:smooth-family}, and using $\varphi(q) \le q$, the sum
of the lemma is at most
\[
  \frac{A_0 q^L}{\varphi(q)} \bigl(a_1 q^{-7/400000} + a_2 q^{-1} + a_3(12 q^{-\varepsilon_2} + q^{-e_1})\bigr),
\]
where $a_1$, $a_2$ and $a_3$ depend only on $K_0$, $J_0$ and $F_0$. The three terms come from the smooth families, from
the tails and from the pivot counts. Each power of $q$ in the bracket is at most $q^{-2\delta}$, so for large $q$ the sum
is at most $A_0 q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$. Here $\delta$ depends only on $J_0$ and $e_1$, and Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2}
was used once for the smoothed family and once for each near-boundary family.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs of Propositions~\ref{M-prop:harman} and~\ref{M-prop:harman-ib}, and of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}}\label{app:harman}

\begin{proof}
Write $\mathcal P[y, z)$ for the set of primes in $[y, z)$, and say that $n$ is \emph{$z$-rough} if it has no prime
factor below $z$. For $\mathcal C = \mathcal A$ or $\mathcal B$ and $d$ whose prime factors are all at least $z$,
$S(\mathcal C_d, z)$ is the number of $z$-rough multiples of $d$ in $\mathcal C$. Throughout, $x = q^L$, and the
integers of $(x, 2x]$ are those of $(N_1, N_2]$ with $N_1 = \lfloor x \rfloor$ and $N_2 = \lfloor 2x \rfloor$. The tuples $(r_1, \dots, r_j)$ are ordered, and $r = r_1 \cdots r_j$.

\emph{The constants.} They are chosen before $q$ and depend only on $L$, $u_1$, $u_2$, $j$, $J_0$, $\Lambda_1$, $a_0$,
$b_0$ and $\varepsilon_H$. Let $c = (L - 1 - b_0)/4 > 0$. Let $C_0 \ge 1$ be an absolute constant with
$\sum_{p \in \mathcal P[y, y^2)} 1/p \le C_0$ for every $y \ge 1$; it exists by Mertens' theorem (the library
takes it from the prime number theorem). Put
$K_1 = (\log((L + 1)/a_0) + 1)^j$ and
\[
  \zeta_k = e^{2C_0} \cdot \frac{2 C_0^k}{k!} \cdot \exp\Bigl(e^{2C_0} \frac{2 C_0^{k + 1}}{(k + 1)!}\Bigr).
\]
Since $(2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k \to 0$, there is an even $k \ge 2$ with $2C_0^2 \le (k + 1)(k + 2)$, $c < (2k + 4) a_0$
and $(2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k \le \varepsilon_H c^2/(4e(K_1 + 1) a_0)$. Put $\varepsilon_w = c/(2k + 4)$, so
that $0 < \varepsilon_w < a_0$ and $(2k + 7) \varepsilon_w \le 2c$, and $S_0 = \lceil L/\varepsilon_w \rceil$. At $q$,
put $w = \lceil q^{\varepsilon_w} \rceil$, $z_0 = \lceil q^{a_0} \rceil$ and $X' = \lfloor q^{b_0} \rfloor$. Then
$w \le z_0$, and by hypothesis every tuple in the sum has $r_i \ge z_0$ and $r \le X'$.

\emph{The tree identity.} Let $r$ have all its prime factors at least $z_0$, and let $r \le X'$. For a set $P$ of primes
write $d_P = r \prod_{p \in P} p$, and let $\rho_z(n) = 1$ if $n$ is $z$-rough and $\rho_z(n) = 0$ otherwise. Then for
every $n$,
\begin{multline}\label{eq:tree}
  \mathbf 1[r \mid n] \, \rho_{z_0}(n)
  = \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P[w, z_0)} (-1)^{|P|} \, \mathbf 1[d_P \le X'] \, \mathbf 1[d_P \mid n] \, \rho_w(n) \\
  + \sum_{p \in \mathcal P[w, z_0)} \ \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P(p, z_0)} (-1)^{|P| + 1} \,
    \mathbf 1[d_P \le X' < p \, d_P] \, \mathbf 1[p \, d_P \mid n] \, \rho_p(n),
\end{multline}
where $\mathcal P(p, z_0)$ is the set of primes in $(p, z_0)$. This is what iterating Buchstab's identity
$S(\mathcal C_m, z) = S(\mathcal C_m, w) - \sum_{w \le p < z} S(\mathcal C_{mp}, p)$ from the root $r$ at $z_0$ gives,
when a child $mp$ is stopped as soon as $mp > X'$: the first sum collects the \emph{leaves} $S(\mathcal C_d, w)$,
$d = d_P \le X'$, and the second the \emph{stops} $S(\mathcal C_W, p)$, $W = p \, d_P$. It is proved directly,
for each $n$. If $r \nmid n$ or $n$ is not $w$-rough, every term vanishes. Otherwise let $Q$ be the set of the primes of
$[w, z_0)$ that divide $n$; the left side is $\mathbf 1[Q = \emptyset]$. Since the primes of $r$ are at least $z_0$, a
leaf $P$ counts $n$ exactly when $P \subseteq Q$, and a stop $(p, P)$ exactly when $p = \min Q$ and
$P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p\}$. If $Q = \emptyset$, only the leaf $P = \emptyset$ counts $n$, with
$\mathbf 1[r \le X'] = 1$. If $Q \ne \emptyset$, let $p_0 = \min Q$; grouping $P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p_0\}$ with
$P \cup \{p_0\}$, the right side is
\[
  \sum_{P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p_0\}} (-1)^{|P|}
  \bigl(\mathbf 1[d_P \le X'] - \mathbf 1[p_0 d_P \le X'] - \mathbf 1[d_P \le X' < p_0 d_P]\bigr) = 0.
\]
Multiplying \eqref{eq:tree} by $\mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]/\varphi(q)$ and summing over $n$ and over
the tuples, the sum of the proposition is the sum over the leaves of
$(-1)^{|P|}(S(\mathcal A_d, w) - S(\mathcal B_d, w)/\varphi(q))$ plus the sum over the stops of
$(-1)^{|P| + 1}(S(\mathcal A_W, p) - S(\mathcal B_W, p)/\varphi(q))$.

\emph{The stops.} A stop has $X' < W \le p X' < z_0 X'$, and $W$ is $p$-rough, since the primes of $r$ are at least
$z_0 > p$ and those of $P$ exceed $p$. So the $p$-rough multiples of $W$ in $(N_1, N_2]$ are the $n = Wm$ whose cofactor
$m$ is $p$-rough, and every such $m$ lies in $(M_1, M_2]$ with $M_1 = \lfloor N_1/(z_0 X') \rfloor$ and
$M_2 = \lfloor N_2/(X' + 1) \rfloor$. For large $q$, $q^{u_1 L} \le M_1$, since $5q^{u_1 L + a_0 + b_0} \le q^L$ by
$a_0 + b_0 < (1 - u_1)L$, and $M_2 \le q^{u_2 L}$, since $2q^{L - b_0} \le q^{u_2 L}$ by $(1 - u_2)L < b_0$. So the
cofactor $m$ lies in the window's range, and $W$ in its reflection. A stop has $|P| \le S_0$, since
$\prod P \ge w^{|P|} \ge q^{\varepsilon_w |P|}$ and $\prod P \le X' \le q^L$.

For each $s \le S_0$, the stops with $|P| = s$ form one sum of the kind that Lemma~\ref{M-lem:family-sharp} bounds, with
$m \in (M_1, M_2]$ and $a_m = 1$, whose primes are $r_1, \dots, r_j$, the elements $y_1 > \dots > y_s$ of $P$ and
$p$, and whose comparisons are the given ones on the $r_i$ and
\begin{gather*}
  y_1 > \dots > y_s > p, \quad y_1 < z_0, \quad p \ge w, \quad r y_1 \cdots y_s \le X' < r y_1 \cdots y_s \, p, \\
  p \le \min\{\ell \text{ prime} : \ell \mid m\}, \qquad N_1 < m r y_1 \cdots y_s p \le N_2 .
\end{gather*}
For $s = 0$ the condition $y_1 < z_0$ reads $p < z_0$. Each condition is of the form
$\lambda(m) + \sum_i e_i \log x_i > 0$ or $\ge 0$, with coefficients in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ and a pivot. There are at most
$J_0 + s + 7$ of them, and $\lambda(m) \le (\Lambda_1 + L + 1) \log q$ for $m \le M_2$. Wherever they
all hold, every prime is at least $w \ge 2q^{\varepsilon_w/2}$ and $m r y_1 \cdots y_s p \le 2x$. So
Lemma~\ref{M-lem:family-sharp}, with $K_0 = j + S_0 + 2$, $J_0 + S_0 + 7$ comparisons, $e_1 = \varepsilon_w/2$, $E_0 = 1$
and $\Lambda_1 + L + 1$ in place of $\Lambda_1$, bounds each of these $S_0 + 1$ sums by $q^{L - \delta_T}/\varphi(q)$,
with $\delta_T > 0$ depending only on the constants above. For large $q$ the stops therefore total at most
$q^{L - \delta_1}/\varphi(q)$, with $\delta_1 = \delta_T/2$.

\emph{A leaf.} Let $d$ have all its prime factors at least $w$. We show
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaf}
  \Bigl|S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr|
  \le \frac{2 \zeta_k (N_2 - N_1)}{d \varphi(q) \log w} + 24 \, w^{2k + 7}.
\end{equation}
If $(d, q) > 1$ both counts vanish, so let $(d, q) = 1$. Sieve by the primes below $w$, in the ranges
$T_i = \mathcal P[w^{2^{-i - 1}}, w^{2^{-i}})$ for $0 \le i < J_w = 2 \lfloor \log_2 w \rfloor + 2$, which cover them,
at the even levels $k_i = k + 2i$, which grow with $i$ so that the errors $C_0^{k_i}/k_i!$ below have a sum that does
not depend on $J_w$. Brun's sieve \cite{brun}, in the following form, is used, for disjoint sets $T_i$ of
primes and even levels $k_i \ge 2$. Let $\mathcal C$ be a finite set of integers,
$Y \ge 0$, and $g$ a function on the primes of $\bigcup T_i$ with $0 \le g \le 1$. Suppose that for every choice of
$s_i \subseteq T_i$, with $\Pi = \prod_i \prod_{p \in s_i} p$, the count $\#\{n \in \mathcal C : \Pi \mid n\}$ is
$Y \prod_i \prod_{p \in s_i} g(p)$ within $R$. Then $\#\{n \in \mathcal C : (n, \prod_i \prod_{T_i} p) = 1\}$ is
$Y \prod_i \prod_{p \in T_i} (1 - g(p))$ within
\[
  Y \sum_i e_{k_i}(T_i) \prod_{l \ne i} F_{k_l}(T_l)
  + (J_w + 1) \, R \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m},
\]
where $e_m(T) = \sum_{s \subseteq T,\ |s| = m} \prod_{p \in s} g(p)$ and $F_k(T) = \sum_{m \le k} (-1)^m e_m(T)$.
Bonferroni's inequalities say that $F_{k - 1}(T) \le \prod_{p \in T}(1 - g(p)) \le F_k(T)$ for every even $k$, and the
same for any weights in $[0, 1]$ in place of $g$. Take them for each $n \in \mathcal C$ with the weights
$\mathbf 1[p \mid n]$, and use the elementary inequality
$\prod_i \mathrm{hi}_i - \sum_i (\mathrm{hi}_i - \mathrm{lo}_i) \prod_{l \ne i} \mathrm{hi}_l \le \prod_i \varrho_i$ for
$\varrho_i \ge 0$ and $\mathrm{lo}_i \le \varrho_i \le \mathrm{hi}_i$ (the $\mathrm{lo}_i$, odd truncations, may be
negative). Summed over $n$, and expanded by the hypothesis, they place the count, up to $(J_w + 1)$ times
$R \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m}$, in the interval from $Y \prod_i F_{k_i} - Y \sum_i e_{k_i} \prod_{l \ne
i} F_{k_l}$ to $Y \prod_i F_{k_i}$. The model $Y \prod_i V_i$, with $V_i = \prod_{p \in T_i}(1 - g(p))$, lies in the
same interval: Bonferroni's inequalities for $g$ give $F_{k_i} - e_{k_i} = F_{k_i - 1} \le V_i \le F_{k_i}$, hence
$0 \le \prod_i F_{k_i} - \prod_i V_i \le \sum_i e_{k_i} \prod_{l \ne i} F_{k_l}$.

Take $g(p) = 1/p$ for $p \nmid q$ and $g(p) = 0$ for $p \mid q$. For $\mathcal A_d$, take
$\mathcal C = \{n \in (N_1, N_2] : n \equiv a,\ d \mid n\}$, $Y = (N_2 - N_1)/(dq)$ and $R = 1$: if no prime of $\Pi$
divides $q$, Lemma~\ref{M-lem:type1} with $M = d\Pi$ gives the count within $1$, and otherwise the count and the model
both vanish. For $\mathcal B_d$, take $\mathcal C = \{n \in (N_1, N_2] : (n, q) = 1,\ d \mid n\}$, $Y\varphi(q)$ and
$R = \varphi(q)$, and divide by $\varphi(q)$. Both main terms are then $YV$ with $V = \prod_{p < w,\ p \nmid q}(1 -
1/p)$, so that
\begin{gather*}
  \Bigl|S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr| \le 2(YG + \mathrm{rem}), \\
  G = \sum_i e_{k_i}(T_i) \prod_{l \ne i} F_{k_l}(T_l), \qquad
  \mathrm{rem} = (J_w + 1) \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m}.
\end{gather*}
Each $T_i$ lies in some $\mathcal P[y, y^2)$, so $\sum_{p \in T_i} g(p) \le C_0$ and, since $1 - t \ge e^{-2t}$ for
$0 \le t \le 1/2$, $V_i \ge e^{-2C_0}$. With $e_m \le C_0^m/m!$,
$F_{k_l} \le V_l + e_{k_l + 1} \le V_l (1 + e^{2C_0} C_0^{k_l + 1}/(k_l + 1)!)$ and
$\prod_{l \ne i} V_l \le e^{2C_0} V$, this gives
$G \le V e^{2C_0} \sum_i \frac{C_0^{k_i}}{k_i!} \prod_l \bigl(1 + e^{2C_0} \frac{C_0^{k_l + 1}}{(k_l + 1)!}\bigr)$. Since
$2C_0^2 \le (k + 1)(k + 2)$, the terms of $\sum_i C_0^{k + 2i}/(k + 2i)!$ decrease at least geometrically with ratio
$1/2$, so the sum is at most $2C_0^k/k!$; likewise, since $2C_0^2 \le (k + 2)(k + 3)$,
$\sum_l C_0^{k_l + 1}/(k_l + 1)! \le 2C_0^{k + 1}/(k + 1)!$; and with $\prod (1 + t_l) \le \exp \sum t_l$ we get
$G \le \zeta_k V$. So
the difference is at most $2\zeta_k YV + 2\,\mathrm{rem}$. Next, $V \le q/(\varphi(q) \log w)$. Indeed
$V \prod_{p < w,\ p \mid q}(1 - 1/p) = \prod_{p < w}(1 - 1/p)$, the second product on the left is at least
$\prod_{p \mid q}(1 - 1/p) = \varphi(q)/q$, and $\prod_{p < w}(1 - 1/p) \le 1/\log w$, since
$\prod_{p < w}(1 - 1/p)^{-1}$ is the sum of $1/m$ over the integers $m \ge 1$ whose prime factors are all below $w$,
which is at least $\sum_{m < w} 1/m \ge \log w$. So $YV \le (N_2 - N_1)/(d \varphi(q) \log w)$. Finally
$\sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m} \le 2 \max(1, |T_i|)^{k_i}$ with $|T_i| \le w^{2^{-i}}$,
$\sum_i 2^{-i}(k + 2i) \le 2k + 4$, $J_w + 1 \le 3w$ and $2^{J_w} \le 4w^2$ give $\mathrm{rem} \le 12 w^{2k + 7}$.

\emph{The leaves together.} In \eqref{eq:leaf}, $N_2 - N_1 \le x + 1 \le 2x$ and $\log w \ge \varepsilon_w \log q$.
With $1 \le X'/d$ for the remainder, the leaves total at most
\[
  \Bigl(\frac{4 \zeta_k x}{\varphi(q) \varepsilon_w \log q} + 24 \, w^{2k + 7} X'\Bigr)
  \sum_{(r_1, \dots, r_j)} \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P[w, z_0)} \frac1{r \prod P},
\]
and the double sum is $\sum 1/r \cdot \prod_{w \le p < z_0}(1 + 1/p)$. Each $r_i$ is a prime in $[q^{a_0}, 2q^L]$, so
for large $q$, by Mertens' theorem (in the library, the prime number theorem), $\sum 1/r \le K_1$ and $\prod_{w \le p < z_0}(1 + 1/p) \le e a_0/\varepsilon_w$.
The main part is then at most
\[
  \frac{4 e K_1 a_0 \zeta_k}{\varepsilon_w^2} \cdot \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log q}
  = \frac{4 e K_1 a_0 (2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k}{c^2} \cdot \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log q}
  \le \frac{\varepsilon_H \, x}{\varphi(q) \log q},
\]
by the choice of $k$. Since $w \le 2q^{\varepsilon_w}$ and $(2k + 7)\varepsilon_w \le 2c$, the remainder part is at most
$24 \cdot 2^{2k + 7} K_1 e a_0 \varepsilon_w^{-1} q^{b_0 + 2c} \le q^{b_0 + 3c} = q^{L - 1 - c} \le q^{L - c}/\varphi(q)$
for large $q$, using $\varphi(q) \le q$.

\emph{Conclusion.} The sum of the proposition is at most
$\varepsilon_H x/(\varphi(q) \log q) + (q^{L - c} + q^{L - \delta_1})/\varphi(q)$, which for large $q$ is at most
$\varepsilon_H x/(\varphi(q) \log q) + q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ with $\delta = \min(c, \delta_1)/2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The spline weights of Section~\ref{M-sec:type1}}

For integers $A < B$, $\ell \ge 1$ and $r \ge 1$ let $w(k)$ be $\ell^{-r}$ times the number of
$(i, j_1, \dots, j_r)$ with $A < i \le B$, $0 \le j_s < \ell$ and $i + j_1 + \cdots + j_r = k$. It is the average over
the $\ell^r$ tuples $j$ of the indicators of the intervals $(A + \sum j_s, B + \sum j_s]$, so $0 \le w \le 1$,
$w = 1$ on $(A + r(\ell - 1), B]$, $w = 0$ off $(A, B + r(\ell - 1)]$, and $\sum_k w(k) = B - A$. Its transform
factors: $\hat w(h) = \ell^{-r} \bigl(\sum_{A < i \le B} e_q(hi)\bigr) \bigl(\sum_{j < \ell} e_q(hj)\bigr)^r$. For $h$
the residue of an integer $t$ with $0 < |t| \le q/2$, a geometric sum of length $Y$ at $e_q(t)$ is at most
$q/(2|t|)$ in size, so $|\hat w(h)| \le (B - A)(q/(2\ell|t|))^r$, which is at most $(B - A)(q/(2\ell(H + 1)))^r$ when
$|t| > H$. If each residue class mod $q$ holds at most $M$ elements of the support, Parseval's identity and
$|w_k w_{k'}| \le \tfrac12(|w_k|^2 + |w_{k'}|^2)$ over the pairs in a class give
$\sum_h |\hat w(h)|^2 \le qM \sum_k |w(k)|^2$; for the support, an interval of length $n = B - A + r(\ell - 1)$,
$M = \lfloor n/q \rfloor + 1$. Finally $|E_w| \le d(q)$: for the indicator of an interval $(C, D]$, Möbius inversion over
the divisors of $q$ writes $E_w$ as $\sum_{d \mid q} \mu(d)\bigl((\lfloor D/d \rfloor - \lfloor C/d \rfloor) -
(D - C)/d\bigr)$, and each bracket is less than $1$ in size; the spline averages such intervals.

\subsection*{Proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}}

\begin{proof}
Write $R(m)$ for the indicator of the integers with no prime factor below $w$, $\mathrm{rng}(d, m)$ for that of
$N_1 < dm \le N_2$, and $c(\mathbf y_1, \mathbf y_2)$ for the indicator of the comparisons. For $d$ with no prime factor
below $w$, $S(\mathcal A_d, w) - S(\mathcal B_d, w)/\varphi(q) = \sum_m \mathrm{rng}(d, m) R(m)\, \vartheta(dm)$.

\emph{Brun's weights.} Sift by the primes below $w$ in the ranges $T_i$ of the proof of
Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman}, at the even levels $k_i = k + 2i$. Bonferroni's inequalities give weights $\lambda^+$ above
and $\lambda^-$ below $R$: $\sum_\tau \lambda^-_\tau \mathbf 1[f_\tau \mid m] \le R(m) \le
\sum_\sigma \lambda^+_\sigma \mathbf 1[f_\sigma \mid m]$, with moduli $f \le w^{2k + 4}$, $\sum |\lambda| \le 2\,\mathrm{rem}$
and $\sum |\lambda|/f \le (3 + 4\log q)^{C}$ for a constant $C$, and the gap $\Gamma$ between the two weights' main terms,
$\sum_\sigma \lambda^+_\sigma \mathbf 1[(f_\sigma, q) = 1]/f_\sigma - \sum_\tau \lambda^-_\tau \mathbf 1[(f_\tau, q) = 1]/f_\tau$,
is at most $\zeta_k q/(\varphi(q) \log w)$ in size, as the gap $G$ of that proof.

\emph{The sandwich.} Split each $\lambda$ into its positive and negative parts. With splines $w_- \le \mathrm{rng}(d
f_\sigma, \cdot) \le w_+$, chosen below for each block, the count of $\mathcal A$ is at most $\sum_\sigma
(\lambda^+_\sigma)_+ \sum_k w_+(k) \mathbf 1[d f_\sigma k \equiv a] - \sum_\sigma (\lambda^+_\sigma)_- \sum_k w_-(k)
\mathbf 1[d f_\sigma k \equiv a]$, and at least the same expression with the weights $\lambda^-$ and the splines
exchanged; and the same holds for $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$. The difference between the $\mathcal A$- and the $\mathcal
B/\varphi(q)$-counts is thus bounded by two kinds of term: the difference of the main terms, which carries the gap
$\Gamma$ and the difference of the splines' masses, and bilinear sums $\sum \lambda \sum_k w(k)\, \vartheta(d f k)$.

\emph{Blocks.} For $\rho = 1 + q^{-s}$ group the pairs $(d_1 f, d_2)$, $d_1 = \prod \mathbf y_1$ and $d_2 = \prod \mathbf y_2$,
by the blocks $\lfloor \log(d_1 f)/\log \rho \rfloor$ and $\lfloor \log d_2/\log \rho \rfloor$; a block pair
$(\beta_1, \beta_2)$ puts $d_1 f d_2$ in $[\rho^{\beta_1 + \beta_2}, \rho^{\beta_1 + \beta_2 + 2})$. At the scale
$D' = \rho^{\beta_1 + \beta_2}$ one majorant $w_+$, equal to $1$ on $(\lfloor N_1/(D'\rho^2) \rfloor, \lfloor N_2/D' \rfloor]$,
and one minorant $w_-$, supported in $(\lfloor N_1/D' \rfloor, \lfloor N_2/(D'\rho^2) \rfloor]$, both splines of step
$\ell = \lfloor \eta N_1/(rD'\rho^2) \rfloor + 1$ with $\eta = q^{-s}$, sandwich $\mathrm{rng}(d, \cdot)$ for every $d$ of
the block pair, and their masses differ by at most $(N_1 + N_2)(1 - \rho^{-2})/D' + 2\eta N_1/(D'\rho^2) + 2$.

\emph{A block pair.} On a block pair Lemma~\ref{M-lem:typeIB} applies, with $\mathcal D_1$ the values $d_1 f$, whose
coefficients aggregate the fibres $\{(\mathbf y_1, \sigma) : \prod \mathbf y_1 \cdot f_\sigma = d_1 f\}$ (at most $(J_w
+ 1) d(d_1 f)$ elements, by the uniqueness of the factorisation), $\mathcal D_2$ the values $d_2$ (a decreasing tuple of
primes is determined by its product), $P_\iota = \rho^{\beta_\iota + 1}$, $D = \lfloor P_2 \rfloor$, $H = \lfloor (q -
1)/(2D) \rfloor$, so that $2HD < q$ and $q/(2\ell(H + 1)) \le D/\ell$, and $m = \lfloor P_1/q \rfloor + 1$, since the
values $d_1 f \le P_1$ with a given inverse lie in one residue class. The comparisons are replaced by smooth cut-offs of
their forms, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:family} (\S S2): the smooth part is separated by Fourier inversion into
bilinear forms of the lemma's kind, at a cost of the cut-offs' Fourier masses, and the part near the boundaries is
treated as in \S S2: with $|\vartheta(n)| \le \vartheta(n) + 2 \cdot \mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]/\varphi(q)$, the part with
$\vartheta$ is again a bilinear form of the lemma's kind, with the narrow cut-offs, and the part with $2/\varphi(q)$ is
counted along the pivots. A block pair is so bounded by its Fourier masses and fibre sizes times
\[
  \sqrt{P_1 P_2 (P_1/q + 1) F (n/q + 1) n} + (B - A)(P_2/\ell)^r P_1 P_2 + P_1 P_2\, d(q)/\varphi(q),
\]
with $n$ the length of the spline, plus the counted parts near the boundaries.

\emph{The bound in powers of $q$.} Take $s = \min\bigl((\kappa_1 - \kappa_0)/(2(J_0 + 6)),\ \kappa_2/2,\ \varepsilon_w,\
1/(4(J_0 + 6))\bigr)$, $r = \lceil 4/\kappa_2 \rceil$ and $F = \lfloor C_d q^s \rfloor$, a bound for the number of
divisors of the integers below $q$. On a block pair that holds a leaf, with $X_1 = q^{L - 1 - \kappa_1}$, $X_2 = q^{L -
\kappa_2}$ and $X_3 = q^{L - 1 - \kappa_0}$ in the lemma's conditions, the three terms are at most $\sqrt{816 C_d}\,
q^{L - 1 - (\kappa_1 - \kappa_0 - s)/2}$, $16(16r)^r q^{L - 2}$ and $4C_d q^{L - 1/2 + s}/\varphi(q)$; a block pair that
holds no leaf contributes nothing. There are at most $(3Lq^s \log q)^2$ block pairs, the Fourier masses of at most $J_0$
cut-offs are at most $(J_0 + 1)(Cq^s)^{J_0}$, and the fibres at most $q^{c_1}$ with $c_1 < s/4$; so the bilinear sums
total at most $C(\log q)^2 q^{L - 3s/4}/\varphi(q)$, by the choice of $s$.

\emph{The main terms.} The difference of the main terms is $(N_2 - N_1)/q \cdot \Gamma \cdot \sum c/d$, plus the
transition from the splines' masses, at most $(V\Theta/q) \sum c/d + (4W/q + 2W d(q)/\varphi(q)) \sum c$ with $\Theta
\le 11 q^{L - s}$, $V = \sum |\lambda|/f$ and $W = \sum |\lambda|$. Since $(d_1 d_2)^2 \le X_1 X_2$ on the leaves, $\sum
c \le q^{L - 1/2} B^{K_1 + K_2}$, with $B$ bounding the sums of $1/p$ over the primes' ranges; and the counted parts
near the boundaries are at most $(12\theta + 1/w)B^{K_1 + K_2 - 1}$ times $2(1 + \eta)\rho^2 N_2 V/\varphi(q)$, by the
pivots, since every prime is at least $w$, with $\theta = q^{-s}$. All these are at most $C(\log q)^2 q^{L -
3s/4}/\varphi(q)$, which is below $q^{L - s/4}/\varphi(q)$ for large $q$. With $|\Gamma| \le \zeta_k q/(\varphi(q) \log
w)$ this is the lemma, with $\delta = s/4$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman-ib}}

\begin{proof}
The notation is that of the proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman}.

\emph{The constants.} Let $c' = \min\bigl(1/16,\ (L - 2)/16,\ (L - 1 - D_1)/16,\ (L - D_1 - 2D_2)/16\bigr) > 0$. Take an
even $k$ as in that proof, with $c'$ in place of $c$, and put $\varepsilon_w = c'/(2k + 4)$, so that
$(2k + 4)\varepsilon_w = c' \le 1/16$, and $s_{\max} = \lceil (L + 2)/\varepsilon_w \rceil$. At $q$, put
$w = \lceil q^{\varepsilon_w} \rceil$ and $z_0 = \lceil q^{a_0} \rceil$, and let $(P_1, P_2] = (\lceil q^{u_1 L} \rceil,
\lfloor q^{u_2 L} \rfloor]$ be the window's range and $(W_0, W_1] = (\lceil N_2/q^{u_2 L} \rceil,
\lfloor N_1/\lceil q^{u_1 L} \rceil \rfloor]$ its reflection, where Lemma~\ref{M-lem:chains}(c) applies.

\emph{The patterns.} Split the tuples by the position of $r_{\mathcal S_0} = \prod_{i \in \mathcal S_0} r_i$:
$r_{\mathcal S_0} \le P_1$, $r_{\mathcal S_0}$ in the window's range, $P_2 < r_{\mathcal S_0} \le W_0$, $r_{\mathcal
S_0}$ in the reflection, $r_{\mathcal S_0} > W_1$. The last is empty for large $q$, since $r_{\mathcal S_0} \le
q^{\sigma^+}$ and $\sigma^+ < (1 - u_1)L$. Each of the others is a family of the same kind, with two more comparisons.

\emph{$r_{\mathcal S_0}$ in the window or its reflection.} By Buchstab's identity,
$S(\mathcal A_r, z_0) = S(\mathcal A_r, r_j) + \sum_{z_0 \le p < r_j} S(\mathcal A_{rp}, p)$, and the same for
$\mathcal B$; both sums are of the kind of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:chains}, (b) with $\mathcal I = \mathcal S_0$ or (c) with
$\mathcal J = \mathcal S_0$, the second with the chain extended by $p$. So these patterns contribute at most
$2(q^{L - \delta_1} + q^{L - \delta_2})/\varphi(q)$.

\emph{$r_{\mathcal S_0}$ below the window, or between it and its reflection.} Let $E = P_1 + 1$ in the first case and $E
= W_0 + 1$ in the second. Iterate Buchstab's identity from $z_0$ down to $w$, stopping a branch as soon as $r_{\mathcal
S_0}$ times its primes reaches $E$. As in \eqref{eq:tree}, with the condition $r_{\mathcal S_0} \prod P < E$ in place of
$d_P \le X'$, this writes $S(\mathcal A_r, z_0)$ as the sum over the \emph{leaves}, the sets $P$ of primes of $[w, z_0)$
with $r_{\mathcal S_0} \prod P < E$, of $(-1)^{|P|} S(\mathcal A_{r \prod P}, w)$, plus the sum over the \emph{stops},
the pairs $(p, P)$ with $P$ a leaf of primes above $p$ and $r_{\mathcal S_0} \prod P \cdot p \ge E$, of $(-1)^{|P| + 1}
S(\mathcal A_{r \prod P \cdot p}, p)$; the identity is proved for each $n$ in the same way, since the products
$r_{\mathcal S_0} \prod_{p \in P,\, p \ge y} p$ of the upper parts of a set grow by factors below $z_0$ as $y$
decreases, so that a set with $r_{\mathcal S_0} \prod P \ge E$ has exactly one first upper part that stops. A stop has
$E \le r_{\mathcal S_0} \prod P \cdot p < E z_0$, in the window's range, or in its reflection, for large $q$, since $a_0
< (u_2 - u_1)L$. So for each size of $P$, at most $s_{\max}$ of them, the stops form one sum that
Lemma~\ref{M-lem:chains}(b), or~(c), bounds, with $\mathcal S_0$ and the primes of $P$ and $p$ as $\mathcal I$, or
$\mathcal J$.

\emph{The leaves.} A leaf has $\prod P < E/r_{\mathcal S_0} \le 4q^t$ for large $q$, by the hypotheses on $t$, since
$r_{\mathcal S_0} \ge q^{\sigma^-}$, and $r_{\mathcal S_0} > P_2$ in the second case; and $d = r \prod P$. Those with $d
\le q^{L - 1 - 4c'}$ are counted by \eqref{eq:leaf}, and total at most $(2\zeta_k (N_2 - N_1)/(\varphi(q) \log w) + 24
w^{2k + 7} q^{L - 1 - 4c'}) \sum 1/d$. The others are split into $d_1$, the product of the $r_i$ with $i \in \mathcal
S_1$ and, if $\iota = 1$, of the primes of $P$, and $d_2$, the rest. Then $d_1 \le 4q^{D_1}$ and $d_2 \le 4q^{D_2}$, so
$d_1 w^{2k + 4} \le q^{L - 1 - 8c'}$ and $d_1 w^{2k + 4} d_2^2 \le q^{L - 8c'}$ for large $q$, by the choice of $c'$;
the primes of $P$ are below $z_0 \le r_j$, so the merged tuples are strictly decreasing; and the comparisons with no
nonzero coefficient, constant on the family, can be dropped. So Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}, with $\kappa_1 = \kappa_2 =
8c'$ and $\kappa_0 = 4c'$, bounds the large leaves, for each pair $(K_1, K_2)$ of tuple lengths, by $\zeta_k (N_2 -
N_1)/(\varphi(q) \log w) \sum 1/d + q^{L - \delta_3}/\varphi(q)$.

\emph{Conclusion.} As in the proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman}, $\sum 1/d \le K_1 e a_0/\varepsilon_w$ for large $q$,
so the main parts of the leaves total at most $\varepsilon_H x/(\varphi(q) \log q)$ by the choice of $k$, and the
remainders are at most $q^{L - c'}/\varphi(q)$. The sum of the proposition is therefore at most
$\varepsilon_H x/(\varphi(q) \log q) + q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ for large $q$, with
$\delta = \min(c', \delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3)/2$, $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$ those of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:chains}, (b)
and~(c), for these families, and $\delta_3$ that of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:rough}}\label{app:rough}

\begin{proof}
Write $c_* = 0.565073$ and $\bar\omega = 0.5671433$, $u = \log 2y/\log z$, and say that an integer is $z$-rough if it has no
prime factor below $z$. Four facts are used.

\emph{(a) Prime sums.} By the prime number theorem with an error $o(t/\log t)$ (in the library, the bound
$\psi(t) - t = O(t^{11/12 + \varepsilon})$, which it derives from the case of $\zeta$ of Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane};
the classical theorem would do), for every $\varepsilon_3 > 0$,
$|\theta(t) - t| \le \varepsilon_3 t/\log t$ for large $t$. With partial summation this gives, for large $a \le b$,
\[
  \sum_{a < p \le b} \frac1{p \log p} \le \frac1{\log a} - \frac1{\log b} + \frac{4\varepsilon_3}{\log^2 a},
\]
and, if $2 \log b \le \mathcal L$,
\[
  \sum_{a < p \le b} \frac1{p(\mathcal L - \log p)}
  \le \frac{1 + \varepsilon_3/\log a}{\mathcal L} \log \frac{\log b \, (\mathcal L - \log a)}{\log a \, (\mathcal L -
  \log b)} + \frac{6\varepsilon_3}{\mathcal L \log^2 a};
\]
and, for large $y$, there are at most $(1 + \varepsilon_3) y/\log 2y$ primes in $(y, 2y]$.

\emph{(b) Buchstab's inequality.} For $y \ge 2$,
\[
  \Phi(y, z) \le \#\{p : y < p \le 2y\} + \sum_{z \le p \le \sqrt{2y}} \Phi(y/p, p),
\]
since a $z$-rough integer $m \in (y, 2y]$ that is not prime has its least prime factor $p$ in $[z, \sqrt{2y}]$, and
$m/p$ is a $p$-rough integer in $(y/p, 2y/p]$; the map $m \mapsto (p, m/p)$ is injective. Also $\Phi(y, z) = 0$ if
$y \ge 1$ and $2y < z$, since every integer of $(y, 2y]$ is then at least $2$ and below $z$.

\emph{(c) Buchstab's function on $[2, 3]$.} For $u > 1$, $1 + \log(u - 1) \le \bar\omega u$: the inequality $\log t \le t - 1$ at
$t = \bar\omega(u - 1)$ gives $1 + \log(u - 1) \le \bar\omega(u - 1) - \log \bar\omega \le \bar\omega u$, since $-\log \bar\omega \le \bar\omega$, that is $\bar\omega e^{\bar\omega} \ge 1$, which
holds because $e^{\bar\omega} \ge \sum_{m \le 12} \bar\omega^m/m! > 1/\bar\omega$. So $\omega^* \le \bar\omega$ on $[2, 3]$, and since $c_* < \bar\omega$,
$\omega^* \le \bar\omega$ on $[2, \infty)$; and $\omega^* \le c_*$ on $[3, \infty)$, since $\omega^*(3) = (1 + \log 2)/3 < c_*$.

\emph{(d) A delay inequality.} Let $M(u) = \log(u - 1)$ for $2 \le u \le 3$ and
$M(u) = \log 2 + \bar\omega \min(u - 3, 1) + c_* \max(u - 4, 0)$ for $u > 3$, which is $\int_1^{u - 1} \omega^*(t)\, dt$ with
$\omega^*$ replaced by $\bar\omega$ on $[2, 3]$. Then $1 + M(u) \le u \omega^*(u)$ for $u \ge 2$. For $u \le 3$ it is an
equality. For $u > 3$, $\omega^*(u) = c_*$, and $1 + M(u) - c_* u$ is affine on $[3, 4]$, with slope $\bar\omega - c_* > 0$, and
constant on $[4, \infty)$; so it suffices that it is at most $0$ at $u = 4$, that is $1 + \log 2 + \bar\omega \le 4c_*$, which
holds since $\log 2 < 0.6931471808$ and $2.2602904808 < 2.260292 = 4c_*$.

We prove by induction on the integer $k \ge 0$ the statement $\mathrm C(k)$: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is
$z_1$ such that $\Phi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$ for all $z \ge z_1$ and all $y \ge 1$ with
$u \le k$. $\mathrm C(0)$ holds trivially, since $u > 0$, and the lemma is $\mathrm C(\lceil U \rceil)$. Assume
$\mathrm C(k)$, let $0 < \varepsilon \le 1$ (which suffices, since the bound for one $\varepsilon$ implies it for every
larger one) and $\varepsilon_3 = \varepsilon/100$, let $z$ be large in terms of
$\varepsilon$ and of the $z_1$ of $\mathrm C(k)$ for $\varepsilon/4$, and let $y \ge 1$ with
$\mathcal L = \log 2y = u \log z \le (k + 1) \log z$. If $2y < z$, then $\Phi(y, z) = 0$. Otherwise $u \ge 1$, $y \ge
z/2$ is large, and (a) and (b) give
\[
  \Phi(y, z) \le \Bigl(1 + \frac\varepsilon4\Bigr) \frac{y}{\mathcal L} + \sum_{z \le p \le \sqrt{2y}} \Phi(y/p, p).
\]
If $u < 2$, then $\sqrt{2y} < z$, the sum is empty, and
$\Phi(y, z) \le (1 + \varepsilon/4) y/(u \log z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$. Let $u \ge 2$. A prime
$p \in [z, \sqrt{2y}]$ has $y/p \ge 1$ and
$\log(2y/p) = \mathcal L - \log p \le k \log p$, since $\mathcal L \le (k + 1) \log p$. So $\mathrm C(k)$ gives
$\Phi(y/p, p) \le (\omega^*(u_p) + \varepsilon/4) \, y/(p \log p)$ with $u_p = (\mathcal L - \log p)/\log p$. The term
$p = z$, if $z$ is prime, contributes at most $2y/(z \log z)$, since $\omega^* \le 1$. By (a), the parts
$\varepsilon/4$ of the other terms contribute at most $(\varepsilon/4)(1/\log z + 4\varepsilon_3/\log^2 z) \, y$. For
the parts $\omega^*(u_p)$: if $p \le (2y)^{1/4}$ then $u_p \ge 3$ and $\omega^*(u_p) \le c_*$; if
$(2y)^{1/4} < p \le (2y)^{1/3}$ then $2 \le u_p < 3$ and $\omega^*(u_p) \le \bar\omega$; and if $(2y)^{1/3} < p \le (2y)^{1/2}$ then
$1 \le u_p < 2$ and $\omega^*(u_p)/(p \log p) = 1/(p(\mathcal L - \log p))$, by the definition of $\omega^*$ on
$[1, 2)$. By (a), applied on $(z, \sqrt{2y}]$ if
$u \le 3$, on $(z, (2y)^{1/3}]$ and $((2y)^{1/3}, \sqrt{2y}]$ if $3 < u \le 4$, and also with the first range split at
$(2y)^{1/4}$ if $u > 4$,
\[
  \sum_{z < p \le \sqrt{2y}} \frac{\omega^*(u_p)}{p \log p} \le \frac{M(u) + 12\varepsilon_3}{\mathcal L}
  + \frac{8\varepsilon_3}{\log z}.
\]
Collecting the terms, with $\mathcal L = u \log z$,
\[
  \Phi(y, z) \le \frac y{\log z} \Bigl(\frac{1 + M(u)}u + \frac{\varepsilon/4 + 12\varepsilon_3}u + \frac2z
  + 8\varepsilon_3 + \frac\varepsilon4 + \frac{\varepsilon \varepsilon_3}{\log z}\Bigr).
\]
By (d) the first term is at most $\omega^*(u)$, and since $u \ge 1$ and $z$ is large the others add up to at most
$0.96\varepsilon$. So $\Phi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$, which proves $\mathrm C(k + 1)$.
\end{proof}

\emph{A fact used in Section~\ref{M-sec:certificate}.} The bound $\Omega$ of that section, though not the proof above,
uses that on $[2, 3]$, $\omega^*(u) = (1 + \log(u - 1))/u$ increases up to $2.76$ and decreases from $2.77$ on. For
$2 \le u \le u' \le 2.76$, $\omega^*(u) \le \omega^*(u')$ is $(u' - u)(1 + \log(u - 1)) \le u \log((u' - 1)/(u - 1))$:
the right side is at least $u(u' - u)/(u' - 1)$, by $\log t \ge 1 - 1/t$, and the left at most $(u' - u)u/1.76$, by
$\log t \le t - 1$ at $t = (u - 1)/1.76$ and $\log 1.76 \le 1/1.76$. For $2.77 \le u \le u'$ the reverse inequality
follows in the same way from $\log((u' - 1)/(u - 1)) \le (u' - u)/(u - 1)$ and
$\log(u - 1) \ge \log 1.77 \ge 1/1.77 \ge 1/(u - 1)$. The two constants come from the exponential's series.

implies $16h \le v$ and $4h + 2h_C = 12h \le v$. The condition $2 < L$ of the theorem is a field of

Proposition~\ref{M-prop:certificate} are checked by \lean{decide} (\lean{SieveCert.gridOK22000E},
\lean{SieveCert.gridOKC22000E} and \lean{Linnik22000E.deepGrid22000E}). The prime number theorem of
Sections~\ref{M-sec:type1} to~\ref{M-sec:proof} is derived from the case of $\zeta$ of Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane}, in
the form $\psi(t) - t = O(t^{11/12 + \varepsilon})$ (\lean{HalfPlanePNT.\allowbreak psi\_isBigO\_eleven\_twelfths}).

\subsection*{The library's proof of Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane}}

The library proves Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane} as \lean{Eis.LFunction\_ne\_zero\_of\_re\_gt}, along the route of OpenAI's
companion preprint \cite{openai-companion}, on its own toolchain; it imports nothing from OpenAI's release
(\file{formalisations/openai-half-plane.md}). The route has five steps. Power savings for the smoothed Möbius sums of the
Dedekind zeta function of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ give the half-plane. The target is embedded in a family of sextic
twists, over which a mean square bounds it. Poisson summation and the Gauss--Jacobi identities turn the Möbius function
times sextic Gauss sums into cubic Gauss sums. Kubota's cubic theta function \cite{kubota} supplies the transformation,
with Patterson's formula for its coefficients \cite{patterson}, Dunn and Radziwi\l\l's cusp expansions
\cite{dunn-radziwill}, and the quadratic large sieve over $\mathbb Z[\omega]$ \cite{hb-quadratic, goldmakher-louvel},
which bounds the dual mean square. Möbius inversion removes the cube factors and gives the exponent $11/12$. The chain
gives $\zeta$ and $L(s, \chi_{-3})$ first, then the real characters, the characters of order dividing $6$, and every
other character through the family twisted by a character modulo $4q$. The classical results that the route uses,
among them cubic and sextic reciprocity \cite{ireland-rosen}, are theorems of the library. The import cone of the
theorem in the library has $228$ files and $108696$ lines of Lean, all of them in \texttt{lib/core}.

\subsection*{Independent checking of the inputs}

Independent checking of Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane} is partial, and little of it is independent of OpenAI. OpenAI's
release has its own Lean formalisation of the main preprint's result. Its catalogue \texttt{lean/formalization.yaml}
\cite{openai-lean}, read on 25 October 2026, lists that preprint among the papers with a formalised main result, and
names the theorem \lean{OAI.DirichletCharacter.\allowbreak LFunction\_ne\_zero\_of\_seven\_eighths\_lt\_re}. Its
statement is for every modulus $q \ge 1$, every character mod $q$ and every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re} s > 7/8$,
against Mathlib's $L$-function, with the same exception at $s = 1$ for the trivial character, so it implies
Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane} as stated here. It is OpenAI's own record, not an independent one, and the catalogue gives
its scope as partial progress and its review status as unchecked. The author read that release's statements,
scanned its Lean and rebuilt it on 7 October 2026, which showed its two main theorems to depend on Lean's standard
three axioms only, but did not read its proofs in full. This library's proof of Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane} is a new
formalisation of the companion preprint's route, on a different Lean and Mathlib; it imports nothing from the release
and was not compared with it. It is a machine-checked proof of the companion preprint's theorem, but the route is
OpenAI's and the formalisation is the author's, written with the AI assistance described in the paper's acknowledgements, so
it is not a review of the mathematics that is independent of OpenAI and of the author. The check recorded by the tracker AIProofWatch \cite{aiproofwatch} on 25 October 2026 concerns the
specialisation to the Riemann zeta function at $7/8$, not Dirichlet $L$-functions in general. So the author knows of no
review of the mathematics for Dirichlet $L$-functions in general that is independent of OpenAI and of the author.

For Input~\ref{M-inp:density} the author knows of no independent check either. The library's proof of Chen, Gupta and
Li's Proposition~3.1 is the author's formalisation of their text, written in the same way. Lean's kernel checks that
proof; it does not check that its statement is theirs.

\subsection*{What the kernel checks}

The library's own sources, the directories \texttt{lib/core}, \texttt{lib/pnt}, \texttt{lib/dh},
\texttt{lib/zeta23} and \texttt{lib/check}, contain no \lean{sorry}, no declared axiom and no \lean{native\_decide},
which \texttt{scripts/check\_hygiene.py} checks. The library vendors 19 files of PrimeNumberTheoremAnd \cite{pnt-plus}
and, in \texttt{lib/vendor/zeta23}, 59 files of the \texttt{zeta23} folder of Anthropic's \texttt{formal-math}
\cite{formal-math}, each ported to the
library's toolchain by a patch that the repository records. That scan does not cover them; but the import cone of
Theorem~\ref{M-thm:main} contains 15 of the PrimeNumberTheoremAnd files and none of the \texttt{zeta23} ones, and not
\texttt{Wiener.lean}, the vendored file that contains \lean{sorry}. In any case the axiom check of the theorems in the
table of Appendix~\ref{M-app:claims} covers the whole dependency cone of each, and a \lean{sorry} in it would appear there as \lean{sorryAx}.

The certificate of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:certificate} is checked by kernel evaluation, \lean{decide +kernel}, and not
by compiled code, split into 1136 parts in 114 files. The $272$ rows of the grid form $229$ ranges of consecutive rows;
$32$ of the ranges are parts, and each of the other $197$, the heaviest single rows, is cut into pieces of columns,
$1104$ in all. Each part proves that its sum is at most a bound $\lceil 1.001 \times \text{part} \rceil$, computed
exactly, and the bounds sum to $1075756859466551505 < 2^{60}$. The exact total, $\Sigma = 1074682177289261666$, is
computed from the same definitions by \file{tools/linnik/mirror8.py}, and \file{tools/linnik/gen\_cert8.py} cuts it into
the parts by the kernel's predicted time. The kernel's check of the 114 files took $6.2$ hours of one core in all, and
$1.6$ hours on four cores, with at most $2.8$ GiB for any one file. The definitions the kernel evaluates are arithmetic
on Lean's core natural numbers, lists and booleans; their files import from Mathlib only the notation $\mathbb N$.
Lean's kernel reduces the operations of the natural numbers on literals (addition, multiplication, division, comparison
and the like) by the GMP library's arithmetic rather than by their definitions, so this check trusts GMP as well as the
kernel.

The library, the paper and this supplement are in the repository \cite{repo}; this version of the paper and of the

\section{Computations around the exponent}\label{sup:computations}

This section gives the computations behind the room that the window of Section~\ref{M-sec:window-L} leaves, behind the
remark there on the Hal\'asz--Montgomery branch, and behind the last two paragraphs of Section~\ref{M-sec:comparison}
of the paper. They are computations with the definitions of the window and of the certificate, by the scripts of
\file{tools/linnik/}, and not theorems of the library; nothing in the paper or the library depends on them.

\subsection*{The room in the window}

The window of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:window} has little room. All the corners are exact rational inequalities, so there
is no numerical tolerance. With $u_1$ and $u_2$ fixed, they hold exactly for $L \ge 2.1999832\ldots$, so at
$219999/100000$ but not at $219998/100000$ (\texttt{route\_limits.py corners g7d 219999/100000 43457 46959 100000});
raising the first step's exponent by more than $4.4404 \cdot 10^{-5}$ moves the lower end out. The Type~II estimate on
this window does not depend on $L$ otherwise, and Theorem~\ref{M-thm:route} also needs $2 < L \le 5/2$ and $L U_2 > D$,
which still hold. The certificate depends on $L$ only through the bilinear test's condition $a_1 < 1 - 1/L$, which the
block test also applies and which is stricter for smaller $L$; on the grid of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:certificate} its
total at $L = 137499/62500 = 2.199984$ is the same integer as at $11/5$ (\texttt{mirror8.py 43457 46959 272
137499/62500}). So with this window the route also holds at $2.199984$, about $1.6 \cdot 10^{-5}$ below $11/5$; a window
chosen afresh for a smaller $L$ goes further (below). The corners' slack grows along the narrow steps, to $8.6 \cdot
10^{-4}$ on the fourteenth, and from there steps of $1/280$ leave the window as it is: the least slack of a corner on
them is $6.8 \cdot 10^{-4}$, on the first of them, and on the last step, $[163/210, 131/168]$, which contains $7/9$, it
is $3.95 \cdot 10^{-3}$. Steps of $1/140$ in place of those of $1/280$, from the end of the fourteenth step, $5/7 +
1/120$, would leave the window as it is too, with a least slack of $3.8 \cdot 10^{-4}$ on them; at $278/125$, with the
window of the earlier version, they moved its lower end. Inside the last step Huxley's $3/(3\sigma - 1)$, which is below
$4/(1 + \sigma)$ from $\sigma = 7/9$ on, is below the step's exponent $2.25207$ from $\sigma \approx 0.7774$ on, by up
to $1.2 \cdot 10^{-2}$ at $131/168$; taking it there would not change the window, since the corners on that step do not
bind. These slacks are the corner inequalities of \file{tools/linnik/route\_limits.py}, evaluated on the curve with
those steps changed.

\subsection*{The Hal\'asz--Montgomery branch}

The top piece of the curve is the single step $101/50$ on $[5/6, 11/12]$ (\lean{LinnikStep.charDensity\_step}), and on
it the window needs the Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality (Section~\ref{M-sec:window-L}). Orthogonality alone would do
with a smaller exponent near $11/12$: with $101/50$ kept on $[5/6, 7/8]$, where orthogonality allows any constant
exponent up to $2.1356$, and a constant exponent of at most $1.9595$ on $[7/8, 11/12]$, every corner holds by
orthogonality, and Huxley's estimate would give $3/(3 \cdot 7/8 - 1) + 1/50 = 24/13 + 1/50 \approx 1.866$ there, with
the excess of the top piece. The library has no such step. Its steps of Huxley's estimate,
\lean{LinnikHuxStep.charDensity\_gen}, bound the zeros that the integral detects by the fourth moment of the
$L$-functions, and the condition that this asks of a step $[\sigma_-, \sigma_+]$ (\lean{h3} of
\lean{LinnikHuxStep.Par.Good}) is $A(1 - \sigma) \ge (201/100)(1/2 - \sigma) + 1/500 + 1 + 10^{-5}$ on the step, that
is $A \ge 2.01 - 0.00299/(1 - \sigma_-)$; since the steps lie in $[3/4, 11/12]$, every one of them has
$A > 2.01 - 12 \cdot 0.00299 > 1.974$. Huxley's exponents above $5/6$ are in the library only under Petrow and Young's
Weyl bound for every character, taken as a hypothesis (\lean{LinnikWeyl.PYWeyl}, in \file{LinnikStairs7.lean}), which
empties that class of zeros and is not a theorem of the library. So with the density estimates that the library
proves, the branch is needed.

\subsection*{The classical estimates in place of Chen, Gupta and Li's}

Take $g^*$ with its steps on $[5/7, 131/168]$ replaced by steps of length $1/1680$ with the classical exponents and the
excess $1/2000$ of the library's steps: Ingham's \cite{ingham} $3/(2 - \sigma_+) + 1/2000$ up to $3/4$ and Huxley's
\cite{huxley75} $3/(3\sigma_- - 1) + 1/2000$ from $3/4$. The window is then set at $3/4$, where both exponents are
$12/5$, and the closed forms of Section~\ref{M-sec:ends} become $u_1 \ge 16/(5L) - 1$ and $u_2 \le 1 - 6/(5L)$, which
meet at $L = 11/5$. The script \file{tools/linnik/density\_payoff.py} (its variant \texttt{classical}, with
\texttt{--deep}) finds a window admissible at $L$, checked at the corners in exact rationals, evaluates the
certificate's total on it on $272$ cells a side under the rule at $L$, and lowers $L$ in steps of $10^{-3}$ while the
total stays below $0.999\mathcal K$ and the grid satisfies the conditions of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:route}, then tries one
further step of $5 \cdot 10^{-4}$. It finds the window $[42950, 46385]/100000$ admissible at $L = 2239/1000 = 2.239$,
and the certificate's total on it $0.94509\mathcal K$. The search stops there at the grid, not at the total: at
$2.238$ the window it finds, $[43014, 46361]/100000$, is too narrow for $20h \le v$ on $272$ cells a side, so the
script does not evaluate it, though \file{mirror8.py}'s total on that grid is $0.99209\mathcal K$; on $280$ cells a
side the window satisfies the grid conditions, but finer grids were not tried. So the sieve brings Huxley's $12/5$
down to $2.239$ at least, with Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane} as the only input that is not refereed. The library has
no steps of Ingham's or Huxley's estimate of length $1/1680$ on $[5/7, 131/168]$ (its Ingham steps end at $5/7$, and
its Huxley steps, of length $1/280$, start at $16/21$); the parameters of \lean{LinnikHuxStep.charDensity\_gen} at this
width were checked for the first of these steps only; and the certificate on this grid has not been checked by the
kernel.

\subsection*{Below $11/5$}

At $11/5$ the certificate's total is $0.9321382\mathcal K$ (Section~\ref{M-sec:certificate}); the kernel checks that
it is below $0.9330704\mathcal K$. Of it, $0.7173\mathcal K$ comes from the $7289$ candidate cells that are dropped,
$0.6790\mathcal K$ of that from the $6896$ of them that reach the line $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 = 1 - U_1/D$, that is whose
box's upper sum is at least $1 - U_1/D$; the decomposed cells cost $0.2149\mathcal K$. More depth can lower only the
cost of the decomposed cells and of the $22$ cells that are cheaper dropped: the other dropped cells, which cost
$0.7151\mathcal K$, are dropped because the rule fails on their box or on a box of depth three below them (where the
block test is the rule itself), and that does not depend on the depth. The two deepest levels matter for the rest: to
depth six the total would be $1.55502\mathcal K$, since the cells of depth six that pass the block test would cost
$0.669\mathcal K$ at their own bounds, against $0.049\mathcal K$ decomposed to depth eight. Without the bilinear test,
that is with (R1) alone as the rule, $10974$ candidate cells are decomposed in place of $15884$, and the total would
be $2.43064\mathcal K$. These counts and costs are printed by \file{tools/linnik/breakdown8.py}, and the totals without
the deeper levels or without the bilinear test by its variants \texttt{depth6}, \texttt{depth4}, \texttt{lim} and
\texttt{lim6}.

The cells that reach the line hold the integers that the Type~II estimate cannot reach. Let $\varepsilon > 0$, and let
$n = p_1 p_2 m \le 2x$ be an integer that such a cell counts, with $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 \ge 1 - U_1/D + \varepsilon$ and
$\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ outside $\mathcal U$. For large $x$, no product of some of its prime factors has its exponent
in $\mathcal U$, whatever the factorisation of $m$: a product with $p_1$ and another prime has exponent at least
$\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 > 1 - U_1/D$, since the prime factors of $m$ are at least $p_2$; one without $p_1$ that omits $p_2$
or a prime of $m$ has exponent at most $1 - \alpha_1 - \alpha_2 + o(1) < U_1/D$; and the others are $p_1$ and $p_2 m$,
of exponents $\alpha_1$, outside $\mathcal U$, and $1 - \alpha_1 + o(1) > 1/2$. In the decomposition such integers can
be counted only by Type~I estimates, and $p_1 p_2$ is beyond the Type~I level $X'$: the bilinear estimate of
Lemma~\ref{M-lem:typeIB} reaches them where the bilinear test passes. Of the $6896$ dropped cells that reach the line,
$1725$ fail the bilinear test themselves, $5162$ pass it but have a box of depth three below them on which the rule
fails, and $9$ are cheaper dropped.

With the window chosen afresh, the method goes a little below $11/5$. With the same curve, rule, depth and bounds, the
window's corners checked in exact rationals and the grid satisfying the conditions of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:route}, the
certificate's total is $0.96047\mathcal K$ at $L = 2.19925$, on the window $[43516, 46942]/100000$ and $272$ cells a
side; $0.96729\mathcal K$ at $2.199$, on $[43538, 46935]/100000$ and $276$ cells; and $1.01027\mathcal K$ at $2.198$, on
$[43617, 46911]/100000$ and $284$ cells (\file{mirror8.py} and \texttt{route\_limits.py corners} on these windows). So
the method reaches about $2.198$; $11/5$, about $2 \cdot 10^{-3}$ above that, is the round exponent that the paper
proves. A narrower window needs a finer grid: the condition $20h \le v$ of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:route} asks for $N \ge
20(1/2 - v)/v$, which with $4 \mid N$ is $N \ge 268$ on the window of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:certificate}. Going much
lower would need a wider window, that is a better density exponent near $\sigma = 5/7$, or Type~I information on longer
products than the bilinear estimate gives. The half-plane $\operatorname{Re} s > 7/8$ of OpenAI's main preprint
\cite{openai-main}, in place of Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane}, would not help: the window condition
(Definition~\ref{M-def:window}) would be needed only for $\beta \le 7/8$, and the exponent of the kernel bound would
become $b(\mu) = 3/8 + \mu/8 + \delta'(1 - \mu) + 2\varepsilon'$, which is no larger for $\mu \le 1$; neither change
reaches the corners that set the window's ends, on the first step at $5/7$.

On the density exponent, Chen, Gupta and Li note that the worst case for their estimate is $T = 1$ with no divisor
$q_1$ of $q$ of a suitable size, as for prime $q$, that the critical situation is then at $\sigma = 5/7$, and that
improving the term which dominates there is hard (\cite[Remark~12.3]{cgl}, quoted in Section~\ref{M-sec:comparison}).
