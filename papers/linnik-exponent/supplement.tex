\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:prime-sum}, and the kernel bound}\label{app:prime-sum}

Lemma~\ref{M-lem:prime-sum} bounds the prime sum $S_Q(\chi, y) = \sum_{p \le 2Q} \chi(p) (p/Q)^{-iy} f_0(p/Q)$, with
$f_0$ smooth, real and supported in $[1/2, 2]$, when $L(s, \chi)$ has no zero in $\operatorname{Re} s > \theta$,
$|\operatorname{Im} s - y| < H + 1$. In it $\ell_{q, y} = \log(q + 2) + \log(|y| + 4) + 1$, and $M_\delta(\theta)$ is
the sum of two values of the constant $C_{\log}$ of \eqref{eq:logderiv} below. The proof writes $S_Q$, up to prime
powers, as a sum weighted by $\Lambda(n)$, and turns this by Perron's formula into an integral of $-L'/L$ against the
Mellin transform of the weight. It then bounds $L'/L$ in zero-free discs and moves the line of integration to
$\operatorname{Re} s = \theta + \delta$ at the heights near $y$ and to $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta$ elsewhere.

\begin{proof}
Throughout, $Q \ge 4$, so that $\log Q > 1$, and $L_\chi$ stands for $L(\cdot, \chi)$, which is entire since $\chi$ is
not trivial. Put $\sigma_* = 11/12 + \delta$ and $\tau = (1 + H)^{-J}$.

\emph{The weight.} It carries a factor $\log Q/\log(uQ)$, so that $\Lambda$ becomes $\log Q$ at the primes:
\[
  f_Q(u) = f_0(u) \log Q/\log(uQ) \ \text{ if } f_0(u) \ne 0, \qquad f_Q(u) = 0 \ \text{ otherwise},
\]
which is well defined since $f_0(u) \ne 0$ needs $u \ge 1/2$, so $uQ \ge 2$. Let $w_y(u) = u^{-iy} f_Q(u)$. If
$f_0(n/Q) \ne 0$ then $n \ge Q/2 \ge 2$ and $f_Q(n/Q) = f_0(n/Q) \log Q/\log n$, so that
$\Lambda(n) f_Q(n/Q) = \log Q \cdot (\Lambda(n)/\log n) f_0(n/Q)$. Since $\Lambda(p)/\log p = 1$,
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w_y(n/Q) = \log Q \, \bigl(S_Q(\chi, y) + E\bigr), \\
  E = \sum_{\substack{p^k \le 2Q \\ k \ge 2}} \frac{\chi(p^k)}{k} \Bigl(\frac{p^k}{Q}\Bigr)^{-iy}
  f_0\Bigl(\frac{p^k}{Q}\Bigr).
\end{gather*}
Each term of $E$ is at most $C = \max |f_0|$ in absolute value (this is the $C$ of the lemma), and a proper prime
power $p^k \le 2Q$ has $p \le \sqrt{2Q}$ and $k \le \log_2(2Q)$, so $|E| \le C(\sqrt{2Q} + 1)(\log_2(2Q) + 1)$. It
remains to prove, with $B$ the constant of \eqref{eq:mellin-decay} below,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sw-bound}
  \Bigl|\sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w_y(n/Q)\Bigr| \le M_\delta(\theta) \, \ell_{q, y} \, B \bigl(Q^{\theta + \delta} +
  Q^{\sigma_*} \tau\bigr).
\end{equation}

\emph{The Mellin transform.} Write $\mathcal M f(s) = \int_0^\infty u^{s - 1} f(u) \, du$. The substitution
$u = e^{-v}$ gives, with $c_Q = 1/\log Q \in (0, 1/\log 4]$,
\[
  \mathcal M f_Q(\sigma + it) = \int_{\mathbb R} e^{-itv} V(v) \, dv, \qquad
  V(v) = e^{-\sigma v} f_0(e^{-v}) (1 - c_Q v)^{-1}.
\]
The function $V$ vanishes for $|v| > \log 2$, and $c_Q v \le 1/2$ for $v \le \log 2$; the pole $v = \log Q \ge \log 4$
of
$(1 - c_Q v)^{-1}$ lies where $V$ vanishes, so $V$ is smooth. By Leibniz's
rule, with $|(d/dv)^i (1 - c_Q v)^{-1}| = i! \, c_Q^i |1 - c_Q v|^{-1 - i} \le i! \, 2^{i + 1}$ on $|v| \le \log 2$,
the $j$-th derivative of $V$ is bounded by a constant $V_j$ that depends only on $f_0$ and $j$, uniformly in
$0 \le \sigma \le 2$ and $Q \ge 4$. Integration by parts $j$ times gives
$|t|^j |\mathcal M f_Q(\sigma + it)| \le 2 \log 2 \cdot V_j$. With $j = 0$ and $j = J + 3$, and
$(1 + |t|)^{J + 3} \le 2^{J + 3}(1 + |t|^{J + 3})$,
\[
  (1 + |t|)^{J + 3} |\mathcal M f_Q(\sigma + it)| \le B \qquad (0 \le \sigma \le 2,\ t \in \mathbb R,\ Q \ge 4),
\]
where $B = 2^{J + 4} \log 2 \, (V_0 + V_{J + 3})$, the $B$ of the lemma, depends only on $f_0$ and $J$. Since $f_Q$
is continuous on
$(0, \infty)$ and vanishes near $0$ and near $\infty$, $\mathcal M f_Q$ is entire. Finally
$\mathcal M w_y(s) = \mathcal M f_Q(s - iy)$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mellin-decay}
  (1 + |t - y|)^{J + 3} |\mathcal M w_y(\sigma + it)| \le B \qquad (0 \le \sigma \le 2,\ t \in \mathbb R).
\end{equation}

\emph{Perron's formula.} For $\operatorname{Re} s > 1$, $-L_\chi'/L_\chi(s) = \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) n^{-s}$.
Mellin inversion on the line $\operatorname{Re} s = 2$ at each point $n/Q$, with the exchange of sum and integral
justified by \eqref{eq:mellin-decay}, gives
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w_y(n/Q) = \frac1{2\pi} \int_{\mathbb R} F(2 + it) \, dt, \\
  F(s) = -\frac{L_\chi'}{L_\chi}(s) \, \mathcal M w_y(s) \, Q^s.
\end{gather*}

\emph{The logarithmic derivative.} Let $1/2 \le \theta_0 < \theta' < 3/2$ and $\lambda = 3/2 - \theta_0$. There is
$C_{\log}(\theta_0, \theta')$ such that the following holds for every $q$, every non-trivial $\chi$ mod $q$ and every
$t \in \mathbb R$. If $L_\chi$ has no zero in the open disc $D_t$ of centre $3/2 + it$ and radius $\lambda$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logderiv}
  \Bigl|\frac{L_\chi'}{L_\chi}(\sigma + it)\Bigr| \le C_{\log}(\theta_0, \theta') \bigl(\log(q + 2) + \log(|t| +
  4)\bigr) \qquad (\sigma \ge \theta').
\end{equation}
For $\sigma \ge 3/2$ this holds as soon as $C_{\log}(\theta_0, \theta') \ge -\zeta'/\zeta(3/2)$, by the Dirichlet series
of $L_\chi'/L_\chi$. For $\theta' \le \sigma < 3/2$ it follows from a lemma of Landau's type: if $f$ is analytic near
the closed unit disc, $f(0) = 1$, $|f| \le B_0$ on $|z| \le R$ with $B_0 > 1$, $f$ has no zero in $|z| < 1$ and
$0 < r' < r < R' < R < 1$, then
\[
  |f'/f(z)| \le \Bigl(\frac{16 r^2}{(r - r')^3} + \frac1{(R^2/R' - R') \log(R/R')}\Bigr) \log B_0 \qquad (|z| \le r').
\]
(The library takes this lemma from PrimeNumberTheoremAnd \cite{pnt-plus}. There it is proved with a Blaschke product,
the Borel--Carath\'eodory inequality and Jensen's formula, for $f$ with zeros, as a bound for $f'/f$ less the sum over
the zeros in $|z| \le r$; here that sum is empty.)

Apply the lemma of Landau's type to $f(z) = L_\chi(3/2 + it + \lambda z)/L_\chi(3/2 + it)$ at
$z = (\sigma - 3/2)/\lambda$, with $r' = (3/2 - \theta')/\lambda$ and with $r$, $R'$ and $R$ a quarter, a half and
three quarters of the way from $r'$ to $1$; then $f'/f(z) = \lambda \, L_\chi'/L_\chi(\sigma + it)$.

The bound $B_0$ comes from two estimates. First,
$|L_\chi(s)| \le 3(q + 1)(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 2)$ for $1/2 \le \operatorname{Re} s \le 3$, which follows from the
partial-summation bound $|L_\chi(s)| \le (q + 1)|s|/\operatorname{Re} s$ for $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$. Second,
$|L_\chi(3/2 + it)|^{-1} \le \zeta(3/2)$, from $1/L_\chi(s) = \sum \mu(n) \chi(n) n^{-s}$. Together they give
$|f| \le B_0 = 2 + 6(q + 1)\zeta(3/2)(|t| + 4)$ on $|z| \le R$, and $\log B_0 \ll \log(q + 2) + \log(|t| + 4)$. The
constant $C_{\log}(\theta_0, \theta')$ is explicit and of order $(\theta' - \theta_0)^{-3}$.

The disc $D_t$ lies in $\operatorname{Re} s > \theta_0$ and, since $\lambda \le 1$, in $|\operatorname{Im} s - t| < 1$.
So
\eqref{eq:logderiv} applies in two cases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] with $\theta_0 = 11/12$ and every $t$, by Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane};
\item[(ii)] with $\theta_0 = \theta$ and every $t$ with $|t - y| \le H$, by the hypothesis of the lemma.
\end{enumerate}
Since $|t| + 4 \le (|y| + 4)(1 + |t - y|)$ and $\log(1 + u) \le u$, we have
$\log(q + 2) + \log(|t| + 4) \le \ell_{q, y}(1 + |t - y|)$. With \eqref{eq:mellin-decay} this gives, in either case
(in case (ii) for $|t - y| \le H$ only),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-bound}
  |F(\sigma + it)| \le C_{\log}(\theta_0, \theta') \, \ell_{q, y} \, B \, Q^\sigma (1 + |t - y|)^{-J - 2} \qquad
  (\theta'
  \le \sigma \le 2).
\end{equation}

\emph{The contour.} By Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane}, $F$ is holomorphic on $\sigma_* \le \operatorname{Re} s \le 2$,
and by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (i), with $\theta' = \sigma_*$, it is $O((1 + |t - y|)^{-2})$ there uniformly. So
Cauchy's theorem on the rectangles $[\sigma_*, 2] \times [-t_0, t_0]$, with $t_0 \to \infty$, moves the line of
integration from $2$ to $\sigma_*$. By the hypothesis of the lemma, $F$ is holomorphic on the rectangle
$\theta + \delta \le \operatorname{Re} s \le \sigma_*$, $|\operatorname{Im} s - y| \le H$. So, by Cauchy's theorem
again, the path of integration can be taken to be the line $\operatorname{Re} s = \sigma_*$ for $|t - y| \ge H$, the
segment of the line $\operatorname{Re} s = \theta + \delta$ for $|t - y| \le H$, and the two horizontal sides at the
heights $y \pm H$ that join them.

\emph{The pieces.} Put $C_1 = C_{\log}(11/12, \sigma_*)$ and $C_2 = C_{\log}(\theta, \theta + \delta)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item On the line $\sigma_*$ with $|t - y| \ge H$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (i) and
  $(1 + u)^{-J - 2} \le (1 + H)^{-J} (1 + u^2)^{-1}$ for $u \ge H$,
  $|F| \le C_1 \ell_{q, y} B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau/(1 + (t - y)^2)$. Since $\int_{\mathbb R} du/(1 + u^2) = \pi$, each of
  the two tails contributes at most $\pi C_1 \ell_{q, y} B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau$.
\item On the line $\theta + \delta$ with $|t - y| \le H$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (ii),
  $|F| \le C_2 \ell_{q, y} B Q^{\theta + \delta}/(1 + (t - y)^2)$, so this segment contributes at most
  $\pi C_2 \ell_{q, y} B Q^{\theta + \delta}$.
\item On each horizontal side, of length $11/12 - \theta \le 1$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (ii), with
  $Q^\sigma \le Q^{\sigma_*}$, $|F| \le C_2 \ell_{q, y} B Q^{\sigma_*} (1 + H)^{-J - 2} \le C_2 \ell_{q, y} B
  Q^{\sigma_*} \tau$.
\end{itemize}
Adding the pieces and dividing by $2\pi$, the sum in \eqref{eq:Sw-bound} is at most
\begin{multline*}
  C_1 \ell_{q, y} B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau + \tfrac12 C_2 \ell_{q, y} B Q^{\theta + \delta} + \tfrac1\pi C_2 \ell_{q, y} B
  Q^{\sigma_*} \tau \\
  \le (C_1 + C_2) \, \ell_{q, y} \, B \bigl(Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{\sigma_*} \tau\bigr),
\end{multline*}
which is \eqref{eq:Sw-bound} with $M_\delta(\theta) = C_{\log}(\theta, \theta + \delta) + C_{\log}(11/12, 11/12 +
\delta)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The kernel bound of Section~\ref{M-sec:two-bounds}}

Let $\delta' = \varepsilon' = 1/1000$, and recall that $k_Y(\psi) = \sum_{n \ge 1} \psi(n) e^{-n/Y}$. We prove that
there is $C$ such that for every $q$, every non-trivial character $\psi$ mod $q$ and every $Y \ge 1$,
\[
  |k_Y(\psi)| \le C Y^{1/12 - \delta'} (2q)^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}.
\]
Fix such $q$, $\psi$ and $Y$. By Mellin inversion,
\[
  k_Y(\psi) = \frac1{2\pi i} \int_{(2)} L(s, \psi) \Gamma(s) Y^s \, ds.
\]
Since $L(\cdot, \psi)$ is entire and $1/12 - \delta' > 0$, the line moves to $\operatorname{Re} s = 1/12 - \delta'$
without crossing a pole.

On that line the functional equation of the primitive character $\psi^*$ that induces $\psi$, of conductor $q^*$, gives
$|L(s, \psi^*)| \ll (q^*(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 2))^{5/12 + \delta'} |L(1 - s, \overline{\psi^*})|$, and $w = 1 - s$ lies
on $\operatorname{Re} w = 11/12 + \delta'$.

On the line $\operatorname{Re} w = 11/12 + \delta'$, Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane} gives the Lindelöf-type bound
$|L(w, \chi)|^{\pm 1} \ll ((r + 2)(|\operatorname{Im} w| + 4))^{\varepsilon'}$, uniformly in $r$ and in the
non-trivial characters $\chi$ mod $r$; we use it with $\chi = \overline{\psi^*}$ and $r = q^*$. The argument is the
one by which the Riemann hypothesis implies the Lindelöf hypothesis \cite[\S 14.2]{titchmarsh}, in three steps. Let
$X = \log((r + 2)(|\operatorname{Im} w| + 4))$. First, \eqref{eq:logderiv} in case (i) bounds $|L'/L(w, \chi)|$ by a
multiple of $X$ on $\operatorname{Re} w \ge 11/12 + \delta'/2$. Second, the maximum principle for $L'/L$ times a Gaussian
weight, on rectangles of half-height comparable to $X$ (the weight makes the horizontal sides harmless), interpolates
this against $|L'/L| \ll 1$ on $\operatorname{Re} w = 3/2$, and gives $|L'/L| \ll X^\kappa$ with $\kappa < 1$ on
$11/12 + \delta' \le \operatorname{Re} w \le 3/2$. Third, integrating $L'/L$ from $\operatorname{Re} w = 3/2$, where
$|\log |L|| \le \log \zeta(3/2)$, gives $|\log |L|| \ll X^\kappa + 1$. Since $\kappa < 1$, this is at most
$\varepsilon' X + O(1)$, which is the bound for $|L|$ and $1/|L|$.

Back on $\operatorname{Re} s = 1/12 - \delta'$, $L(s, \psi) = L(s, \psi^*) \prod_{p \mid q} (1 - \psi^*(p) p^{-s})$.
These Euler factors are at most $2$ each, which is $\ll q^{\varepsilon'}$ in all. With the two bounds above, this gives
$|L(s, \psi)| \ll (q(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 2))^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}$ on the new line. The decay of
$\Gamma$ makes the integral converge, and the bound for $|k_Y(\psi)|$ follows, with $C$ independent of $q$, $\psi$
and $Y$.

\section{Proofs of Lemmas~\ref{M-lem:family-two} and~\ref{M-lem:family-sharp-two}}\label{app:family}

Both lemmas are cases of two bounds for families in which the variable $m$ runs over a finite set $X$ of any kind (the
pairs of integers $(m_1, m_2)$ for the two lemmas, and pairs of tuples of primes in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{M-lem:chains-two}). The first, the bound with smooth weights, gives the primes smooth weights at given scales
and is proved directly. The second, the bound without the weights, lets the primes run over all the primes up to a bound
and is reduced to the first by a dyadic partition of unity. In both, $m$ enters only through an integer value $\nu(m)$,
a coefficient $c_m$ and the parts $u_j(m)$ of the comparisons' forms. Besides bounds on sizes, both assume one
hypothesis, \eqref{eq:H}: the Type~II
bound at each frequency of a separation of variables. Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2} enters only through it, and the
subsection on two variables checks it for the two lemmas.

\subsection*{Families with an abstract polynomial part}

Fix a smooth function $f_0 \ge 0$ supported in $[1/2, 2]$, and let $F_0 = \max f_0$ and $\eta = 10^{-5}$, the largest
height exponent that Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2} allows. Let $X$ be a finite set, and for $m \in X$ let $\nu(m) \ge 1$ be
an integer, $c_m$ a complex coefficient and $u_1(m), \dots, u_J(m)$ reals. Let $p_1, \dots, p_K$ be primes
at the scales $Q_1, \dots, Q_K > 0$, and let there be $J$ comparisons: conditions on $(m, p)$ that hold when
$\ell_j(m, p) > 0$ and fail when $\ell_j(m, p) < 0$, where $\ell_j(m, p) = u_j(m) + \sum_i e_{ji} \log p_i$, each
with a pivot $\mathrm{piv}(j)$, an index with $e_{j, \mathrm{piv}(j)} = \pm 1$. Let $\mathcal T$ be the set of the $t =
(m, p_1, \dots, p_K)$ with $m \in X$ and each $p_i$ a prime at most $2Q_i$; for $t \in \mathcal T$ put $w(t) = c_m
\prod_i f_0(p_i/Q_i)$ and $n(t) = \nu(m) p_1 \cdots p_K$, and for a function $v$ on $\mathcal T$ put
\[
  \mathcal D(v) = \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} v(t) \, \vartheta(n(t)),
\]
with $\vartheta$ the weight of Section~\ref{M-sec:type1}. The family's sum over $\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ is
$\mathcal D(w \prod_j I_j)$, with $I_j(t) = 1$ if the $j$-th comparison holds at $t$ and $I_j(t) = 0$ otherwise. The
hypothesis, for a constant $B_1 \ge
0$, is that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \Bigl|\sum_{m \in X} c'_m \, e^{2\pi i (\kappa + \sum_j \xi_j u_j(m))} \,
  \chi(\nu(m))\Bigr| \prod_i |S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i)| \le B_1
\end{equation}
for $c' = c$ and $c' = |c|$, every $\xi \in \mathbb R^J$, every $\kappa \in \mathbb R$ and all heights $|h_i| \le
q^\eta$, with the prime sums of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2} for this $f_0$.

\emph{The bound with smooth weights.} Given integers $K_0 \ge 1$ and $J_0 \ge 0$ and reals $L$, $e_1 > 0$ and $E_0,
\Lambda_0 \ge 0$, there are $\delta > 0$, which depends only on $J_0$ and $e_1$, and $q_0$ such that the following holds
for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item $\sum_m |c_m| \le P A_0$ for some $P > 0$ and $A_0 \ge 0$;
\item $1 \le K \le K_0$, $Q_i \ge q^{e_1}$ and $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/P$;
\item $J \le J_0$, $|e_{ji}| \le E_0$ and $\ell_j \le \Lambda_0 \log q$ on $\mathcal T$;
\item \eqref{eq:H} holds with $B_1 = A_0 q^{L - 1/50000}$.
\end{itemize}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fam-gen}
  \Bigl|\mathcal D\Bigl(w \prod_j I_j\Bigr)\Bigr| \le \frac{A_0 \, q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\end{equation}
% Lean: SieveFamilyGen.sharp_gen; its steps SieveFamilyGen.family_bound_gen (smooth family), Dsum_sharp_le_gen (sharp
% against smooth), sharp_explicit_gen, box_le_gen, pivot_le_gen

\begin{proof}
The comparisons are replaced by smooth cut-offs of their forms. The smoothed family is bounded through \eqref{eq:H} by
Fourier inversion; the difference, which lies near the boundaries $\ell_j = 0$, is bounded through \eqref{eq:H} again
and by a count along the pivot.

\emph{Orthogonality.} For $a$ prime to $q$, $\vartheta(n) = \varphi(q)^{-1} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*}
\overline\chi(a) \chi(n)$, so for every $v$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orth}
  |\mathcal D(v)| \le \frac1{\varphi(q)} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \Bigl|\sum_{t \in \mathcal T} v(t)
  \chi(n(t))\Bigr|.
\end{equation}

\emph{Smoothed families.} Let $g_1, \dots, g_J$ be continuous and integrable, with integrable Fourier transforms $\hat
g_j(\xi) = \int g_j(u) e^{-2\pi i u \xi} \, du$, and let $T_0 \ge 1$ with $2\pi \sum_j |e_{ji}| \, T_0 \le q^{\eta}$ for
every $i$. We show
\begin{multline}\label{eq:smooth-family}
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \Bigl|\sum_{t \in \mathcal T} w(t) \prod_j g_j(\ell_j(t)) \, \chi(n(t))\Bigr|
  \le B_1 \prod_j \|\hat g_j\|_1 \\
  + \varphi(q) \, \mathfrak B \sum_j \int_{|\xi| > T_0} |\hat g_j| \prod_{l \ne j} \|\hat g_l\|_1, \qquad
  \mathfrak B = \sum_{m \in X} |c_m| \prod_i \sum_{p \le 2Q_i} f_0(p/Q_i).
\end{multline}
% Lean: SieveFamilyGen.inner_factor_gen, SieveFamilyGen.family_bound_gen, SieveSep.sum_sep, SieveSep.sep_bound
By Fourier inversion, and since the phase factorises over the variables,
\begin{gather*}
  \prod_j g_j(\ell_j) = \int_{\mathbb R^J} \prod_j \hat g_j(\xi_j) \, e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \ell_j} \, d\xi, \\
  e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \ell_j(t)} = e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j u_j(m)} \prod_i p_i^{2\pi i H_i}, \qquad
  H_i = \sum_j \xi_j e_{ji}.
\end{gather*}
So the inner sum in \eqref{eq:smooth-family} is $\int \prod_j \hat g_j(\xi_j) \, W_\xi(\chi) \prod_i Q_i^{2\pi i H_i}
S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i) \, d\xi$, where $W_\xi(\chi) = \sum_m c_m e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j u_j(m)} \chi(\nu(m))$ and $h_i
= -2\pi H_i$. Take absolute values, sum over $\chi$, and bound the integrand for each $\xi$. If $|\xi_j| \le T_0$ for
every $j$, then $|h_i| \le q^{\eta}$, and \eqref{eq:H}, with $c' = c$ and $\kappa = 0$, bounds it by $B_1$. Otherwise
some $|\xi_j| > T_0$, and the trivial bound $\varphi(q) \mathfrak B$ holds. Integrating gives \eqref{eq:smooth-family}.
The same holds for a single function at one of the forms shifted by a real constant, $\ell_j + s$, with the
coefficients $|c_m|$ in place of $c_m$: the shift multiplies the integrand by the constant phase $e^{2\pi i \xi s}$, and
\eqref{eq:H} with $c' = |c|$ bounds it. This is why the hypothesis asks for $|c|$. The phase $\kappa$ of \eqref{eq:H}
is such a constant phase; the absolute value removes it, so it costs nothing.

\emph{The cut-off.} Let $g$ be smooth, with $g = 0$ on $(-\infty, 0]$, $g = 1$ on $[1, \infty)$ and $0 \le g \le 1$, and
let $\gamma_k = \int |g^{(k)}|$. For $0 < \theta \le 2\Lambda$ put $G_{\theta, \Lambda}(u) = g(u/\theta) - g((u -
2\Lambda)/\theta)$. It is smooth, $0 \le G_{\theta, \Lambda} \le 1$, it vanishes outside $[0, 2\Lambda + \theta]$, and
$G_{\theta, \Lambda}(u) = g(u/\theta)$ for $u \le 2\Lambda$. So if $u \le \Lambda$ and a condition holds when $u > 0$
and fails when $u < 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
  \bigl|\mathbf 1[\text{condition}] - G_{\theta, \Lambda}(u)\bigr| \le G_{\theta, \theta}(u + \theta),
\end{equation}
since the left side vanishes unless $0 \le u < \theta$, where $G_{\theta, \theta}(u + \theta) = 1$. Integration by parts
gives $(2\pi |\xi|)^k |\hat G_{\theta, \Lambda}(\xi)| \le \|G_{\theta, \Lambda}^{(k)}\|_1 \le 2\theta^{1 - k} \gamma_k$,
and $|\hat G_{\theta, \Lambda}| \le 2\Lambda + \theta$. Hence
\begin{gather*}
  \|\hat G_{\theta, \Lambda}\|_1 \le \pi(2\Lambda + \theta) + \frac{\gamma_2}{2\pi\theta}, \\
  \int_{|\xi| > T_0} |\hat G_{\theta, \Lambda}| \le \frac{4\pi \gamma_k}{(2\pi)^k} \, \theta^{1 - k} T_0^{2 - k}
  \qquad (k \ge 2,\ T_0 \ge 1).
\end{gather*}

\emph{Sharp against smoothed.} Let $\Lambda = \Lambda_0 \log q + 1$, so that $\ell_j \le \Lambda$ on $\mathcal T$, and put
$G_j = G_{\theta, \Lambda}(\ell_j)$ and $M_j = G_{\theta, \theta}(\ell_j + \theta)$. All these values lie in $[0, 1]$,
so by \eqref{eq:cutoff} $|\prod_j I_j - \prod_j G_j| \le \sum_j |I_j - G_j| \le \sum_j M_j$. Since $|\vartheta(n)| \le
\vartheta(n) + 2 \cdot \mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]/\varphi(q)$ and $f_0 \ge 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-smooth}
  \Bigl|\mathcal D\Bigl(w \prod_j I_j\Bigr)\Bigr| \le \Bigl|\mathcal D\Bigl(w \prod_j G_j\Bigr)\Bigr|
  + \sum_j \Bigl(|\mathcal D(|w| M_j)| + \frac2{\varphi(q)} \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} |w(t)| M_j(t)\Bigr).
\end{equation}
% Lean: SieveFamilyGen.Dsum_sharp_le_gen (with SieveFamily.norm_Dsum_sharp_le, SieveFamily.abs_ind_sub_G_le)
The first term is the \emph{smoothed family}, with $g_j = G_{\theta, \Lambda}$; each $\mathcal D(|w| M_j)$ is a
\emph{near-boundary family}, with the coefficients $|c_m|$ and the single function $G_{\theta, \theta}$ at the form
$\ell_j + \theta$. Both are bounded by \eqref{eq:orth} and \eqref{eq:smooth-family}. A trivial count would not do for
them, since it would lose
the factor $1/\varphi(q)$ that the condition $n \equiv a$ gives. The last sum, which carries that factor already, is
counted trivially, by the pivot. Fix $m$ and the primes other than $p_{\mathrm{piv}(j)}$; then $\ell_j$ is a constant
plus or minus $\log p_{\mathrm{piv}(j)}$, and $M_j \ne 0$ forces $-\theta \le \ell_j \le 2\theta$, which confines
$p_{\mathrm{piv}(j)}$ to an interval $[y, e^{3\theta} y]$. At most $(e^{3\theta} - 1) 2Q_{\mathrm{piv}(j)} + 1$ integers
up to $2Q_{\mathrm{piv}(j)}$ lie in it, so
\[
  \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} |w(t)| M_j(t)
  \le F_0 \bigl((e^{3\theta} - 1) 2Q_{\mathrm{piv}(j)} + 1\bigr) \sum_m |c_m| \prod_{i \ne \mathrm{piv}(j)} \sum_{p \le
  2Q_i} f_0(p/Q_i).
\]

\emph{The parameters.} Put $\varepsilon_2 = 1/(400000(J_0 + 1))$, $\theta = q^{-\varepsilon_2}$, $T_0 = q^{1/200000}$,
$k = 1200003$ and $\delta = \min(\varepsilon_2, e_1)/2$. For large $q$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $2\pi J_0 E_0 T_0 \le q^{\eta}$, so \eqref{eq:smooth-family} applies, as $\sum_j |e_{ji}| \le J_0 E_0$;
\item $\pi(2\Lambda + 1) \le q^{\varepsilon_2}$, so $\|\hat G_{\theta, \Lambda}\|_1$ and $\|\hat G_{\theta, \theta}\|_1$
  are at most $C q^{\varepsilon_2}$ with $C = 1 + \gamma_2/(2\pi)$, and a product of at most $J_0$ of them is at most
  $C^{J_0 + 1} q^{1/400000}$;
\item $\theta^{1 - k} T_0^{2 - k} = q^{\varepsilon_2(k - 1) - (k - 2)/200000} \le q^{-3}$, which bounds the tails;
  $k = 1200003$ is the least $k$ for which this holds whatever $J_0$.
\end{itemize}
Moreover $\mathfrak B \le P A_0 (3F_0)^K \prod_i Q_i \le A_0
(3F_0 + 1)^{K_0} 2^{K_0} q^L$, since at most $3Q$ integers lie in $[1, 2Q]$ for $Q \ge 1$. Finally $e^{3\theta} - 1 \le
6\theta$ for $\theta \le 1/3$ and $Q_{\mathrm{piv}(j)} \ge q^{e_1}$ give $(12\theta Q_{\mathrm{piv}(j)} + 1) \prod_{i
\ne \mathrm{piv}(j)} Q_i \le (12\theta + q^{-e_1}) \prod_i Q_i$. Putting these into \eqref{eq:sharp-smooth}, with
\eqref{eq:orth} and \eqref{eq:smooth-family} at $B_1 = A_0 q^{L - 1/50000}$, and using $\varphi(q) \le q$, the sum is at
most
\[
  \frac{A_0 q^L}{\varphi(q)} \bigl(a_1 q^{-7/400000} + a_2 q^{-1} + a_3(12 q^{-\varepsilon_2} + q^{-e_1})\bigr),
\]
where $a_1$, $a_2$ and $a_3$ depend only on $K_0$, $J_0$ and $F_0$. The three terms come from the smoothed and the
near-boundary families, from the tails and from the pivot counts. Each power of $q$ in the bracket is at most
$q^{-2\delta}$, so for large $q$ the sum is at most $A_0 q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$.
\end{proof}

\emph{The bound without the weights.} Take for $f_0$ the fixed function $f_0(y) = F(y) - F(y/2)$, with $F$ smooth and
nondecreasing, $0$ on $(-\infty, 1/2]$ and $1$ on $[1, \infty)$. It is supported in $[1/2, 2]$, takes values in $[0,
1]$, and $\sum_{0 \le t \le t_*} f_0(y/2^t) = F(y) - F(y/2^{t_* + 1}) = 1$ for $1 \le y \le 2^{t_*}$. Given integers
$K_0 \ge 1$, $J_0 \ge 0$ and $c_0 \ge 0$ and reals $0 < L \le 5/2$, $e_1 > 0$ and $E_0, \Lambda_1 \ge 0$, there are
$\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let $1 \le K \le
K_0$ primes $p_1, \dots, p_K$ run over all the primes up to $N_2 = \lfloor 2q^L \rfloor$, and suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item $X$ is cut into blocks $X_b$, labelled by at most $(3 + 4 \log q)^{c_0}$ labels $b$, each with a size $P_b > 0$
  such that $\nu(m) > P_b$ on $X_b$ and $\sum_{m \in X_b} |c_m| \le P_b A_0$, for some $A_0 \ge 0$;
\item the $(m, p)$ are cut out by $J \le J_0$ comparisons as above, with $|e_{ji}| \le E_0$, $u_j(m) \le \Lambda_1
  \log q$ on $X$ and pivots;
\item wherever the comparisons all hold, every $p_i \ge 2q^{e_1}$ and $\nu(m) p_1 \cdots p_K \le 2q^L$;
\item \eqref{eq:H} holds on each block $X_b$ in place of $X$, with $B_1 = A_0 q^{L - 1/50000}$, for all scales $Q_i
  \ge q^{e_1}$ with $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0 + 1} q^L/P_b$.
\end{itemize}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fam-unw}
  \Bigl|\sum_{m \in X} \sum_{p_1, \dots, p_K \le N_2} c_m \prod_j I_j(m, p) \, \vartheta(\nu(m) p_1 \cdots p_K)\Bigr|
  \le \frac{A_0 \, q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proof}
Let $t_* = \lfloor \log_2 N_2 \rfloor + 1$, so that every prime up to $N_2$ is below $2^{t_*}$, and $t_* + 1 \le 3 + 4
\log q$ since $L \le 5/2$. Split the sum over the blocks, and in each block insert $\sum_{0 \le t_i \le t_*}
f_0(p_i/2^{t_i}) = 1$ for each prime. This writes the sum as a sum of at most $(3 + 4 \log q)^{c_0 + K_0}$ pieces, one
for each block and each choice of the scales $Q_i = 2^{t_i}$, in which $m$ runs over the block and the primes carry the
weights $f_0(p_i/Q_i)$. In a piece the primes may run over the ranges $p_i \le 2Q_i$ of a family instead of the primes
up to $N_2$: the weights vanish for $p_i > 2Q_i$, and a term with some $p_i > N_2$ violates the comparisons, which force
$\nu(m) \prod_i p_i \le 2q^L$. In a piece $p_i \le 2Q_i \le 8q^L$, so every form is at most $(\Lambda_1 + K_0 E_0 (L +
3)) \log q$ for $q \ge 2$.

Two kinds of piece vanish:
\begin{itemize}
\item a piece with some $Q_i < q^{e_1}$, since its weights force $p_i < 2q^{e_1}$;
\item a piece with $\prod_i Q_i > 2^{K_0 + 1} q^L/P_b$, since its weights force $p_i \ge Q_i/2$, and so $\prod_i Q_i
  \le 2^K \prod_i p_i \le 2^{K + 1} q^L/\nu(m) < 2^{K_0 + 1} q^L/P_b$ wherever the comparisons hold.
\end{itemize}
Every other piece is a family of the bound with smooth weights, with $K_0 + 1$ in place of $K_0$ (so that the
factor $2^{K_0 + 1}$ is allowed), $F_0 = 1$, $P = P_b$ and $\Lambda_0 = \Lambda_1 + K_0 E_0 (L + 3)$, whose hypothesis
\eqref{eq:H} is the one assumed on its block; so \eqref{eq:fam-gen} bounds it by $A_0 q^{L - \delta_1}/\varphi(q)$.
For large $q$ the number of pieces is at most $q^{\delta_1/2}$, and
$\delta = \delta_1/2$ will do.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Two variables}

This subsection checks \eqref{eq:H} for the two lemmas. Here $\mathcal R$ is the region fixed before
Lemma~\ref{M-lem:family-two}, on which the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2} is assumed. For integers $P_1, P_2
\ge 1$ with $(\log_q P_1, \log_q P_2) \in \mathcal R$, let $S_1 \subseteq (P_1, 2P_1]$ and $S_2 \subseteq (P_2, 2P_2]$
be sets of integers, and let $a'$ and $b'$ be coefficients with $|a'_{m_1}| \le A_0$ and $|b'_{m_2}| \le B_0$. For reals
$u_j(m_1)$, $v_j(m_2)$, $\xi_j$ and $\kappa$, the phase $\kappa + \sum_j \xi_j(u_j(m_1) + v_j(m_2))$ is
a phase on $m_1$ plus a phase on $m_2$, and $\chi(m_1 m_2) = \chi(m_1) \chi(m_2)$, so the twisted sum over the pairs is
a product of two Dirichlet polynomials:
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_{m_1 \in S_1} \sum_{m_2 \in S_2} a'_{m_1} b'_{m_2} \, e^{2\pi i(\kappa + \sum_j \xi_j(u_j(m_1) +
  v_j(m_2)))} \chi(m_1 m_2) = W_1(\chi) W_2(\chi), \\
  W_1(\chi) = \sum_{m_1 \in S_1} a'_{m_1} e^{2\pi i(\kappa + \sum_j \xi_j u_j(m_1))} \chi(m_1), \\
  W_2(\chi) = \sum_{m_2 \in S_2} b'_{m_2} e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j v_j(m_2)} \chi(m_2).
\end{gather*}
These are the polynomials of Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2} at the heights $t_1 = t_2 = 0$, with coefficients of moduli
$|a'_{m_1}|$ and $|b'_{m_2}|$. Since $S_\iota$ has at most $P_\iota$ elements, the conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2} on $\mathcal R$, with this $\eta$, gives for $q$ beyond its $q_0$
\begin{multline}\label{eq:pair}
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} |W_1(\chi) W_2(\chi)| \prod_i |S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i)| \\
  \le q^{L - 1/50000} \Bigl(\frac1{P_1} \sum_{S_1} |a'|^2\Bigr)^{1/2} \Bigl(\frac1{P_2} \sum_{S_2} |b'|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}
  \le A_0 B_0 \, q^{L - 1/50000}
\end{multline}
whenever $K \le K_0$, $Q_i \ge q^{e_1}$, $|h_i| \le q^\eta$ and $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/(P_1 P_2)$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:family-two}]
% Lean: SieveFamilyTwo.typeII_sharp_two
Take for $X$ the pairs $(m_1, m_2) \in S_1 \times S_2$, with the values $m_1 m_2$, the coefficients $a_{m_1} b_{m_2}$
and the parts $u_j(m_1) + v_j(m_2)$ of the forms. Then $\sum |a_{m_1} b_{m_2}| \le P_1 P_2 A_0 B_0$, and the

$b' = b$ or $|b|$, is the hypothesis \eqref{eq:H} with $B_1 = A_0 B_0 q^{L - 1/50000}$. The bound with smooth weights,
with $P = P_1 P_2$ and $A_0 B_0$ in place of $A_0$, is the lemma; its $\delta$ depends only on $J_0$ and $e_1$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:family-sharp-two}]
% Lean: SieveUnwTwo.typeII_unw_two
Here $S_\iota \subseteq (P_\iota, P_\iota']$ with $P_\iota' \le 2q^L$, and $\mathcal R$ contains the rectangle $[\log_q
P_1, \log_q P_1'] \times [\log_q P_2, \log_q P_2']$. Take $X$ as in the previous proof, with the function $f_0$ of the
bound without the weights, and cut it into the blocks
of the pairs with $P_1 2^{b_1} < m_1 \le P_1 2^{b_1 + 1}$ and $P_2 2^{b_2} < m_2 \le P_2 2^{b_2 + 1}$. Since $m_\iota

The proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:chains-two} applies the bound without the weights directly. There $X$ is the set of the

\section{Proofs of Propositions~\ref{M-prop:harman-two} and~\ref{M-prop:harman-fill}, and of
  Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}}\label{app:harman}

This section proves Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves} first, since the proofs of both propositions apply it. Three subsections
prepare the proofs: the tree identity, used by the proofs of the propositions; the bound at a leaf, used by them too,
whose ranges and estimates the proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves} reuses; and the spline weights of
Section~\ref{M-sec:type1}, used by that proof. The three proofs follow.

Write $\mathcal P[y, z)$ for the set of the primes in $[y, z)$ and $\mathcal P(y, z)$ for the set of those in $(y, z)$;
say that $n$ is \emph{$z$-rough} if it has no prime factor below $z$, and let $R_z(n)$ be $1$ if $n$ is $z$-rough and
$0$ otherwise. Throughout, $x = q^L$, $N_1 = \lfloor x \rfloor$, $N_2 = \lfloor 2x \rfloor$, and $\prod P$ is the
product of the elements of a set $P$ of primes; in the proofs of the propositions, $r = r_1 \cdots r_j$ for a tuple
$(r_1, \dots, r_j)$. For
$\mathcal C = \mathcal A$ or $\mathcal B$ and every $d \ge 1$, $S(\mathcal C_d, z)$ is the number of $z$-rough multiples
of $d$ in $\mathcal C$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SAB}
  S(\mathcal A_d, z) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, z)}{\varphi(q)} = \sum_{N_1 < n \le N_2} \vartheta(n) \, \mathbf 1[d \mid
  n]
  \, R_z(n).
\end{equation}

\subsection*{Buchstab's tree with a stopping rule}

The identity \eqref{eq:tree} below iterates Buchstab's identity with a rule that stops a branch early. Let $1 \le w
\le z$, let $d_0$ be a positive integer whose prime factors are all at least $z$, and let $\gamma$ and $E$
be reals with $0 \le \gamma < E$. For a set $P$ of primes put $d_P = d_0 \prod P$ and $\gamma_P = \gamma \prod P$. Then
for every $n$
\begin{multline}\label{eq:tree}
  \mathbf 1[d_0 \mid n] \, R_z(n)
  = \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P[w, z)} (-1)^{|P|} \, \mathbf 1[\gamma_P < E] \, \mathbf 1[d_P \mid n] \, R_w(n) \\
  + \sum_{p \in \mathcal P[w, z)} \ \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P(p, z)} (-1)^{|P| + 1} \,
    \mathbf 1[\gamma_P < E \le \gamma_P \, p] \, \mathbf 1[p \, d_P \mid n] \, R_p(n).
\end{multline}
% Lean: SieveTreeStop.tree_weighted_stop, SieveP6Tree.tree_split, SieveP6Tree.tree_chains (z = z_0);
% SieveFillTree.tree_split_below, SieveFillTree.tree_last (z the last prime of a chain, gamma = 1)
This is what iterating Buchstab's identity $S(\mathcal C_m, z) = S(\mathcal C_m, w) - \sum_{w \le p < z} S(\mathcal
C_{mp}, p)$ from $d_0$ at $z$ gives, when a branch $m = d_P$ is stopped as soon as $\gamma_P$ reaches $E$: the first sum
collects the \emph{leaves} $S(\mathcal C_d, w)$, $d = d_P$, and the second the \emph{stops} $S(\mathcal C_W, p)$, $W = p
\, d_P$, whose last prime $p$ is the least.

We prove \eqref{eq:tree} directly, for each $n$. If $d_0 \nmid n$ or $n$ is not
$w$-rough, every term vanishes. Otherwise let $Q$ be the set of the primes of $[w, z)$ that divide $n$; the left side is
$\mathbf 1[Q = \emptyset]$. Since the primes of $d_0$ are at least $z$, a leaf $P$ counts $n$ exactly when $P \subseteq
Q$, and a stop $(p, P)$ exactly when $p = \min Q$ and $P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p\}$. If $Q = \emptyset$, only the leaf
$P = \emptyset$ counts $n$, with $\mathbf 1[\gamma < E] = 1$. If $Q \ne \emptyset$, let $p_0 = \min Q$; grouping $P
\subseteq Q \setminus \{p_0\}$ with $P \cup \{p_0\}$, the right side is
\[
  \sum_{P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p_0\}} (-1)^{|P|} \bigl(\mathbf 1[\gamma_P < E] - \mathbf 1[\gamma_P \, p_0 < E]
  - \mathbf 1[\gamma_P < E \le \gamma_P \, p_0]\bigr) = 0,
\]
since $\gamma_P \le \gamma_P \, p_0$.

Multiplying \eqref{eq:tree} by $\vartheta(n)$ and summing over $n \in (N_1, N_2]$,
\eqref{eq:SAB} writes $S(\mathcal A_{d_0}, z) - S(\mathcal B_{d_0}, z)/\varphi(q)$ as the sum over the leaves of
$(-1)^{|P|}(S(\mathcal A_d, w) - S(\mathcal B_d, w)/\varphi(q))$ plus the sum over the stops of $(-1)^{|P| +
1}(S(\mathcal A_W, p) - S(\mathcal B_W, p)/\varphi(q))$. The proofs below write a set $P$ as the decreasing tuple $y_1 >
\dots > y_s$ of its elements; with the primes of $d_0$ in front, the leaves and the stops are then decreasing tuples
of primes.

\subsection*{A leaf by Brun's sieve}

Let $C_0 \ge 1$ be an absolute constant with $\sum_{p \in \mathcal P[y, y^2)} 1/p \le C_0$ for every $y \ge 1$; it
exists by Mertens' theorem. The number $\zeta_k$ of
Section~\ref{M-sec:type1} is
\[
  \zeta_k = e^{2C_0} \cdot \frac{2 C_0^k}{k!} \cdot \exp\Bigl(e^{2C_0} \frac{2 C_0^{k + 1}}{(k + 1)!}\Bigr),
\]
and $(2k + 4)^3 \zeta_k \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$, since the last factor tends to $1$ and $C_0^k/k!$ decays faster than
any power of $1/k$. Let $k \ge 2$ be even with $2C_0^2 \le (k + 1)(k + 2)$, let $w \ge 2$, and let $d$ have all its
prime factors at least $w$. We show
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaf}
  \Bigl|S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr|
  \le \frac{2 \zeta_k (N_2 - N_1)}{d \varphi(q) \log w} + 24 \, w^{2k + 7}.
\end{equation}
% Lean: SieveProp6.leaf_bound; SieveBrun.fundamental (Brun's sieve), SieveLeaf.leaf_gap (the gap),
% SieveLeaf.prod_T_le, SieveLeaf.prod_primesBelow_le (the main term)
If $(d, q) > 1$ both counts vanish, so let $(d, q) = 1$. Sieve by the primes below $w$, in the ranges $T_i = \mathcal
P[w^{2^{-i - 1}}, w^{2^{-i}})$ for $0 \le i < J_w = 2 \lfloor \log_2 w \rfloor + 2$, which cover them, with the even
parameters $k_i = k + 2i$ of Brun's sieve, which grow with $i$ so that the errors $C_0^{k_i}/k_i!$ below have a sum
that does not depend on $J_w$.

Brun's sieve \cite{brun} is used in the following form, for $J$ disjoint sets $T_0, \dots, T_{J - 1}$ of primes and even
parameters $k_i \ge 2$. Let $\mathcal C$ be a finite set of integers, $Z \ge 0$, and $g$ a function on the primes of
$\bigcup T_i$ with
$0 \le g \le 1$. Suppose that for every choice of $s_i \subseteq T_i$, with $\Pi = \prod_i \prod_{p \in s_i} p$, the
count $\#\{n \in \mathcal C : \Pi \mid n\}$ is $Z \prod_i \prod_{p \in s_i} g(p)$ within $\Delta$. Then $\#\{n \in
\mathcal C : (n, \prod_i \prod_{T_i} p) = 1\}$ is $Z \prod_i \prod_{p \in T_i} (1 - g(p))$ within
\[
  Z \sum_i e_{k_i}(T_i) \prod_{l \ne i} \Psi_{k_l}(T_l) + (J + 1) \, \Delta \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m},
\]
where $e_m(T) = \sum_{s \subseteq T,\ |s| = m} \prod_{p \in s} g(p)$ and $\Psi_k(T) = \sum_{m \le k} (-1)^m e_m(T)$.

To prove this form, recall Bonferroni's inequalities: $\Psi_{k - 1}(T) \le \prod_{p \in T}(1 - g(p)) \le \Psi_k(T)$ for
every even $k$, and the
same for any weights in $[0, 1]$ in place of $g$. Take them for each $n \in \mathcal C$ with the weights $\mathbf 1[p
\mid n]$, and use the elementary inequality $\prod_i \mathrm{hi}_i - \sum_i (\mathrm{hi}_i - \mathrm{lo}_i) \prod_{l \ne
i} \mathrm{hi}_l \le \prod_i \varrho_i$ for $\varrho_i \ge 0$ and $\mathrm{lo}_i \le \varrho_i \le \mathrm{hi}_i$ (the
$\mathrm{lo}_i$, odd truncations, may be negative). Summed over $n$, and expanded by the hypothesis, these inequalities
place the count, up to $(J + 1)$ times $\Delta \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m}$, in the interval from
$Z \prod_i \Psi_{k_i} - Z \sum_i e_{k_i} \prod_{l \ne i} \Psi_{k_l}$ to $Z \prod_i \Psi_{k_i}$. The model
$Z \prod_i V_i$, with $V_i = \prod_{p \in T_i}(1 - g(p))$, lies in the same interval: Bonferroni's inequalities for $g$
give $\Psi_{k_i} - e_{k_i} = \Psi_{k_i - 1} \le V_i \le \Psi_{k_i}$, hence $0 \le \prod_i \Psi_{k_i} - \prod_i V_i \le
\sum_i e_{k_i} \prod_{l \ne i} \Psi_{k_l}$.

Apply this form with $J = J_w$, the ranges $T_i$ above, and $g(p) = 1/p$ for $p \nmid q$ and $g(p) = 0$ for $p \mid
q$. For $\mathcal A_d$, take $\mathcal C = \{n \in (N_1,
N_2] : n \equiv a,\ d \mid n\}$, $Z = (N_2 - N_1)/(dq)$ and $\Delta = 1$: if no prime of $\Pi$ divides $q$,
Lemma~\ref{M-lem:type1} with $M = d\Pi$ gives the count within $1$, and otherwise the count and the model both vanish.
For $\mathcal B_d$, take $\mathcal C = \{n \in (N_1, N_2] : (n, q) = 1,\ d \mid n\}$, $Z\varphi(q)$ in place of $Z$, and
$\Delta =
\varphi(q)$, and divide by $\varphi(q)$. Both main terms are then $ZV$ with $V = \prod_{p < w,\ p \nmid q}(1 - 1/p)$, so
that
\begin{gather*}
  \Bigl|S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr| \le 2(ZG + \mathrm{rem}), \\
  G = \sum_i e_{k_i}(T_i) \prod_{l \ne i} \Psi_{k_l}(T_l), \qquad
  \mathrm{rem} = (J_w + 1) \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m}.
\end{gather*}

Each $T_i$ lies in some $\mathcal P[y, y^2)$, so $\sum_{p \in T_i} g(p) \le C_0$ and, since $1 - t \ge e^{-2t}$ for $0
\le t \le 1/2$, $V_i \ge e^{-2C_0}$. With $e_m \le C_0^m/m!$, $\Psi_{k_l} \le V_l + e_{k_l + 1} \le V_l (1 + e^{2C_0}
C_0^{k_l + 1}/(k_l + 1)!)$ and $\prod_{l \ne i} V_l \le e^{2C_0} V$, this gives $G \le V e^{2C_0} \sum_i
\frac{C_0^{k_i}}{k_i!} \prod_l \bigl(1 + e^{2C_0} \frac{C_0^{k_l + 1}}{(k_l + 1)!}\bigr)$. Since $2C_0^2 \le (k + 1)(k +
2)$, the terms of $\sum_i C_0^{k + 2i}/(k + 2i)!$ decrease at least geometrically with ratio $1/2$, so the sum is at
most $2C_0^k/k!$; likewise, since $2C_0^2 \le (k + 2)(k + 3)$, $\sum_l C_0^{k_l + 1}/(k_l + 1)! \le 2C_0^{k + 1}/(k +
1)!$; and with $\prod (1 + t_l) \le \exp \sum t_l$ we get $G \le \zeta_k V$. So the difference is at most $2\zeta_k ZV +
2\,\mathrm{rem}$.

Next, $V \le q/(\varphi(q) \log w)$. Indeed $V \prod_{p < w,\ p \mid q}(1 - 1/p) = \prod_{p < w}(1 -
1/p)$, the second product on the left is at least $\prod_{p \mid q}(1 - 1/p) = \varphi(q)/q$, and $\prod_{p < w}(1 -
1/p) \le 1/\log w$, since $\prod_{p < w}(1 - 1/p)^{-1}$ is the sum of $1/m$ over the integers $m \ge 1$ whose prime
factors are all below $w$, which is at least $\sum_{m < w} 1/m \ge \log w$. So $ZV \le (N_2 - N_1)/(d \varphi(q) \log
w)$.

Finally $\sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m} \le 2 \max(1, |T_i|)^{k_i}$ with $|T_i| \le w^{2^{-i}}$, $\sum_i
2^{-i}(k + 2i) \le 2k + 4$, $J_w + 1 \le 3w$ and $2^{J_w} \le 4w^2$ give $\mathrm{rem} \le 12 w^{2k + 7}$.

\subsection*{The spline weights of Section~\ref{M-sec:type1}}

For integers $A < B$, $\ell \ge 1$ and $r \ge 1$ let $w(k)$ be $\ell^{-r}$ times the number of $(i, j_1, \dots, j_r)$
with $A < i \le B$, $0 \le j_\nu < \ell$ and $i + j_1 + \cdots + j_r = k$. It is the average over the $\ell^r$ tuples
$(j_\nu)$ of the indicators of the intervals $(A + \sum j_\nu, B + \sum j_\nu]$, so $0 \le w \le 1$, $w = 1$ on $(A +
r(\ell - 1), B]$, $w = 0$ off $(A, B + r(\ell - 1)]$, and $\sum_k w(k) = B - A$. Its transform factors: $\hat w(h) =
\ell^{-r} \bigl(\sum_{A < i \le B} e_q(hi)\bigr) \bigl(\sum_{j < \ell} e_q(hj)\bigr)^r$. For $h$ the residue of an
integer $t$ with $0 < |t| \le q/2$, a geometric sum at $e_q(t)$, of any length, is at most $q/(2|t|)$ in size, so $|\hat
w(h)| \le (B - A)(q/(2\ell|t|))^r$, which is at most $(B - A)(q/(2\ell(H + 1)))^r$ when $|t| > H$. If each residue class
mod $q$ holds at most $M$ elements of the support, Parseval's identity and $|w(k) w(k')| \le \tfrac12(|w(k)|^2 +
|w(k')|^2)$ over the pairs in a residue class give $\sum_h |\hat w(h)|^2 \le qM \sum_k |w(k)|^2 \le qM(B - A) \le qMn$,
since $0 \le w \le 1$; for the support, an interval
of length $n = B - A + r(\ell - 1)$, $M = \lfloor n/q \rfloor + 1$. Finally $|E_w| \le d(q)$: for the indicator of an
interval $(A', B']$, Möbius inversion over the divisors of $q$ writes $E_w$ as $\sum_{d \mid q} \mu(d)\bigl((\lfloor
B'/d \rfloor - \lfloor A'/d \rfloor) - (B' - A')/d\bigr)$, and each bracket is less than $1$ in size; the spline
averages such intervals.

\subsection*{Proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}}
% Lean: SieveP6LeavesWide.leaves_chain_wide, SieveP6LeavesWide.leavesOK_wide, SieveTypeIBAsymWide.leaves_ib_wide

\begin{proof}
Write $R = R_w$, $\mathrm{rng}(d, m)$ for the indicator of $N_1 < dm \le N_2$, and $I(\mathbf y_1, \mathbf y_2)$ for
that of the comparisons. For $d$ with no prime factor below $w$, by \eqref{eq:SAB}, $S(\mathcal A_d, w) - S(\mathcal
B_d, w)/\varphi(q) = \sum_m \mathrm{rng}(d, m) R(m) \, \vartheta(dm)$. The proof has four steps, one or two
paragraphs each: Brun's weights for $R$; a sandwich of the cofactor's range by splines; blocks of the moduli, after the
choice of the parameters; and Lemma~\ref{M-lem:typeIB} on each pair of blocks. Three paragraphs then choose the cut
on the frequencies, bound the terms of that lemma in powers of $q$, and bound the main terms. The cut lets the diagonal
of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:typeIB} hold more than one integer, and so lets $d_1 d_2$ exceed $x/q$ and $d_1 d_2^2$ exceed $x$.

\emph{Brun's weights.} Sift by the primes below $w$ in the ranges $T_i$ of the proof of \eqref{eq:leaf}, with the even
parameters $k_i = k + 2i$. Bonferroni's inequalities, with the elementary inequality of the proof of \eqref{eq:leaf}
for the weights below, give weights $\lambda^+$ above and $\lambda^-$ below $R$: $\sum_\tau \lambda^-_\tau \mathbf
1[f_\tau \mid m] \le R(m) \le \sum_\sigma \lambda^+_\sigma \mathbf 1[f_\sigma \mid m]$, with moduli $f \le w^{2k + 4}$
and $\sum |\lambda| \le 2\,\mathrm{rem}$. Every $|\lambda|$ is $1$, so $\sum |\lambda|/f$ is at most $(J_w + 2)
\prod_i \prod_{p \in T_i}(1 + 1/p) \le (J_w + 2) e^{C_0(\log_2 \log_2 w + 1)}$, a power of $\log q$, as at most
$\log_2 \log_2 w + 1$ of the $T_i$ hold a prime. The gap $\Gamma$ between the two weights' main terms, $\sum_\sigma
\lambda^+_\sigma \mathbf 1[(f_\sigma, q) = 1]/f_\sigma - \sum_\tau \lambda^-_\tau \mathbf 1[(f_\tau, q) = 1]/f_\tau$,
is at most $\zeta_k q/(\varphi(q) \log w)$ in size, as the gap $G$ of that proof.

\emph{The sandwich.} Split each $\lambda$ into its positive and negative parts. With splines $\omega_- \le
\mathrm{rng}(d f_\sigma, \cdot) \le \omega_+$ (weights of the previous subsection, written $\omega$ since $w$ bounds the
sifting primes), chosen below for each block, the count of $\mathcal A$ is at most $\sum_\sigma (\lambda^+_\sigma)_+
\sum_m \omega_+(m) \mathbf 1[d f_\sigma m \equiv a] - \sum_\sigma (\lambda^+_\sigma)_- \sum_m \omega_-(m) \mathbf 1[d
f_\sigma m \equiv a]$, and at least the same expression with the weights $\lambda^-$ and the splines
exchanged; and the same holds for $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$. The difference between the $\mathcal A$- and the $\mathcal
B/\varphi(q)$-counts is thus bounded by two kinds of term: the difference of the main terms, which carries the gap
$\Gamma$ and the difference of the splines' masses, and bilinear sums
$\sum \lambda \sum_m \omega(m) \, \vartheta(d f m)$.

\emph{The parameters.} Put $X_1 = q^{L - 1 - \xi}$, $X_2 = q^{L + c}$ and $X_3 = q^{L - 1 - \delta'}$, so that every
leaf
has $d_1 f \le X_1$, $d_1 f d_2^2 \le X_2$ and $d_1 d_2 \ge X_3$ for every modulus $f$ of Brun's weights. Let
\begin{itemize}
\item $s = \min(\kappa/(4(J_0 + 6)), \varepsilon_w, 1/(4(J_0 + 6)))$, the exponent of the losses: the number of
  blocks, the cut-offs and the divisor bound each cost a power of $q^s$, up to powers of $\log q$;
\item $\theta = q^{-s}$, the relative width of the blocks, of the splines' slopes and of the smooth cut-offs;
\item $\rho = 1 + \theta$, the ratio of the blocks (not related to the $\rho_1$, $\rho_2$ of the propositions);
\item $y = \min(\kappa/4, 1/8)$ and $Y = q^y$, which set the cut on the frequencies;
\item $r = \lceil 2/y \rceil$, the power of the splines, so that $(2Y)^{-r} \le q^{-2}$;
\item $F = \lfloor C_d q^s \rfloor$, where $d(n) \le C_d n^{s/(L + 2)}$ for every $n \ge 1$, so that $F$ bounds the
  number of divisors of $q$ and of every integer up to $q^{L + 2}$.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Blocks.} Group the pairs $(d_1 f, d_2)$, $d_1 = \prod \mathbf y_1$ and $d_2 = \prod \mathbf y_2$, by the blocks
$\lfloor \log(d_1 f)/\log \rho \rfloor$ and $\lfloor \log d_2/\log \rho \rfloor$; a block pair $(\beta_1, \beta_2)$ puts
$d_1 f d_2$ in $[\rho^{\beta_1 + \beta_2}, \rho^{\beta_1 + \beta_2 + 2})$. At the scale $D' = \rho^{\beta_1 + \beta_2}$
one majorant $\omega_+$, equal to $1$ on $(\lfloor N_1/(D'\rho^2) \rfloor, \lfloor N_2/D' \rfloor]$, and one minorant
$\omega_-$,
supported in $(\lfloor N_1/D' \rfloor, \lfloor N_2/(D'\rho^2) \rfloor]$, both splines of step $\ell = \lfloor \theta
N_1/(rD'\rho^2) \rfloor + 1$ and power $r$, sandwich $\mathrm{rng}(d, \cdot)$ for every $d$ of the block pair, and their
masses differ by at most $(N_1 + N_2)(1 - \rho^{-2})/D' + 2\theta N_1/(D'\rho^2) + 2$. Since $d_1 f \le X_1$ and $d_2
\le X_2^{1/2}$, at most $(3Lq^s \log q)^2$ block pairs hold a leaf.

\emph{A block pair.} On a block pair Lemma~\ref{M-lem:typeIB} applies, at each frequency of the separation below, with
$\mathcal D_1$ the values $d_1 f$, whose coefficients aggregate the fibres $\{(\mathbf y_1, \sigma) : \prod \mathbf y_1
\cdot f_\sigma = d_1 f\}$ (at most $(J_w + 1) d(d_1 f)$ elements, by the uniqueness of the factorisation), $\mathcal
D_2$ the values $d_2$ (a decreasing tuple of primes is determined by its product), $P_\iota = \rho^{\beta_\iota + 1}$,
$D = \lfloor P_2 \rfloor$, and the cut $H = \lceil qY/\ell \rceil$; each residue class contains the inverses of at most
$\lfloor P_1/q \rfloor + 1$ of the values $d_1 f$, since those with a given inverse lie in one residue class mod $q$.
The values $d_1 f$ or $d_2$ not prime to $q$ are left out, since $\vartheta(d_1 f d_2 m) = 0$ for them.

The comparisons are replaced by smooth cut-offs $G_j$ of their forms, of width $\theta$, as in the paragraph \emph{Sharp
against smoothed} of \S\ref{app:family}: the smooth part is separated by Fourier inversion into bilinear forms of the
lemma's kind, at a cost of the norms $\|\hat G_j\|_1$, and the part
near the boundaries is treated as there: with $|\vartheta(n)| \le \vartheta(n) + 2 \cdot \mathbf 1[(n, q) =
1]/\varphi(q)$, the part with $\vartheta$ is again a bilinear form of the lemma's kind, with the narrow cut-offs $M_j$,
and
the part with $2/\varphi(q)$ is counted along the pivots. Provided $2H < q$ and $F$ bounds the number of divisors of the
integers up to $HD$, the four bilinear sums of a block pair (two weights, each split into its positive and negative
parts) are so bounded by $4\bigl(\prod_j \|\hat G_j\|_1 + \sum_j \|\hat M_j\|_1\bigr)$
times the bounds on the fibres times
\begin{multline}\label{eq:block-pair}
  \sqrt{P_1 P_2 (P_1/q + 1) F (2HP_2/q + 1) (n/q + 1) n} \\
  + (B - A) \Bigl(\frac q{2\ell(H + 1)}\Bigr)^r P_1 P_2 + P_1 P_2 \, \frac{d(q)}{\varphi(q)},
\end{multline}
with $n$ the length of the spline's support and $B - A$ its mass, plus the parts near the boundaries counted along the
pivots.
% Lean: SieveTypeIBPairWide.pair_le_wide (typeIB_smooth_wide, typeIB_sharp0_wide), SieveTypeIB.typeIB_bound_wide,
% SieveSpline.typeIB_spline_wide

\emph{The cut.} On a block pair that holds a leaf, $D' \le d_1 f d_2 < D'\rho^2$ and $(d_1 f d_2)^2 \le d_1 f \cdot d_1
f d_2^2 \le X_1 X_2 \le q^{2L - 1}$, since $c \le \xi$. So $\theta N_1/(rD'\rho^2) \le \theta N_1/(rX_3) \le q^{1 +
\delta' - s}$, and the step has $\ell \le q^{1 + \delta' - s} + 1 \le qY$ for large $q$, since $\delta' \le y$: this is
where $\delta' \le \min(\kappa/4, 1/8)$ enters. Hence $qY/\ell \ge 1$ and $H \le 2qY/\ell$, and since $\rho^{\beta_2} D'
\le d_2 \cdot d_1 f d_2 \le X_2$ and $\ell \ge \theta N_1/(rD'\rho^2)$,
\[
  \frac{2HP_2}q + 1 \le \frac{4YP_2}\ell + 1 \le \frac{4Yr\rho^3 X_2}{\theta N_1} + 1.
\]
Moreover $H \le qYr\rho^2 D'/(\theta N_1) + 1 \le 8rq^{1/2 + y + s} + 1 < q/2$ for large $q$, since $D' \le (X_1
X_2)^{1/2} \le q^{L - 1/2}$ and $y + s \le 1/6$; and $HD \le 2qYr\rho^3 X_2/(\theta N_1) \le q^{L + 2}$, so $F$ bounds
the divisors that the lemma needs. Since $H + 1 > qY/\ell$, the second term of \eqref{eq:block-pair} has $(q/(2\ell(H +
1)))^r \le (2Y)^{-r} \le q^{-yr} \le q^{-2}$, whatever the size of $d_2$.
% Lean: SieveTypeIBSumWide.bdPW_le (the cut H = ceil(qY/l)), SieveTypeIBSumWide.witness_le_wide

\emph{The bound in powers of $q$.} On a block pair that holds a leaf, $P_1 P_2 n$ and $(B - A) P_1 P_2$ are at most
$\rho^2(1 + \theta) N_2 \le 16 q^L$, $P_1/q + 1 \le \rho X_1/q + 1 \le 3q^{L - 2 - \xi}$ since $\xi \le L - 2$, $n/q + 1
\le
(1 + \theta)\rho^2 N_2/(X_3 q) + 1 \le 17 q^{\delta'}$, and
\[
  F\Bigl(\frac{2HP_2}q + 1\Bigr) \le C_d q^s \bigl(64 r q^{y + s + c} + 1\bigr)
  \le (64r + 1) C_d \, q^{\xi - \delta' - \kappa + y + 2s},
\]
since $\max(c, 0) \le \xi - \delta' - \kappa$: this is where the lemma's condition on $c$ enters, and $c$ may be
positive.
So the three terms of \eqref{eq:block-pair} are at most
\[
  \sqrt{816(64r + 1) C_d} \, q^{L - 1 - (\kappa - y - 2s)/2}, \qquad 16 q^{L - 2}, \qquad
  4C_d \, q^{L - 1/2 + s}/\varphi(q),
\]
the last since $P_1 P_2 \le \rho^2 (X_1 X_2)^{1/2} \le 4q^{L - 1/2}$ and $d(q) \le C_d q^s$; a block pair that holds no
leaf contributes nothing. For at most $J_0$ cut-offs, $\prod_j \|\hat G_j\|_1 + \sum_j \|\hat M_j\|_1 \le (J_0 +
1)(C q^s)^{J_0}$, with $C$
that of \S\ref{app:family}, and the fibres at most $q^{s/8}$ for large $q$. Since $\kappa - y - 2s \ge 3\kappa/4 - 2s
\ge (3J_0 + 16)s$ by the choice of $s$, the bilinear sums total at most $C'(\log q)^2 q^{L - 3s/4}/\varphi(q)$, with
$C'$ independent of $q$.
% Lean: SieveTypeIBAsymWide.bdUW_le_pow, SieveTypeIBSumWide.blocks_le_wide, SieveTypeIBSumWide.leaves_family_wide

\emph{The main terms.} Write $\Sigma_0 = \sum |\lambda|$, $\Sigma_1 = \sum |\lambda|/f$ and $\Theta = (N_1 + N_2)(\rho^2
- 1) + 2\theta N_1$, which is $D'\rho^2$ times the bound of \emph{Blocks} on the difference of the splines' masses, less
its term $2D'\rho^2$; since $\rho^2 - 1 \le 3\theta$ and $N_1 + N_2 \le 3q^L$, $\Theta \le 11 q^{L - s}$. The
difference of the main terms is $(N_2 - N_1)/q \cdot \Gamma \cdot \sum I/d$, plus the transition from the splines'
masses, at most $(\Sigma_1 \Theta/q) \sum I/d + (4\Sigma_0/q + 2\Sigma_0 d(q)/\varphi(q)) \sum I$. Since $(d_1
d_2)^2 \le X_1 X_2 \le q^{2L - 1}$ on the leaves, $\sum I \le q^{L - 1/2} \Sigma_*^{K_1 + K_2}$, where $\Sigma_* =
\log((L + 1)/\varepsilon_w) + 1$ bounds the sums of $1/p$ over the primes in $[w, N_2]$ for large $q$; and the counted
parts near the boundaries are at most $(12\theta + 1/w) \Sigma_*^{K_1 + K_2 - 1}$ times $2(1 + \theta)\rho^2 N_2
\Sigma_1/\varphi(q)$, by the pivot count of \S\ref{app:family}, since every prime is at least $w \ge q^s$. With
$\Sigma_0 \le 2\,\mathrm{rem} \le
24 w^{2k + 7} \le q^{1/4}$, all these are at most $(\log q)^{C''} q^{L - 3s/4}/\varphi(q)$ for a constant $C''$; added
to the bilinear sums, they are below $q^{L - s/4}/\varphi(q)$ for large $q$. With $|\Gamma| \le \zeta_k q/(\varphi(q)
\log w)$ this is the lemma, with $\delta = s/4$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman-two}}
% Lean: SieveTwoComp.prop6_two_at (prop6_two); at one q SieveTwoP6.prop6_two_core, with SieveTwoP6.root_bound2,
% SieveTwoP6.stops_bound2, SieveTwoP6.tree_pattern2_ib, SieveTwoTerm.chainOK2_of_zone, SieveP6TreeLeaves.tree_leaves_le

\begin{proof}
The proof follows the sketch after Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman-two}. It chooses the constants, splits the tuples
into two cases, and in the case $r_{\mathcal S_0} \le P_1$ applies the tree identity \eqref{eq:tree}; the stops are
bounded by Lemma~\ref{M-lem:term-two}, and the leaves by \eqref{eq:leaf} and Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}.

\emph{The constants.} They are chosen before $q$ and depend only on the data of the proposition. Let $D_1$ and $D_2$ be
as in (d), and
\[
  c' = \tfrac1{32} \min\bigl(1,\ L - 1 - \max(D_1, 1),\ 2L - 1 - \max(D_1, 1) - D_1 - 2D_2\bigr),
\]
which is positive by (d) and $L > 2$. Put $M_0 = (\log((L + 1)/a_0) + 1)^j$, which bounds $\sum 1/r$ in the
conclusion. Since $(2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k \to 0$, there is
an even $k \ge 2$ with $2C_0^2 \le (k + 1)(k + 2)$, $c' < (2k + 4)a_0$ and $(2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k \le \eta c'^2/(4e(M_0 +
1)a_0)$. Put $\varepsilon_w = c'/(2k + 4)$, so that $0 < \varepsilon_w < a_0$, $(2k + 4)\varepsilon_w = c' \le 1/16$ and
$(2k + 7)\varepsilon_w \le 2c'$; and put $s_{\max} = \lceil (L + 2)/\varepsilon_w \rceil$, which bounds the number of
primes that the tree adds; $\Lambda_1' = \Lambda_1 + L + 2$, so that $\Lambda_1' \log q$ bounds the constant terms of
the stops' comparisons; and $\Lambda_0 = \Lambda_1' + (j + s_{\max})(L + 1)$, so that $\Lambda_0 \log q$ bounds the
forms of the leaves' comparisons. Let $\delta_1 >

Lemma~\ref{M-lem:term-two} gives, with $e_0 = \varepsilon_w$, $J_0 + s_{\max} + 5$ comparisons and $\Lambda_1'$, for the

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:rough}}\label{app:rough}

\begin{proof}
The proof is an induction on $u$ through Buchstab's inequality (b). In it, the induction hypothesis and the prime sums
of (a) bound the sum over the least prime factor $p$ by about $M(u)\, y/\mathcal L$, where $\mathcal L = \log 2y$ and
$M(u)$ is defined in (d). The delay inequality (d), $1 + M(u) \le u\omega^*(u)$, then closes the induction. Write $c_*
= 0.565073$, the value of $\omega^*$ above $3$, and $\bar\omega = 0.5671433$, an upper bound for the maximum of
$\omega$ on $[2, 3]$; let $u = \log 2y/\log z$, and say that an integer is $z$-rough if it has no prime factor below
$z$. Four facts are used.

\emph{(a) Prime sums.} By the prime number theorem with an error $o(t/\log t)$ (in the library, the bound
$\psi(t) - t = O(t^{11/12 + \varepsilon})$, which it derives from the case of $\zeta$ of Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane};
the classical theorem would do), for every $\varepsilon_3 > 0$,
$|\theta(t) - t| \le \varepsilon_3 t/\log t$ for large $t$. With partial summation this gives, for large $a \le b$,
\[
  \sum_{a < p \le b} \frac1{p \log p} \le \frac1{\log a} - \frac1{\log b} + \frac{4\varepsilon_3}{\log^2 a},
\]
and, if $2 \log b \le \mathcal L$,
\[
  \sum_{a < p \le b} \frac1{p(\mathcal L - \log p)}
  \le \frac{1 + \varepsilon_3/\log a}{\mathcal L} \log \frac{\log b \, (\mathcal L - \log a)}{\log a \, (\mathcal L -
  \log b)} + \frac{6\varepsilon_3}{\mathcal L \log^2 a};
\]
and, for large $y$, there are at most $(1 + \varepsilon_3) y/\log 2y$ primes in $(y, 2y]$.

\emph{(b) Buchstab's inequality.} For $y \ge 2$,
\[
  \Phi(y, z) \le \#\{p : y < p \le 2y\} + \sum_{z \le p \le \sqrt{2y}} \Phi(y/p, p),
\]
since a $z$-rough integer $m \in (y, 2y]$ that is not prime has its least prime factor $p$ in $[z, \sqrt{2y}]$, and
$m/p$ is a $p$-rough integer in $(y/p, 2y/p]$; the map $m \mapsto (p, m/p)$ is injective. Also $\Phi(y, z) = 0$ if
$y \ge 1$ and $2y < z$, since every integer of $(y, 2y]$ is then at least $2$ and below $z$.

\emph{(c) Buchstab's function on $[2, 3]$.} For $u > 1$, $1 + \log(u - 1) \le \bar\omega u$. Indeed $\log t \le t - 1$
at $t = \bar\omega(u - 1)$ gives $1 + \log(u - 1) \le \bar\omega(u - 1) - \log \bar\omega \le \bar\omega u$, since
$-\log \bar\omega \le \bar\omega$, that is $\bar\omega e^{\bar\omega} \ge 1$, which holds because $e^{\bar\omega} \ge
\sum_{m \le 12} \bar\omega^m/m! > 1/\bar\omega$. So $\omega^* \le \bar\omega$ on $[2, 3]$, and hence on $[2, \infty)$,
since $c_* < \bar\omega$. And $\omega^* \le c_*$ on $[3, \infty)$: above $3$ it is $c_*$, and $\omega^*(3) = (1 + \log
2)/3 < c_*$.

\emph{(d) A delay inequality.} Let $M(u) = \log(u - 1)$ for $2 \le u \le 3$ and
$M(u) = \log 2 + \bar\omega \min(u - 3, 1) + c_* \max(u - 4, 0)$ for $u > 3$, which is $\int_1^{u - 1} \omega^*(t)\, dt$
with
$\omega^*$ replaced by $\bar\omega$ on $[2, 3]$. Then $1 + M(u) \le u \omega^*(u)$ for $u \ge 2$. For $u \le 3$ it is an
equality. For $u > 3$, $\omega^*(u) = c_*$, and $1 + M(u) - c_* u$ is affine on $[3, 4]$, with slope $\bar\omega - c_* >
0$, and
constant on $[4, \infty)$; so it suffices that it is at most $0$ at $u = 4$, that is $1 + \log 2 + \bar\omega \le 4c_*$,
which
holds since $\log 2 < 0.6931471808$ and $2.2602904808 < 2.260292 = 4c_*$.

We prove by induction on the integer $k \ge 0$ the statement $\mathrm C(k)$: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is
$z_1$ such that $\Phi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$ for all $z \ge z_1$ and all $y \ge 1$ with
$u \le k$. $\mathrm C(0)$ holds trivially, since $u > 0$, and the lemma is $\mathrm C(\lceil U \rceil)$. Assume
$\mathrm C(k)$, let $0 < \varepsilon \le 1$ (which suffices, since the bound for one $\varepsilon$ implies it for every
larger one) and $\varepsilon_3 = \varepsilon/100$, let $z$ be large in terms of
$\varepsilon$ and of the $z_1$ of $\mathrm C(k)$ for $\varepsilon/4$, and let $y \ge 1$ with
$\mathcal L = \log 2y = u \log z \le (k + 1) \log z$. If $2y < z$, then $\Phi(y, z) = 0$. Otherwise $u \ge 1$, $y \ge
z/2$ is large, and (a) and (b) give
\[
  \Phi(y, z) \le \Bigl(1 + \frac\varepsilon4\Bigr) \frac{y}{\mathcal L} + \sum_{z \le p \le \sqrt{2y}} \Phi(y/p, p).
\]
If $u < 2$, then $\sqrt{2y} < z$, the sum is empty, and
$\Phi(y, z) \le (1 + \varepsilon/4) y/(u \log z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$.

Let $u \ge 2$. A prime
$p \in [z, \sqrt{2y}]$ has $y/p \ge 1$ and
$\log(2y/p) = \mathcal L - \log p \le k \log p$, since $\mathcal L \le (k + 1) \log p$. So $\mathrm C(k)$ gives
$\Phi(y/p, p) \le (\omega^*(u_p) + \varepsilon/4) \, y/(p \log p)$ with $u_p = (\mathcal L - \log p)/\log p$. The term
$p = z$, if $z$ is prime, which (a) does not cover, contributes at most $2y/(z \log z)$, since $\omega^* \le 1$ and
$\varepsilon \le 1$. By (a), the parts
$\varepsilon/4$ of the other terms contribute at most $(\varepsilon/4)(1/\log z + 4\varepsilon_3/\log^2 z) \, y$. For
the parts $\omega^*(u_p)$, the primes $p \in (z, \sqrt{2y}]$ fall into three ranges; on each, (a) bounds the sum of
$\omega^*(u_p)/(p \log p)$ by the following, up to its terms in $\varepsilon_3$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $p \le (2y)^{1/4}$, which occurs only if $u > 4$: then $u_p \ge 3$ and $\omega^*(u_p) \le c_*$, and the first
  bound of (a) gives $c_*(u - 4)/\mathcal L$.
\item $(2y)^{1/4} < p \le (2y)^{1/3}$, which occurs only if $u > 3$ and starts at $z$ if $u \le 4$: then $2 \le u_p <
  3$ and $\omega^*(u_p) \le \bar\omega$, and the first bound gives $\bar\omega \min(u - 3, 1)/\mathcal L$.
\item $(2y)^{1/3} < p \le \sqrt{2y}$, which starts at $z$ if $u \le 3$: then $1 \le u_p < 2$ and $\omega^*(u_p)/(p
  \log p) = 1/(p(\mathcal L - \log p))$, by the definition of $\omega^*$ on $[1, 2)$, and the second bound of (a), with
  $\log b = \mathcal L/2$, gives $(\log 2)/\mathcal L$, or $\log(u - 1)/\mathcal L$ if $u \le 3$.
\end{itemize}
These add up to $M(u)/\mathcal L$, and with the terms in $\varepsilon_3$,
\[
  \sum_{z < p \le \sqrt{2y}} \frac{\omega^*(u_p)}{p \log p} \le \frac{M(u) + 12\varepsilon_3}{\mathcal L}
  + \frac{8\varepsilon_3}{\log z}.
\]
Collecting the terms, with $\mathcal L = u \log z$,
\[
  \Phi(y, z) \le \frac y{\log z} \Bigl(\frac{1 + M(u)}u + \frac{\varepsilon/4 + 12\varepsilon_3}u + \frac2z
  + 8\varepsilon_3 + \frac\varepsilon4 + \frac{\varepsilon \varepsilon_3}{\log z}\Bigr).
\]
By (d) the first term is at most $\omega^*(u)$, and since $u \ge 1$ and $z$ is large the others add up to at most
$0.96\varepsilon$. So $\Phi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$, which proves $\mathrm C(k + 1)$.
\end{proof}

\emph{A fact used in Section~\ref{M-sec:decomp}.} The bound $\Omega$ of that section, though not the proof above,
uses that on $[2, 3]$, $\omega^*(u) = (1 + \log(u - 1))/u$ increases up to $2.76$ and decreases from $2.77$ on. For
$2 \le u \le u' \le 2.76$, $\omega^*(u) \le \omega^*(u')$ is $(u' - u)(1 + \log(u - 1)) \le u \log((u' - 1)/(u - 1))$:
the right side is at least $u(u' - u)/(u' - 1)$, by $\log t \ge 1 - 1/t$, and the left at most $(u' - u)u/1.76$, by
$\log t \le t - 1$ at $t = (u - 1)/1.76$ and $\log 1.76 \le 1/1.76$; and $u/1.76 \le u/(u' - 1)$ since $u' \le 2.76$.
For $2.77 \le u \le u'$ the reverse inequality
follows in the same way from $\log((u' - 1)/(u - 1)) \le (u' - u)/(u - 1)$ and
$\log(u - 1) \ge \log 1.77 \ge 1/1.77 \ge 1/(u - 1)$. The inequalities $\log 1.76 \le 1/1.76$ and $\log 1.77 \ge
1/1.77$ are checked with partial sums of the exponential series, the second with a bound for the series' tail.

\section{The formalisation in detail}\label{sup:lean}

This section completes Section~\ref{M-sec:lean} of the paper.

\subsection*{The statements}

The statements of the density estimates count the characters $\chi$ mod $q$, as elements of Mathlib's
\lean{DirichletCharacter ℂ q}, for which \lean{LFunction χ s = 0} at some $s$ in the rectangle. Mathlib's $L$-function
of a character mod $q$ is the analytic continuation of $\sum_n \chi(n) n^{-s}$, with the character taken mod $q$ and not
reduced to a primitive one. So $L(s, \chi)$ is the $L$-function of the primitive character inducing $\chi$ times the
Euler factors at the primes dividing $q$, whose zeros lie on $\operatorname{Re} s = 0$ and do not affect the counts for
$\sigma \ge 1/2$.

Huxley's estimate is proved in the library in its own form, for the characters mod $q$
(\file{formalisations/huxley-density.md}): each step is an instance of \lean{LinnikHuxStep.charDensity\_gen} at
parameters $(M, a, \lambda_{\mathrm H}, A_i, A)$ (the largest power $M$, the exponent $a$ of the target of the power
trick, the exponent $\lambda_{\mathrm H}$ of the lengths of the powers, an inner exponent $A_i$, and the exponent $A$ of
the step's bound $(qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)}$) that satisfy the conditions \lean{LinnikHuxStep.Par.Good}, linear inequalities
at
the step's two ends with an internal $\varepsilon = 10^{-5}$. The step of $g_{\mathrm H}$ on $[7789/10000, 131/168]$,
\lean{SieveCurveHx.charDensity\_hxT}, has $M = 1497$, $a = 18703/25000$, $\lambda_{\mathrm H} = 224439/200000$, $A_i =
224447/100000$ and $A = 7014/3125$, the parameters of least $A$ on a grid, found and checked in exact rationals by
\file{tools/linnik/gen\_zone.py}; the fifteen steps from $131/168$ on are \lean{LinnikStairs7.charDensity\_hx7\_5},
\dots, \lean{LinnikStairs7.charDensity\_hx7\_19}, and the exponent $101/50$ from $5/6$ on is
\lean{LinnikStep.charDensity\_step}. \file{formalisations/} records, for each result from the literature, the
differences between the Lean statement and the published one.

\subsection*{The library's proof of Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane}}

The library proves Input~\ref{M-inp:half-plane} as \lean{Eis.LFunction\_ne\_zero\_of\_re\_gt}, along the route of
OpenAI's
companion preprint \cite{openai-companion}, on its own toolchain; it imports nothing from OpenAI's release
(\file{formalisations/openai-half-plane.md}). The route has five steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Power savings for the smoothed Möbius sums of the Dedekind zeta function of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ give the
  half-plane.
\item The target is embedded in a family of sextic twists, over which a mean square bounds it.
\item Poisson summation and the Gauss--Jacobi identities turn the Möbius function times sextic Gauss sums into cubic
  Gauss sums.
\item Kubota's cubic theta function \cite{kubota} supplies the transformation, with Patterson's formula for its
  coefficients \cite{patterson} and Dunn and Radziwi\l\l's cusp expansions \cite{dunn-radziwill}; the quadratic large
  sieve over $\mathbb Z[\omega]$ \cite{hb-quadratic, goldmakher-louvel} bounds the dual mean square.
\item Möbius inversion removes the cube factors and gives the exponent $11/12$.
\end{enumerate}
The route gives $\zeta$ and $L(s, \chi_{-3})$ first, then the real characters, and then every character with $\chi^2
\ne 1$
through the family twisted by a character modulo $4q$. The classical results that the route uses,
among them cubic and sextic reciprocity \cite{ireland-rosen}, are theorems of the library. The import cone of the
theorem in the library has $223$ files, all of them in \texttt{lib/core}.

\section{Proofs for the resonant window}\label{sup:window}

This section holds the proofs that Section~\ref{M-sec:window} of the paper leaves to the supplement: that of
Proposition~\ref{M-prop:tuples-rest}, the tuples of primes with a rest, and those of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:window-kernel}(a)
and~(b), the tests of the window that Lean's kernel checks, with the details of those tests. The notation is that of

\subsection*{Tuples of primes with a rest}

This block proves Proposition~\ref{M-prop:tuples-rest}. It follows \S S2: (A) the bound without the weights, with a
lower bound on each prime; (B) the window at each frequency, for a polynomial over a finite set; then the tuples, (C)
with the variable in a range, (D) with the product in $(N_1, N_2]$, and finally in exponents. Throughout, $f_0$ is the
fixed function
$f_0(y) = F(y) - F(y/2)$ of the bound without the weights, the prime sums are taken with it, $\eta = 10^{-5}$, $N_1 =
\lfloor q^L \rfloor$ and $N_2 = \lfloor 2q^L \rfloor$.

\emph{(A) Families with a lower bound on each prime.} The bound without the weights holds with numbers $\sigma_1,
\dots, \sigma_K$ for the primes, chosen after $q$, in the following form. Its hypotheses are those of the bound without
the weights (stated before \eqref{eq:fam-unw}), with two changes: wherever the comparisons all hold, every $p_i \ge
2q^{\sigma_i}$ as well as $p_i \ge 2q^{e_1}$; and \eqref{eq:H}, the Type~II bound at each frequency, is asked on each
block only for the scales with $Q_i \ge q^{\sigma_i}$ as well as $Q_i \ge q^{e_1}$ and
$Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0 + 1} q^L/P_b$. Its conclusion is \eqref{eq:fam-unw}, with $\delta$ and $q_0$ depending on
$K_0$, $J_0$, $c_0$, $L$, $e_1$, $E_0$ and $\Lambda_1$ only, not on the $\sigma_i$.
% Lean: SieveUnwGen.unw_gen_lo

\begin{proof}
The proof of \eqref{eq:fam-unw}, with one more piece that vanishes. On a piece, a term that is not zero has $p_i \ge
Q_i/2$, from the support of $f_0$, and $p_i \le 2Q_i$; with $p_i \ge 2q^{\sigma_i}$ this gives $Q_i \ge q^{\sigma_i}$,
as $p_i \ge 2q^{e_1}$ gives $Q_i \ge q^{e_1}$, and $\prod_i Q_i \le 2^K \prod_i p_i \le 2^{K + 1} q^L/\nu(m) \le 2^{K_0
+ 1} q^L/P_b$. So a piece whose scales miss one of these bounds vanishes, and every other piece is a family of the bound
with smooth weights, with $K_0 + 1$ in place of $K_0$, $P = P_b$ and $\Lambda_0 = \Lambda_1 + K_0 E_0 (L + 3)$, whose
hypothesis \eqref{eq:H} is the one assumed. The count of the pieces is that of \eqref{eq:fam-unw}.
\end{proof}

\emph{(B) The window at each frequency, for a polynomial over a finite set.} Assume Inputs~\ref{M-inp:half-plane}
and~\ref{M-inp:cgl}. Given a real $L$, an integer $K_0$ and $e_1 > 0$, there is $q_0$ such that the following holds for
every $q \ge q_0$. Let $X$ be a finite set, $P \ge 1$ an integer and $\nu(m) \in (P, 2P]$ integers for $m \in X$, with
at most $D_0$ elements of $X$ for each value of $\nu$, for an integer $D_0$, and let $|c_m| \le 1$ on $X$. Let $1 \le
K \le K_0$, $Q_j \ge q^{e_1}$ and $|h_j| \le q^\eta$ with $Q_1
\cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/P$, and suppose $\operatorname{Res}_L(\log_q P; \log_q Q_1, \dots, \log_q Q_K)$. Then
\[
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \Bigl|\sum_{m \in X} c_m \chi(\nu(m))\Bigr| \prod_{j = 1}^K |S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)|
  \le D_0 \, q^{L - 1/50000}.
\]
% Lean: SieveTypeIIHM.box_res_gen

\begin{proof}
The sum over $X$ is the Dirichlet polynomial over the values $\nu(X) \subseteq (P, 2P]$, at the height $0$, with the
coefficients $b_n = \sum_{\nu(m) = n} c_m$. By Cauchy--Schwarz over each fibre, $|b_n|^2 \le D_0 \sum_{\nu(m) = n}
|c_m|^2$, so $V = \sum_n |b_n|^2 \le D_0 |X| \le D_0^2 P$. Theorem~\ref{M-thm:window} with this $K_0$, $e_1$ and $\eta$
bounds the sum by $q^{L - 1/50000} (V/P)^{1/2} \le D_0 q^{L - 1/50000}$.
\end{proof}

\emph{(C) Tuples with the variable in a range.} Assume Inputs~\ref{M-inp:half-plane} and~\ref{M-inp:cgl}. Let $0 < L
\le 5/2$, $e_0 > 0$, $k$ and $J_0$ integers and $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$ a real. There are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that
the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let $i_1, \dots, i_K$ be distinct indices of
$\{1, \dots, k\}$, $\mathcal S_r$ their set, and $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_K$ reals; let $\mathbf z$ run over the
$k$-tuples of primes up to $N_2$ that satisfy at most $J_0$ comparisons with constant terms at most $\Lambda_1 \log
q$; and let $P \ge 1$ and $P'$ be integers. Suppose that every tuple of primes that satisfies the comparisons has $z_t
\ge q^{e_0}$ for every $t$, $z_{i_j} \ge 2q^{\sigma_j}$ for every $j$, $P < \prod_{t \notin \mathcal S_r} z_t \le P'$
and $z_1 \cdots z_k \le 2q^L$; and that $\operatorname{Res}_L(\log_q P''; \log_q Q_1, \dots, \log_q Q_K)$ holds for
every integer $P'' \ge P$ and all $Q_j \ge q^{e_0/2}$ with $Q_j \ge q^{\sigma_j}$ and $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{k + 1}
q^L/P''$. Then $|\sum_{\mathbf z} \vartheta(z_1 \cdots z_k)| \le q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$.
% Lean: SieveTypeIIHM.chain_res_core

\begin{proof}
Write each tuple as its part on the indices outside $\mathcal S_r$, padded with $1$ on $\mathcal S_r$, times its rest
$(z_{i_1}, \dots, z_{i_K})$ put at $\mathcal S_r$; then $K \le k$, and the variable is the product $\nu$ of the padded
part. A comparison with a nonzero coefficient at an index of $\mathcal S_r$ is pivoted there, and its form is $u(\mathbf
z) + \sum_j e_j \log z_{i_j}$ with $u(\mathbf z) = c + \sum_{t \notin \mathcal S_r} e_t \log z_t \le (\Lambda_1 + k(L +
1))\log q$, since $z_t \le N_2 \le q^{L + 1}$. A comparison with no such coefficient depends on the padded part alone.
Let $X$ be the set of the padded parts $m$ with $P < \nu(m) \le P'$ and $\nu(m) \le N_2$ that satisfy those comparisons,
with the value $\nu(m)$ and the coefficient $c_m = 1$. Cut $X$ into the blocks $P 2^b < \nu(m) \le P 2^{b + 1}$, at most
$3 + 4\log q$ of them. At most $D_0 = (k + 1)^k$ padded parts have a given product, since their entries are $1$ or prime
factors of it; so the block $b$, of size $P_b = P 2^b$, has at most $D_0 P_b$ elements, and $\sum_{m \in X_b} |c_m|
\le P_b A_0$ with $A_0 = D_0$.

Apply (A), with $K_0 = k$, $J_0$, $c_0 = 1$, $e_1 = e_0/2$, $E_0 = 1$ and $\Lambda_1 + k(L + 1)$, and with the numbers
$\sigma_j$, and write $\delta_1$ for its $\delta$. Its support hypothesis holds: $z_{i_j} \ge q^{e_0} \ge
2q^{e_0/2}$ for large $q$, $z_{i_j} \ge 2q^{\sigma_j}$, and $\nu(m) \prod_j z_{i_j} \le 2q^L$. Its hypothesis
\eqref{eq:H} on a block that holds an element is (B) for the polynomial over that block, with $K_0 = k + 1$, $e_1 =
e_0/2$, $D_0$, the coefficients $c'_m e^{2\pi i(\kappa + \sum_j \xi_j u_j(m))}$, of modulus $1$ for $c'_m = c_m$ and
$c'_m = |c_m|$ alike ($c_m = 1$), and $\operatorname{Res}_L$ at $P'' = P_b \ge P$. With $K_0 = k + 1$, (B) allows the
scales up to $2^{k + 1} q^L/P_b$, which is what (A) asks with $K_0 = k$. A block with no element contributes nothing.
The bound is $D_0 q^{L -
\delta_1}/\varphi(q) \le q^{L - \delta_1/2}/\varphi(q)$ for large $q$, and $\delta = \delta_1/2$. No condition between
two variables arises, so Lemma~\ref{M-lem:cross} is not used.
\end{proof}

\emph{(D) Tuples with the product in $(N_1, N_2]$.} (C) holds, with the same $\delta$, with the hypotheses on each
tuple $z_t \ge q^{e_0}$, $z_{i_j} \ge 2q^{\sigma_j}$, $R_- < \prod_j z_{i_j} \le R_+$ and $N_1 < z_1 \cdots z_k \le
N_2$ (in place of $P < \prod_{t \notin \mathcal S_r} z_t \le P'$ and $z_1 \cdots z_k \le 2q^L$), for integers $R_- \ge
0$ and $R_+$ with $\lfloor N_1/R_+ \rfloor \ge 1$, and $\operatorname{Res}_L$ asked at every integer $P'' \ge \lfloor
N_1/R_+ \rfloor$ (in place of $P'' \ge P$).
% Lean: SieveTypeIIHM.full_res_boundG, each prime its own part

\begin{proof}
Take $P = \lfloor N_1/R_+ \rfloor$ and $P' = \lfloor N_2/(R_- + 1) \rfloor$. Since $\prod_t z_t$ is the variable times
the rest's product, $N_1 < \prod_t z_t$ and $\prod_j z_{i_j} \le R_+$ give $P < \nu$; $\prod_j z_{i_j} \ge R_- + 1$ and
$\prod_t z_t \le N_2$ give $\nu \le P'$; and $\prod_t z_t \le N_2 \le 2q^L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:tuples-rest}]
% Lean: SieveTypeIIHM.full_res_exp
Let $\delta$ be that of (D), for $k$, $J_0$ and $\Lambda_1$, and take $q$ so
large that $q^\varepsilon \ge 8$, $q^{L - \rho_2} \ge 2$, $q \ge 2$, $q^{L - \rho_2 - \varepsilon} \ge 2^{k + 1}$ and
$q^{10^{-5}} \ge 2^{k + 1}$. Apply it with the numbers $\sigma_j - \varepsilon$, $R_- = \lceil q^{\rho_1} \rceil - 1$
and $R_+ = \lfloor q^{\rho_2} \rfloor$. The ranges hold: $2q^{\sigma_j - \varepsilon} \le q^{\sigma_j} \le z_{i_j}$;
$q^{\rho_1} \le \prod_j z_{i_j}$ gives $\lceil q^{\rho_1} \rceil \le \prod_j z_{i_j}$; and $\prod_j z_{i_j} \le
\lfloor q^{\rho_2} \rfloor$. The variable's lower end: $8 q^{L - \rho_2 - \varepsilon} q^{\rho_2} \le q^L$ and $2
q^{\rho_2} \le q^L$ give $\lceil 4 q^{L - \rho_2 - \varepsilon} \rceil \lfloor q^{\rho_2} \rfloor \le (4 q^{L - \rho_2 -
\varepsilon} + 1) q^{\rho_2} \le q^L$, so $\lfloor N_1/R_+ \rfloor \ge 4 q^{L - \rho_2 - \varepsilon} \ge 1$. The
condition: let $P'' \ge \lfloor N_1/R_+ \rfloor$ and $Q_j \ge q^{\sigma_j - \varepsilon}$ with $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le
2^{k + 1} q^L/P''$, and put $m = \log_q P''$ and $s_j = \log_q Q_j$. Then $m \ge L - \rho_2 - \varepsilon$ and $s_j \ge
\sigma_j - \varepsilon$; $P'' \ge q^{L - \rho_2 - \varepsilon} \ge 2^{k + 1}$ gives $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le q^L$, that is
$\sum_j s_j \le L$; and $Q_1 \cdots Q_K P'' \le 2^{k + 1} q^L \le q^{L + 10^{-5}}$ gives $\sum_j s_j + m \le L +
10^{-5}$. So $\operatorname{Res}_L(m; s_1, \dots, s_K)$ holds, by the hypothesis on the quadrant. The lower bound $Q_j
\ge q^{e_0/2}$ is not needed here.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The kernel's test of the resonance condition}

This subsection proves Lemma~\ref{M-lem:window-kernel}(a) and~(b). For (a): if the test
$\operatorname{Res}^{\mathrm k}_L(c_1, \dots, c_K; H)$ passes, an inequality $(\star)$ holds that gives the condition on
the quadrant (\emph{the target}). One inequality settles $\gamma \le 0$. On each interval of $(0, 1]$ that the hint
gives, every term gets an upper bound in $\gamma$ that is affine or convex, and the sum is checked at the two ends. The
test has two variants, without and with the large values' term, and the hint chooses between them; (a) covers both.
For (b), the test runs at two corners of the rest's cells. How the hints are written, which the proofs do not use, is
in the last two paragraphs.

Exponents are of $q$ throughout; $L/D$ converts a unit of the cells into an exponent of $q$, and $\tau = 10^{-6}$. The
fixed point has the unit $1/Z$, $Z = 2^{64}$: an integer $n$ stands for $n/Z$, a product is
rounded down ($\lfloor xy/Z \rfloor$) or up ($\lceil xy/Z \rceil$), and every rounding goes the way that keeps the
computed bound above the true one.

\emph{The target.} The test reads the rest's lower ends $c_1, \dots, c_K$ and the sum $H$ of its upper ends, and checks

\[

\]
for all reals $\beta$, $\gamma$, $m$, $s_j$ and $t_j$ with
\begin{itemize}
\item $1/2 \le \beta \le 11/12$ and $\gamma \le g_{\mathrm H}(\beta)$;
\item $m \ge \tilde m_0$, $s_j \ge \tilde\sigma_j$ for every $j$, and $\sum_j s_j \le L$;
\item $t_j \le \beta + 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$ and $\gamma \le \mathfrak m_k(s_j, t_j - \tau)$ for every $j$ and every $k \ge
  1$.
\end{itemize}
Here $\operatorname{cl}(\gamma, m) = \min(\max(1 - m, 0)/2, \max(0, \gamma/2 + y_0(m)))$ is the clamp, with $y_0(m) =
(5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon' - m(11/12 + \delta'))/2$ of Section~\ref{M-sec:corners}, $\delta' = \varepsilon' =
1/1000$.

inequality $(\star)$ implies that $(E, \gamma, m)$ fit at $L$ (Definition~\ref{M-def:fit}) with $E = \sum_j s_j(t_j -

\tau)$ and $\sum_j s_j + m \le L + 10^{-5}$ of the condition are used only by the variant with the large values, below.

\emph{Negative $\gamma$.} For $\gamma \le 0$, $t_j - 1 \le \beta + 4 \cdot 10^{-6} - 1 \le -(1/12 - 4 \cdot 10^{-6})$ by

\begin{itemize}
\item For $u < 7/10 + \epsilon_{\mathrm{lv}}$, $s(u - 1) \le -\tilde\sigma(3/10 - \epsilon_{\mathrm{lv}})$.
\item For $7/10 + \epsilon_{\mathrm{lv}} \le u \le 4/5 - \epsilon_{\mathrm{lv}}$, $k\tilde\sigma \ge 2/3 +

\item $u > 4/5 - \epsilon_{\mathrm{lv}}$ is impossible, since there $\mathfrak v_k(s, u) \le \Gamma_k(\tilde\sigma,

\emph{Why two ends suffice.} The lines ($l_A$), ($l_B$), ($l_C$), ($l_W$) and those of the clamp are affine in $\gamma$,
so their sum with $\gamma/2$ is at most $0$ on $[a, b]$ when it is at both ends. The function
$\operatorname{LV}_k(\cdot\,; \tilde\sigma)$ is a maximum of four affine functions, so
it is convex: for $\gamma = (1 - \theta)a + \theta b$, $\operatorname{LV}_k(\gamma; \tilde\sigma) \le (1 -
\theta)\operatorname{LV}_k(a; \tilde\sigma) + \theta \operatorname{LV}_k(b; \tilde\sigma)$. The test uses its value
at $a$ in the check at $a$ and its value at $b$ in the check at $b$, so the total at $\gamma$ is at most $(1 - \theta)$
times the total at $a$ plus $\theta$ times that at $b$, which is at most $0$.

of its row of the table after Definition~\ref{M-def:leaf-region}, with the $c_1$ and $c_2$ of that row in place of $M_1$
and $M_2$, as comparing coefficients shows. A piece is of the kind \lean{zonePiece4\_OE} when $A(1 - \beta_+) \ge T_* =
1/2 - 2\varepsilon' = 249/500$, the $T_*$ of Section~\ref{M-sec:region}, a rule of the script, not a

\emph{The extension above $\sqrt q$.} The set $\mathcal R^{\mathrm{lf}}_L$ is \lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak
LeafZoneX}, and the proposition for it is \lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak zone\_leafX}: for $m_2 \le 1/2$ it is
\lean{SieveTypeIIHM.\allowbreak zone\_leaf} at $L$, and for $m_2 \ge 1/2$ it is \lean{SieveTypeIIHM.\allowbreak
zone\_leaf} at $L - m_2 + 1/2$ applied to $(m_1, 1/2)$, with \lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak twoBound4\_shift}. That
lemma keeps $M_1$ and takes $M_2 + (m_2 - 1/2)$, by
\lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak sqExp4\_shift}: an exponent with the third bound for the length $q^{1/2}$ gives one for
$q^m$, $m \ge 1/2$, larger by $m - 1/2$, for each of the three bounds, given $\delta' \ge 0$. For the third bound the
shifted exponent is the exponent $g/2 + m + 2\varepsilon'$ itself; for orthogonality it is $m + 1/2 \ge \max(m, 1)$, a
weaker bound.
The shift avoids a second case analysis. Above $m_2 = 1/2$ the region in $(m_1, \rho)$ is the polygon with the
vertices $(0.9, 0.792218)$, $(0.932874, 0.718773)$, $(0.980270, 0.621606)$, $(1, 0.587006)$, $(1.2, 0.587006)$, $(1.2,
1)$ and $(0.9, 1)$, cut out by the box, (C3), (C5), (C6) and (C7) at $m_2 = 1/2$ (a computation).

\emph{The test of the region on a rectangle.} Lean's kernel tests the region in the form of $19$ half-planes,
\lean{SieveLeafZoneX.\allowbreak LeafZoneXL} (\file{lib/pnt/SieveLeafZoneX.lean}): the inequalities of $\mathcal
R^{\mathrm{lf},0}_L$ but $m_2 \le 1/2$, that is, the five others of the box and the seven cuts, and the seven cuts
again with $1/2$ in place of $m_2$.
\lean{SieveLeafZoneX.\allowbreak leafZoneX\_of\_XL} proves that $\mathcal R^{\mathrm{lf}}_L$ contains their
intersection, by the cases $m_2 \le 1/2$ and $m_2 \ge 1/2$. The two sets are in fact equal, since every cut has the
coefficient $1/2$ on $m_2$; the formalisation does not need this. The test \lean{SieveLeafZoneX.\allowbreak leafXR}
(\file{lib/pnt/SieveLeafZoneXDefs.lean}, in natural numbers only, written by the same script) takes the
coordinates $(m_1, \rho)$, with $m_2 = L - m_1 - \rho$, on a rectangle $[\kappa P_1, \kappa P_2] \times [\kappa R_1,
\kappa R_2]$, at $L = \kappa D$; in the sieve $\kappa = L/D$ and $P_i$, $R_i$ are exponents of $x$ in units of $1/D$.
With $L = L_n/L_d$, each half-plane is multiplied by $L_d D$ and by its denominator, and is checked at the corner of
the rectangle where it is hardest, which the signs of its coefficients on $(m_1, \rho)$, once $m_2$ is eliminated,
decide:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
half-planes & coefficients on $m_1$, $\rho$ & checked at \\
\midrule
(C1), (C3), (C5) & $0$, negative & $\rho = \kappa R_2$ \\
(C2), (C4), (C6), (C7), and $m_2 \ge 2/5$ & negative, negative & $(\kappa P_2, \kappa R_2)$ \\
the seven cuts at $m_2 = 1/2$ & nonnegative, nonnegative & $(\kappa P_1, \kappa R_1)$ \\

The other four inequalities of the box are checked at an end of one range: $m_1 \ge 9/10$ at $\kappa P_1$, $m_1 \le
6/5$ at $\kappa P_2$, $\rho \ge 3/10$ at $\kappa R_1$ and $\rho \le 1$ at $\kappa R_2$. In integers, $m_2 \ge 2/5$, that
is $5m_1 + 5\rho + 2 \le 5L$, is $5L_nP_2 + 5L_nR_2 + 2L_dD \le 5L_nD$.

The soundness of the test is \lean{SieveLeafZoneX.\allowbreak leafXR\_sound}: for integers $L_d, D > 0$ and a real
$\kappa$ with $L_n = L_d D\kappa$, if the test passes, then every $(m_1, \rho)$ of the rectangle gives a point $(m_1,
\kappa D - m_1 - \rho)$ of the $19$ half-planes at $L = \kappa D$. The proof casts each inequality to the reals and
divides it by $L_d D$ times its denominator; it does not use the sign of $\kappa$. The test (L$_3$) of
Definition~\ref{M-def:leaves3} applies it to the rectangle of a box's third range, with $P_1 = A^* - 1$, $P_2 = A +
1$, $R_1 = B^* - 1$ and $R_2 = B + 1$, for the integers $A$, $B$, $A^*$ and $B^*$ of that definition (the $A$ and $B$
of the next subsection are other numbers).

\emph{The leaves.} Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2-leaves} is \lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak typeIITwoAt\_leafX}; the same
estimate on $\mathcal R^{\mathrm{lf},0}_L$ alone, \lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak typeIITwoAt\_leaf}, is proved and not
used. Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaf-term} is \lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak leavesE\_of\_zone}, whose conclusion
\lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak LeavesE} is the bound for every length $K \le K_0$; it follows from
\lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak leaf2\_bound}, the bound for one length, by induction on $K_0$. In
\lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak leaf2\_bound} the cofactor is written as tuples by \lean{SieveChain.\allowbreak
sum\_mult\_eq} and \lean{SieveChain.\allowbreak sum\_rough\_tuples}, the pairs $(\mathbf y, \mathbf z)$ of the proof of
Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaf-term} are the $(K + t)$-tuples cut out by the comparisons \lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak fullLW}
(\lean{SieveLeafTwo.\allowbreak sum\_fullLW\_eq}), and
Lemma~\ref{M-lem:chains-two} is \lean{SieveTwoChain.\allowbreak chain\_two}. The sifting function is the paper's:
$S(\mathcal C_d, w)$ counts the multiples of $d$ in $\mathcal C$ with no prime factor below $w$
(\lean{SieveTree.\allowbreak rough}).

\subsection*{The comparisons with the leaves cut in three}
% Lean: SieveTwoCompE.prop6_two3_at, SieveFillCompE.prop6_fill3_at

This subsection proves Propositions~\ref{M-prop:harman-two3} and~\ref{M-prop:harman-fill3}. The notation is that of
\S\ref{app:harman}: $N_1 = \lfloor x \rfloor$, $N_2 = \lfloor 2x \rfloor$, $w = \lceil q^{\varepsilon_w} \rceil$,
$\zeta_k$ the constant of \eqref{eq:leaf}, and $Z_0 = \zeta_k (N_2 - N_1)/(\varphi(q) \log w)$, the coefficient of the
main
term of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}. The proofs are those of \S\ref{app:harman} but for the large leaves, which are cut in
three; the first three paragraphs treat the leaves, and the two proofs follow.

\emph{One family cut in three.} This bound is used below for the large leaves. Let $\mathcal Y$ be a
family of $K$-tuples $\mathbf y$ of primes up to $N_2$; let $I$ be a set of indices with $I$ and its complement
nonempty, $d_1 = \prod_I y_i$, $d_2 = \prod_{i \notin I} y_i$ and $d = d_1 d_2$; and let $X_1, X_2 > 0$ be two
thresholds (new ones, not those of the proof of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}). Suppose that
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(Y1)] $\mathcal Y$ is cut out by at most $J_0 - 2$ comparisons, none of them constant (so each has a pivot), its
  tuples are strictly decreasing with entries at least $w$, and every tuple has $d \ge q^{L - 1 - \delta'}$;
\item[(Y2)] the forms of these comparisons and of the cuts $d_1 \le X_1$, $d_1 > X_1$ and $d_2 \le X_2$ are at most
  $\Lambda_0 \log q$;
\item[(Y3)] the conclusion of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves} holds at $q$ with $(\xi_1, c_1)$ and with $(\xi_2, c_2)$, for
  the same $\delta'$, $\varepsilon_w$, $k$, $\Lambda_0$, $J_0$, tuple lengths and $\delta$;
\item[(Y4)] the tuples with $d_1 \le X_1$ satisfy the lemma's conditions on $d_1 w^{2k + 4}$ and $d_1 w^{2k + 4} d_2^2$
  at $(\xi_1, c_1)$, and those with $d_1 > X_1$ and $d_2 \le X_2$ at $(\xi_2, c_2)$;
\item[(Y5)] the sum of $S(\mathcal A_d, w) - S(\mathcal B_d, w)/\varphi(q)$ over the tuples with $d_1 > X_1$ and $d_2 >
  X_2$ is at most $q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ in absolute value.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{\mathbf y \in \mathcal Y} \Bigl(S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr)\Bigr|
  \le Z_0 \sum_{\mathbf y \in \mathcal Y} \frac1d + \frac{3q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\]

Indeed, the comparisons $d_1 \le X_1$ and then $d_2 \le X_2$ split $\mathcal Y$ into three disjoint families, (T1) the
tuples with $d_1 \le X_1$, (T2) those with $d_1 > X_1$ and $d_2 \le X_2$, and (T3) those with $d_1 > X_1$ and $d_2 >
X_2$, of at most $J_0$ comparisons each; the new ones have pivots, since $I$ and its complement are nonempty, and their
forms are at
most $\Lambda_0 \log q$. Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves} at $(\xi_1, c_1)$ bounds the first by $Z_0 \sum_{(\mathrm{T1})} 1/d +
q^{L -
\delta}/\varphi(q)$, at $(\xi_2, c_2)$ the second by $Z_0 \sum_{(\mathrm{T2})} 1/d + q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$, and the
third is
the assumed bound. Since $Z_0 \ge 0$ and $1/d > 0$ on the third family, $Z_0\sum_{(\mathrm{T1})} 1/d +
Z_0\sum_{(\mathrm{T2})} 1/d \le Z_0
\sum_{\mathcal Y} 1/d$, and the triangle inequality gives the bound. The main term is that of the uncut family, and the
error is three times Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}'s.

\emph{A tree's leaves, size by size.} In the proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman-two}, the leaves of Buchstab's
tree come in at most $s_{\max}$ sizes $s$, the number of their new primes; those of a size are the tuples $(r_1, \dots,
r_j, y_1, \dots, y_s)$, and the set $I_s$ of
the places of $d_1$ is $\mathcal S_1$ with, if $\iota = 1$, the places of the $s$ new primes. Every leaf has $d_1 \le
4q^{D_1}$ and $d_2 \le 4q^{D_2}$. At each size, split the leaves by $d \le q^{L - 1 - \delta'}$, $\delta' = 4c'$: the
small ones are counted by \eqref{eq:leaf} as before. For the large ones, one of three cases holds. If $I_s$ is empty,
then $d_1 = 1 \le q^{D_1^*}$, and all of them satisfy the conditions of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves} at $(\xi_1, c_1)$. If
its
complement is empty, then $d_2 = 1 \le q^{D_2^*}$, and all of them satisfy those at $(\xi_2, c_2)$. Otherwise they are
cut in three as above, with $X_1 = q^{D_1^*}$ and $X_2 = q^{D_2^*}$. The size conditions follow from the bounds of the
blocks
and $w^{2k + 4} \le 2^{2k + 4} q^{c'}$, $c' = (2k + 4)\varepsilon_w$, once
\begin{gather*}
  4 \cdot 2^{2k + 4} \le q^{L - 1 - \xi_1 - D_1^* - c'}, \qquad 64 \cdot 2^{2k + 4} \le q^{L + c_1 - D_1^* - 2D_2 - c'},
  \\
  4 \cdot 2^{2k + 4} \le q^{L - 1 - \xi_2 - D_1 - c'}, \qquad 64 \cdot 2^{2k + 4} \le q^{L + c_2 - D_1 - 2D_2^* - c'},
\end{gather*}
which hold for large $q$ by the choice of the exponents below. Summing over the sizes, with the sum of $1/d$ over the
leaves bounded as in \S\ref{app:harman}, the alternating sum over the leaves of the tree is at most
\[
  \Bigl(2Z_0 + 24 w^{2k + 7} q^{L - 1 - \delta'}\Bigr) \sum \frac1r \prod_{w \le p < z_0} \Bigl(1 + \frac1p\Bigr) +
  \frac{3s_{\max}\, q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)},
\]
with $24 w^{2k + 7}$ the remainder of \eqref{eq:leaf} and the sum over the tuples $(r_1, \dots, r_j)$. The cut adds two
comparisons to those of each size. One family of leaves, not a tree, is bounded in the same way, with the error
$3q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$.

\emph{The third range's rectangle.} Let $q > 1$ and $D_1^*, D_2^*, D_1, D_2 \ge 0$ with $64 \le q^{L - D_1 - D_2}$ and
$64 \le q^{\varepsilon_E}$, and take the ranges $A = \lfloor q^{D_1^*} \rfloor < d_1 \le B = \lfloor 4q^{D_1} \rfloor$
and $A_r = \lfloor q^{D_2^*} \rfloor < d_2 \le B_r = \lfloor 4q^{D_2} \rfloor$: these are the $P_1$, $P_1'$, $R_-$ and
$R_+$ of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaf-term}. Then $A, B, B_r \ge 1$ and $BB_r \le 16q^{D_1 + D_2} \le q^L/4 \le N_1$, and the
rectangle of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaf-term} lies in that of (d$_3$):
\begin{itemize}
\item $\log_q A \ge D_1^* - \varepsilon_E$, since $A \ge q^{D_1^*}/2$, and $\log_q B \le D_1 + \varepsilon_E$;
\item if $\lfloor N_1/B_r \rfloor \le 4q^{m_1 + m_2}$, then $m_1 + m_2 \ge L - D_2 - \varepsilon_E$;
\item if $q^{m_1 + m_2} \le \lfloor N_2/(A_r + 1) \rfloor$, then $m_1 + m_2 \le L - D_2^* + \varepsilon_E$, since
  $\lfloor N_2/(A_r + 1) \rfloor \le 2q^{L - D_2^*}$.
\end{itemize}
So the condition of (d$_3$) on the region implies the lemma's hypothesis on the region for these ranges, with $\mathcal
R^{\mathrm{lf}}_L$, on which Theorem~\ref{M-thm:type2-leaves} holds.

The leaves of the third range are sifted at $w$, with $d_1$ on $I_s$ and $d_2$ on
its complement: this is the lemma's setting, with $\mathcal S = I_s$ and $\mathcal S_r$ the complement. The lemma bounds
their sum, for all the tuple lengths up to a bound and all the families of a bounded number of comparisons, by $q^{L -
\delta_4}/\varphi(q)$, with $\delta_4$ and $q_0$ that do not depend on $q$, $a$, $w \ge q^{\varepsilon_w}$ or the
family.

\emph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman-two3}.} The constants are chosen before $q$. Let
\begin{gather*}
  \omega_1 = L - 1 - \max(D_1^*, 1), \qquad \omega'_1 = 2L - 1 - \max(D_1^*, 1) - D_1^* - 2D_2, \\
  \omega_2 = L - 1 - \max(D_1, 1), \qquad \omega'_2 = 2L - 1 - \max(D_1, 1) - D_1 - 2D_2^*,
\end{gather*}
positive by (d$_3$) and $L > 2$, and $c' = \min(1, \omega_1, \omega'_1, \omega_2, \omega'_2)/32$.
Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves} is taken at $\xi_\nu = \omega_\nu - 2c'$ and $c_\nu = \xi_\nu - 20c'$ for $\nu = 1, 2$, with
$\delta' = 4c'$ and $\kappa = 16c'$ (the parameter of Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves}, not the $\kappa$ of the test of the
region); its conditions hold as in \S\ref{app:harman}, since $\xi_\nu \le L - 2$ and $c_\nu = \omega_\nu - 22c' \ge
0$. With these, $L - 1 - \xi_1 - D_1^* - c' = \max(D_1^*, 1) - D_1^* + c' \ge c'$ and $L + c_1 - D_1^* - 2D_2 - c' =
\omega'_1 - 23c' \ge 9c'$, and the same for $\nu = 2$, so the four size conditions above hold for large $q$. The
constants $M_0$, $k$ (the parameter of Brun's sieve), $\varepsilon_w = c'/(2k + 4)$, $s_{\max}$, $\Lambda_1'$ and
$\Lambda_0$ are chosen as in \S\ref{app:harman}, with this $c'$. All the $\delta$'s below are taken with $J_0 +
s_{\max}^2 + 2s_{\max} + 8$ comparisons. Let $\delta_1$ be given by Lemma~\ref{M-lem:term-two} for the tuples of the
lengths up to $j + s_{\max} + 1$. Let $\delta_3^{(1)}$ and $\delta_3^{(2)}$ be given by Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaves} at
the two pairs of exponents, and $\delta_4$ by Lemma~\ref{M-lem:leaf-term} with $e_0 = \varepsilon_w$, all three for
the tuple lengths up to $j + s_{\max}$. Put $\delta_3 = \min(\delta_3^{(1)}, \delta_3^{(2)}, \delta_4)$ and $\delta =
\min(c', \delta_1, \delta_3)/2$. The count of comparisons is the formalisation's; it is larger than the $J_0 +
s_{\max} + 5$ of \S\ref{app:harman} plus the cut's two, and only $\delta$ and $q_0$ depend on it.

At $q$, $w$, $z_0$, $P_1$, $P_2$, $R_-$ and $R_+$ are as in \S\ref{app:harman}. For large $q$, (i) to (v) of
\S\ref{app:harman} hold, and so do the four size conditions, $64 \le q^{L - D_1 - D_2}$ (since $D_1 + D_2 < L$) and
$64 \le q^{\varepsilon_E}$. The
tuples with $r_{\mathcal S_0} > P_1$ and the stops are bounded as there, by $(s_{\max} + 2)q^{L - \delta_1}/\varphi(q)$
in all. The leaves are bounded size by size as above, and their third ranges as in the paragraph on the third range's
rectangle. The main
parts of the leaves total at most $\eta x/(\varphi(q) \log q)$ by the choice of $k$, as in \S\ref{app:harman}, and the
remainders at most $q^{L - c'}/\varphi(q)$. So the sum of the proposition is at most
\[
  \frac{\eta \, x}{\varphi(q) \log q} + \frac{q^{L - c'} + (s_{\max} + 2) q^{L - \delta_1} + 3s_{\max} q^{L -
  \delta_3}}{\varphi(q)} \le \frac{\eta \, x}{\varphi(q) \log q} + \frac{q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}
\]
for large $q$.

\emph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman-fill3}.} The notation is that of the proof of
Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman-fill} in \S\ref{app:harman}, with the changes of the previous proof. The exponents
$\omega_\nu$, $\omega'_\nu$, $c'$, $\xi_\nu$ and $c_\nu$ are defined from (d$_3$) with the growth $t + \rho_2$ in $D_1$
and $D_2$. The parameter $k$ of Brun's sieve is chosen as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{M-prop:harman-fill}, with the

trees. The $\delta_1$, $\delta_3^{(1)}$, $\delta_3^{(2)}$ and $\delta_4$ are taken for the lengths up to $j + 2s_{\max}$
and $J_0 + 2s_{\max}^2 + 5s_{\max} + 7$ comparisons (again the formalisation's count), with $\Lambda_0 = \Lambda_1' + (j
+ 2s_{\max})(L + 1)$. At $q$, (i) to (iii) and (v) to (viii) of \S\ref{app:harman} hold for large $q$, with the
conditions of the previous proof.

Every leaf, of the first tree, of a tree below one of its stops, or of the tree of
$r_{\mathcal S_0} > P_1$, has its modulus $r$ times new primes of product below $4q^{t + \rho_2}$, so its blocks have
$d_1 \le 4q^{D_1}$ and $d_2 \le 4q^{D_2}$. The leaves of the first tree and of the tree of $r_{\mathcal S_0} > P_1$ are
bounded size by size as above. Below each stop, the leaves of each pair of sizes form one family of decreasing tuples,
bounded by the bound for one family cut in three, with its three cases from the bounds of the blocks. There are at most
$2s_{\max} + s_{\max}^2$ families of large leaves, each with the error $3q^{L - \delta_3}/\varphi(q)$, and at most

terms are bounded as in \S\ref{app:harman}, with the one more factor $B_c/\varepsilon_w$. So the sum of the proposition

\[
  \frac{\eta \, x}{\varphi(q) \log q} + \frac{q^{L - c'} + (s_{\max} + s_{\max}^2) q^{L - \delta_1} + 3(2s_{\max} +
  s_{\max}^2) q^{L - \delta_3}}{\varphi(q)},
\]
which for large $q$ is at most $\eta x/(\varphi(q) \log q) + q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$, with $\delta = \min(c',
\delta_1, \delta_3)/2$. For $j = 0$ the argument is that of the first tree.
