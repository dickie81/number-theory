\title{The least prime in an arithmetic progression: the exponent $278/125$ from a half-plane zero-free region, a zero-density estimate and a formalised sieve}
\author{Ritchie Comley}
\date{Draft, 28 October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove, in Lean~4, that there is a constant $K$ such that, for every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$, there is a
prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with $p \le K q^{278/125}$. The Lean theorem has no hypotheses and depends on no axioms beyond
Lean's standard three; its truth rests on Lean's kernel, Mathlib and the pinned toolchain, not on any preprint. Its
proof uses two recent results that have not been refereed, OpenAI's zero-free half-plane $\operatorname{Re} s > 11/12$
for every Dirichlet $L$-function and Chen, Gupta and Li's zero-density estimate, and the library proves both: the
half-plane along the route of OpenAI's companion preprint, and for the density estimate their Proposition~3.1, from
which it derives exponents within $10^{-4}$ of their $4/(1 + \sigma)$ at the left ends of $30$ short steps. From the two
results, as their authors state them, the classical deduction gives every exponent above $7/3$ (from the library's
thirty steps, every exponent above $2.3334$). The proof here goes below $7/3$: a Type~II estimate that sorts
the characters by the real parts of their $L$-functions' zeros feeds Harman's sieve, and the sieve's loss is bounded
by a certificate computed exactly in integers and checked by Lean's kernel. The exponent $278/125$ is to be compared
with that $7/3$, not with the exponents $5.5$ and $5$ of Heath-Brown and Xylouris: the half-plane with Huxley's
classical density estimate alone already gives every exponent above $12/5$, and the proofs of Heath-Brown and
Xylouris use refereed results only. With the half-plane and only the classical density estimates of Ingham and Huxley,
the same sieve gives about $2.27$, by a computation that is not formalised.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is $K$ such that for every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$ there is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with
$p \le K q^{278/125}$.
\end{theorem}

Linnik \cite{linnik44, linnik44b} proved that there are absolute constants $K$ and $L$ such that every reduced residue
class mod $q$ contains a prime $p \le K q^L$. The admissible exponent $L$ has been lowered many times. Heath-Brown
\cite{heath-brown92} reached $L = 5.5$, Xylouris $L = 5.18$ \cite{xylouris-acta} and then $L = 5$ \cite{xylouris11},
and again $L = 5$ in a short version, which notes that a slightly smaller value follows \cite{xylouris18}.
Under the generalised Riemann hypothesis every $L > 2$ is admissible \cite{lls, lls-corr}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has
$L = 278/125 = 2.224$. It is not on the same footing as the exponents of Heath-Brown and Xylouris, whose proofs use
refereed results only, and the distance to them comes from the inputs of Section~\ref{sec:inputs}: with the zero-free
half-plane, the classical deduction gives every $L > 12/5$ from Huxley's density estimate and every $L > 7/3$ from
Chen, Gupta and Li's (Section~\ref{sec:comparison}). What the method of this paper adds is measured from $7/3$: the
sieve brings $7/3 \approx 2.333$ down to $278/125 = 2.224$. The gain does not depend on Chen, Gupta and Li's estimate:
with the half-plane and the classical estimates of Ingham and Huxley alone, the same sieve gives about $2.27$ in place
of $12/5$, by a computation that is not formalised (Section~\ref{sec:comparison}).

\subsection*{Status and formalisation}

The Lean statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has no hypotheses, so the theorem is unconditional. Its proof uses two
recent results that are not yet refereed, OpenAI's zero-free half-plane (Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}) and Chen, Gupta
and Li's zero-density estimate (Input~\ref{inp:density}), which Section~\ref{sec:inputs} states. They are called inputs
because the argument of the paper is written under them. The library proves both in Lean, so the Lean theorem does not
assume them: the half-plane along OpenAI's companion preprint, and the density estimate from Chen, Gupta and Li's
Proposition~3.1, in the form for one point per character that their proof uses, with the exponents of
Input~\ref{inp:density} derived from it; their Theorem~1.2 is not formalised as they state it. The truth of the Lean theorem therefore rests on Lean's kernel, Mathlib and the pinned toolchain (with the vendored upstream
ports and the provenance of the build cache), not on the preprints. Whether the Lean statements of the two inputs
faithfully capture the preprints' theorems matters for attributing and interpreting them, not for the truth of the Lean
theorem, and the kernel does not check it.

Every step of the proof, and every result from the literature that it uses, is proved in Lean~4 \cite{lean4} with the
Mathlib library \cite{mathlib}. The Lean theorem \lean{Linnik22240R.linnik\_22240r} is
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The certificate's sum is checked by Lean's kernel in 5066 parts. Section~\ref{sec:lean} names
the Lean theorem behind each claim and says what is known of independent checking of the inputs, and the directory
\file{formalisations/} of the repository \cite{repo} documents each result from the literature: its source, its Lean
statement and the differences between them. For Chen, Gupta and Li's paper, the library's proof of their
Theorem~10.1, a large-values bound with characters that they credit to Heath-Brown \cite{hb79b}, follows the arguments
of Kerr \cite{kerr} and Jutila \cite{jutila}; some lemmas of their paper were formalised from an outline of their
proofs; and the formalisation found six places where the text of their paper needs a small repair, none affecting its
results (\file{formalisations/chen-gupta-li-density.md}).

The proofs of the paper are those of the Lean library, written at the level of detail of a paper, and
Section~\ref{sec:lean} names the Lean theorem behind each of them. Where the Lean proof fixes a constant whose value the
argument does not need, the paper usually says only that the constant exists, and gives the Lean value where that helps
to compare the two. The argument, the formalisation and this text were developed with the help of AI systems (see the
acknowledgements); the Lean proofs are checked by Lean's kernel, and the author is responsible for the paper.

\subsection*{The zeros near $\operatorname{Re} s = 1$}

The difficulty in Linnik's theorem is the zeros of the $L$-functions mod $q$ near the line $\operatorname{Re} s = 1$.
By the explicit formula, a zero $\rho = \beta + i\gamma$ of $L(s, \chi)$ contributes about $x^{\beta - 1}$, relative to
the main term, to the count of primes $p \le x$ in a class mod $q$. A zero-density estimate with exponent $A$, a
bound of about $(qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)}$ for the number of zeros with real part at least $\sigma$, makes the zeros of
real part about $\sigma$ contribute about $(q^A/x)^{1 - \sigma}$ together. Classically a zero may have
$1 - \beta$ as small as $c/\log q$, and at $x = q^L$ the contribution $(q^A/x)^{1 - \beta}$ is then a constant, not a
small quantity. The record proofs control these zeros with zero-free regions, density estimates without logarithmic
factors and the Deuring--Heilbronn phenomenon, and the constants of all three enter the exponent.

The zero-free half-plane of OpenAI's release \cite{openai-main, openai-companion}, Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, removes
the zeros with $\beta > 11/12$. Every remaining zero then contributes at most a power $x^{-1/12}$, and the problem
becomes one of density: a density exponent $A \ge 2$ for $1/2 \le \sigma \le 11/12$ gives Linnik's theorem with
every exponent $L > A$. Huxley's density estimate \cite{huxley75} gives $A = 12/5$. Chen, Gupta and Li \cite{cgl}, whose
method is Guth and Maynard's \cite{guth-maynard}, prove $A = 7/3$: their Theorem~1.2 gives it uniformly in $q$ and $T$.
That theorem bounds the count, for a divisor $q_1$ of $q$, by four terms; at $q_1 = q$ the first has the exponent
$4/(1 + \sigma)$, the largest of the four on $[7/10, 4/5]$, which is $7/3$ at $\sigma = 5/7$ and smaller beyond. With
Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} the classical deduction of Section~\ref{sec:comparison} turns the density exponent $7/3$
into Linnik's theorem with every exponent above $7/3$. Chen, Gupta and Li do not state a Linnik exponent, but their
Corollary~1.4, on primes in progressions in short intervals under a zero-free region, contains this one: applied to the
whole interval $(x, 2x]$, with Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} as the zero-free region, it gives every exponent above $7/3$.
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} gives the details.

\subsection*{Below the density exponent}

This paper goes below $7/3$ with a sieve. Sieve methods combined with zero-density estimates go back to the work on
primes in short intervals, among it Heath-Brown and Iwaniec's \cite{hb-iwaniec}, and there Harman's sieve with a
numerical bound for its loss is the method of Baker, Harman and Pintz \cite{bhp} and of R.~Li \cite{li-harman}. Harman's
sieve has been used on Linnik's problem before, for special moduli. R.~Li \cite{li-harman} adapts the calculation behind his theorem on primes in short intervals to primes in
progressions to large moduli $q$ whose prime factors are all below $q^C$, for an absolute constant $C > 0$ whose
existence his theorem asserts without giving it, and for which the $L$-functions of the primitive
characters to the divisors of $q$ have no zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > 1 - (\log q)^{-3/4}$ and
$|\operatorname{Im} s| \le \exp(\varepsilon (\log q)^{3/4})$, with the $\varepsilon > 0$ of his Theorem~9, and deduces Linnik's theorem with the
exponent $1/0.4752 < 2.1044$ when $q$ is a power of a fixed prime (his Theorem~11), improving the exponent $2.1115$ of
Banks and Shparlinski \cite{banks-shparlinski} for those moduli (the value as cited in \cite[footnote~1]{cgl}).
He presents these theorems as modifications of that calculation, without separate proofs. Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}
of Section~\ref{sec:inputs} implies the zero-free hypothesis of his Theorem~9 for every large $q$, so if that theorem
holds as stated, it gives with Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} the exponent $1/0.4752$ for every large modulus whose prime
factors are all below $q^C$, with his constant $C$, not only for powers of a fixed prime. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} holds for every modulus.
Harman's sieve \cite{harman83, harman} bounds from below the number of primes in the set $\mathcal A$ of integers
$n \in (x, 2x]$ with $n \equiv a \pmod q$ by comparing it with the set $\mathcal B$ of the integers $n \in (x, 2x]$
prime to $q$, weighted by $1/\varphi(q)$. It needs two kinds of information. Type~I information is the comparison of
$\mathcal A$ with $\mathcal B$ on the multiples of each small integer, which counting in residue classes gives.
Type~II information is the comparison on bilinear sums $\sum_{mn} a_m b_n$, with $m$ in a range $(x^{u_1}, x^{u_2})$,
bounded coefficients $a_m$, and $b_n$ supported on products of primes, which needs the zeros. A comparison over all
such sums is not available at $x = q^{278/125}$. What is available is a window $[u_1, u_2]$ of exponents on which it
holds, and the sieve recovers the primes from the sums it can compare, losing a fraction of them that a finite
computation bounds.

The paper's contributions are three.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A Type~II estimate over the zeros' real parts} (Section~\ref{sec:type2}). The characters mod $q$ are
  sorted into bins by the largest real part of the zeros of their $L$-functions up to a height. In each bin the
  bilinear sum is bounded by the zeros, which make the Dirichlet polynomial of the primes small, and by the number
  of characters in the bin, which a density estimate bounds; the better of orthogonality and the
  Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality is taken in each bin. Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density},
  with the classical density estimates of Ingham and Huxley, give the window
  $[u_1, u_2] = [41556/100000,\ 47531/100000]$ at $L = 278/125$.
\item \emph{A decomposition by Buchstab's identity, with a refinement} (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}). The
  identity is applied to depth six, the primes of the two deepest levels on a coarser grid. The terms in which the
  product of some of the prime variables has its exponent in the window, or in its reflection $[1 - u_2, 1 - u_1]$,
  are Type~II sums and are evaluated; of the others, a term with sign $-$ is decomposed further, and a term with sign
  $+$ is dropped or decomposed further. The refinement keeps, in a dropped term of depth two, the part whose integers
  have exactly two prime factors in the cofactor and are, in some grouping of their four prime factors, a Type~II sum.
\item \emph{A certificate} (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}). The fraction of the primes the decomposition loses is
  bounded above by a sum over the cells of a grid of the exponents, computed exactly in integers, with Buchstab's
  function itself where its argument lies in $[2, 3]$ and the trapezoid rule for the cells' prime sums. On a grid of
  $340$ cells a side (and $85$ cells of four for the deepest primes) the sum is $0.99754$, and Lean's kernel checks
  that it is below $0.99854$, so below $1$: a positive proportion of the primes remains.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{The exponent}

The exponent $278/125$ uses Chen, Gupta and Li's Proposition~3.1 at every point of $[5/7, 131/168]$: on each of
$30$ steps, fourteen of length $1/1680$ and then sixteen of length $1/280$, the exponent is just above $4/(1 + \sigma)$
at the step's left end. It also uses
Ingham's estimate on its last step, $[99/140, 5/7]$, with a slack of $1/100000$ in place of the $1/2000$ of the other
steps, so that its exponent is $7/3 + 1/100000$. Both ends of the window are set where the density exponent is
largest. Neglecting the margins and the steps, take a piece $g(\beta) = A(1 - \beta)$ of the density curve of
Section~\ref{sec:inputs}, and a variable $m$ of size $P = q^\mu$ with $\mu = uL$. The orthogonality bound of
Section~\ref{sec:type2} then suffices when $(L - \mu - A/2)(1 - \beta) \ge (1 - \mu)/2$ for $\mu \le 1$, and when
$\mu \le L - A/2$ for $\mu \ge 1$. At $\beta = 5/7$ the exponents of Ingham and of Chen, Gupta and Li are both
$A = 7/3$, and the two conditions give
\[
  u_1 \ge \frac{35}{9L} - \frac43, \qquad u_2 \le 1 - \frac7{6L},
\]
which are $0.41527$ and $0.47542$ at $L = 278/125$, against the window's $0.41556$ and $0.47531$. The two bounds meet at
$L = 13/6$, below which these exponents leave no window at all, whatever the certificate. At the lower end the rest,
$2.8 \cdot 10^{-4}$ in all, is lost to the first step of $[5/7, 131/168]$, whose exponent $2.3334$, taken at its left
end, is used up to its right end $1201/1680$ ($1.66 \cdot 10^{-4}$), to the margin of Section~\ref{sec:type2}
($0.84 \cdot 10^{-4}$), to the excess $2.3334 - 7/3$ ($0.20 \cdot 10^{-4}$) and to the width of the bins
($0.11 \cdot 10^{-4}$); at the upper end the loss, $0.9 \cdot 10^{-4}$, is mostly the margin's ($0.63 \cdot 10^{-4}$).
The window used, found by a sampled scan, lies $1.1 \cdot 10^{-5}$ and $2.4 \cdot 10^{-5}$ inside the exact one. The
tightest corners of the window, at both ends, are on the first step (Section~\ref{sec:type2}), and this is why the first
steps are narrow. The corners' slack grows along them, to $4.3 \cdot 10^{-4}$ on the fourteenth, and from there steps of
$1/280$ leave the window as it is: the least slack of a corner on them is $4.4 \cdot 10^{-5}$, on the first of them
(steps of $1/140$ would move the window's lower end). The next
constraint, at $\sigma = 7/9$, where $4/(1 + \sigma)$ meets Huxley's $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ at $A = 9/4$, gives
$u_1 \ge 27/(10L) - 4/5$, which binds only above $L = 107/48 \approx 2.229$; so at $278/125$ the exponents near
$7/9$ do not change the window. What lets the certificate close at $278/125$ is the decomposition's two deeper levels,
its kept parts, and the bounds of its cells, which take Buchstab's function itself on $[2, 3]$ and the prime sums of
the cells by the trapezoid rule (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}).

The rest is not new. Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density} are OpenAI's and Chen, Gupta and Li's,
and the density estimates of Ingham \cite{ingham} and Huxley \cite{huxley72, huxley75}, the Hal\'asz--Montgomery
inequality \cite{halasz, montgomery69}, Buchstab's identity \cite{buchstab}, Brun's sieve \cite{brun} and Harman's
sieve are classical. Section~\ref{sec:inputs} states the inputs and Section~\ref{sec:type1} the form of the sieve that
is used.

\section{Notation and the two inputs}\label{sec:inputs}

Throughout, $q \ge 1$ is the modulus and $a$ is prime to $q$. The Dirichlet characters mod $q$ are $\chi$, the trivial
one is $\chi_0$, and $L(s, \chi)$ is the $L$-function of $\chi$, continued to the whole plane (in Lean, Mathlib's
\lean{DirichletCharacter.LFunction}). For $1/2 \le \sigma \le 1$ and $T \ge 2$ let
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) = \#\{\chi \bmod q : L(s, \chi) = 0 \text{ for some } s \in \mathcal R(\sigma, T)\},
\]
where $\mathcal R(\sigma, T) = \{s : \operatorname{Re} s \ge \sigma,\ |\operatorname{Im} s| \le T\}$: the number of
characters mod $q$ whose $L$-function has a zero in the rectangle. It counts characters, not zeros, so a bound
for the number of zeros bounds it. The proof is a sieve on the integers of $(x, 2x]$, $x = q^L$, with $L = 278/125$ in
the end, and $q$ is taken large.

The paper states two inputs, written \emph{Input} and not \emph{hypothesis} because the library proves them in Lean.
A statement below that holds `under' an input is conditional in the text of the paper. In Lean the corresponding
theorems either take as a hypothesis a statement that the inputs supply (as \lean{SieveTypeIIHM.typeII\_gen\_hm}
takes a density curve's counts) or use the library's proof of the input directly; the final theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, has no hypotheses.

\begin{inputthm}[the zero-free half-plane]\label{inp:half-plane}
For every $q \ge 1$, every Dirichlet character $\chi$ mod $q$ and every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re} s > 11/12$,
$L(s, \chi) \ne 0$, except at $s = 1$ for the trivial character.
\end{inputthm}

The exception is the pole of $L(s, \chi_0)$ at $s = 1$, where Mathlib's function, like every function in Lean, still
has a value; the Lean statement leaves that point aside.

This is OpenAI's result: their companion preprint \cite{openai-companion} proves it, and their main preprint
\cite{openai-main} proves the stronger $\operatorname{Re} s > 7/8$. These preprints have not been refereed. The theorem
of this paper uses the result, which the library proves as \lean{Eis.LFunction\_ne\_zero\_of\_re\_gt}, following the
companion preprint. Independent checking of the result is partial, and little of it is independent of OpenAI;
Section~\ref{sec:lean} gives the details, and \file{formalisations/openai-half-plane.md} the attribution and the
differences between the Lean statement and the source's.

\begin{inputthm}[zero-density estimates from $5/7$ to $131/168$]\label{inp:density}
Let $\sigma_k = 5/7 + k/1680$ for $0 \le k \le 14$ and $\sigma_k = \sigma_{14} + (k - 14)/280$ for $14 \le k \le 30$, so
that $\sigma_{30} = 131/168$. There are exponents $A_k$, for $0 \le k < 30$, with
$4/(1 + \sigma_k) < A_k < 4/(1 + \sigma_k) + 1/10000$ such that for each $k$ there is $C$ with the following property:
for every $q \ge 1$, every $\sigma_k \le \sigma \le \sigma_{k+1}$ and every $T \ge 2$, at most
$C (qT)^{A_k (1 - \sigma)} (\log qT)^{10}$ characters mod $q$ have an $L$-function with a zero in
$\operatorname{Re} s \ge \sigma$, $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$.
\end{inputthm}

The exponent $A_k$ is the exponent $4/(1 + \sigma)$ of the first term of Chen, Gupta and Li's Theorem~1.2 \cite{cgl}
at $q_1 = q$, the largest of its four terms on $[7/10, 4/5]$, taken at the step's left end $\sigma_k$, up to $10^{-4}$; the library's $A_k$ are multiples of $10^{-5}$, from $A_0 = 2.3334$ down to
$A_{29} = 2.25207$. Their estimate counts zeros, which gives the count of characters stated here. The library does not
prove their Theorem~1.2. It formalises their Proposition~3.1, the large-values estimate, in the form for one point per
character that their proof uses (distinct primitive characters, each with one height, and sums over $[6N/5, 9N/5]$ with
$N \ge (qT)^{2/3}/2$ and $7/10 \le \sigma \le 4/5$), and derives each step from it (Section~\ref{sec:lean} names the
theorems); other parts of their paper are not formalised. \file{formalisations/chen-gupta-li-density.md} documents
what is, and lists six small repairs that the formalisation needed in the text of their paper, none affecting its
results. Their paper \cite{cgl} is an arXiv preprint and has not been refereed.

\subsection*{The density curve}

The Type~II estimate of Section~\ref{sec:type2} needs a bound for $N^*(\sigma, T; q)$ at every $\sigma$ in
$[1/2, 1]$. It takes it in the form of a curve.

\begin{definition}\label{def:curve}
A function $g : \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ is a \emph{density curve} if there is $C \ge 0$ such that for every
$q \ge 1$, every $1/2 \le \sigma \le 1$ and every $T \ge 2$,
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le C\, (qT)^{g(\sigma)} \bigl(1 + \log qT\bigr)^{10}.
\]
\end{definition}

The curve of this paper is the function $g_7$ defined as follows (in Lean, \lean{SieveTypeIIU22240D.g7d}); each of
its pieces is a known density estimate, and the steps make each piece other than the trivial count below $4/7$ a
constant multiple of $1 - \sigma$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $g_7(\sigma) = 1$ for $\sigma < 4/7$.
\item On $[4/7, 5/7)$, divided into twenty steps $[\sigma_-, \sigma_+)$ of length $1/140$,
  $g_7(\sigma) = \bigl(3/(2 - \sigma_+) + 1/2000\bigr)(1 - \sigma)$: Ingham's exponent at the step's right end plus a
  slack of $1/2000$, except on the last step $[99/140, 5/7)$, where the slack is $1/100000$ and the exponent is
  $7/3 + 1/100000$.
\item On $[5/7, 131/168)$, divided into the $30$ steps $[\sigma_k, \sigma_{k+1})$ of Input~\ref{inp:density},
  $g_7(\sigma) = A_k(1 - \sigma)$.
\item On $[131/168, 5/6)$, divided into fifteen steps $[\sigma_-, \sigma_+)$ of length $1/280$,
  $g_7(\sigma) = \bigl(3/(3\sigma_- - 1) + 1/2000\bigr)(1 - \sigma)$: Huxley's exponent at the step's left end plus
  $1/2000$.
\item $g_7(\sigma) = (101/50)(1 - \sigma)$ for $\sigma \ge 5/6$.
\end{itemize}
The exponents are written in Lean as fractions; the first step's is $3/(2 - 81/140) + 1/2000 = 840199/398000$. On
$[4/7, 5/7]$ the exponent rises from about $2.111$ to $7/3 + 1/100000$. On $[5/7, 131/168]$ it falls from $2.3334$ to
$2.25207$, and on $[131/168, 5/6]$, where Huxley's steps take over at $2.2405$, it falls to about $2.015$. On each step
$A(1 - \sigma) \le A(1 - \sigma_-) \le 1$, so $g_7 \le 1$ everywhere, and $g_7(1/2) = 1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve}
Under Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density}, $g_7$ is a density curve.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A finite union of ranges on each of which the bound holds, with its own constant, is a range on which it holds with
the sum of the constants, so it suffices to prove the bound on each piece.
\begin{itemize}
\item For $\sigma < 4/7$: $N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le \varphi(q) \le qT$.
\item On each step of $[4/7, 5/7]$: Ingham's estimate \cite{ingham} in the form over the characters mod $q$, which
  bounds $N^*(\sigma, T; q)$ by $C(qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)}(\log qT)^{10}$ on $[\sigma_-, \sigma_+]$ for every $A$
  above $3/(2 - \sigma_+)$ (\file{formalisations/ingham-density.md}). Ingham proved his estimate for the zeros of
  $\zeta$; the form over the characters is Theorem~12.1 of Montgomery's book \cite{montgomery-topics} (as cited by
  Huxley \cite{huxley75}).
\item On each step of $[5/7, 131/168]$: Input~\ref{inp:density}.
\item On each step of $[131/168, 5/6]$: Huxley's estimate \cite{huxley75} over the characters mod $q$, with the
  exponent $3/(3\sigma_- - 1) + 1/2000$ on $[\sigma_-, \sigma_+]$ (\file{formalisations/huxley-density.md}). His
  (1.1) is a bound $(Q^2 T/q_0)^{12(1 - \sigma)/5 + \varepsilon}$ for the zeros of the $L$-functions of the primitive
  characters to the moduli up to $Q$ divisible by $q_0$, and his (2.23) has the exponent $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ there;
  with $Q = q_0 = d$ for each $d \mid q$, the case of one modulus (which, he remarks on p.~435, is the one Jutila
  treats), it bounds the characters
  mod $q$, each of which is induced by a primitive character mod some $d \mid q$ with the same zeros in
  $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$, at a cost $\tau(q) \ll q^\varepsilon$. The library proves its own form of the estimate.
\item For $\sigma \ge 5/6$: the exponent $101/50$. On $[5/6, 11/12]$ Huxley's exponent $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ is at most
  $2$, and his zero-detection argument, with its parameters fixed for this range, gives $2 + 1/50$
  (\lean{LinnikStep.charDensity\_step}); above $11/12$ at most the trivial character is counted, by
  Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} (\file{formalisations/huxley-density.md}).
\end{itemize}
Each of these bounds is a theorem of the library, with a factor $(\log qT)^{10}$ at most $(1 + \log qT)^{10}$; the
claims table of Section~\ref{sec:lean} names them.
\end{proof}

Two pieces of $g_7$ are not classical. One is the steps of Input~\ref{inp:density} on $[5/7, 131/168]$: their
exponents start just above $7/3$ ($2.3334$) at $5/7$ and decrease like $4/(1 + \sigma)$, and they replace the classical exponent
$\min(3/(2 - \sigma), 3/(3\sigma - 1))$, which is Ingham's up to $\sigma = 3/4$, where both equal $12/5$, and Huxley's
after. The other is the part above $11/12$, which Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} gives. Huxley's $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ is below
$4/(1 + \sigma)$ from $\sigma = 7/9$ on, and $g_7$ takes it from $131/168$ on. Inside the last step,
$[163/210, 131/168]$, it is below the step's exponent $2.25207$ from $\sigma \approx 0.7774$ on, by up to
$1.2 \cdot 10^{-2}$ at $131/168$; taking it there would not change the window of Section~\ref{sec:type2}, whose corners
on that step have a slack of at least $3.2 \cdot 10^{-4}$. Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}
also enters the Type~II estimate (Section~\ref{sec:type2}) and, in the library, the prime number theorem
(Section~\ref{sec:proof}).

\section{The Type II estimate}\label{sec:type2}

Fix a smooth real function $f_0$ supported in $[1/2, 2]$. For a character $\chi$, a scale $Q \ge 4$ and a
height $h \in \mathbb R$, the smoothed prime sum is
\[
  S_Q(\chi, h) = \sum_{p \le 2Q} \chi(p) \Bigl(\frac{p}{Q}\Bigr)^{-ih} f_0\Bigl(\frac{p}{Q}\Bigr),
\]
and for a finite set $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$ of integers, coefficients $a_m \in \mathbb C$ and a height $h_0$,
\[
  W(\chi) = \sum_{m \in S} a_m \chi(m) m^{-ih_0}.
\]
The sieve of Section~\ref{sec:type1} needs, for the integers with a factor $m$ in a range $(x^{u_1}, x^{u_2})$ and
the rest a product of primes, that the non-trivial characters contribute less than the main term. In the Dirichlet
polynomials above this is the following estimate. Write $\mathcal X^*$ for the non-trivial characters mod $q$.

\begin{theorem}[the Type~II estimate]\label{thm:type2}
Assume Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density}, and let $L = 278/125$, $u_1 = 41556/100000$ and
$u_2 = 47531/100000$. Let
$K_0 \ge 0$ be an integer, $e_1 > 0$ and $0 < \eta \le 10^{-5}$. There is $q_0$ such that for every $q \ge q_0$, every
integer $P$ with $q^{u_1 L} \le P \le q^{u_2 L}$, every $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$, all $a_m$ and $h_0$, every $K \le K_0$,
and all $Q_1, \dots, Q_K \ge q^{e_1}$ and $|h_1|, \dots, |h_K| \le q^\eta$ with $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/P$,
\[
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} |W(\chi)| \prod_{j=1}^K |S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)|
  \le q^{L - 1/50000} \Bigl(\frac1P \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

At $x = q^L$ the condition on $P$ is $x^{u_1} \le P \le x^{u_2}$. By Cauchy--Schwarz and
$Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/P$, the trivial character's term is at most a constant times
$q^L (\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$, and the bound saves the power $q^{-1/50000}$ against it.
The proof has three parts: the prime sums are bounded through the zeros of each character's $L$-function, the
characters are sorted by those zeros, and in each class the sum over the characters is bounded in two ways.

\subsection{Prime sums in a zero-free rectangle}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:prime-sum}
Assume Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. For every integer $J \ge 0$ there are $B, C \ge 0$ such that the following holds
for every $q \ge 1$, every
$\chi \in \mathcal X^*$, every $1/2 \le \theta \le 11/12$, $0 < \delta \le 1/24$, $H \ge 0$, $Q \ge 4$ and
$y \in \mathbb R$. If $L(s, \chi) \ne 0$ for $\operatorname{Re} s > \theta$, $|\operatorname{Im} s - y| < H + 1$,
then
\begin{multline*}
  |S_Q(\chi, y)| \le M_\delta(\theta)\, \lambda\, B\,
  \frac{Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{11/12 + \delta}(1 + H)^{-J}}{\log Q} \\
  + C\bigl(\sqrt{2Q} + 1\bigr)\bigl(\log_2 (2Q) + 1\bigr),
\end{multline*}
where $\lambda = \log(q + 2) + \log(|y| + 4) + 1$ and $M_\delta(\theta)$, the constant of the bound for $L'/L$ in
Appendix~\ref{app:prime-sum}, depends only on $\theta$ and $\delta$.
\end{lemma}

The argument is the classical one for primes in progressions, with a smooth weight in place of a sharp cut-off:
Perron's formula for $-L'/L$ against the Mellin transform of the weight, a bound for $L'/L$ in zero-free discs by a
lemma of Landau's type, and a contour that runs along $\operatorname{Re} s = \theta + \delta$ at the heights near $y$
and along $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta$ elsewhere. The only use of Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} is to move the
line of integration to $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta$ and to bound $L'/L$ on that line and to its right.
Appendix~\ref{app:prime-sum} gives the proof.

\subsection{The bins}\label{sec:bins}

Let $I = 500000$ and $\beta_i = 1/2 + 5i/(12I)$ for $0 \le i \le I$, so that $\beta_0 = 1/2$, $\beta_I = 11/12$ and
the bins have width $1/1200000$. For a height bound $T \ge 2$ the \emph{bin} of $\chi \in \mathcal X^*$ is the largest
$i < I$ such that $L(s, \chi)$ has a zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > \beta_i$ and $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$, and
$0$ if there is none. If $\chi$ is in the bin $i$ then:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L(s, \chi)$ has no zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > \beta_{i+1}$ and $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$: for
  $i + 1 < I$ by the choice of $i$, and for $i + 1 = I$ by Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. So
  Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum} applies with $\theta = \beta_{i+1}$ to every height $|y| \le T - H - 1$.
\item For $i \ge 1$, $\chi$ is counted by $N^*(\beta_i, T; q)$, so a density curve $g$ bounds the number of characters
  in the bin by $C(qT)^{g(\beta_i)}(1 + \log qT)^{10}$. For $i = 0$ the bound $\varphi(q) \le qT$ is the same, with
  $g(1/2) = 1$ and $\max(C, 1)$ in place of $C$, which the bound for every $i$ then uses.
\end{itemize}
The bins depend on $T$, and so on all the zeros up to the height $T$, but not on the heights $h_j$. The proof takes
$\delta = 10^{-6}$, $H = q^\eta$ and $T = 2H + 1 \le 3q^\eta$, so that every height $|h_j| \le q^\eta$ has
$|h_j| + H + 1 \le T$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum} with $J = \lceil 5(1 + L)/(12\eta) \rceil$. As
$Q_j \ge q^{e_1} \ge 1$ and $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/P$, each $Q_j \le q^{1 + L}$ for large $q$, so that
$Q_j^{11/12 + \delta}(1 + H)^{-J} \le Q_j^{1/2 + \delta}$. The hypothesis $Q_j \ge q^{e_1}$ gives
$\lambda/\log Q_j \ll 1/e_1$, and $M_\delta$ is bounded on the $I + 1$ values $\beta_i$. So for large $q$,
$|S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)| \le 2 Q_j^{\beta_{i+1} + 2\delta}$ in the bin $i$, and the product of the prime sums is at most
$4^{K_0} (q^L/P)^{\beta_i + \varpi}$ with $\varpi = 1/1200000 + 2\delta < 2.9 \cdot 10^{-6}$.

\subsection{Two bounds in a bin}\label{sec:two-bounds}

Let $\mathcal X_i$ be the set of the characters in the bin $i$. Since the prime sums are bounded on $\mathcal X_i$,
it remains to bound $\sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X_i} |W(\chi)|$. There are two bounds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Orthogonality.} For $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$ and every $t$,
  \[
    \sum_\chi \Bigl|\sum_{m \in S} c_m \chi(m) m^{-it}\Bigr|^2 \le (P + q) \sum_{m \in S} |c_m|^2,
  \]
  summed over all characters mod $q$. Indeed the left side is $\varphi(q)$ times the sum, over the classes mod $q$
  prime to $q$, of the squared moduli of the sums over the classes; each class has at most $P/q + 1$ elements in $(P, 2P]$, and
  Cauchy--Schwarz in each class and $\varphi(q) \le q$ give the bound. With Cauchy--Schwarz over $\mathcal X_i$,
  \[
    \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X_i} |W(\chi)| \le |\mathcal X_i|^{1/2} \Bigl((P + q) \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
  \]
\item \emph{The Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality} \cite{halasz, montgomery69}, in the form over the characters mod
  $q$ at one height (\file{formalisations/halasz-montgomery.md}):
  \[
    \Bigl(\sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X_i} |W(\chi)|\Bigr)^2 \le e \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2 \cdot
    \bigl(2P |\mathcal X_i| + |\mathcal X_i|^2 \mathcal H\bigr),
  \]
  where $\mathcal H$ bounds $|K_{2P}(\psi, 0)|$ for every non-trivial character $\psi$ mod $q$, with
  $K_\Lambda(\psi, z) = \sum_{n \ge 1} \psi(n) n^{-z} e^{-n/\Lambda}$. Indeed, with $|c_\chi| \le 1$ and
  $c_\chi W(\chi) = |W(\chi)|$, $\sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X_i} |W(\chi)| = \sum_m a_m m^{-ih_0}
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X_i} c_\chi \chi(m)$; Cauchy--Schwarz with the weight
  $e^{-m/2P}$, which is at least $e^{-1}$ on $(P, 2P]$, and extending the second sum to all $m \ge 1$ give
  $(\sum_\chi |W(\chi)|)^2 \le e \sum_m |a_m|^2 \sum_{\chi, \chi'} c_\chi \overline{c_{\chi'}}
  K_{2P}(\chi \overline{\chi'}, 0)$. The diagonal terms are at most $\sum_{n \ge 1} e^{-n/2P} \le 2P$, and the
  others at most $\mathcal H$, since $\chi \overline{\chi'}$ is non-trivial for $\chi \ne \chi'$.
\end{enumerate}
For the kernels, let $\delta' = \varepsilon' = 1/1000$. There is $C$ such that for every $q$, every non-trivial
character $\psi$ mod $q$ and every $\Lambda \ge 1$,
\[
  |K_\Lambda(\psi, 0)| \le C \Lambda^{1/12 - \delta'} (2q)^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}.
\]
By Mellin inversion, $K_\Lambda(\psi, 0) = (2\pi i)^{-1} \int_{(2)} L(s, \psi) \Gamma(s) \Lambda^s \, ds$. Since
$L(\cdot, \psi)$ is entire and $1/12 - \delta' > 0$, the line moves to $\operatorname{Re} s = 1/12 - \delta'$ without
crossing a pole. There the functional equation of the primitive character $\psi^*$ that induces $\psi$, of conductor
$q^*$, gives $|L(s, \psi^*)| \ll (q^*(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 2))^{5/12 + \delta'} |L(1 - s, \overline{\psi^*})|$,
and $1 - s$ lies on $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta'$. There Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} gives the Lindelöf-type
bound $|L(s, \chi)|^{\pm 1} \ll ((q + 2)(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 4))^{\varepsilon'}$, uniformly in $q$ and
$\chi \ne \chi_0$ (\lean{LinnikLindelof.lindelof}): Landau's lemma, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum},
bounds $|L'/L(s, \chi)|$ by a multiple of $\log((q + 2)(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 4))$ on
$\operatorname{Re} s \ge 11/12 + \delta'/2$; the three-lines theorem, on rectangles of height comparable to that
logarithm, interpolates this against $|L'/L| \ll 1$ on $\operatorname{Re} s = 3/2$; and integrating $L'/L$ from
$\operatorname{Re} s = 3/2$, where $|\log L| \le \log \zeta(3/2)$, gives the bound for $|L|$ and $1/|L|$. The Euler factors at the primes dividing $q$ cost $2^{\omega(q)} \ll q^{\varepsilon'}$, and
together $|L(s, \psi)| \ll (q(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 2))^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}$ on the new line; the decay of
$\Gamma$ makes the integral converge. At $\Lambda = 2P$ this gives
$\mathcal H = C(2P)^{1/12 - \delta'}(2q)^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}$. This is the bound the library proves and
uses, but the window has room for much less: any $\mathcal H \ll q^{0.68}$ would do (the corners that involve it, those of
$E_3$ on the top piece in Proposition~\ref{prop:window}, hold with a slack of about $0.09$), for instance
$|K_\Lambda(\psi, 0)| \le \max_N |\sum_{n \le N} \psi(n)| \ll_\varepsilon q^{1/2 + \varepsilon}$, by partial summation
and the Pólya--Vinogradov inequality \cite{davenport}. So the window does not need Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} for the
kernel bound.

The first bound is the better one when the bin is large, the second when it is small, which is the case for $\beta$
near $11/12$, on the top piece $[5/6, 11/12]$ of the curve. The proof takes the better of the two in each bin.

\subsection{The window condition}

Write $P = q^\mu$, and let $\beta = \beta_i$. Since $T \le 3q^\eta$ and $g \le 1$, the bin has
$|\mathcal X_i| \le 3C q^{g(\beta) + \eta}(1 + \log qT)^{10}$, and the product of the prime sums is at most
$4^{K_0} q^{(L - \mu)(\beta + \varpi)}$. Up to constants, powers of $1 + \log qT$ and the factor $q^\eta$, the two
bounds are $q^E (\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$ with the exponents
\[
  E_1 = (L - \mu)(\beta + \varpi) + \frac{g(\beta)}2 + \frac\mu2 + \frac{\max(1, \mu)}2
\]
by orthogonality, and, by the Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality with
$(2P|\mathcal X_i| + |\mathcal X_i|^2 \mathcal H)^{1/2} \le (2P|\mathcal X_i|)^{1/2} + |\mathcal X_i| \mathcal H^{1/2}$,
the larger of
\begin{align*}
  E_2 &= (L - \mu)(\beta + \varpi) + \frac{g(\beta)}2 + \mu, \\
  E_3 &= (L - \mu)(\beta + \varpi) + \frac{g(\beta)}2 + \frac\mu2 + \frac{g(\beta) + b(\mu)}2,
\end{align*}
with $b(\mu) = 5/12 + \mu/12 + \delta'(1 - \mu) + 2\varepsilon'$ the exponent of $\mathcal H$. For $\mu \ge 1$,
$E_2 = E_1$, so the Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality never helps there, and the better of the two bounds has the
exponent
\[
  E(\beta, \mu, \varpi) = (L - \mu)(\beta + \varpi) + \frac{g(\beta)}2 + \frac\mu2
  + \frac12 \max\bigl(\mu, \min(1, g(\beta) + b(\mu))\bigr).
\]

In this section a window is an interval $[\mu_1, \mu_2]$ of exponents of $q$, for the scale $P = q^\mu$ of the Type~II
variable; the other sections write it $[u_1, u_2]$, in the exponents of $x = q^L$, so that $\mu_i = u_i L$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:window}
A window $[\mu_1, \mu_2]$ is \emph{admissible} for a curve $g$ at $L$ if
$E(\beta, \mu, \varpi) \le L - 1/25000$ for every $1/2 \le \beta \le 11/12$, every $\mu_1 \le \mu \le \mu_2$ and
every $0 \le \varpi \le 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$, with $\delta' = \varepsilon' = 1/1000$.
\end{definition}

For $\mu \le 1$, $E = \min(E_1, \max(E_2, E_3))$, and for $\mu \ge 1$, $E = E_1$. So the condition says that
$E_1 \le L - 1/25000$, or $\mu \le 1$ and $\max(E_2, E_3) \le L - 1/25000$, which is how the Lean states it
(\lean{SieveCurveGlue.WindowOKHM}).

\begin{lemma}[one bin]\label{lem:bin}
Let $g \le 1$ be a function and $[\mu_1, \mu_2]$ a window admissible for $g$ at $L$. Given $C_0$, $C_d$ and $C_K$, there
is $C'$ such that the following holds. Let $q > 1$, $\mu_1 \le \mu \le \mu_2$, $P = q^\mu$, $1 \le T \le 3q^\eta$
with $\eta \ge 0$, $1/2 \le \beta \le 11/12$ and $0 \le \varpi \le 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$. Let $\mathcal X$ be a set of at
most $C_d (qT)^{g(\beta)} (1 + \log qT)^{10}$ characters on which the product of the prime sums is at most
$C_0 (q^L/P)^{\beta + \varpi}$, and let $\mathcal H \le C_K (2P)^{1/12 - \delta'} (2q)^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}$
bound the off-diagonal kernels on $\mathcal X$. Then the product of the prime sums times the better of the two
bounds for $\sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X} |W(\chi)|$ is at most
$C' (1 + \log qT)^{10} q^{L - 1/25000 + \eta} (\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At $(\beta, \mu, \varpi)$ the window condition gives $E_1 \le L - 1/25000$ or $\max(E_2, E_3) \le L - 1/25000$. In the
first case, the orthogonality bound with $(P + q)^{1/2} \le \sqrt2\, q^{\max(1, \mu)/2}$ and $|\mathcal X|^{1/2} \le
(3C_d)^{1/2} q^{(g(\beta) + \eta)/2}(1 + \log qT)^5$ gives the claim with the exponent $E_1 + \eta/2$. In the second, the
Hal\'asz--Montgomery bound, split as above, gives two terms: $(2P|\mathcal X|)^{1/2}$ carries the exponent
$E_2 + \eta/2$, and $|\mathcal X| \mathcal H^{1/2}$ the exponent $E_3 + \eta$, with $(1 + \log qT)^{10}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:type2-gen}
Assume Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. Let $g$ be a density curve with $g \le 1$ and $g(1/2) = 1$, and let $[\mu_1, \mu_2]$ be
admissible for $g$ at $L$. Then the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} holds with $q^{\mu_1} \le P \le q^{\mu_2}$ in
place of $q^{u_1 L} \le P \le q^{u_2 L}$, with $L$ in place of $278/125$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the bins of Section~\ref{sec:bins} and the kernel bound of Section~\ref{sec:two-bounds}, each bin $i$ satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:bin} at
$\beta = \beta_i$ and $\varpi = 1/1200000 + 2\delta$, with constants that do not depend on $q$, so each contributes at
most $C' (1 + \log qT)^{10} q^{L - 1/25000 + \eta}(\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$. There are $I$ bins, and for large $q$,
$I C' (1 + \log qT)^{10} \le q^{1/100000}$, so the total is at most
$q^{L - 1/25000 + \eta + 1/100000}(\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$. Since $\eta \le 10^{-5}$,
$L - 1/25000 + \eta + 1/100000 \le L - 1/50000$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:window}
The window $[u_1 L, u_2 L]$, with $u_1 = 41556/100000$ and $u_2 = 47531/100000$, is admissible for $g_7$ at
$L = 278/125$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On each piece of $g_7$, $g_7(\beta) = A(1 - \beta)$ with $A$ constant, or $g_7 = 1$, so each $E_i$ is affine in
$\beta$ on the piece. Each $E_i$ increases with $\varpi$, since $L - \mu > 0$. As a function of $\mu$, $E_1$ is
convex: it decreases for $\mu < 1$, with slope $1/2 - \beta - \varpi \le 0$, and increases for $\mu > 1$, with slope
$1 - \beta - \varpi > 0$. On the top piece $[5/6, 11/12]$, $E_2$ increases with $\mu$, so for $\mu \le 1$ it is at most
$E_2(\beta, 1, \varpi) = E_1(\beta, 1, \varpi) \le E_1(\beta, \mu_2, \varpi)$, and $E_3$ decreases with $\mu$, with
slope $13/24 - \delta'/2 - \beta - \varpi < 0$. As a function of $\beta$ on a piece $A(1 - \beta)$, $E_1$ has slope
$L - \mu - A/2$, which is positive for $\mu \le \mu_2$ since every exponent of $g_7$ is at most
$2.3334 < 2(L - \mu_2)$ (on $g_7 = 1$ the slope is $L - \mu > 0$), and on the top piece $E_3$ has slope
$L - \mu - A < 0$. Since $\mu_1 < 1 < \mu_2$, it therefore suffices that $E_1 \le L - 1/25000$ at the right end
$\beta_+$ of every piece below $5/6$ for $\mu \in \{\mu_1, \mu_2\}$, and at $(11/12, \mu_2)$ on the top piece, and that
$E_3 \le L - 1/25000$ at $(5/6, \mu_1)$, all at $\varpi = 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$. These are $134$ inequalities between
rational numbers, and each holds, the least with a slack of $5.3 \cdot 10^{-6}$. Solved for $\mu$, with
$X = L - 1/25000$, they say that $\mu_1$ is at least
$\bigl(L(\beta_+ + \varpi) + g(\beta_+)/2 + 1/2 - X\bigr)/(\beta_+ + \varpi - 1/2)$ and $\mu_2$ at most
$\bigl(X - L(\beta_+ + \varpi) - g(\beta_+)/2\bigr)/(1 - \beta_+ - \varpi)$ at every right end $\beta_+$, with
$g(\beta_+)$ the piece's value there, besides the top piece's condition on $E_3$. Both bounds are set at
$\beta_+ = 1201/1680$, the right end of the first step of $[5/7, 131/168]$, and give the exact window
$[0.4155489\ldots, 0.4753342\ldots] \cdot L$, which contains the proposition's. At $\varpi = 0$, with no margin and
$\beta_+ = 5/7$, $A = 7/3$, they reduce to the closed forms of the introduction. The $134$ inequalities are among the
$1080$ corners that \file{tools/linnik/route\_limits.py} checks in exact rationals and the Lean checks by
\lean{norm\_num}. The Lean (\lean{SieveTypeIIU22240D.window\_22240d}) uses neither the convexity nor the monotonicity:
it interpolates on $[\mu_1, 1]$ and on $[1, \mu_2]$ separately, at the corners with $\varpi \in \{0, 4 \cdot 10^{-6}\}$
and $\mu \in \{\mu_1, 1, \mu_2\}$, $16$ on each piece below $5/6$, and $24$ on the top piece, where it checks $E_2$ and
$E_3$ for $\mu \le 1$.
\end{proof}

The window has little room. At each end the tightest corner is on the first step of $[5/7, 131/168]$, whose exponent
is $2.3334$, at $\beta = 1201/1680$ and $\varpi = 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$: it holds with a slack (below $L - 1/25000$) of
about $5.3 \cdot 10^{-6}$ at $\mu = u_1 L$, and of about $1.54 \cdot 10^{-5}$ at $\mu = u_2 L$. All the corners are
exact rational inequalities, so there is no numerical tolerance. With $u_1$ and $u_2$ fixed, they hold exactly for
$L \ge 2.2239858\ldots$, so at $222399/100000$ but not at $222398/100000$; raising the first step's exponent by
about $4 \cdot 10^{-5}$ moves the lower end out. Since the Type~II estimate on this window and the certificate of
Section~\ref{sec:certificate} on its grid do not depend on $L$ otherwise (Theorem~\ref{thm:route} also needs
$2 < L \le 5/2$ and $L U_2 > D$, with $U_2$ and $D$ as in Section~\ref{sec:certificate}, which still hold), the
window leaves about $1.4 \cdot 10^{-5}$ of room in the exponent. On every other step the slack is larger: on the
last step, $[163/210, 131/168]$, which contains $7/9$, it is $3.3 \cdot 10^{-4}$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:curve}, $g_7$ is a density curve under the two inputs, with $g_7 \le 1$ and $g_7(1/2) = 1$.

\end{proof}

The Hal\'asz--Montgomery branch is used only on the top piece of the curve, at the corners with $\mu \le 1$, where the

\section{Type I estimates and Harman's sieve}\label{sec:type1}

Let $2 < L \le 5/2$ and $0 < u_1 < u_2 < 1$; this section and the next use nothing else of $L$ and the window. Below,
`the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}' means the Type~II estimate of that theorem at this $L$ on the window
$[u_1 L, u_2 L]$, which Proposition~\ref{prop:type2-gen} gives on every admissible window; Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} is its
case $L = 278/125$, $u_1 = 41556/100000$, $u_2 = 47531/100000$. Let $x = q^L$ and let $a$ be prime to $q$. The
\emph{window's range} is $[x^{u_1}, x^{u_2}]$, the range of the scale $P$ of the Type~II variable, which runs over
$(P, 2P]$, and its \emph{reflection} is $[x^{1 - u_2}, x^{1 - u_1}]$. The sets of the sieve are
\[
  \mathcal A = \{n \in (x, 2x] : n \equiv a \ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\}, \qquad
  \mathcal B = \{n \in (x, 2x] : (n, q) = 1\},
\]
whose integers are those of $(N_1, N_2]$ with $N_1 = \lfloor x \rfloor$ and $N_2 = \lfloor 2x \rfloor$.
For a set $\mathcal C$ of integers, an integer $r$ and $z \ge 1$, $\mathcal C_r$ is the set of the multiples of $r$
in $\mathcal C$, and $S(\mathcal C_r, z)$ the number of $n \in \mathcal C_r$ with no prime factor of $n/r$ below $z$.
With $z_{\mathrm{top}} = \lfloor N_2^{1/2} \rfloor + 1$, an integer of $(N_1, N_2]$ with no prime factor below
$z_{\mathrm{top}}$ is prime, so $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ is the number of primes in $\mathcal A$. The sieve
compares $\mathcal A$ with $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ through the weight
\[
  \mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \frac{\mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]}{\varphi(q)}
  = \frac1{\varphi(q)} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \overline\chi(a) \chi(n),
\]
which is orthogonality: the trivial character cancels, and the other characters carry the difference. This section
states the tools of Harman's sieve \cite{harman83, harman} in the form the proof uses. None of them is new;
\file{formalisations/harman-sieve.md} gives the sources of Proposition~\ref{prop:harman} and Lemma~\ref{lem:rough}, and
the differences between the Lean statements and the classical ones; Lemmas~\ref{lem:type1} to~\ref{lem:chains} use
standard arguments, and \file{formalisations/README.md} lists the Fourier separation of variables behind
Lemmas~\ref{lem:family} to~\ref{lem:chains} among the standard results.

\subsection{Type~I}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:type1}
Let $N_1 \le N_2$ be integers and let $M > 0$ be prime to $q$. The number of $n \in (N_1, N_2]$ with $n \equiv a \pmod q$ and
$M \mid n$ is $(N_2 - N_1)/(Mq)$ within $1$, and the number of $n \in (N_1, N_2]$ prime to $q$ with $M \mid n$ is
$\varphi(q)(N_2 - N_1)/(Mq)$ within $\varphi(q)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $(M, q) = 1$, the Chinese remainder theorem gives $c$ with $c \equiv 0 \pmod M$ and $c \equiv a \pmod q$, and
the two conditions $n \equiv a \pmod q$ and $M \mid n$ together say $n \equiv c \pmod{Mq}$. For $r \ge 1$ the number of
$n \in (N_1, N_2]$ with $n \equiv c \pmod r$ is $\lfloor (N_2 - c)/r \rfloor - \lfloor (N_1 - c)/r \rfloor$, which
differs from $(N_2 - N_1)/r$ by less than $1$. With $r = Mq$ this is the first count. The integers prime to $q$ form the
$\varphi(q)$ residue classes $b \bmod q$ with $(b, q) = 1$, and adding the first count over these classes gives the
second.
\end{proof}

So $|\mathcal A_M| - |\mathcal B_M|/\varphi(q)$ is at most $2$ in absolute value for every $M$ prime to $q$. This is
all the Type~I information the sieve needs. It is used at the leaves of Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}, where what
matters is that the number of counts, a power of $q$ below $q^{L - 1}$, keeps their errors small against the size
$x/q$ of the main terms; the size of each modulus does not.

\subsection{Type~II}

Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} bounds Dirichlet polynomials. The sieve needs sums over $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ of
integers $n = m p_1 \cdots p_K$ whose primes are cut out by comparisons. Fix a smooth function $f_0 \ge 0$ supported
in $[1/2, 2]$, integers $K_0, J_0 \ge 0$ and reals $e_1 > 0$ and $E_0, \Lambda_0, A_0 \ge 0$. A \emph{family} consists of
\begin{itemize}
\item an integer scale $P$ with $x^{u_1} \le P \le x^{u_2}$, a set $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$ and coefficients $|a_m| \le A_0$;
\item between $1$ and $K_0$ primes $p_i \le 2Q_i$ with weights $f_0(p_i/Q_i)$, at scales $Q_i \ge q^{e_1}$ with
  $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} x/P$;
\item at most $J_0$ comparisons, conditions on $(m, p)$ that hold when $\ell_j(m, p) > 0$ and fail when
  $\ell_j(m, p) < 0$, where $\ell_j(m, p) = \lambda_j(m) + \sum_i e_{ji} \log p_i$ with $|e_{ji}| \le E_0$ and
  $\ell_j \le \Lambda_0 \log q$ for every $m \in S$ and all primes $p_i \le 2Q_i$, each comparison with a
  \emph{pivot}, a prime $p_i$ with $e_{ji} = \pm 1$.
\end{itemize}
Its sum over $\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ is the sum of $a_m \prod_i f_0(p_i/Q_i)$ times the weight above at
$n = m p_1 \cdots p_K$, over the $(m, p)$ that satisfy the comparisons. The integers $n$ need not lie in $(x, 2x]$; where
they must, two comparisons say so.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:family}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. Given $f_0$, $K_0$, $e_1$, $J_0$, $E_0$, $\Lambda_0$ and $A_0$, there
are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that for every $q \ge q_0$, every $a$ prime to $q$ and every family, its sum over
$\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ is at most $A_0 q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ in absolute value.
\end{lemma}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:family}. By orthogonality the family's sum is a sum over the non-trivial characters.
Replacing each comparison by a smooth cut-off of its form makes the family smooth; Fourier inversion then separates the
variables, and Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} bounds the smooth family at each frequency. The difference between the sharp
and the smooth comparisons lies near the boundaries $\ell_j = 0$; the proof bounds it by families of the same kind,
again through Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, and by a count along the pivot.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:family-sharp}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. Given $K_0$, $J_0$, $e_1 > 0$, $E_0 \ge 0$ and $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$, there
are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let $m$ run
over a set $S \subseteq (P_1, P_2]$ of integers with $q^{u_1 L} \le P_1$ and $P_2 \le q^{u_2 L}$, with coefficients
$|a_m| \le 1$, and let $1 \le K \le K_0$ primes $p_1, \dots, p_K$ run over all the primes up to $2x$. Let $J \le J_0$
comparisons, with forms $\ell_j = \lambda_j(m) + \sum_i e_{ji} \log p_i$, $|e_{ji}| \le E_0$,
$\lambda_j(m) \le \Lambda_1 \log q$ for $m \in S$, and pivots, cut out the $(m, p)$, and suppose that wherever they all
hold, for $m \in S$ and any primes $p_i$, every $p_i \ge 2q^{e_1}$ and $m p_1 \cdots p_K \le 2x$. Then the sum of
$a_m(\mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]/\varphi(q))$ at $n = m p_1 \cdots p_K$ over the $(m, p)$ that satisfy
the comparisons is at most $q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ in absolute value.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Split $(P_1, P_2]$ into the dyadic blocks $(2^b P_1, 2^{b + 1} P_1]$, and each prime variable by a smooth partition of
unity: take for the $f_0$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:family} a smooth function supported in $[1/2, 2]$, with values in $[0, 1]$
and $\sum_{t \ge 0} f_0(y/2^t) = 1$ for $1 \le y \le 2^M$, $M = \lfloor \log_2 (2x) \rfloor + 1$. This writes the sum as a sum of at most
$(3 + 4 \log q)^{K_0 + 1}$ pieces. In each, $m$ runs over a block, which lies in $(P, 2P]$ with $P$ in the window's
range, and the primes carry the weights $f_0(p_i/2^{t_i})$ at the scales $Q_i = 2^{t_i}$; on its box every form is at
most $(\Lambda_1 + K_0 E_0 (L + 3)) \log q$. A piece with some $2^{t_i} < q^{e_1}$ vanishes, since its weights force
$p_i < 2q^{e_1}$; and so does a piece with $\prod_i 2^{t_i} > 2^{K_0 + 1} x/P$, since its weights force
$m \prod_i p_i > 2x$. In a piece the primes may run over the range $p_i \le 2Q_i$ of a family instead of the primes up
to $2x$, since a term with some $p_i > 2x$ violates the comparisons, which force $m \prod_i p_i \le 2x$. Every other
piece is a family, with $K_0 + 1$ in place of $K_0$, $A_0 = 1$ and
$\Lambda_0 = \Lambda_1 + K_0 E_0 (L + 3)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:family} bounds it by $q^{L - \delta'}/\varphi(q)$. For
large $q$ the number of pieces is at most $q^{\delta'/2}$, and $\delta = \delta'/2$ will do.
\end{proof}

Every use of Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp} below has coefficients $a_m \in \{0, 1\}$. For nonnegative coefficients a
minorant and a majorant of the sharp comparisons, $G(\ell_j) \le \mathbf 1[\ell_j > 0] \le G(\ell_j + \nu)$ with a
cut-off $G$ of width $\nu$, would replace the near-boundary families of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:family} by the
count along the pivot. The library proves the lemma for complex coefficients, as stated, and the paper follows it.

The sieve meets these sums with the rest of $n$ given only as a rough number. The next lemma writes such sums as
families. A comparison of a tuple $(r_1, \dots, r_k)$ is a condition $c + \sum_i e_i \log r_i > 0$ (or $\ge 0$) with
$e_i \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ and a constant term $c$.

\begin{lemma}[chains of primes]\label{lem:chains}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. Given $k \ge 1$, $J_0$, $e_0 > 0$ and $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$, there are
$\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let
$\mathbf r = (r_1, \dots, r_k)$ run over the $k$-tuples of primes up to $N_2$ that satisfy at most $J_0$ comparisons
with constant terms at most $\Lambda_1 \log q$, and suppose that every tuple of primes that satisfies the comparisons has
$r_i \ge q^{e_0}$ for every $i$, and that either every such tuple satisfies (a), or every such tuple satisfies (b):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $r_k \le r_i$ for every $i$, and $W_0 < r_1 \cdots r_k \le W_1$, where $W_1 \le N_1$,
  $q^{u_1 L} \le \lfloor N_1/W_1 \rfloor$ and $\lfloor N_2/(W_0 + 1) \rfloor \le q^{u_2 L}$;
\item[(b)] $r_1 > \dots > r_k$, and $P_1 < \prod_{i \in T} r_i \le P_2$ for a fixed set $T$ of indices, where
  $q^{u_1 L} \le P_1$ and $P_2 \le q^{u_2 L}$.
\end{enumerate}
Then, with $r = r_1 \cdots r_k$,
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{\mathbf r} \Bigl(S(\mathcal A_r, r_k) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_r, r_k)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr)\Bigr|
  \le \frac{q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
Under (b) the same bound holds for the exact products: for the sum over $\mathbf r$ with $N_1 < r \le N_2$ of
$\mathbf 1[r \equiv a] - \mathbf 1[(r, q) = 1]/\varphi(q)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An integer $n$ counted for $\mathbf r$ is $n = rm \in (N_1, N_2]$ with $m$ free of prime factors below $r_k$; since every
$r_i \ge r_k$, this says that $n$ itself has no prime factor below $r_k$.

(a) Here $m$, the rest of $n$, is the Type~II variable. Since $W_0 < r \le W_1$, $m$ lies in
$S = (\lfloor N_1/W_1 \rfloor, \lfloor N_2/(W_0 + 1) \rfloor]$, inside the window's range. So the sum is one of the kind
of Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp}, with $a_m = 1$ on $S$ and the primes $r_1, \dots, r_k$. Its comparisons are the given
ones, each pivoted on a prime with a nonzero coefficient (one with none is a constant condition); the roughness of $m$,
$\log \mathrm{lpf}(m) - \log r_k \ge 0$, with $\mathrm{lpf}(m)$ the least prime factor of $m$ and the pivot $r_k$; and
$N_1 < mr \le N_2$, two comparisons with the forms $\log m + \sum_i \log r_i - \log N_1$ and its counterpart. Their
$m$-parts are at most $(L + 1) \log q$. Wherever they hold, every $r_i \ge q^{e_0} \ge 2q^{e_0/2}$ and $mr \le 2x$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp} applies, with $K_0 = k + 1$, $J_0 + 3$ comparisons, $e_0/2$ in place of $e_1$ and
$\Lambda_1 + L + 1$ in place of $\Lambda_1$.

(b) Here $m = \prod_{i \in T} r_i$ is the variable, and the rest of $n$ must be written in primes. The cofactor
$n/r$ has no prime factor below $r_k \ge q^{e_0}$ and is at most $2x \le q^{L + 1}$, so it is the product of a unique
non-increasing tuple $y_1 \ge \dots \ge y_s \ge r_k$ of primes with $s \le s_1 = \lceil (L + 1)/e_0 \rceil$. For each
$s$, regroup the pairs $(\mathbf r, y)$ by $m$: since $\mathbf r$ is strictly decreasing, $m$ is a squarefree integer
of $(P_1, P_2]$ with $|T|$ prime factors, which, read in decreasing order, are the $r_i$ with $i \in T$, so $m$
determines them. The other variables are the primes $r_i$ with $i \notin T$ and $y_1, \dots, y_s$. The conditions are
the given comparisons (which imply $r_1 > \dots > r_k$), $y_b \le y_a$ for $a \le b$, $y_a \ge r_k$ for every $a$, and
$N_1 < m \prod_{i \notin T} r_i \prod_l y_l \le N_2$, each a comparison whose $m$-part reads the $r_i$ with $i \in T$
off $m$. Those that involve only these $r_i$ are conditions on $m$, and give coefficients $a_m \in \{0, 1\}$ on
$(P_1, P_2]$; each of the others has a nonzero coefficient at a variable outside $T$, its pivot. There are at most
$J_0 + s_1^2 + s_1 + 2$ of them, with $m$-parts at most $(\Lambda_1 + 2L + 2) \log q$, and wherever they hold every
prime is at least $q^{e_0}$ and the product is at most $2x$. If $T = \{1, \dots, k\}$ and $s = 0$, no prime is left:
then $n = m \le P_2 < N_1$ for large $q$, and that sum is empty. Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp}, with
$K_0 = s_1 + k + 1$, $e_0/2$ in place of $e_1$ and $\Lambda_1 + 2L + 2$ in place of $\Lambda_1$, bounds each of the
other sums by $q^{L - \delta'}/\varphi(q)$; there are at most $s_1 + 1$ of them, and $\delta = \delta'/2$ will do for
large $q$. The exact products are the case $s = 0$ alone, with no cofactor: the same regrouping makes their sum one sum
of the kind of Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp} (in Lean, \lean{SieveExact.chainT0\_bound}).
\end{proof}

In (a) the product $r$ lies in the reflection of the window, and in (b) a product of some of the primes lies in the
window. If $W_0 + 1 \ge x^{1 - u_2'}$ (for instance $W_0 = \lfloor x^{1 - u_2'} \rfloor$) and
$W_1 = \lfloor x^{1 - u_1'} \rfloor$ with $u_1 < u_1' < u_2' < u_2$, the
conditions of (a) hold for large $q$, since $\lfloor N_1/W_1 \rfloor \ge x^{u_1'} - 2 \ge x^{u_1}$ and
$\lfloor N_2/(W_0 + 1) \rfloor \le 2x^{u_2'} \le x^{u_2}$. Section~\ref{sec:certificate} uses (a) in this way, with
$u_1' = U_1/D$ and $u_2' = U_2/D$: the margins absorb the factor $2$ of the upper end $2x$ and the rounding of
$N_1/W_1$.

\subsection{Rough numbers}

The sieve needs an upper bound for the number $\Phi(y, z)$ of integers in $(y, 2y]$ with no prime factor below $z$
(\lean{SieveRough.Psi} in the library). Let
\[
  \omega^*(u) = \begin{cases}
    0, & u < 1, \\
    1/u, & 1 \le u < 2, \\
    (1 + \log(u - 1))/u, & 2 \le u \le 3, \\
    0.565073, & u > 3.
  \end{cases}
\]
On $[1, 3]$ this is Buchstab's function $\omega$, whose maximum on $[2, 3]$ is $0.567143\ldots$, the root $t$ of
$t e^t = 1$, at $u = 1 + 1/t$, where $(u - 1)\log(u - 1) = 1$ \cite{cheer-goldston}; above $3$ it is the constant
that the induction of the next lemma needs.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rough}
For every $U$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, for all large integers $z$ and all $y \ge 1$ with $u = \log 2y/\log z \le U$,
$\Phi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon)\, y/\log z$.
\end{lemma}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:rough}) is an induction on $u$ with Buchstab's inequality, the prime number theorem and
a delay inequality: $1 + M(u) \le u \omega^*(u)$ for $u \ge 2$, where $M(u)$ is $\int_1^{u - 1} \omega^*$ with
$\omega^*$ replaced by $0.5671433$ on $[2, 3]$, a bound for it there since $0.5671433 \ge 0.567143\ldots$. On $[2, 3]$ the
inequality is the equality $1 + \log(u - 1) = u\omega^*(u)$. Above $3$ it asks
$1 + \log 2 + 0.5671433 \min(u - 3, 1) + c_* \max(u - 4, 0) \le c_* u$ of the constant $c_*$ of $\omega^*$ there,
which holds for every $u > 3$ exactly when $1 + \log 2 + 0.5671433 \le 4c_*$, that is when $c_* \ge 0.5650726\ldots$;
so $0.565073$ is that bound rounded up. The certificate of Section~\ref{sec:certificate} uses the lemma, through $\omega^*$, to bound
the share of the integers in each term it drops.

\subsection{Harman's comparison}

\begin{proposition}[Harman's comparison, in the form used]\label{prop:harman}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. Fix $j$, $J_0$, $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$, $\varepsilon_H > 0$, and exponents
$a_0 > 0$ and $b_0$ with $a_0 + b_0 < (1 - u_1)L$ and $(1 - u_2)L < b_0 < L - 1$. There are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such
that the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let $(r_1, \dots, r_j)$ run over the ordered
$j$-tuples of primes up to $2x$ that satisfy at most $J_0$ comparisons, with coefficients at most $1$ in absolute value,
pivots and constant terms at most $\Lambda_1 \log q$, which force every $r_i \ge z_0 = \lceil q^{a_0} \rceil$ and
$r = r_1 \cdots r_j \le q^{b_0}$. Then
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{(r_1, \dots, r_j)} \Bigl(S(\mathcal A_r, z_0) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_r, z_0)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr)\Bigr|
  \le \frac{\varepsilon_H\, x}{\varphi(q) \log q} + \frac{q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
\end{proposition}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:harman}. It iterates Buchstab's identity \cite{buchstab} from $z_0$ down to a small
power $w$ of $q$, stopping a branch as soon as its product exceeds $X' = \lfloor q^{b_0} \rfloor$. A stopped branch has
its product in $(X', z_0 X']$, the reflection of the window, and its cofactor in the window's range, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp} evaluates the stops. The leaves are sifted only by the primes below $w$, and Brun's sieve
\cite{brun} compares their $\mathcal A$- and $\mathcal B$-counts, with an error that the elementary bound
$\prod_{p < w}(1 - 1/p) \le 1/\log w$ and the choice of the sieve's levels make at most $\varepsilon_H x/(\varphi(q) \log q)$.

\section{The decomposition and its certificate}\label{sec:certificate}

This section bounds $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ from below. The decomposition writes it by Buchstab's identity as
a signed sum of sifted terms. Each term is evaluated, that is compared with its $\mathcal B$-version by
Lemma~\ref{lem:chains} or Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}, or, if it has sign $+$, dropped, which costs its
$\mathcal B$-version. The certificate is an upper bound for the total cost, computed exactly in integers.

\subsection{Exponents and the grid}

The exponent of a prime $p$ is $\alpha = \log p/\log x$. Fix a denominator $D = 2^{40}$ and round the window's ends
inward: $U_1 = \lceil u_1 D \rceil$ and $U_2 = \lfloor u_2 D \rfloor$; since $u_1 D$ and $u_2 D$ are not integers,
$u_1 < U_1/D < U_2/D < u_2$. Let $v = (U_2 - U_1)/D$. The decomposition sifts the primes of exponent at least $v$. The
grid has $N$ cells a side, of side $h = \lceil (D/2 - (U_2 - U_1))/N \rceil / D$; the cell $k$ is the range
$[e_k, e_k + h)$ of exponents with $e_k = v + kh$, for $0 \le k < N$, so that the cells cover $[v, v + Nh)$, and
$v + Nh > 1/2$. Here $N = 340$. The primes of the two deepest levels of the decomposition, $p_5$ and $p_6$, have their
exponents in the cells of a \emph{coarse grid} with the same window and $N_C = N/4 = 85$ cells a side, each four
consecutive cells of the grid: its side is $h_C = 4h$, and its cell $b$ is $[e^C_b, e^C_b + h_C)$ with
$e^C_b = v + b h_C = e_{4b}$.

The set of \emph{evaluable} exponents is
\[
  \mathcal U = (U_1/D,\ U_2/D) \cup (1 - U_2/D,\ 1 - U_1/D).
\]
A box, a product of cells, is \emph{surely evaluable} if for some subset of its coordinates the sum of the exponents
lies in $\mathcal U$ throughout the box. A box is tested exactly: the subset sums of a box range over intervals with
rational ends.

\subsection{The decomposition}

\emph{The parameters.} Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:harman} with $a_0 = vL$ and $b_0 = (1 - U_2/D)L$, so that
$z_0 = \lceil x^v \rceil$ and $X' = \lfloor x^{1 - U_2/D} \rfloor$. Its hypotheses hold:
$a_0 + b_0 = (1 - U_1/D)L < (1 - u_1)L$, since $U_1/D > u_1$; $b_0 > (1 - u_2)L$, since $U_2/D < u_2$; and
$b_0 < L - 1$ is the condition $L U_2 > D$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:route}. For large $x$, $z_{\mathrm{top}} \le X'$, since
$2U_2 < D$, and $z_0 X' \le x^{1 - U_1/D}$ up to rounding: $v$ is the largest floor for which the stops of
Proposition~\ref{prop:harman} stay in the reflection of the rounded window $[U_1/D, U_2/D]$.

\emph{The tree.} Buchstab's identity
\[
  S(\mathcal A_m, z) = S(\mathcal A_m, z_0) - \sum_{z_0 \le p < z} S(\mathcal A_{mp}, p),
\]
applied to $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ and repeated, introduces primes $p_1 > p_2 > \cdots$, each at least
$z_0$, with exponents $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots$. A \emph{term of depth $k$} is a sum of $S(\mathcal A_{p_1 \cdots p_k}, p_k)$
over the primes whose exponents lie in a box of cells, fine for $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_4$ and coarse for $\alpha_5$ and
$\alpha_6$; its sign is $(-1)^k$. Each use of the identity on a term with $p_1 \cdots p_k \le X'$ also gives the term
$S(\mathcal A_{p_1 \cdots p_k}, z_0)$, which Proposition~\ref{prop:harman} evaluates, with the cells, the order of the
primes and the earlier splits as its comparisons. A term of depth $k$ is treated by the first of the following rules
that applies.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(E)] If its box is surely evaluable, it is evaluated by Lemma~\ref{lem:chains}.
\item[(O)] If its box lies in $\alpha_k \ge \alpha_{k - 1}$, that is, the cell of $\alpha_k$ starts at or above the end of
  the cell of $\alpha_{k - 1}$, or if its lower corner $(e_1, \dots, e_k)$ has $e_1 + \cdots + e_{k - 1} + 2e_k > 1$, so
  that $\alpha_1 + \cdots + \alpha_{k - 1} + 2\alpha_k \ge 1 + 1/D$ on the box, it is empty for $k \ge 3$ (below), and
  for $k = 2$ it is empty or counts only products $p_1 p_2$; these \emph{corner} cells of depth two are dropped, at a
  negligible cost (Lemma~\ref{lem:drop}).
\item[(S)] Otherwise, a term with sign $-$ is split at $X'$: the part with $p_1 \cdots p_k > X'$ is evaluated by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:chains}(a), with the cofactor as the Type~II variable, and the part with $p_1 \cdots p_k \le X'$ is
  decomposed by Buchstab's identity, which gives $S(\mathcal A_{p_1 \cdots p_k}, z_0)$ and terms of depth $k + 1$.
\item[(D)] A term with sign $+$ is dropped, or, if the sharp rule below allows it and the certificate chooses it,
  split and decomposed as in (S).
\end{enumerate}
For $k \ge 3$ the order of (E) and (O) does not matter, since the terms of (O) are empty; the library tests (O) first at
depths three and five.
At depth one there is no split: a term whose cell lies inside the window is evaluated by Lemma~\ref{lem:chains}(b) with
$T = \{1\}$, and every other one is decomposed, since $p_1 < z_{\mathrm{top}} \le X'$. The terms of depth six are
evaluated or dropped, and not decomposed. At depth two a cell to which (E) and (O) do not apply is a
\emph{candidate}; at depths four and six such a cell is \emph{counted}. Table~\ref{tab:tree} sums up.

\emph{Empty terms.} A term of depth $k \ge 3$ lies below a split, so $p_1 \cdots p_{k - 1} \le X'$ on it, and
$p_k < p_{k - 1} < X'^{1/2}$, since $p_{k-1}^2 < p_1 p_2 \le X'$. If its box has $\alpha_k \ge \alpha_{k - 1}$, it is empty.
If the box has $\alpha_1 + \cdots + \alpha_{k - 1} + 2\alpha_k \ge 1 + 1/D$, then for large $x$,
$p_1 \cdots p_k^2 \ge x^{1 + 1/D} > 2x$, so a cofactor $m$ with no prime factor below $p_k$ is $1$, and
$n = p_1 \cdots p_k < X'^{3/2} < N_1$, since $\tfrac32 (1 - U_2/D) < \tfrac32 (1 - 1/L) \le 9/10$ by $L U_2 > D$ and
$L \le 5/2$; so the term counts no integer of $(N_1, N_2]$. At depth two the same holds for
$\alpha_2 \ge \alpha_1$, while outside $\alpha_1 + 2\alpha_2 \le 1$ the cofactor is $1$ and the term counts the
products $n = p_1 p_2 \in (x, 2x]$.

\emph{The sharp rule.} It allows a candidate cell $(i, j)$ to be decomposed only if the upper corner of its box has
$(e_i + h) + (e_j + h) < 1 - U_1/D$, and the upper corner of each of its cells $(i, j, k)$ of depth three to which (E)
and (O) do not apply has $(e_i + h) + (e_j + h) + (e_k + h) < 1 - U_1/D$; and a counted cell of depth four only if the
same tests hold for its box and for the boxes of its cells of depth five to which (E) and (O) do not apply, whose last
cell is coarse, with upper end $e^C_b + h_C$. Then the cell, and every term below it that is split at $X'$, has
$\alpha_1 + \cdots + \alpha_k < 1 - U_1/D$ on its box, so that its part above $X'$ has
$X' < p_1 \cdots p_k < x^{1 - U_1/D}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:chains}(a) applies there, with $W_0 = X'$ and $W_1 = \lfloor x^{1 - U_1/D} \rfloor$: the cofactor lies in
$(x^{U_1/D}, 2x^{U_2/D}]$, inside the window's range for large $q$.

\emph{Evaluation.} Let the box of a term of depth $k$ be surely evaluable, with $\sum_{i \in T} \alpha_i \in \mathcal U$
on it. If the sum lies in $(U_1/D, U_2/D)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:chains}(b) applies with this $T$, the cells, the order of
the primes and the earlier splits as comparisons. If it lies in $(1 - U_2/D, 1 - U_1/D)$, then $T$ is the set of all
the primes, since a proper subset has product at most $p_1 \cdots p_{k - 1} \le X' \le x^{1 - U_2/D}$; so the cofactor
is the variable, and Lemma~\ref{lem:chains}(a) applies.

\subsection{The cost of a dropped term}

A dropped term of depth two over the cell $(i, j)$ counts the $n = p_1 p_2 m \in (x, 2x]$ whose cofactor $m$ has no
prime factor below $p_2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:rough} and the prime number theorem its $\mathcal B$-version is, for large
$q$, at most $x/\log x$ times
\[
  \iint_{\text{cell}} \omega^*\Bigl(\frac{1 - \alpha_1 - \alpha_2}{\alpha_2}\Bigr)
  \frac{d\alpha_1\, d\alpha_2}{\alpha_1 \alpha_2^2},
\]
plus $\varepsilon$, and similarly at depth four with
$d\alpha_1 \cdots d\alpha_4/(\alpha_1 \alpha_2 \alpha_3 \alpha_4^2)$ and the argument
$(1 - \alpha_1 - \cdots - \alpha_4)/\alpha_4$, and at depth six with
$d\alpha_1 \cdots d\alpha_6/(\alpha_1 \cdots \alpha_5 \alpha_6^2)$ and the argument
$(1 - \alpha_1 - \cdots - \alpha_6)/\alpha_6$. The certificate bounds each integral by a bound $\Omega$ for $\omega^*$
on the cell's range of arguments times the integral of the rest, a product of integrals of $d\alpha/\alpha$ and one
of $d\alpha/\alpha^2$ over the cells. It bounds the first kind from above by the trapezoid rule, since $1/\alpha$ is
convex, and takes the second exactly:
\begin{gather*}
  \int_e^{e + h} \frac{d\alpha}\alpha = \log\Bigl(1 + \frac he\Bigr) \le t(e, h) = \frac{h(2e + h)}{2e(e + h)}, \\
  \int_e^{e + h} \frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^2} = s(e, h) = \frac h{e(e + h)}.
\end{gather*}
It works in units of $1/\mathcal K$ with $\mathcal K = 2^{60}$, every quotient rounded up, except the constants $\kappa$
of the kept parts below, which are rounded down. The bound $\Omega$ for
$\omega^*$ on a cell of depth two, whose arguments lie in $[u_{\min}, u_{\max})$ with
$u_{\min} = (1 - e_i - e_j - 2h)/(e_j + h)$ and $u_{\max} = (1 + 1/D - e_i - e_j)/e_j$, is found by cases, since
$\omega^*$ is not monotone:
\begin{itemize}
\item $0$ if $u_{\max} \le 1$;
\item $0.565073$, rounded up in units of $1/\mathcal K$, if $u_{\min} \ge 3$;
\item $1/\max(u_{\min}, 1)$, rounded up, if $u_{\max} \le 2$;
\item otherwise the largest of $1/\max(u_{\min}, 1)$, if $u_{\min} < 2$; of a bound for $\omega^*$ on the part of the
  range in $[2, 3]$; and of $0.565073$, if $u_{\max} > 3$.
\end{itemize}
On $[2, 3]$, $\omega^* = \omega$ increases up to $2.76$ and decreases from $2.77$ on (Appendix~\ref{app:rough}), so the
bound there is $\omega(u_{\max})$ if $u_{\max} \le 2.76$, $\omega(u_{\min})$ if $u_{\min} \ge 2.77$, and $0.5671433$, at
least its maximum $0.567143\ldots$ there, otherwise. At a rational $w \in [2, 3]$ the certificate bounds $\omega(w) = (1 + \log(w - 1))/w$ by
$\log(w - 1) \le \ell_k + (y - 1/y)/2$, where $r_k = 1 + k/16 \le w - 1 < r_k + 1/16$, $y = (w - 1)/r_k \ge 1$, and
$\ell_k \ge \log r_k$ is one of seventeen rationals of a table, each checked against the exponential's series; the
second term is $\log y \le (y - 1/y)/2$, the trapezoid rule again, which here errs by less than $4 \cdot 10^{-5}$. At
depths four and six $\Omega$ is found in the same way, from the sum of the other exponents and the cells' widths. The
\emph{bound} of a cell is then
\begin{gather*}
  \Delta_2(i, j) = \bigl\lceil \mathcal K \Omega\, t(e_i, h)\, s(e_j, h) \bigr\rceil, \\
  \Delta_4(i, j, k, l) = \bigl\lceil \mathcal K \Omega\, t(e_i, h)\, t(e_j, h)\, t(e_k, h)\, s(e_l, h) \bigr\rceil, \\
  \Delta_6(i, j, k, l, b, c) = \bigl\lceil \mathcal K \Omega\, t(e_i, h)\, t(e_j, h)\, t(e_k, h)\, t(e_l, h)\,
    t(e^C_b, h_C)\, s(e^C_c, h_C) \bigr\rceil,
\end{gather*}
with $\mathcal K \Omega$ the rounded value; at depth six $b$ and $c$ are coarse cells.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:drop}
For every $\varepsilon_c > 0$ and all large $q$, with $X = x/\log x$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the $\mathcal B$-version of the dropped term of a candidate cell $(i, j)$ is at most
  $(\Delta_2(i, j)/\mathcal K + \varepsilon_c) X$;
\item that of a dropped counted cell of depth four is at most $(\Delta_4/\mathcal K + \varepsilon_c) X$, and that of a
  dropped counted cell of depth six at most $(\Delta_6/\mathcal K + \varepsilon_c) X$;
\item the corner cells together contribute $o(X)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a cell $k$, write $\mathcal P_k$ for the set of the primes $p$ with $x^{e_k} \le p < x^{e_k + h}$.

(1) The $\mathcal B$-version of the term counts the $n \in (x, 2x]$ prime to $q$ of the form $n = p_1 p_2 m$ with
$(p_1, p_2)$ in the cell and $m$ without prime factors below $p_2$. Dropping the conditions $(n, q) = 1$, $p_2 < p_1$,
$z_0 \le p_2$ and $p_1 < z_{\mathrm{top}}$, and mapping $n$ to $m$, it is at most
\[
  \sum_{p_1 \in \mathcal P_i} \sum_{p_2 \in \mathcal P_j} \Phi\Bigl(\frac{x}{p_1 p_2}, p_2\Bigr).
\]
A candidate has $e_i + 2e_j \le 1$, and $e_j \ge v \ge 4h$, so $e_i + e_j + 2h < 1$ and $y = x/(p_1 p_2) \ge 1$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:rough}, with $U = 2/e_j$, applies to $\Phi(y, p_2)$, with
$u = \log 2y/\log p_2 = (1 - \alpha_1 - \alpha_2 + \tau)/\alpha_2$ and $\tau = \log 2/\log x$. Since $\tau < 1/D$ once
$\log x > D \log 2$, $u < u_{\max}$, and $u \ge u_{\min}$ since $\tau \ge 0$. So $\Omega$ also bounds $\omega^*(u)$,
and
$\Phi(y, p_2) \le (\Omega + \varepsilon') x/(p_1 p_2 \log p_2)$ for large $x$. The term is at most
\[
  (\Omega + \varepsilon') \, x \sum_{p_1 \in \mathcal P_i} \frac1{p_1} \sum_{p_2 \in \mathcal P_j}
  \frac1{p_2 \log p_2}.
\]
By the prime number theorem and partial summation, for large $x$,
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_{p \in \mathcal P_i} \frac1p \le \log\Bigl(1 + \frac h{e_i}\Bigr) + \varepsilon' \le t(e_i, h) + \varepsilon', \\
  \log x \sum_{p \in \mathcal P_j} \frac1{p \log p} \le \frac1{e_j} - \frac1{e_j + h} + \varepsilon'
  = s(e_j, h) + \varepsilon'.
\end{gather*}
These are the integrals of $d\alpha/\alpha$ and $d\alpha/\alpha^2$ over the cells; the first bound,
$\log(1 + h/e_i) \le t(e_i, h)$, is $\xi \le \sinh \xi$ at $\xi = \log(1 + h/e_i)$, since
$t(e, h) = \sinh \log(1 + h/e)$. Since
$\Delta_2(i, j)/\mathcal K \ge \Omega\, t(e_i, h)\, s(e_j, h)$, the term is at most
$(\Delta_2(i, j)/\mathcal K + \varepsilon_c) X$ once $\varepsilon'$ is small.

(2) The same argument bounds a dropped counted cell $(i, j, k, l)$ of depth four, with
$\Phi(x/(p_1 \cdots p_4), p_4)$, three sums $\sum 1/p$ and one sum $\log x \sum 1/(p \log p)$, and a dropped counted
cell of depth six, whose last two primes lie in coarse cells. Here $y \ge 1$ follows, as in (1), from the test of
(O) at the lower corner of a counted cell, $e_i + e_j + e_k + 2e_l \le 1$ at depth four and
$e_i + e_j + e_k + e_l + e^C_b + 2e^C_c \le 1$ at depth six, with $4h \le v$ at depth four and $4h + 2h_C \le v$ at
depth six.

(3) A corner cell $(i, j)$ has its cell $i$ outside the window, and, since $2e_i < 1$ and the term is not empty,
$e_i + 2e_j > 1$. Then $p_1 p_2^2 > 2x$ for large $x$, so the cofactor, which has no prime factor below $p_2$, is $1$,
and $n = p_1 p_2 > x$ with $p_1, p_2 < z_{\mathrm{top}}$. Both primes then lie in $(z_{\mathrm{top}}/4, z_{\mathrm{top}}]$,
so there are at most $(12\sqrt x/\log x)^2$ such integers for large $x$. The corner cells, at most $N^2$ of them,
together contribute $O(x/\log^2 x) = o(X)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The refinement: keeping two-prime parts}

A dropped term $S(\mathcal A_{p_1 p_2}, p_2)$ counts, among others, the $n = p_1 p_2 \ell_1 \ell_2$ with primes
$p_2 < \ell_1 < \ell_2$. Such an $n$ is a product of four primes, and if some of their exponents sum into $\mathcal U$ it
belongs to a Type~II sum. The refinement keeps these parts instead of dropping them.

Let the cell $(i, j)$ be a candidate that is not decomposed, with $e_j + h \le e_i$ and $2(e_i + h) \le 1$, so that
$\alpha_2 < \alpha_1 < 1/2$ on its box. A cell $k$ is \emph{kept} below $(i, j)$ if $e_k \ge e_j + h$ (so $\ell_1 > p_2$
on the box), $2(e_k + h) + e_i + e_j + 2h \le 1$ (so $\ell_1 < \ell_2$ on the box), and the box $(i, j, k)$ is surely
evaluable. The \emph{kept part} below $k$ counts the $n = p_1 p_2 \ell_1 \ell_2 \in (x, 2x]$ with $(p_1, p_2)$ in the
cell $(i, j)$, $\ell_1$ a prime of the cell $k$, $\ell_2 > \ell_1$ a prime, and $\ell_1, \ell_2 \ne p_1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kept}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each pair $(p_1, p_2)$ of the dropped term, the kept parts below the cell count distinct integers, all of
  which the term counts for that pair; so their $\mathcal A$-count is at most the term's.
\item Each kept part is the sum of three families of products of four primes in decreasing order, according to the
  position of $p_1$ among $\ell_1 < \ell_2$, and the exact-product case of Lemma~\ref{lem:chains}(b) evaluates each of
  them.
\item For every $\varepsilon_c > 0$ and all large $q$, the $\mathcal B$-version of the kept part below $k$ is at least
  $(\kappa(i, j, k)/\mathcal K - \varepsilon_c)\, x/\log x$, where
  $\kappa(i, j, k) = \lfloor 8\mathcal K h^3 / ((2e_i + h)(2e_j + h)(2e_k + h)(1 - e_i - e_j - e_k)) \rfloor$, so that
  $\kappa(i, j, k)/\mathcal K$ is a lower bound for the integral over the box of the density
  $d\alpha_1\, d\alpha_2\, d\beta/(\alpha_1 \alpha_2 \beta \gamma)$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ the exponents of $\ell_1$
  and $\ell_2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $X = x/\log x$ and $\mathcal P_c$ for the set of primes of the cell $c$. By the definition of the term
$S(\mathcal A_{p_1 p_2}, p_2)$ of the cell $(i, j)$, its pairs $(p_1, p_2)$ have $p_2 < p_1$, $z_0 \le p_2$ and
$p_1 < z_{\mathrm{top}}$.

(1) Fix a pair $(p_1, p_2)$ of the term. An integer $n = p_1 p_2 \ell_1 \ell_2$ of a kept part determines
$\ell_1 \ell_2 = n/(p_1 p_2)$, hence the primes $\ell_1 < \ell_2$, and $\ell_1$ determines the cell $k$, since the cells
are disjoint. So, for this pair, the kept parts below $(i, j)$ count each integer at most once. Each such $n$ is counted
by the term for the pair: $n \in (x, 2x]$, $p_1 p_2 \mid n$, and every prime factor of $n$ is one of $p_1$, $p_2$,
$\ell_1$, $\ell_2$, which are all at least $p_2$, since $\ell_1 \ge x^{e_k} \ge x^{e_j + h} > p_2$ and $\ell_2 > \ell_1$.
Summing over the pairs, with the nonnegative weight $\mathbf 1[n \equiv a]$, gives (1). The kept $n$ are products of
four distinct primes; the integers of the term with a prime-power cofactor stay in the dropped remainder.

(2) For an integer $n = p_1 p_2 \ell_1 \ell_2$ of the kept part below $k$, exactly one of $\ell_2 < p_1$,
$\ell_1 < p_1 < \ell_2$ and $p_1 < \ell_1$ holds, since $\ell_1 < \ell_2$, $\ell_1, \ell_2 \ne p_1$ and $p_2 < \ell_1$.
So the kept part is the sum of three sums over the decreasing $4$-tuples $r_1 > r_2 > r_3 > r_4$ of primes, equal to
$(p_1, \ell_2, \ell_1, p_2)$, $(\ell_2, p_1, \ell_1, p_2)$ and $(\ell_2, \ell_1, p_1, p_2)$ respectively. The
conditions are $N_1 < r_1 r_2 r_3 r_4 \le N_2$, that $p_1$ lies in the cell $i$ with $z_0 \le p_1 < z_{\mathrm{top}}$,
that $p_2 = r_4$ lies in the cell $j$ with $p_2 \ge z_0$, and that $\ell_1$ lies in the cell $k$; $\ell_2$ is subject to
no cell condition. These are $14$ comparisons, with coefficients in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ and constant terms
$O(\log q)$. Since the box $(i, j, k)$ is surely evaluable, the product of a set $T$ of the primes $p_1$, $p_2$,
$\ell_1$ lies, on the box, in $(x^{U_1/D}, x^{U_2/D})$ or in $(x^{1 - U_2/D}, x^{1 - U_1/D})$. In the first case
$m = \prod_T$ lies in the window's range; in the second the complementary product, which contains $\ell_2$, does, for
large $q$, as in the remark after Lemma~\ref{lem:chains}. Group the tuples by this $m$, a squarefree integer whose prime
factors, read in decreasing order, are the primes of its set. The comparisons that involve only these primes then
become conditions on $m$, absorbed into $a_m \in \{0, 1\}$, and the others are pivoted on the remaining primes. Each of
the three sums is then a sum as in Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp}, which bounds its $\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$
by $q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$.

(3) For $p_1 \in \mathcal P_i$, $p_2 \in \mathcal P_j$ and $\ell_1 \in \mathcal P_k$, put $y = x/(p_1 p_2 \ell_1)$ and
$\gamma_0 = 1 - e_i - e_j - e_k$. Since $\alpha_2 < \alpha_1 < 1/2$ on the box, $p_2 < p_1 < x^{1/2}$, so every such pair
belongs to the term. By the second condition of a kept cell, $\gamma_0 \ge e_k + 4h$ and $\ell_1 < y$, so every prime
$\ell_2 \in (y, 2y]$ has $\ell_2 > \ell_1$ and $p_1 p_2 \ell_1 \ell_2 \in (x, 2x]$. Exclude $\ell_2 = p_1$ and the
$\ell_2$ that divide $q$, at most $1 + \omega(q)$ primes with $\omega(q) \le \log q/\log 2$, and exclude the triples with
$\ell_1 = p_1$ or with a prime dividing $q$, each of which has at most $\pi(2y) - \pi(y) \le y + 1$ primes $\ell_2$.
Since there are at most as many excluded triples as triples, the $\mathcal B$-version of the kept part is at least
\[
  \sum_{p_1, p_2, \ell_1} \bigl(\pi(2y) - \pi(y)\bigr) - (2 + \omega(q)) \#\{(p_1, p_2, \ell_1)\}
  - \sum_{\text{excluded triples}} y.
\]
On the box $x^{\gamma_0 - 3h} < y \le x^{\gamma_0}$, so for large $x$ the prime number theorem gives
$\pi(2y) - \pi(y) \ge (1 - \varepsilon) y/\log y \ge (1 - \varepsilon) y/(\gamma_0 \log x)$, and
\[
  \sum_{p_1, p_2, \ell_1} \frac y{\gamma_0 \log x} = \frac X{\gamma_0} \prod_{c \in \{i, j, k\}} \sum_{p \in \mathcal P_c} \frac1p
  \ge \frac X{\gamma_0} \Bigl(\prod_c \frac{2h}{2e_c + h} - 3d\Bigr),
\]
using $\sum_{p \in \mathcal P_c} 1/p \ge \log(1 + h/e_c) - d \ge 2h/(2e_c + h) - d$ for large $x$, which is the prime
number theorem again with $\log(1 + h/e_c) = 2\operatorname{artanh}(h/(2e_c + h)) \ge 2h/(2e_c + h)$, the first term of
the series, and that each sum is at most $1$. The excluded triples contribute $O(x(1 + \omega(q))/z_0)$, since
$\sum_{p \ge z_0,\ p \mid q} 1/p \le \omega(q)/z_0$, and the triples number $O(x^{e_i + e_j + e_k + 3h})$ with
$e_i + e_j + e_k + 3h < 1$. Both are $o(X)$. Finally
$(1/\gamma_0) \prod_c 2h/(2e_c + h) \ge \kappa(i, j, k)/\mathcal K$, because the floor is at most the quotient. Taking
$\varepsilon$ and $d$ small in terms of $\varepsilon_c$ gives (3). The bound is the integral of the density over the box,
read from below: the number of $\ell_2$ is about $y/(\gamma_0 \log x)$, and $\int_e^{e + h} d\alpha/\alpha \ge 2h/(2e + h)$,
the value of $1/\alpha$ at the cell's midpoint times $h$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The certificate's total}

The certificate prices each candidate cell by what it costs: dropped, or decomposed with its own dropped terms.
\begin{itemize}
\item A counted cell $(i, j, k, l)$ of depth four costs
  $\Delta^{\mathrm{dec}}_4(i, j, k, l)$, the sum of the bounds $\Delta_6$ of the counted cells of depth six below its
  cells of depth five to which (E) and (O) do not apply, if it is decomposed, and its bound $\Delta_4(i, j, k, l)$
  otherwise. It is decomposed when the sharp rule allows it and $\Delta^{\mathrm{dec}}_4 \le \Delta_4$.
\item A candidate cell $(i, j)$ costs $\Delta^{\mathrm{dec}}_2(i, j)$, the sum over its cells of depth three to which (E)
  and (O) do not apply of the costs of their counted cells of depth four, if it is decomposed, and otherwise
  $\Delta_2(i, j)$ less $\kappa(i, j) = \sum_k \kappa(i, j, k)$, the sum over the cells $k$ kept below it (none unless
  $e_j + h \le e_i$ and $2(e_i + h) \le 1$), truncated at $0$. It is decomposed when the sharp rule allows it and
  $\Delta^{\mathrm{dec}}_2 \le \Delta_2$.
\item The total is the sum of the costs of the candidate cells, $\Sigma' = \sum_{i, j} \mathrm{cost}'(i, j)$.
\end{itemize}
On the library's grid the choice at depth two is forced: the sharp rule allows $13230$ of the $27020$ candidate cells,
each of them has $\Delta^{\mathrm{dec}}_2 \le (\Delta_2 - \kappa(i, j))_+$, and so each is decomposed, and pricing every
candidate cell at the smaller of its two costs gives the same total.
The other cells of depth two cost nothing in the total: they are evaluated, empty, or corner cells
(Lemma~\ref{lem:drop}(3)). Without the kept parts, that is with $\kappa = 0$, the total $\Sigma$ is about
$1.12341\mathcal K$ on the grid of this paper, and with the decomposition stopped at depth four it is about
$1.45721\mathcal K$ (computations by \file{tools/linnik/mirror2.py}, not in Lean). The kept parts remove about a ninth
of the total.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:decomp-kept}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. If $\Sigma'/\mathcal K \le c$, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$, for
all large $q$ and every $a$ prime to $q$,
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) \ge \frac{1}{\varphi(q)} S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})
  - (c + \varepsilon)\frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The identities.} Buchstab's identity, the division of each prime's range into cells and the splits at $X'$ are
exact, so $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ is the signed sum of the terms of the tree, and so is
$S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})$. Compare the two term by term. A dropped term has sign $+$ and an $\mathcal A$-count at
least $0$, and, for a dropped candidate cell with kept parts, at least the sum of the $\mathcal A$-counts of its kept
parts, by Lemma~\ref{lem:kept}(1). Every other term is evaluated or empty, or is decomposed. A decomposed term is the
signed sum of the terms below it, so one with sign $-$ is bounded from above, and one with sign $+$ from below, by
bounding those terms: the decomposed terms of sign $-$ are those of depths one, three and five, and those of sign $+$
the candidate cells of depth two and the counted cells of depth four that are decomposed.

\emph{The errors.} Let $\mathrm{Err} = \varepsilon_H x/(\varphi(q) \log q) + q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$, with the $\varepsilon_H$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:harman} small and the least of the $\delta$ of Lemmas~\ref{lem:family-sharp}
and~\ref{lem:chains} and Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}. Every evaluated term and every term $S(\cdot, z_0)$ has its $\mathcal A$- and
$\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$-counts within $\mathrm{Err}$, and every kept part within $3\,\mathrm{Err}$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:kept}(2). There are at most $C_{\mathrm{dec}} = 1 + N(1 + N(2 + N(2 + N(N_C(N_C + 2) + 2))))$ terms,
and at most $N^3$ kept parts. So
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) \ge \frac{S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}}) - (D(\mathcal B) - K(\mathcal B))}{\varphi(q)}
  - (C_{\mathrm{dec}} + 3N^3)\,\mathrm{Err},
\]
where $D(\mathcal B)$ is the sum of the $\mathcal B$-versions of the dropped terms and $K(\mathcal B)$ that of the kept
parts. Taking $\varepsilon_H$ small in terms of $\varepsilon$ and $q$ large, the errors are below
$\varepsilon x/(2\varphi(q) \log x)$.

\emph{The dropped terms.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:drop}, a dropped candidate cell contributes at most
$(\Delta_2(i, j)/\mathcal K + \varepsilon_c) X$ to $D(\mathcal B)$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:kept}(3) its kept parts at least
$(\kappa(i, j)/\mathcal K - N\varepsilon_c) X$ to $K(\mathcal B)$; a decomposed candidate cell contributes at most
$(\Delta^{\mathrm{dec}}_2(i, j)/\mathcal K + N^2(1 + N_C^2)\varepsilon_c) X$, the sum over its dropped terms of depths four
and six; and the corner cells $o(X)$. Since truncating at $0$ can only raise a cost, each candidate cell contributes at
most $(\mathrm{cost}'(i, j)/\mathcal K + N^2(1 + N_C^2)\varepsilon_c) X$ to $D(\mathcal B) - K(\mathcal B)$. Adding up and
taking $\varepsilon_c$ small, $D(\mathcal B) - K(\mathcal B) \le (c + \varepsilon/2) X$ for large $q$.
\end{proof}

On the grid of $N = 340$ cells a side, with $N_C = 85$ cells of four a side in the coarse grid, and with

\end{proposition}

The total is a finite sum of rational expressions in natural numbers, rounded to integers, so the proposition is a

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:proof}

The argument of Sections~\ref{sec:type1} and~\ref{sec:certificate} uses of the particular values of $L$, $u_1$, $u_2$
and $N$ only the conditions of the following theorem, of which Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a case.

\begin{theorem}[the route theorem]\label{thm:route}
Let $2 < L \le 5/2$ and $0 < u_1 < u_2 < 1$, and let a grid of $N$ cells a side, with $4 \mid N$, ends $U_1$ and $U_2$
over $D = 2^{40}$ and its coarse grid of $N/4$ cells of four, satisfy $u_1 D < U_1$, $U_2 < u_2 D$ and $L U_2 > D$, and
the grid conditions
\[
  0 < h, \quad U_1 < U_2, \quad 2U_2 < D, \quad 2e_{N - 1} < 1 < 2(v + Nh), \quad 16h \le v.
\]
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} for $L$ on the window $[u_1 L, u_2 L]$, and $\Sigma' < \mathcal K$ on
the grid.
Then there is $K'$ such that for every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$ there is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with
$p \le K' q^L$.
\end{theorem}

In Lean (\lean{SieveMain2.linnik\_route\_lt2}) the grid conditions are \lean{GridOK}, the conditions above with
$4h \le v$ in place of $16h \le v$, and \lean{GridOKC}: \lean{GridOK} for the coarse grid, $4h + 2h_C \le v$ and
$Nh \le N_C h_C$. The Lean's grids also carry a multiplier $\mu$ (\lean{Grid.mult}), the side being rounded up to a
multiple of $\mu/D$; Theorem~\ref{thm:route} is the case $\mu = 1$. For $4 \mid N$ and $\mu = 1$ the coarse cells are
unions of four cells, $h_C = 4h$, $e^C_{N_C - 1} = e_{N - 4}$ and $N_C h_C = Nh$, so beyond \lean{GridOK} the
condition \lean{GridOKC} asks only $4h_C = 16h \le v$, which implies $4h \le v$ and $4h + 2h_C = 12h \le v$.

The grid conditions are what the proofs of Sections~\ref{sec:certificate} and~\ref{sec:proof} use of the grid.
$0 < h$ and $U_1 < U_2$ make the cells and the floor $z_0 = \lceil x^v \rceil$, with $v > 0$, well defined.
$1 < 2(v + Nh)$ makes the cells, and the coarse cells, cover the exponents of the primes below $z_{\mathrm{top}}$.
$2e_{N-1} < 1$ gives $2e_i < 1$ for every cell, hence $v < 1/2$ and $z_0 \le z_{\mathrm{top}}$; with $16h \le v$ it
gives $v + Nh \le 1$, which keeps the thresholds of the comparisons of every cell, the coarse ones included, in
$[1, q^{L + 1}]$; and it bounds the corner cells in Lemma~\ref{lem:drop}(3). $2U_2 < D$ gives $z_{\mathrm{top}} \le X'$. $16h \le v$ gives $y \ge 1$ in
Lemma~\ref{lem:drop} ($4h \le v$ at depths two and four, $12h \le v$ at depth six) and $h \le e_c/4$ in
Lemma~\ref{lem:kept}(3). Of the other conditions, $L U_2 > D$ gives $b_0 < L - 1$, and with $L \le 5/2$ it gives
$X'^{3/2} < N_1$ for the empty terms; $L \le 5/2$ also bounds the number of pieces in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp}.

\begin{proof}
Let $\rho = \Sigma'/\mathcal K < 1$ and $0 < \varepsilon < (1 - \rho)/4$. By the prime number theorem, for large $q$ the
primes in $(x, 2x]$ that do not divide $q$ number at least $(1 - \varepsilon) x/\log x$, and each of them is counted
by $S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})$. Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp-kept} gives, for large $q$ and every $a$ prime to
$q$,
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) \ge (1 - \varepsilon - \rho - \varepsilon) \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x}
  > \frac{1 - \rho}{2} \cdot \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x} > 0.
\]
An integer of $\mathcal A$ counted by $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ is at most $N_2$ and has no prime factor at most
$N_2^{1/2}$, so it is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with $x < p \le 2q^L$. So there is $q_0$ such that the theorem holds
for $q \ge q_0$ with $K' = 2$. Each of the finitely many pairs $(q, a \bmod q)$ with $q < q_0$ has a prime
$p \equiv a \pmod q$ by Dirichlet's theorem, and $K'$ is taken large enough to cover them.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Take $L = 278/125$, $u_1 = 41556/100000$, $u_2 = 47531/100000$ and the grid of Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate}. Its
ends are rounded inward, $278 U_2 > 125 D$, and its grid conditions are checked by \lean{decide}
(\lean{SieveCert.gridOK22240R} and \lean{SieveCert.gridOKC22240R}). Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, under
Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density}, is the Type~II estimate, and Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate} is
$\Sigma' < \mathcal K$. Theorem~\ref{thm:route} gives Theorem~\ref{thm:main} under the two inputs, and the library proves
both: Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} along the companion preprint's route, and Input~\ref{inp:density} from Chen, Gupta
and Li's Proposition~3.1. Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} enters through Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum} and the top bin in
Section~\ref{sec:type2}, through the kernel bound there as the library proves it (Pólya--Vinogradov's inequality would
do instead), through the piece of $g_7$ above $11/12$, and, in the library, through the
prime number theorem of Sections~\ref{sec:type1} to~\ref{sec:proof}, which the library derives from the case of $\zeta$
in the form $\psi(t) - t = O(t^{11/12 + \varepsilon})$ (\lean{HalfPlanePNT.psi\_isBigO\_eleven\_twelfths}).
\end{proof}

The proof does not compute $K$: the threshold $q_0$ comes from limits in $q$ in each step, and the small moduli
from Dirichlet's theorem. Unlike the classical proofs, the argument has no step on an exceptional zero, since
Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} leaves none, and no step is ineffective in the way Siegel's theorem is: every constant could
in principle be computed. But its thresholds are enormous: the inward rounding of the window to
multiples of $2^{-40}$, for one, leaves Proposition~\ref{prop:harman} a margin of order $10^{-12}$ in its exponents,
which only $\log q \gtrsim 10^{12}$ turns into a usable factor. The Lean proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:route} is \lean{SieveMain2.linnik\_route\_lt2}, whose instance arguments \lean{GridOK} and
\lean{GridOKC} are the grid conditions, as above, and the case of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is \lean{Linnik22240R.linnik\_22240r},
which has no hypotheses: the steps of Input~\ref{inp:density} and Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} are theorems of the
library.

\subsection*{The statements}

The Lean statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
theorem linnik_22240r : ∃ K : ℝ, ∀ (q : ℕ) [NeZero q] (a : ℕ),
    a.Coprime q → ∃ p : ℕ, p.Prime ∧ p ≡ a [MOD q] ∧
      (p : ℝ) ≤ K * (q : ℝ) ^ (278 / 125 : ℝ)
\end{verbatim}
}
where \lean{[NeZero q]} says $q \ge 1$ and the power is the real power. The statement of Input~\ref{inp:density} and
those of the density estimates count the characters $\chi$ mod $q$, as elements of Mathlib's \lean{DirichletCharacter ℂ q}, for
which \lean{LFunction χ s = 0} at some $s$ in the rectangle. Mathlib's $L$-function of a character mod $q$ is the
analytic continuation of $\sum_n \chi(n) n^{-s}$, with the character taken mod $q$ and not reduced to a primitive
one. So $L(s, \chi)$ is the $L$-function of the primitive character inducing $\chi$ times the Euler factors at the
primes dividing $q$, whose zeros lie on $\operatorname{Re} s = 0$ and do not affect the counts for $\sigma \ge 1/2$.
In Lean the slack of Ingham's steps is $4\varepsilon$ for the $\varepsilon$ of
\lean{LinnikInghamC.charDensity\_ingham\_c}, whose exponent is $2 + 4c + 4\varepsilon$, with $c$ chosen so that
$2 + 4c = 3/(2 - \sigma_+)$, Ingham's exponent at the step's right end $\sigma_+$: $\varepsilon = 1/8000$ on the
first nineteen steps and $\varepsilon = 1/400000$ on the last. \file{formalisations/} records, for each result from the
literature, the differences between the Lean statement and the published one.

\section{What the inputs give without the sieve}\label{sec:comparison}

This section claims nothing new. It places Theorem~\ref{thm:main} against what the same inputs give by the
classical argument, which has no sieve. The unconditional exponents of Heath-Brown and Xylouris ($5.5$, $5.18$ and
$5$) are larger, and their proofs use neither a zero-free half-plane nor a zero-density estimate beyond the
published ones; the introduction says how Theorem~\ref{thm:main} stands against them. For special moduli smaller
exponents are known: for the powers of a fixed prime, Banks and Shparlinski's $2.1115$ \cite{banks-shparlinski} (as
cited in \cite[footnote~1]{cgl}), and $1/0.4752 < 2.1044$ by R.~Li's Harman-sieve argument
\cite[Theorem~11]{li-harman}.

\subsection*{The classical deduction}

Suppose that for some $A \ge 2$ there is a uniform density estimate
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le C (qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)} (\log qT)^B \qquad (1/2 \le \sigma \le 1,\ T \ge 2).
\]
With Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} it gives Linnik's theorem with every exponent $L > A$. The library's proof,
\lean{Linnik.linnik\_of\_charDensity}, writes the smoothed sum $\sum_{n \equiv a} \Lambda(n) e^{-n/x}$ through the
characters, bounds each non-trivial character's sum by a contour shift to the edge of its largest zero-free rectangle,
at one of the levels $\theta_j = \min(1/2 + j\delta/2, 11/12)$, and counts the characters at each level by the density
estimate at the height $T + 1$, with $T = 2 \log x + 2$: the characters with zeros of real part about $\sigma$
contribute about $(q^A/x)^{1 - \sigma}$ together, and none has a zero of real part above $11/12$. With it the library
proves:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L = 12 + \varepsilon$ from Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} alone: at most $\varphi(q) \le qT$ characters are
  counted for every $\sigma$, and at most one above $11/12$, which is the estimate with $A = 12$;
\item $L = 36/13 + \varepsilon$ with Ingham's estimate, whose exponent $3/(2 - \sigma)$ is $36/13$ at
  $\sigma = 11/12$;
\item $L = 12/5 + \varepsilon$ with Huxley's estimate, which bounds the density exponent by $12/5$ on the whole
  range;
\item $L = 2.3334 + \varepsilon$ with the curve $g_7$, since $g_7(\sigma) \le 2.3334(1 - \sigma)$ on $[1/2, 1]$: on every
  step the exponent is at most $A_0 = 2.3334$, and $g_7 = 1 \le 2.3334(1 - \sigma)$ for $\sigma < 4/7$, as
  $2.3334 \cdot 3/7 > 1$.
\end{itemize}
Section~\ref{sec:lean}'s table names the Lean theorem of each. With Chen, Gupta and Li's estimate itself the exponent is
$7/3 + \varepsilon$, by the same deduction from their Theorem~1.2 \cite{cgl} (exponent $A = 7/3$), which the library
does not formalise as they state it. They do not state a Linnik exponent, but their Corollary~1.4 \cite{cgl}, on primes
in progressions in short intervals under a zero-free region, contains this one: for $h \le x$ it gives
$\psi(x + h; q, a) - \psi(x; q, a) \sim h/\varphi(q)$ whenever
$h/\varphi(q) \ge x^{1/2 + \varepsilon} q^{1/6} + x^{17/30 + \varepsilon}$, and with $h = x$ and
Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} as the zero-free region this holds at $x = q^L$ for every $L > 7/3$ and all large $q$.

\subsection*{Where the gain comes from}

The deduction needs the zeros of every real part to contribute less than the main term, so its exponent is the
largest density exponent on $[1/2, 11/12]$, which is $7/3$, at $\sigma = 5/7$. The sieve needs the zeros
only in bilinear sums with one variable in the window, where Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} trades the density exponent
against the length of the variable, and recovers the primes from those sums at the cost that the certificate bounds.
The refinement of Section~\ref{sec:certificate} lowers that cost by evaluating part of the terms of depth two that the
certificate would otherwise drop. The density curve follows the decrease of Chen, Gupta and Li's exponent below $7/3$
after $\sigma = 5/7$ step by step (Section~\ref{sec:inputs}), but the window is set at $5/7$ itself, where the exponent
is largest (the introduction gives the closed forms).

The gain does not depend on Chen, Gupta and Li's estimate. Take $g_7$ with its steps on $[5/7, 131/168]$ replaced by
steps of length $1/1680$ with the classical exponents and the slack $1/2000$ of the library's steps: Ingham's
$3/(2 - \sigma_+) + 1/2000$ up to $3/4$ and Huxley's $3/(3\sigma_- - 1) + 1/2000$ from $3/4$. The window is then set
at $3/4$, where both exponents are $12/5$, and the closed forms of the introduction become $u_1 \ge 16/(5L) - 1$ and
$u_2 \le 1 - 6/(5L)$, which meet at $L = 11/5$. The search of \file{tools/linnik/density\_payoff.py} described below
(its variant \texttt{classical}) finds the window $[41098, 47082]/100000$ admissible at $L = 4537/2000 = 2.2685$, in
exact rationals, and the certificate's total on it $0.99835\mathcal K$ on $340$ cells a side; at $2.268$ it is
$1.00648\mathcal K$. So the sieve brings Huxley's $12/5$ down to about $2.27$, as it brings $7/3$ down to $2.224$,
and with Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} as the only input that is not refereed. This is a computation with the
certificate's definitions, not a theorem of the library: the library has no steps of Ingham's or Huxley's estimate of
length $1/1680$ on $[5/7, 131/168]$ (its Ingham steps end at $5/7$, and its Huxley steps, of length $1/280$, start at
$16/21$), and the certificate on this grid has not been checked by the kernel.

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum}}\label{app:prime-sum}

\begin{proof}
Throughout, $Q \ge 4$, so that $\log Q > 1$, and $L_\chi$ stands for $L(\cdot, \chi)$, which is entire since $\chi$ is
not trivial. Put $\sigma_* = 11/12 + \delta$ and $\tau = (1 + H)^{-J}$.

\emph{The weight.} Let $f_Q(u) = f_0(u) \log Q/\log(uQ)$ where $f_0(u) \ne 0$, which needs $u \ge 1/2$ and so
$uQ \ge 2$, and $f_Q(u) = 0$ elsewhere; and let $w_y(u) = u^{-iy} f_Q(u)$. If
$f_0(n/Q) \ne 0$ then $n \ge Q/2 \ge 2$ and $f_Q(n/Q) = f_0(n/Q) \log Q/\log n$, so that
$\Lambda(n) f_Q(n/Q) = \log Q \cdot (\Lambda(n)/\log n) f_0(n/Q)$. Since $\Lambda(p)/\log p = 1$,
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w_y(n/Q) = \log Q \, \bigl(S_Q(\chi, y) + R\bigr), \\
  R = \sum_{\substack{p^k \le 2Q \\ k \ge 2}} \frac{\chi(p^k)}{k} \Bigl(\frac{p^k}{Q}\Bigr)^{-iy} f_0\Bigl(\frac{p^k}{Q}\Bigr).
\end{gather*}
Each term of $R$ is at most $C = \max |f_0|$ in absolute value, and a proper prime power $p^k \le 2Q$ has
$p \le \sqrt{2Q}$ and $k \le \log_2(2Q)$, so $|R| \le C(\sqrt{2Q} + 1)(\log_2(2Q) + 1)$. It remains to prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sw-bound}
  \Bigl|\sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w_y(n/Q)\Bigr| \le M_\delta(\theta) \, \lambda \, B \bigl(Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{\sigma_*} \tau\bigr).
\end{equation}

\emph{The Mellin transform.} Write $\mathcal M f(s) = \int_0^\infty u^{s - 1} f(u) \, du$. The substitution
$u = e^{-v}$ gives, with $c_Q = 1/\log Q \in (0, 1/\log 4]$,
\[
  \mathcal M f_Q(\sigma + it) = \int_{\mathbb R} e^{-itv} V(v) \, dv, \qquad
  V(v) = e^{-\sigma v} f_0(e^{-v}) (1 - c_Q v)^{-1}.
\]
The function $V$ vanishes for $|v| > \log 2$, and $c_Q v \le 1/2$ for $v \le \log 2$; the pole $v = \log Q \ge \log 4$ of
$(1 - c_Q v)^{-1}$ lies where $V$ vanishes, so $V$ is smooth. By Leibniz's
rule, with $|(d/dv)^i (1 - c_Q v)^{-1}| = i! \, c_Q^i |1 - c_Q v|^{-1 - i} \le i! \, 2^{i + 1}$ on $|v| \le \log 2$,
the $j$-th derivative of $V$ is bounded by a constant $V_j$ that depends only on $f_0$ and $j$, uniformly in
$0 \le \sigma \le 2$ and $Q \ge 4$. Integration by parts $j$ times gives
$|t|^j |\mathcal M f_Q(\sigma + it)| \le 2 \log 2 \cdot V_j$, and with $j = 0$ and $j = J + 3$,
\[
  (1 + |t|)^{J + 3} |\mathcal M f_Q(\sigma + it)| \le B \qquad (0 \le \sigma \le 2,\ t \in \mathbb R,\ Q \ge 4),
\]
where $B = 2^{J + 4} \log 2 \, (V_0 + V_{J + 3})$ depends only on $f_0$ and $J$. Since $f_Q$ is continuous on
$(0, \infty)$ and vanishes near $0$ and near $\infty$, $\mathcal M f_Q$ is entire. Finally
$\mathcal M w_y(s) = \mathcal M f_Q(s - iy)$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mellin-decay}
  (1 + |t - y|)^{J + 3} |\mathcal M w_y(\sigma + it)| \le B \qquad (0 \le \sigma \le 2,\ t \in \mathbb R).
\end{equation}

\emph{Perron's formula.} For $\operatorname{Re} s > 1$, $-L_\chi'/L_\chi(s) = \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) n^{-s}$.
Mellin inversion on the line $\operatorname{Re} s = 2$ at each point $n/Q$, with the exchange of sum and integral
justified by \eqref{eq:mellin-decay}, gives
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w_y(n/Q) = \frac1{2\pi} \int_{\mathbb R} F(2 + it) \, dt, \\
  F(s) = -\frac{L_\chi'}{L_\chi}(s) \, \mathcal M w_y(s) \, Q^s.
\end{gather*}

\emph{The logarithmic derivative.} For $1/2 \le \theta_0 < \theta' < 3/2$ there is $\kappa(\theta_0, \theta')$ with the
following property, for every $q$, every non-trivial $\chi$ mod $q$ and every $t \in \mathbb R$: if $L_\chi$ has no
zero in the open disc $D_t$ of centre $3/2 + it$ and radius $\rho = 3/2 - \theta_0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logderiv}
  \Bigl|\frac{L_\chi'}{L_\chi}(\sigma + it)\Bigr| \le \kappa(\theta_0, \theta') \bigl(\log(q + 2) + \log(|t| + 4)\bigr)
  \qquad (\sigma \ge \theta').
\end{equation}
For $\sigma \ge 3/2$ this holds as soon as $\kappa \ge -\zeta'/\zeta(3/2)$, by the Dirichlet series of
$L_\chi'/L_\chi$. For $\theta' \le \sigma < 3/2$ it follows from a lemma of Landau's type: if $f$ is analytic near the
closed unit disc, $f(0) = 1$, $|f| \le B_0$ on $|z| \le R$ with $B_0 > 1$, $f$ has no zero in $|z| < 1$ and
$0 < r' < r < R' < R < 1$, then
\[
  |f'/f(z)| \le \Bigl(\frac{16 r^2}{(r - r')^3} + \frac1{(R^2/R' - R') \log(R/R')}\Bigr) \log B_0 \qquad (|z| \le r').
\]
(The library uses the form of this lemma in PrimeNumberTheoremAnd \cite{pnt-plus}, \lean{FinalBound}, which combines a
Blaschke product, the Borel--Carath\'eodory inequality and Jensen's formula, and allows zeros.) Apply it to
$f(z) = L_\chi(3/2 + it + \rho z)/L_\chi(3/2 + it)$ at $z = (\sigma - 3/2)/\rho$, with $r' = (3/2 - \theta')/\rho$ and
with $r$, $R'$ and $R$ a quarter, a half and three quarters of the way from $r'$ to $1$; then
$f'/f(z) = \rho \, L_\chi'/L_\chi(\sigma + it)$. The bound $B_0$ comes from two estimates. First,
$|L_\chi(s)| \le 3(q + 1)(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 2)$ for $1/2 \le \operatorname{Re} s \le 3$, which follows from the
partial-summation bound $|L_\chi(s)| \le (q + 1)|s|/\operatorname{Re} s$ for $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$. Second,
$|L_\chi(3/2 + it)|^{-1} \le \zeta(3/2)$, from $1/L_\chi(s) = \sum \mu(n) \chi(n) n^{-s}$. Together they give
$|f| \le B_0 = 2 + 6(q + 1)\zeta(3/2)(|t| + 4)$ on $|z| \le R$, and $\log B_0 \ll \log(q + 2) + \log(|t| + 4)$. The
constant $\kappa(\theta_0, \theta')$ is explicit and of order $(\theta' - \theta_0)^{-3}$.

The disc $D_t$ lies in $\operatorname{Re} s > \theta_0$ and, since $\rho \le 1$, in $|\operatorname{Im} s - t| < 1$. So
\eqref{eq:logderiv} applies in two cases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] with $\theta_0 = 11/12$ and every $t$, by Input~\ref{inp:half-plane};
\item[(ii)] with $\theta_0 = \theta$ and every $t$ with $|t - y| \le H$, by the hypothesis of the lemma.
\end{enumerate}
Since $|t| + 4 \le (|y| + 4)(1 + |t - y|)$ and $\log(1 + u) \le u$, we have
$\log(q + 2) + \log(|t| + 4) \le \lambda(1 + |t - y|)$. With \eqref{eq:mellin-decay} this gives, in either case,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-bound}
  |F(\sigma + it)| \le \kappa(\theta_0, \theta') \, \lambda \, B \, Q^\sigma (1 + |t - y|)^{-J - 2} \qquad (\theta' \le \sigma \le 2).
\end{equation}

\emph{The contour.} By Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, $F$ is holomorphic on $\sigma_* \le \operatorname{Re} s \le 2$, and by
\eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (i), with $\theta' = \sigma_*$, it is $O((1 + |t - y|)^{-2})$ there uniformly. So Cauchy's
theorem on the rectangles $[\sigma_*, 2] \times [-Y, Y]$, with $Y \to \infty$, moves the line of integration from $2$ to
$\sigma_*$. Split the line $\operatorname{Re} s = \sigma_*$ at the heights $y \pm H$. By the hypothesis of the lemma, $F$
is holomorphic on the rectangle $\theta + \delta \le \operatorname{Re} s \le \sigma_*$, $|\operatorname{Im} s - y| \le H$,
so by Cauchy's theorem the integral over $|t - y| \le H$ on the line $\sigma_*$ equals the integral over the same
heights on the line $\theta + \delta$ plus the integrals over the two horizontal sides at the heights $y \pm H$.

\emph{The pieces.} Put $\kappa_1 = \kappa(11/12, \sigma_*)$ and $\kappa_2 = \kappa(\theta, \theta + \delta)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item On the line $\sigma_*$ with $|t - y| \ge H$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (i) and
  $(1 + u)^{-J - 2} \le (1 + H)^{-J} (1 + u^2)^{-1}$ for $u \ge H$,
  $|F| \le \kappa_1 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau/(1 + (t - y)^2)$. Each of the two tails contributes at most
  $\pi \kappa_1 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau$.
\item On the line $\theta + \delta$ with $|t - y| \le H$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (ii),
  $|F| \le \kappa_2 \lambda B Q^{\theta + \delta}/(1 + (t - y)^2)$, so this part contributes at most
  $\pi \kappa_2 \lambda B Q^{\theta + \delta}$.
\item On each horizontal side, of length $11/12 - \theta \le 1$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (ii), with
  $Q^\sigma \le Q^{\sigma_*}$, $|F| \le \kappa_2 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} (1 + H)^{-J - 2} \le \kappa_2 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau$.
\end{itemize}
Adding the pieces and dividing by $2\pi$, the sum in \eqref{eq:Sw-bound} is at most
\[
  \kappa_1 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau + \tfrac12 \kappa_2 \lambda B Q^{\theta + \delta} + \tfrac1\pi \kappa_2 \lambda B Q^{\sigma_*} \tau
  \le (\kappa_1 + \kappa_2) \, \lambda \, B \bigl(Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{\sigma_*} \tau\bigr),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:Sw-bound} with $M_\delta(\theta) = \kappa(\theta, \theta + \delta) + \kappa(11/12, 11/12 + \delta)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:family}}\label{app:family}

\begin{proof}
Let the family have the scale $P$, the set $S$, the coefficients $a_m$, the primes $p_1, \dots, p_K$ at the scales
$Q_i$, and the comparisons $C_1, \dots, C_J$ with forms $\ell_j$ and pivots $\pi(j)$. Let $\mathcal T$ be the set of the
$t = (m, p_1, \dots, p_K)$ with $m \in S$ and each $p_i$ a prime at most $2Q_i$; for $t \in \mathcal T$ put
$w(t) = a_m \prod_i f_0(p_i/Q_i)$ and $n(t) = m p_1 \cdots p_K$, and for a function $v$ on $\mathcal T$ put
\[
  \mathcal D(v) = \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} v(t) \, \Delta(n(t)), \qquad
  \Delta(n) = \mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \frac{\mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
The sum of the lemma is $\mathcal D(w \prod_j I_j)$, with $I_j(t) = \mathbf 1[C_j \text{ holds at } t]$. Let $F_0 = \max f_0$ and
$\eta_0 = 10^{-5}$.

\emph{Orthogonality.} For $a$ prime to $q$,
$\Delta(n) = \varphi(q)^{-1} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \overline\chi(a) \chi(n)$, so for every $v$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orth}
  |\mathcal D(v)| \le \frac1{\varphi(q)} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \Bigl|\sum_{t \in \mathcal T} v(t) \chi(n(t))\Bigr|.
\end{equation}

\emph{A smooth family.} Let $g_1, \dots, g_J$ be continuous and integrable, with integrable Fourier transforms
$\hat g_j(\xi) = \int g_j(u) e^{-2\pi i u \xi} \, du$, and let $T_0 \ge 1$ with
$2\pi \sum_j |e_{ji}| \, T_0 \le q^{\eta_0}$ for every $i$. We show that for $q$ larger than the $q_0$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} (with $f_0$, $K_0$, $e_1$ and $\eta_0$),
\begin{multline}\label{eq:smooth-family}
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \Bigl|\sum_{t \in \mathcal T} w(t) \prod_j g_j(\ell_j(t)) \, \chi(n(t))\Bigr|
  \le q^{L - 1/50000} \mathcal N \prod_j \|\hat g_j\|_1 \\
  + \varphi(q) \, \mathfrak B \sum_j \int_{|\xi| > T_0} |\hat g_j| \prod_{l \ne j} \|\hat g_l\|_1,
\end{multline}
where
\[
  \mathcal N = \Bigl(\frac1P \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2\Bigr)^{1/2} \le A_0, \qquad
  \mathfrak B = \sum_m |a_m| \prod_i \sum_{p \le 2Q_i} f_0(p/Q_i).
\]
By Fourier inversion, and since the phase factorises over the variables,
\begin{gather*}
  \prod_j g_j(\ell_j) = \int_{\mathbb R^J} \prod_j \hat g_j(\xi_j) \, e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \ell_j} \, d\xi, \\
  e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \ell_j(t)} = e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \lambda_j(m)} \prod_i p_i^{2\pi i H_i}, \qquad
  H_i = \sum_j \xi_j e_{ji}.
\end{gather*}
So the inner sum in \eqref{eq:smooth-family} is
$\int \prod_j \hat g_j(\xi_j) \, W_\xi(\chi) \prod_i Q_i^{2\pi i H_i} S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i) \, d\xi$, where
$W_\xi(\chi) = \sum_m a_m e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \lambda_j(m)} \chi(m)$ and $h_i = -2\pi H_i$. Take absolute values,
sum over $\chi$, and bound the integrand for each $\xi$. If $|\xi_j| \le T_0$ for every $j$, then $|h_i| \le q^{\eta_0}$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, with the coefficients $a_m e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \lambda_j(m)}$, which have the moduli
$|a_m|$, and the heights $h_i$, gives
$\sum_\chi |W_\xi(\chi)| \prod_i |S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i)| \le q^{L - 1/50000} \mathcal N$. Otherwise some $|\xi_j| > T_0$, and the
trivial bound $\varphi(q) \mathfrak B$ holds. Integrating gives \eqref{eq:smooth-family}.

\emph{The cut-off.} Let $g$ be smooth, with $g = 0$ on $(-\infty, 0]$, $g = 1$ on $[1, \infty)$ and $0 \le g \le 1$, and
let $c_k = \int |g^{(k)}|$. For $0 < \nu \le 2\Lambda$ put $G_{\nu, \Lambda}(u) = g(u/\nu) - g((u - 2\Lambda)/\nu)$.
It is smooth, $0 \le G_{\nu, \Lambda} \le 1$, it vanishes outside $[0, 2\Lambda + \nu]$, and
$G_{\nu, \Lambda}(u) = g(u/\nu)$ for $u \le 2\Lambda$. So if $u \le \Lambda$ and a condition holds when $u > 0$ and
fails when $u < 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
  \bigl|\mathbf 1[\text{condition}] - G_{\nu, \Lambda}(u)\bigr| \le G_{\nu, \nu}(u + \nu),
\end{equation}
since the left side vanishes unless $0 \le u < \nu$, where $G_{\nu, \nu}(u + \nu) = 1$. Integration by parts
gives $(2\pi |\xi|)^k |\hat G_{\nu, \Lambda}(\xi)| \le \|G_{\nu, \Lambda}^{(k)}\|_1 \le 2\nu^{1 - k} c_k$, and
$|\hat G_{\nu, \Lambda}| \le 2\Lambda + \nu$. Hence
\begin{gather*}
  \|\hat G_{\nu, \Lambda}\|_1 \le \pi(2\Lambda + \nu) + \frac{c_2}{2\pi\nu}, \\
  \int_{|\xi| > T_0} |\hat G_{\nu, \Lambda}| \le \frac{4\pi c_k}{(2\pi)^k} \, \nu^{1 - k} T_0^{2 - k}
  \qquad (k \ge 2,\ T_0 \ge 1).
\end{gather*}

\emph{Sharp against smooth.} Let $\Lambda = \Lambda_0 \log q + 1$, so that $\ell_j \le \Lambda$ on $\mathcal T$, and put
$G_j = G_{\nu, \Lambda}(\ell_j)$ and $M_j = G_{\nu, \nu}(\ell_j + \nu)$. All these values lie in $[0, 1]$, so
by \eqref{eq:cutoff} $|\prod_j I_j - \prod_j G_j| \le \sum_j |I_j - G_j| \le \sum_j M_j$. Since
$|\Delta(n)| \le \Delta(n) + 2 \cdot \mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]/\varphi(q)$ and $f_0 \ge 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-smooth}
  \Bigl|\mathcal D\Bigl(w \prod_j I_j\Bigr)\Bigr| \le \Bigl|\mathcal D\Bigl(w \prod_j G_j\Bigr)\Bigr|
  + \sum_j \Bigl(|\mathcal D(|w| M_j)| + \frac2{\varphi(q)} \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} |w(t)| M_j(t)\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The first term and each $\mathcal D(|w| M_j)$ are smooth families: the first with $g_j = G_{\nu, \Lambda}$, the second with
the coefficients $|a_m|$ and the single function $G_{\nu, \nu}$ at the form $\ell_j + \nu$. Both are bounded by
\eqref{eq:orth} and \eqref{eq:smooth-family}. A trivial count would not do for them, since it would lose the factor $1/\varphi(q)$ that the condition
$n \equiv a$ gives. The last sum, which carries that factor already, is counted trivially, by the pivot. Fix $m$ and the primes
other than $p_{\pi(j)}$; then $\ell_j = c + s \log p_{\pi(j)}$ with $s = \pm 1$, and $M_j \ne 0$ forces
$-\nu \le \ell_j \le 2\nu$, which confines $p_{\pi(j)}$ to an interval $[Y, e^{3\nu} Y]$. At most
$(e^{3\nu} - 1) 2Q_{\pi(j)} + 1$ integers up to $2Q_{\pi(j)}$ lie in it, so
\[
  \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} |w(t)| M_j(t)
  \le F_0 \bigl((e^{3\nu} - 1) 2Q_{\pi(j)} + 1\bigr) \sum_m |a_m| \prod_{i \ne \pi(j)} \sum_{p \le 2Q_i} f_0(p/Q_i).
\]

\emph{The parameters.} Put $\varepsilon_2 = 1/(400000(J_0 + 1))$, $\nu = q^{-\varepsilon_2}$, $T_0 = q^{1/200000}$,
$k = 1200003$ and $\delta = \min(\varepsilon_2, e_1)/2$. For large $q$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $2\pi J_0 E_0 T_0 \le q^{\eta_0}$, so \eqref{eq:smooth-family} applies, as $\sum_j |e_{ji}| \le J_0 E_0$;
\item $\pi(2\Lambda + 1) \le q^{\varepsilon_2}$, so $\|\hat G_{\nu, \Lambda}\|_1$ and $\|\hat G_{\nu, \nu}\|_1$
  are at most $C q^{\varepsilon_2}$ with $C = 1 + c_2/(2\pi)$, and a product of at most $J_0$ of them is at most
  $C^{J_0 + 1} q^{1/400000}$;
\item $\nu^{1 - k} T_0^{2 - k} = q^{\varepsilon_2(k - 1) - (k - 2)/200000} \le q^{-3}$, which bounds the tails.
\end{itemize}
The constants come from $\eta_0 = 10^{-5}$: the norms lose at most $q^{\eta_0/4}$, $T_0 = q^{\eta_0/2}$ leaves room
for the factor $2\pi J_0 E_0$ below $q^{\eta_0}$, and $k = 1200003$ is the least $k$ with
$(k - 1)\eta_0/4 - (k - 2)\eta_0/2 \le -3$, so that the tails are at most $q^{-3}$ whatever $J_0$.
Moreover $\mathfrak B \le P A_0 (3F_0)^K \prod_i Q_i \le A_0 (3F_0 + 1)^{K_0} 2^{K_0} q^L$, since at most $3Q$ integers
lie in $[1, 2Q]$ for $Q \ge 1$ and $P \prod_i Q_i \le 2^{K_0} x$. Finally $e^{3\nu} - 1 \le 6\nu$ for
$\nu \le 1/3$ and $Q_{\pi(j)} \ge q^{e_1}$ give
$(12\nu Q_{\pi(j)} + 1) \prod_{i \ne \pi(j)} Q_i \le (12\nu + q^{-e_1}) \prod_i Q_i$. Putting these into
\eqref{eq:sharp-smooth}, with \eqref{eq:orth} and \eqref{eq:smooth-family}, and using $\varphi(q) \le q$, the sum of the
lemma is at most
\[
  \frac{A_0 q^L}{\varphi(q)} \bigl(a_1 q^{-7/400000} + a_2 q^{-1} + a_3(12 q^{-\varepsilon_2} + q^{-e_1})\bigr),
\]
where $a_1$, $a_2$ and $a_3$ depend only on $K_0$, $J_0$ and $F_0$. The three terms come from the smooth families, from
the tails and from the pivot counts. Each power of $q$ in the bracket is at most $q^{-2\delta}$, so for large $q$ the sum
is at most $A_0 q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$. Here $\delta$ depends only on $J_0$ and $e_1$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}
was used once for the smoothed family and once for each near-boundary family.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}}\label{app:harman}

\begin{proof}
Write $\mathcal P[y, z)$ for the set of primes in $[y, z)$, and say that $n$ is \emph{$z$-rough} if it has no prime
factor below $z$. For $\mathcal C = \mathcal A$ or $\mathcal B$ and $d$ whose prime factors are all at least $z$,
$S(\mathcal C_d, z)$ is the number of $z$-rough multiples of $d$ in $\mathcal C$. Throughout, $x = q^L$, and the
integers of $(x, 2x]$ are those of $(N_1, N_2]$ with $N_1 = \lfloor x \rfloor$ and $N_2 = \lfloor 2x \rfloor$. The tuples $(r_1, \dots, r_j)$ are ordered, and $r = r_1 \cdots r_j$.

\emph{The constants.} They are chosen before $q$ and depend only on $L$, $u_1$, $u_2$, $j$, $J_0$, $\Lambda_1$, $a_0$,
$b_0$ and $\varepsilon_H$. Let $c = (L - 1 - b_0)/4 > 0$. Let $C_0 \ge 1$ be an absolute constant with
$\sum_{p \in \mathcal P[y, y^2)} 1/p \le C_0$ for every $y \ge 1$; it exists by Mertens' theorem (the library
takes it from the prime number theorem). Put
$K_1 = (\log((L + 1)/a_0) + 1)^j$ and
\[
  \zeta_k = e^{2C_0} \cdot \frac{2 C_0^k}{k!} \cdot \exp\Bigl(e^{2C_0} \frac{2 C_0^{k + 1}}{(k + 1)!}\Bigr).
\]
Since $(2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k \to 0$, there is an even $k \ge 2$ with $2C_0^2 \le (k + 1)(k + 2)$, $c < (2k + 4) a_0$
and $(2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k \le \varepsilon_H c^2/(4e(K_1 + 1) a_0)$. Put $\varepsilon_w = c/(2k + 4)$, so
that $0 < \varepsilon_w < a_0$ and $(2k + 7) \varepsilon_w \le 2c$, and $S_0 = \lceil L/\varepsilon_w \rceil$. At $q$,
put $w = \lceil q^{\varepsilon_w} \rceil$, $z_0 = \lceil q^{a_0} \rceil$ and $X' = \lfloor q^{b_0} \rfloor$. Then
$w \le z_0$, and by hypothesis every tuple in the sum has $r_i \ge z_0$ and $r \le X'$.

\emph{The tree identity.} Let $r$ have all its prime factors at least $z_0$, and let $r \le X'$. For a set $P$ of primes
write $d_P = r \prod_{p \in P} p$, and let $\rho_z(n) = 1$ if $n$ is $z$-rough and $\rho_z(n) = 0$ otherwise. Then for
every $n$,
\begin{multline}\label{eq:tree}
  \mathbf 1[r \mid n] \, \rho_{z_0}(n)
  = \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P[w, z_0)} (-1)^{|P|} \, \mathbf 1[d_P \le X'] \, \mathbf 1[d_P \mid n] \, \rho_w(n) \\
  + \sum_{p \in \mathcal P[w, z_0)} \ \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P(p, z_0)} (-1)^{|P| + 1} \,
    \mathbf 1[d_P \le X' < p \, d_P] \, \mathbf 1[p \, d_P \mid n] \, \rho_p(n),
\end{multline}
where $\mathcal P(p, z_0)$ is the set of primes in $(p, z_0)$. This is what iterating Buchstab's identity
$S(\mathcal C_m, z) = S(\mathcal C_m, w) - \sum_{w \le p < z} S(\mathcal C_{mp}, p)$ from the root $r$ at $z_0$ gives,
when a child $mp$ is stopped as soon as $mp > X'$: the first sum collects the \emph{leaves} $S(\mathcal C_d, w)$,
$d = d_P \le X'$, and the second the \emph{stops} $S(\mathcal C_W, p)$, $W = p \, d_P$. It is proved directly,
for each $n$. If $r \nmid n$ or $n$ is not $w$-rough, every term vanishes. Otherwise let $Q$ be the set of the primes of
$[w, z_0)$ that divide $n$; the left side is $\mathbf 1[Q = \emptyset]$. Since the primes of $r$ are at least $z_0$, a
leaf $P$ counts $n$ exactly when $P \subseteq Q$, and a stop $(p, P)$ exactly when $p = \min Q$ and
$P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p\}$. If $Q = \emptyset$, only the leaf $P = \emptyset$ counts $n$, with
$\mathbf 1[r \le X'] = 1$. If $Q \ne \emptyset$, let $p_0 = \min Q$; grouping $P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p_0\}$ with
$P \cup \{p_0\}$, and writing $R = d_P$, the right side is
\[
  \sum_{P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p_0\}} (-1)^{|P|}
  \bigl(\mathbf 1[R \le X'] - \mathbf 1[p_0 R \le X'] - \mathbf 1[R \le X' < p_0 R]\bigr) = 0.
\]
Multiplying \eqref{eq:tree} by $\mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]/\varphi(q)$ and summing over $n$ and over
the tuples, the sum of the proposition is the sum over the leaves of
$(-1)^{|P|}(S(\mathcal A_d, w) - S(\mathcal B_d, w)/\varphi(q))$ plus the sum over the stops of
$(-1)^{|P| + 1}(S(\mathcal A_W, p) - S(\mathcal B_W, p)/\varphi(q))$.

\emph{The stops.} A stop has $X' < W \le p X' < z_0 X'$, and $W$ is $p$-rough, since the primes of $r$ are at least
$z_0 > p$ and those of $P$ exceed $p$. So the $p$-rough multiples of $W$ in $(N_1, N_2]$ are the $n = Wm$ whose cofactor
$m$ is $p$-rough, and every such $m$ lies in $(P_1, P_2]$ with $P_1 = \lfloor N_1/(z_0 X') \rfloor$ and
$P_2 = \lfloor N_2/(X' + 1) \rfloor$. For large $q$, $q^{u_1 L} \le P_1$, since $5q^{u_1 L + a_0 + b_0} \le q^L$ by
$a_0 + b_0 < (1 - u_1)L$, and $P_2 \le q^{u_2 L}$, since $2q^{L - b_0} \le q^{u_2 L}$ by $(1 - u_2)L < b_0$. So the
cofactor $m$ lies in the window's range, and $W$ in its reflection. A stop has $|P| \le S_0$, since
$\prod P \ge w^{|P|} \ge q^{\varepsilon_w |P|}$ and $\prod P \le X' \le q^L$.

For each $s \le S_0$, the stops with $|P| = s$ form one sum of the kind that Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp} bounds, with
$m \in (P_1, P_2]$ and $a_m = 1$, whose primes are $r_1, \dots, r_j$, the elements $y_1 > \dots > y_s$ of $P$ and
$p$, and whose comparisons are the given ones on the $r_i$ and
\begin{gather*}
  y_1 > \dots > y_s > p, \quad y_1 < z_0, \quad p \ge w, \quad r y_1 \cdots y_s \le X' < r y_1 \cdots y_s \, p, \\
  p \le \min\{\ell \text{ prime} : \ell \mid m\}, \qquad N_1 < m r y_1 \cdots y_s p \le N_2 .
\end{gather*}
For $s = 0$ the condition $y_1 < z_0$ reads $p < z_0$. Each condition is of the form
$\lambda(m) + \sum_i e_i \log x_i > 0$ or $\ge 0$ with a pivot: the new ones have coefficients in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$,
and the given ones coefficients at most $1$ in absolute value. There are at most $J_0 + s + 7$ of them, and
$\lambda(m) \le (\Lambda_1 + L + 1) \log q$ for $m \le P_2$. Wherever they
all hold, every prime is at least $w \ge 2q^{\varepsilon_w/2}$ and $m r y_1 \cdots y_s p \le 2x$. So
Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp}, with $K_0 = j + S_0 + 2$, $J_0 + S_0 + 7$ comparisons, $e_1 = \varepsilon_w/2$, $E_0 = 1$
and $\Lambda_1 + L + 1$ in place of $\Lambda_1$, bounds each of these $S_0 + 1$ sums by $q^{L - \delta_T}/\varphi(q)$,
with $\delta_T > 0$ depending only on the constants above. For large $q$ the stops therefore total at most
$q^{L - \delta_1}/\varphi(q)$, with $\delta_1 = \delta_T/2$.

\emph{A leaf.} Let $d$ have all its prime factors at least $w$. We show
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaf}
  \Bigl|S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr|
  \le \frac{2 \zeta_k (N_2 - N_1)}{d \varphi(q) \log w} + 24 \, w^{2k + 7}.
\end{equation}
If $(d, q) > 1$ both counts vanish, so let $(d, q) = 1$. Sieve by the primes below $w$, in the ranges
$T_i = \mathcal P[w^{2^{-i - 1}}, w^{2^{-i}})$ for $0 \le i < J = 2 \lfloor \log_2 w \rfloor + 2$, which cover them, at the
even levels $k_i = k + 2i$, which grow with $i$ so that the errors $C_0^{k_i}/k_i!$ below have a sum that does not
depend on $J$. Brun's sieve \cite{brun}, in the following form, is used, for disjoint sets $T_i$ of
primes and even levels $k_i \ge 2$. Let $\mathcal C$ be a finite set of integers,
$X \ge 0$, and $g$ a function on the primes of $\bigcup T_i$ with $0 \le g \le 1$. Suppose that for every choice of
$s_i \subseteq T_i$, with $D = \prod_i \prod_{p \in s_i} p$, the count $\#\{n \in \mathcal C : D \mid n\}$ is
$X \prod_i \prod_{p \in s_i} g(p)$ within $R$. Then $\#\{n \in \mathcal C : (n, \prod_i \prod_{T_i} p) = 1\}$ is
$X \prod_i \prod_{p \in T_i} (1 - g(p))$ within
\[
  X \sum_i e_{k_i}(T_i) \prod_{l \ne i} F_{k_l}(T_l)
  + (J + 1) \, R \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m},
\]
where $e_m(T) = \sum_{s \subseteq T,\ |s| = m} \prod_{p \in s} g(p)$ and $F_k(T) = \sum_{m \le k} (-1)^m e_m(T)$.
Bonferroni's inequalities say that $F_{k - 1}(T) \le \prod_{p \in T}(1 - g(p)) \le F_k(T)$ for every even $k$, and the
same for any weights in $[0, 1]$ in place of $g$. Take them for each $n \in \mathcal C$ with the weights
$\mathbf 1[p \mid n]$, and use the elementary inequality
$\prod_i \mathrm{hi}_i - \sum_i (\mathrm{hi}_i - \mathrm{lo}_i) \prod_{l \ne i} \mathrm{hi}_l \le \prod_i \rho_i$ for
$\rho_i \ge 0$ and $\mathrm{lo}_i \le \rho_i \le \mathrm{hi}_i$ (the $\mathrm{lo}_i$, odd truncations, may be negative).
Summed over $n$, and expanded by the hypothesis, they place the count, up to $(J + 1)$ times
$R \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m}$, in the interval from $X \prod_i F_{k_i} - X \sum_i e_{k_i} \prod_{l \ne i}
F_{k_l}$ to $X \prod_i F_{k_i}$. The model $X \prod_i V_i$, with $V_i = \prod_{p \in T_i}(1 - g(p))$, lies in the same
interval: Bonferroni's inequalities for $g$ give $F_{k_i} - e_{k_i} = F_{k_i - 1} \le V_i \le F_{k_i}$, hence
$0 \le \prod_i F_{k_i} - \prod_i V_i \le \sum_i e_{k_i} \prod_{l \ne i} F_{k_l}$.

Take $g(p) = 1/p$ for $p \nmid q$ and $g(p) = 0$ for $p \mid q$. For $\mathcal A_d$, take
$\mathcal C = \{n \in (N_1, N_2] : n \equiv a,\ d \mid n\}$, $X = (N_2 - N_1)/(dq)$ and $R = 1$: if no prime of $D$
divides $q$, Lemma~\ref{lem:type1} with $M = dD$ gives the count within $1$, and otherwise the count and the model both
vanish. For $\mathcal B_d$, take $\mathcal C = \{n \in (N_1, N_2] : (n, q) = 1,\ d \mid n\}$, $X\varphi(q)$ and
$R = \varphi(q)$, and divide by $\varphi(q)$. Both main terms are then $XV$ with $V = \prod_{p < w,\ p \nmid q}(1 - 1/p)$,
so that
\begin{gather*}
  \Bigl|S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr| \le 2(XG + \mathrm{rem}), \\
  G = \sum_i e_{k_i}(T_i) \prod_{l \ne i} F_{k_l}(T_l), \qquad
  \mathrm{rem} = (J + 1) \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m}.
\end{gather*}
Each $T_i$ lies in some $\mathcal P[y, y^2)$, so $\sum_{p \in T_i} g(p) \le C_0$ and, since $1 - t \ge e^{-2t}$ for
$0 \le t \le 1/2$, $V_i \ge e^{-2C_0}$. With $e_m \le C_0^m/m!$,
$F_{k_l} \le V_l + e_{k_l + 1} \le V_l (1 + e^{2C_0} C_0^{k_l + 1}/(k_l + 1)!)$ and
$\prod_{l \ne i} V_l \le e^{2C_0} V$, this gives
$G \le V e^{2C_0} \sum_i \frac{C_0^{k_i}}{k_i!} \prod_l \bigl(1 + e^{2C_0} \frac{C_0^{k_l + 1}}{(k_l + 1)!}\bigr)$. Since
$2C_0^2 \le (k + 1)(k + 2)$, the terms of $\sum_i C_0^{k + 2i}/(k + 2i)!$ decrease at least geometrically with ratio
$1/2$, so the sum is at most $2C_0^k/k!$, and with $\prod (1 + t_l) \le \exp \sum t_l$ we get $G \le \zeta_k V$. So
the difference is at most $2\zeta_k XV + 2\,\mathrm{rem}$. Next, $V \le q/(\varphi(q) \log w)$. Indeed
$V \prod_{p < w,\ p \mid q}(1 - 1/p) = \prod_{p < w}(1 - 1/p)$, the second product on the left is at least
$\prod_{p \mid q}(1 - 1/p) = \varphi(q)/q$, and $\prod_{p < w}(1 - 1/p) \le 1/\log w$, since
$\prod_{p < w}(1 - 1/p)^{-1}$ is the sum of $1/m$ over the integers $m \ge 1$ whose prime factors are all below $w$,
which is at least $\sum_{m < w} 1/m \ge \log w$. So $XV \le (N_2 - N_1)/(d \varphi(q) \log w)$. Finally
$\sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m} \le 2 \max(1, |T_i|)^{k_i}$ with $|T_i| \le w^{2^{-i}}$,
$\sum_i 2^{-i}(k + 2i) \le 2k + 4$, $J + 1 \le 3w$ and $2^J \le 4w^2$ give $\mathrm{rem} \le 12 w^{2k + 7}$.

\emph{The leaves together.} In \eqref{eq:leaf}, $N_2 - N_1 \le x + 1 \le 2x$ and $\log w \ge \varepsilon_w \log q$.
With $1 \le X'/d$ for the remainder, the leaves total at most
\[
  \Bigl(\frac{4 \zeta_k x}{\varphi(q) \varepsilon_w \log q} + 24 \, w^{2k + 7} X'\Bigr)
  \sum_{(r_1, \dots, r_j)} \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P[w, z_0)} \frac1{r \prod P},
\]
and the double sum is $\sum 1/r \cdot \prod_{w \le p < z_0}(1 + 1/p)$. Each $r_i$ is a prime in $[q^{a_0}, 2q^L]$, so
for large $q$, by Mertens' theorem (in the library, the prime number theorem), $\sum 1/r \le K_1$ and $\prod_{w \le p < z_0}(1 + 1/p) \le e a_0/\varepsilon_w$.
The main part is then at most
\[
  \frac{4 e K_1 a_0 \zeta_k}{\varepsilon_w^2} \cdot \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log q}
  = \frac{4 e K_1 a_0 (2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k}{c^2} \cdot \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log q}
  \le \frac{\varepsilon_H \, x}{\varphi(q) \log q},
\]
by the choice of $k$. Since $w \le 2q^{\varepsilon_w}$ and $(2k + 7)\varepsilon_w \le 2c$, the remainder part is at most
$24 \cdot 2^{2k + 7} K_1 e a_0 \varepsilon_w^{-1} q^{b_0 + 2c} \le q^{b_0 + 3c} = q^{L - 1 - c} \le q^{L - c}/\varphi(q)$
for large $q$, using $\varphi(q) \le q$.

\emph{Conclusion.} The sum of the proposition is at most
$\varepsilon_H x/(\varphi(q) \log q) + (q^{L - c} + q^{L - \delta_1})/\varphi(q)$, which for large $q$ is at most
$\varepsilon_H x/(\varphi(q) \log q) + q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ with $\delta = \min(c, \delta_1)/2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rough}}\label{app:rough}

\begin{proof}
Write $c_* = 0.565073$ and $\bar\omega = 0.5671433$, $u = \log 2y/\log z$, and say that an integer is $z$-rough if it has no
prime factor below $z$. Four facts are used.

\emph{(a) Prime sums.} By the prime number theorem with an error $o(t/\log t)$ (in the library, the bound
$\psi(t) - t = O(t^{11/12 + \varepsilon})$, which it derives from the case of $\zeta$ of Input~\ref{inp:half-plane};
the classical theorem would do), for every $\varepsilon_3 > 0$,
$|\theta(t) - t| \le \varepsilon_3 t/\log t$ for large $t$. With partial summation this gives, for large $a \le b$,
\[
  \sum_{a < p \le b} \frac1{p \log p} \le \frac1{\log a} - \frac1{\log b} + \frac{4\varepsilon_3}{\log^2 a},
\]
and, if $2 \log b \le \Lambda$,
\[
  \sum_{a < p \le b} \frac1{p(\Lambda - \log p)}
  \le \frac{1 + \varepsilon_3/\log a}{\Lambda} \log \frac{\log b \, (\Lambda - \log a)}{\log a \, (\Lambda - \log b)}
  + \frac{6\varepsilon_3}{\Lambda \log^2 a};
\]
and, for large $y$, there are at most $(1 + \varepsilon_3) y/\log 2y$ primes in $(y, 2y]$.

\emph{(b) Buchstab's inequality.} For $y \ge 2$,
\[
  \Phi(y, z) \le \#\{p : y < p \le 2y\} + \sum_{z \le p \le \sqrt{2y}} \Phi(y/p, p),
\]
since a $z$-rough integer $m \in (y, 2y]$ that is not prime has its least prime factor $p$ in $[z, \sqrt{2y}]$, and
$m/p$ is a $p$-rough integer in $(y/p, 2y/p]$; the map $m \mapsto (p, m/p)$ is injective. Also $\Phi(y, z) = 0$ if
$y \ge 1$ and $2y < z$, since every integer of $(y, 2y]$ is then at least $2$ and below $z$.

\emph{(c) Buchstab's function on $[2, 3]$.} For $u > 1$, $1 + \log(u - 1) \le \bar\omega u$: the inequality $\log t \le t - 1$ at
$t = \bar\omega(u - 1)$ gives $1 + \log(u - 1) \le \bar\omega(u - 1) - \log \bar\omega \le \bar\omega u$, since $-\log \bar\omega \le \bar\omega$, that is $\bar\omega e^{\bar\omega} \ge 1$, which
holds because $e^{\bar\omega} \ge \sum_{m \le 12} \bar\omega^m/m! > 1/\bar\omega$. So $\omega^* \le \bar\omega$ on $[2, 3]$, and since $c_* < \bar\omega$,
$\omega^* \le \bar\omega$ on $[2, \infty)$; and $\omega^* \le c_*$ on $[3, \infty)$, since $\omega^*(3) = (1 + \log 2)/3 < c_*$.
On $[2, 3]$, $\omega^*(u) = (1 + \log(u - 1))/u$ increases up to $2.76$ and decreases from $2.77$ on. For
$2 \le u \le u' \le 2.76$, $\omega^*(u) \le \omega^*(u')$ is $(u' - u)(1 + \log(u - 1)) \le u \log((u' - 1)/(u - 1))$:
the right side is at least $u(u' - u)/(u' - 1)$, by $\log t \ge 1 - 1/t$, and the left at most $(u' - u)u/1.76$, by
$\log t \le t - 1$ at $t = (u - 1)/1.76$ and $\log 1.76 \le 1/1.76$. For $2.77 \le u \le u'$ the reverse inequality
follows in the same way from $\log((u' - 1)/(u - 1)) \le (u' - u)/(u - 1)$ and
$\log(u - 1) \ge \log 1.77 \ge 1/1.77 \ge 1/(u - 1)$. The two constants come from the exponential's series. (This
proof does not use the monotonicity; the bound $\Omega$ of Section~\ref{sec:certificate} does.)

\emph{(d) A delay inequality.} Let $M(u) = \log(u - 1)$ for $2 \le u \le 3$ and
$M(u) = \log 2 + \bar\omega \min(u - 3, 1) + c_* \max(u - 4, 0)$ for $u > 3$, which is $\int_1^{u - 1} \omega^*(t)\, dt$ with
$\omega^*$ replaced by $\bar\omega$ on $[2, 3]$. Then $1 + M(u) \le u \omega^*(u)$ for $u \ge 2$. For $u \le 3$ it is an
equality. For $u > 3$, $\omega^*(u) = c_*$, and $1 + M(u) - c_* u$ is affine on $[3, 4]$, with slope $\bar\omega - c_* > 0$, and
constant on $[4, \infty)$; so it suffices that it is at most $0$ at $u = 4$, that is $1 + \log 2 + \bar\omega \le 4c_*$, which
holds since $\log 2 < 0.6931471808$ and $2.2602904808 < 2.260292 = 4c_*$.

We prove by induction on the integer $k \ge 0$ the statement $\mathrm C(k)$: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is
$z_0$ such that $\Phi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$ for all $z \ge z_0$ and all $y \ge 1$ with
$u \le k$. $\mathrm C(0)$ holds trivially, since $u > 0$, and the lemma is $\mathrm C(\lceil U \rceil)$. Assume
$\mathrm C(k)$, let $0 < \varepsilon \le 1$ (which suffices, since the bound for one $\varepsilon$ implies it for every
larger one) and $\varepsilon_3 = \varepsilon/100$, let $z$ be large in terms of
$\varepsilon$ and of the $z_0$ of $\mathrm C(k)$ for $\varepsilon/4$, and let $y \ge 1$ with
$\Lambda = \log 2y = u \log z \le (k + 1) \log z$. If $2y < z$, then $\Phi(y, z) = 0$. Otherwise $u \ge 1$, $y \ge z/2$
is large, and (a) and (b) give
\[
  \Phi(y, z) \le \Bigl(1 + \frac\varepsilon4\Bigr) \frac y\Lambda + \sum_{z \le p \le \sqrt{2y}} \Phi(y/p, p).
\]
If $u < 2$, then $\sqrt{2y} < z$, the sum is empty, and
$\Phi(y, z) \le (1 + \varepsilon/4) y/(u \log z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$. Let $u \ge 2$. A prime
$p \in [z, \sqrt{2y}]$ has $y/p \ge 1$ and
$\log(2y/p) = \Lambda - \log p \le k \log p$, since $\Lambda \le (k + 1) \log p$. So $\mathrm C(k)$ gives
$\Phi(y/p, p) \le (\omega^*(u_p) + \varepsilon/4) \, y/(p \log p)$ with $u_p = (\Lambda - \log p)/\log p$. The term
$p = z$, if $z$ is prime, contributes at most $2y/(z \log z)$, since $\omega^* \le 1$. By (a), the parts
$\varepsilon/4$ of the other terms contribute at most $(\varepsilon/4)(1/\log z + 4\varepsilon_3/\log^2 z) \, y$. For
the parts $\omega^*(u_p)$: if $p \le (2y)^{1/4}$ then $u_p \ge 3$ and $\omega^*(u_p) \le c_*$; if
$(2y)^{1/4} < p \le (2y)^{1/3}$ then $2 \le u_p < 3$ and $\omega^*(u_p) \le \bar\omega$; and if $(2y)^{1/3} < p \le (2y)^{1/2}$ then
$1 \le u_p < 2$ and $\omega^*(u_p)/(p \log p) = 1/(p(\Lambda - \log p))$, by the definition of $\omega^*$ on
$[1, 2)$. By (a), applied on $(z, \sqrt{2y}]$ if
$u \le 3$, on $(z, (2y)^{1/3}]$ and $((2y)^{1/3}, \sqrt{2y}]$ if $3 < u \le 4$, and also with the first range split at
$(2y)^{1/4}$ if $u > 4$,
\[
  \sum_{z < p \le \sqrt{2y}} \frac{\omega^*(u_p)}{p \log p} \le \frac{M(u) + 12\varepsilon_3}\Lambda
  + \frac{8\varepsilon_3}{\log z}.
\]
Collecting the terms, with $\Lambda = u \log z$,
\[
  \Phi(y, z) \le \frac y{\log z} \Bigl(\frac{1 + M(u)}u + \frac{\varepsilon/4 + 12\varepsilon_3}u + \frac2z
  + 8\varepsilon_3 + \frac\varepsilon4 + \frac{\varepsilon \varepsilon_3}{\log z}\Bigr).
\]
By (d) the first term is at most $\omega^*(u)$, and since $u \ge 1$ and $z$ is large the others add up to at most
$0.96\varepsilon$. So $\Phi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$, which proves $\mathrm C(k + 1)$.
\end{proof}
