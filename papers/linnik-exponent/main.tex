\title{Linnik's constant below the zero-density exponent: $43/20$ from a zero-free half-plane}
\author{Ritchie Comley}
\date{Draft, 12 November 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove that there is a constant $K$ such that every reduced residue class modulo $q$ contains a prime $p \le K
q^{43/20}$. The proof uses one recent result that has not been refereed, OpenAI's zero-free half-plane
$\operatorname{Re} s > 11/12$ for Dirichlet $L$-functions, and Huxley's classical zero-density estimate. From these the
classical argument gives every exponent above $12/5$, and with Chen, Gupta and Li's recent density estimate every
exponent above $7/3$. We go below both with Harman's sieve, fed by a Type~II estimate in two variables that sorts the
characters by the real parts of the zeros of their $L$-functions. In each class of characters, Cauchy--Schwarz is
taken between the two Dirichlet polynomials, and each mean square is bounded by orthogonality or by the
Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality in mean square, so that the number of characters in a class matters only where
Huxley's estimate makes it small; the estimate then holds on an explicit region of the two lengths, for every exponent.
Harman's comparison in two variables, a reversal of the sieve's terms through their largest prime and a bilinear
Type~I estimate proved by Parseval's identity bound the primes the sieve loses by $0.9485$ of them, by a search that
Lean's kernel follows step by step. The whole proof is formalised in Lean~4 with Mathlib, including the half-plane and
Huxley's estimate, and depends only on Lean's standard axioms. The exponent is to be compared with $12/5$, not with
Xylouris's $5$, whose proof uses refereed results only.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is $K$ such that for every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$ there is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with
$p \le K q^{43/20}$.
\end{theorem}

Linnik \cite{linnik44, linnik44b} proved that there are absolute constants $K$ and $L$ such that every reduced residue
class mod $q$ contains a prime $p \le K q^L$ (see \cite[Chapter~18]{iwaniec-kowalski} for an account). The admissible
exponent $L$, Linnik's constant, has been lowered many times: Heath-Brown \cite{heath-brown92} reached $L = 5.5$, and
Xylouris $L = 5.18$ \cite{xylouris-acta} and then $L = 5$ \cite{xylouris11}, and again $L = 5$ in a short version, which
notes that a slightly smaller value follows \cite{xylouris18}. Under the generalised Riemann hypothesis every $L > 2$ is
admissible \cite{lls, lls-corr}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has $L = 43/20 = 2.15$, but it is not on the same footing as
these exponents, whose proofs use refereed results only: its proof uses a recent result that has not been refereed
(Section~\ref{sec:inputs}), and with it and Huxley's density estimate the classical argument already gives every $L >
12/5$. What this paper adds is measured from $12/5$.

\subsection*{The zeros near $\operatorname{Re} s = 1$}

The difficulty in Linnik's theorem is the zeros of the $L$-functions mod $q$ near the line $\operatorname{Re} s = 1$.
By the explicit formula, a zero $\rho = \beta + i\gamma$ of $L(s, \chi)$ contributes about $x^{\beta - 1}$, relative to
the main term, to the count of primes $p \le x$ in a class mod $q$. A zero-density estimate with exponent $A$, a
bound of about $(qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)}$ for the number of zeros with real part at least $\sigma$, makes the zeros of
real part about $\sigma$ contribute about $(q^A/x)^{1 - \sigma}$ together. Classically the zero-free region allows
$1 - \beta$ as small as $c/\log q$, and one exceptional real zero may be closer still; at $x = q^L$ such a zero
contributes a constant, not a small quantity. The record proofs control these zeros with zero-free regions, density
estimates without logarithmic factors and the Deuring--Heilbronn phenomenon, and the constants of all three enter the
exponent.

The zero-free half-plane of OpenAI's release \cite{openai-main, openai-companion}, Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, removes
the zeros with $\beta > 11/12$. Every remaining zero then contributes at most a power $x^{-1/12}$, and the problem
becomes one of density: a density exponent $A \ge 2$ for $1/2 \le \sigma \le 11/12$ gives Linnik's theorem with
every exponent $L > A$. Huxley's density estimate \cite{huxley75} gives $A = 12/5$. Chen, Gupta and Li \cite{cgl}, whose
method is Guth and Maynard's \cite{guth-maynard}, prove $A = 7/3$, uniformly in $q$ and $T$: their Theorem~1.2 bounds
the count, for a divisor $q_1$ of $q$, by four terms, and at $q_1 = q$ the largest of them on $[7/10, 4/5]$ has the
exponent $4/(1 + \sigma)$, which is $7/3$ at $\sigma = 5/7$ and smaller beyond; below $5/7$ Ingham's exponent
$3/(2 - \sigma)$ is at most $7/3$. With Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} the classical deduction turns a density exponent $A$
into every Linnik exponent above $A$ (Section~\ref{sec:comparison}). What it uses is that there are no zeros near
$\operatorname{Re} s = 1$, and in particular no exceptional zero; the sieve of this paper uses the same.

\subsection*{Why a sieve can go below the density exponent}

For primes in short intervals a density exponent $A$ gives intervals of length $x^{1 - 1/A}$, and Harman's sieve goes
further, to $x^{0.525}$ \cite{bhp}. The same happens here. The sieve needs a \emph{Type~II estimate}: a bound for
bilinear sums over the non-trivial characters mod $q$ of Dirichlet polynomials with bounded coefficients times
products of prime sums. Sort the characters by the largest real part $\beta$ of the zeros of their $L$-functions. Each
prime sum of length $Q$ is then at most about $Q^\beta$, and a density estimate with exponent $A$ bounds the number of
characters by $q^{A(1 - \beta)}$.

With one Dirichlet polynomial $W$ of length $P = q^m$ and a product $S$ of prime sums of length $x/P$,
Cauchy--Schwarz over the characters of a class and orthogonality bound $\sum |W(\chi)|$ by the square root of their
number times $((q + P)P)^{1/2}$. Neglecting logarithms and margins, the bound is smaller than the main term for the
lengths $P$ around $q$ exactly when $L$ exceeds $1 + A/2$ for the largest density exponent $A$: $11/5$ with Huxley's
$12/5$, and $13/6$ with Chen, Gupta and Li's $7/3$. With two Dirichlet polynomials $W_1$ and $W_2$ of lengths $q^{m_1}$
and $q^{m_2}$, and a product of prime sums of length $q^\rho$, $\rho = L - m_1 - m_2$, Cauchy--Schwarz is taken
between $W_1$ and $W_2$ instead, and each mean square over a class is at most $(q + q^{m_i})$ times the sum of the
squared coefficients, whatever the number of characters in the class. Against the main term the class then
contributes about $q^{-\rho(1 - \beta) + (s_1^+ + s_2^+)/2}$, with $s_i = 1 - m_i$ and $s^+ = \max(s, 0)$: for two
polynomials of length at least $q$ this is small at every $\beta$ below the half-plane, with no density estimate at
all, and a polynomial shorter than $q$ costs $q^{s_i/2}$, which the zeros' saving $q^{-\rho(1 - \beta)}$ must cover.
That saving vanishes as $\beta$ approaches $1$; there the classes are small, and the Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality in
mean square, with Huxley's density estimate, takes over from orthogonality, from $\beta = 0.7789$ on. So the estimate
holds on an explicit region of the two lengths for every $L$ (Section~\ref{sec:type2}), and the sieve's task is to
bring as many of its terms as it can into that region.

The sieve cannot bring them all, and it pays for the sums it cannot compare. At $L = 43/20$ the primes that the sieve
loses, bounded by a finite computation, are at most $0.94847$ of them (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}). The table
compares what the inputs give with and without the sieve (Section~\ref{sec:comparison}).

\subsection*{Harman's sieve, and what is new}

Harman's sieve \cite{harman83}, \cite[Chapter~3]{harman} bounds from below the number of primes in the set $\mathcal A$
of integers $n \in (x, 2x]$ with $n \equiv a \pmod q$ by comparing it with the set $\mathcal B$ of the integers $n \in
(x, 2x]$ prime to $q$, weighted by $1/\varphi(q)$. It needs two kinds of information. Type~I information is the
comparison of $\mathcal A$ with $\mathcal B$ on the multiples of each integer prime to $q$, which counting in residue
classes gives. Type~II information is the comparison on sums over integers $n = m_1 m_2 r$ with $(m_1, m_2)$ in the
region, bounded coefficients, and $r$ a product of primes, which the Type~II estimate gives. The sieve decomposes the
count of primes in $\mathcal A$ by Buchstab's identity into terms, compares with $\mathcal B$ the terms it can, and
drops the others with a sign that keeps a lower bound; a finite computation bounds the fraction it loses.

The paper's contributions are four.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A Type~II estimate in two variables over the zeros' real parts} (Section~\ref{sec:type2}). The characters
  mod $q$ are sorted into bins by the largest real part of the zeros of their $L$-functions up to a height. In each bin
  Cauchy--Schwarz is taken between the two Dirichlet polynomials, and each mean square is bounded by the better of
  orthogonality and the Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality in mean square, in which the number of characters in the bin
  multiplies only a bound for a kernel. Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} and Huxley's density estimate give the region of
  Definition~\ref{def:region} for every exponent.
\item \emph{Harman's comparison in two variables} (Section~\ref{sec:type1}). A term of the sieve is compared with
  $\mathcal B$ by a Buchstab tree whose stops have a first variable in a range, a second variable that is the
  integer's cofactor, and a rest taken among the term's own primes or among the small primes of its own tree; its
  leaves are evaluated by a bilinear Type~I estimate, proved by Parseval's identity with no bound for Kloosterman sums,
  which reaches moduli beyond $x/q$. Conditions between the two variables, which have no prime to pivot on, cost a
  power of $\log q$.
\item \emph{A decomposition by trees of Buchstab steps, with a reversal} (Section~\ref{sec:decomp}). The terms are
  sums over boxes of primes in cells of exponents. A tree of Buchstab steps decomposes a box, evaluates its terms by
  the tests that the comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:type1} pass, and drops the rest at a cost bounded by counts of
  rough numbers. A term that needs an upper bound and that no test passes may be reversed: rewritten through its
  largest prime as a sum over the multiples of the other primes.
\item \emph{A certificate} (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}). The trees are found by a search, and the certificate is the
  plan of that search, which Lean's kernel follows step by step; its cost, $1093510193343867034 \cdot 2^{-60}
  = 0.94846\ldots$, bounds the fraction of the primes that the sieve loses, and it is below $1$.
\end{enumerate}

The rest is not new. Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} is OpenAI's, and the density estimate of Huxley \cite{huxley72,
huxley75}, the Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality \cite{halasz, montgomery69}, Buchstab's identity \cite{buchstab}, Brun's
sieve \cite{brun} and Harman's sieve are classical.

\subsection*{Status and formalisation}

The Lean statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has no hypotheses, and its axioms are Lean's standard three, so the
theorem is unconditional. Its proof uses one recent result that has not been refereed, OpenAI's zero-free half-plane,
which Section~\ref{sec:inputs} states as Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. The library proves it along OpenAI's companion
preprint, and proves Huxley's density estimate, so the truth of the theorem rests on Lean's kernel, Mathlib and the
pinned toolchain (with the vendored upstream files and the provenance of Mathlib's prebuilt files, \S S5), not on the
preprint. Whether the Lean statement of the input faithfully captures the preprint's theorem matters for attributing
and interpreting it, not for the truth of the theorem, and the kernel does not check it. Every step of the proof, and
every result from the literature that it uses, is proved in the Lean library \cite{repo}, written with Lean~4
\cite{lean4} and Mathlib \cite{mathlib}, or in Mathlib itself, which supplies among others Dirichlet's theorem and the
functional equation of Dirichlet $L$-functions. Appendix~\ref{app:claims} names the theorems, Section~\ref{sec:lean}
says what is formalised and what is known of independent checking of the input, and the directory
\file{formalisations/} of the repository documents each result from the literature: its source, its Lean statement and
the differences between them. The proofs of the paper are those of the library, written at the level of detail of a
paper, except the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:curve}, which is the classical one; where the Lean proof fixes a constant whose value the argument does not need, the paper usually says only that
the constant exists. The argument, the formalisation and this text were developed with the help of AI systems (see the
acknowledgements); the author is responsible for the paper.

\subsection*{Related work}

Heath-Brown and Iwaniec \cite{hb-iwaniec} combined a sieve with zero-density estimates, which bound Dirichlet
polynomials over primes in the $t$-aspect; the bins of Section~\ref{sec:type2} are the analogue in the $q$-aspect,
over the characters mod $q$. Harman's sieve with a numerical bound for its loss is the method of Baker, Harman and
Pintz \cite{bhp} and of R.~Li \cite{li-harman}. R.~Li also adapts his calculation to moduli all of whose prime factors
are below $q^C$, under a zero-free region of width $(\log q)^{-3/4}$, and obtains $1/0.4752 < 2.1044$ for the powers of
a fixed prime; Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} implies his hypothesis, so for such moduli his theorem, if it holds as stated,
gives a smaller exponent (Section~\ref{sec:comparison}). Theorem~\ref{thm:main} holds for every modulus.

\section{Notation and the input}\label{sec:inputs}

Throughout, $q \ge 1$ is the modulus and $a$ is prime to $q$. The Dirichlet characters mod $q$ are $\chi$, the trivial
one is $\chi_0$, and $L(s, \chi)$ is the $L$-function of $\chi$, continued to the whole plane. For
$1/2 \le \sigma \le 1$ and $T \ge 2$ let
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) = \#\{\chi \bmod q : L(s, \chi) = 0 \text{ for some } s \in \mathcal R(\sigma, T)\},
\]
where $\mathcal R(\sigma, T) = \{s : \operatorname{Re} s \ge \sigma,\ |\operatorname{Im} s| \le T\}$: the number of
characters mod $q$ whose $L$-function has a zero in the rectangle. It counts characters, not zeros, so a bound
for the number of zeros bounds it. The proof is a sieve on the integers of $(x, 2x]$, $x = q^L$, with $L = 43/20$ in
the end, and $q$ is taken large.

The paper states one input, written \emph{Input} and not \emph{hypothesis} because the library proves it
(Section~\ref{sec:lean}). A statement below that holds `under' the input is conditional in the text of the paper.

\begin{inputthm}[the zero-free half-plane]\label{inp:half-plane}
For every $q \ge 1$, every Dirichlet character $\chi$ mod $q$ and every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re} s > 11/12$,
$L(s, \chi) \ne 0$, except at $s = 1$ for the trivial character.
\end{inputthm}

The exception is the pole of $L(s, \chi_0)$ at $s = 1$. This is OpenAI's result: their companion preprint
\cite{openai-companion} proves it, and their main preprint \cite{openai-main} proves the stronger
$\operatorname{Re} s > 7/8$. The library proves it along the companion preprint's route (\S S5).

\subsection*{The density curve}

The Type~II estimate of Section~\ref{sec:type2} counts the characters by a curve.

\begin{definition}\label{def:curve}
A function $g : \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ is a \emph{density curve} if there is $C \ge 0$ such that for every
$q \ge 1$, every $1/2 \le \sigma \le 1$ and every $T \ge 2$,
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le C\, (qT)^{g(\sigma)} \bigl(1 + \log qT\bigr)^{10}.
\]
\end{definition}

The estimate uses the curve only from the threshold $\beta^\dagger = 7789/10000$ on (Section~\ref{sec:region}), and there
it checks its condition piece by piece, on pieces of the form $A(1 - \sigma)$. The curve of this paper is the trivial
count below $\beta^\dagger$ and Huxley's exponent $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ above, frozen on short steps and rounded up, with a
constant exponent from $5/6$ on, where Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} removes the zeros beyond $11/12$. The curve
$g_{\mathrm H}$ is the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item $g_{\mathrm H}(\sigma) = 1$ for $\sigma < 7789/10000$.
\item $g_{\mathrm H}(\sigma) = (7014/3125)(1 - \sigma)$ on $[7789/10000, 131/168)$: Huxley's exponent at the left end
  is $30000/13367 = 2.244333\ldots$, and $7014/3125 = 2.24448$.
\item On $[131/168, 5/6)$, divided into fifteen steps $[\sigma_-, \sigma_+)$ of length $1/280$,
  $g_{\mathrm H}(\sigma) = \bigl(3/(3\sigma_- - 1) + 1/2000\bigr)(1 - \sigma)$: Huxley's exponent at the step's left end
  plus $1/2000$.
\item $g_{\mathrm H}(\sigma) = (101/50)(1 - \sigma)$ for $\sigma \ge 5/6$.
\end{itemize}
The exponent falls from $2.24448$ at $\beta^\dagger$ to about $2.015$ on the last step before $5/6$; from $5/6$ on it is
$101/50$. On each step $A(1 - \sigma) \le A(1 - \sigma_-) \le 1$, so $g_{\mathrm H} \le 1$ everywhere, and
$g_{\mathrm H}(1/2) = 1$. From $\beta^\dagger$ on, $g_{\mathrm H}(\sigma) \le (7014/3125)(1 - \beta^\dagger) =
0.49625\ldots$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve}
Under Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, $g_{\mathrm H}$ is a density curve.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A finite union of ranges on each of which the bound holds, with its own constant, is a range on which it holds with
the sum of the constants, so it suffices to prove the bound on each piece.
\begin{itemize}
\item For $\sigma < 7789/10000$: $N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le \varphi(q) \le qT$.
\item On each step of $[7789/10000, 5/6]$: Huxley's estimate \cite{huxley75} over the characters mod $q$. His
  (1.1) is a bound $(Q^2 T/q_0)^{12(1 - \sigma)/5 + \varepsilon}$ for the zeros of the $L$-functions of the primitive
  characters to the moduli up to $Q$ divisible by $q_0$, and his (2.23) has the exponent $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ there;
  with $Q = q_0 = d$ for each $d \mid q$, the case of one modulus, it bounds the characters mod $q$, each of which is
  induced by a primitive character mod some $d \mid q$ with the same zeros in $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$, at a cost
  $\tau(q) \ll q^\varepsilon$. On a step $[\sigma_-, \sigma_+]$ the exponent $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ is at most its value at
  $\sigma_-$, and the step's exponent exceeds that value by $1/2000$, or by about $1.5 \cdot 10^{-4}$ on the first step;
  since $1 - \sigma \ge 1/6$ there, the excess absorbs this $q^\varepsilon$ and Huxley's own $\varepsilon$.
\item For $\sigma \ge 5/6$: the exponent $101/50$. On $[5/6, 11/12]$ Huxley's exponent $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ is at most
  $2$, so his estimate gives $2 + 1/50$, the excess $1/50$ absorbing the same two factors $q^\varepsilon$, since
  $(1 - \sigma)/50 \ge 1/600$ there; above $11/12$ no character is counted, by Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}.
\end{itemize}
These bounds need no factor $(1 + \log qT)^{10}$; the definition allows it because the formalisation's bounds carry
it.
\end{proof}

The formalisation does not deduce the steps from Huxley's published statements: it proves each step by its own
large-values argument, at parameters that satisfy linear conditions at the step's two ends (\S S5).

Only the part of $g_{\mathrm H}$ from $\beta^\dagger$ on matters: below $\beta^\dagger$ the Type~II estimate bounds both
polynomials by orthogonality, whatever the curve (Section~\ref{sec:region}). So the paper uses no density estimate
below $\beta^\dagger$, neither Ingham's \cite{ingham} nor Chen, Gupta and Li's, and the trivial count there costs
nothing. Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} also enters the Type~II estimate (Section~\ref{sec:type2}) and, in the
formalisation, the prime number theorem (Section~\ref{sec:proof}).

\section{The Type II estimate in two variables}\label{sec:type2}

Fix a smooth real function $f_0$ supported in $[1/2, 2]$. For a character $\chi$, a scale $Q \ge 4$ and a
height $h \in \mathbb R$, the smoothed prime sum is
\[
  S_Q(\chi, h) = \sum_{p \le 2Q} \chi(p) \Bigl(\frac{p}{Q}\Bigr)^{-ih} f_0\Bigl(\frac{p}{Q}\Bigr).
\]
For two finite sets $S_1 \subseteq (P_1, 2P_1]$ and $S_2 \subseteq (P_2, 2P_2]$ of integers, coefficients $a_m, b_m \in
\mathbb C$ and heights $t_1, t_2 \in \mathbb R$, let
\[
  W_1(\chi) = \sum_{m \in S_1} a_m \chi(m) m^{-it_1}, \qquad W_2(\chi) = \sum_{m \in S_2} b_m \chi(m) m^{-it_2},
\]
and $V_1 = \sum_{m \in S_1} |a_m|^2$, $V_2 = \sum_{m \in S_2} |b_m|^2$. Write $\mathcal X^*$ for the non-trivial
characters mod $q$. The sieve of Section~\ref{sec:type1} needs, for the integers $n = m_1 m_2 r$ with $m_1$ and $m_2$ in
ranges near $q$ and $r$ a product of primes, that the non-trivial characters contribute less than the main term. In
the Dirichlet polynomials above this is the following estimate, on an explicit region of the two lengths.

\begin{definition}\label{def:region}
Let $L$ be real, $\lambda = 27637/62500 = 0.442192$ and $\mu = 1/12500$. The \emph{region} $\mathcal R_L$ is the set of
the pairs $(m_1, m_2)$ of reals such that, with $s_i = 1 - m_i$ and $\rho = L - m_1 - m_2$,
\[
  \rho \ge \frac1{2000}, \quad m_1, m_2 \ge \frac23, \quad s_1 + \mu \le \lambda\rho, \quad s_2 + \mu \le \lambda\rho,
  \quad s_1 + s_2 + \mu \le \lambda\rho.
\]
\end{definition}

For $P_i = q^{m_i}$, the number $\rho$ is the exponent of the rest $q^L/(P_1 P_2)$, the length of the product of the
prime sums. Two variables of length at least $q$ ($s_i \le 0$) are in the region as soon as $\rho \ge 1/2000$; a
variable shorter than $q$ may fall short of it by the factor $q^{-s_i}$ with $s_i \le \lambda\rho - \mu$, and the two
together by $q^{-s_1 - s_2}$ with $s_1 + s_2 \le \lambda\rho - \mu$. Since $s_1 + s_2 = 2 - L + \rho$, the last
condition is $\rho \le (L - 2 - \mu)/(1 - \lambda)$, so for each $L$ the region is a bounded polygon. The number
$\lambda$ is $2(1 - \beta^\dagger - 4 \cdot 10^{-6})$ at the threshold $\beta^\dagger = 7789/10000$ of
Section~\ref{sec:inputs}, and Section~\ref{sec:region} explains it.

\begin{theorem}[the Type~II estimate in two variables]\label{thm:type2}
Assume Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. Let $L$ be real, let $K_0 \ge 0$ be an integer, $e_1 > 0$ and $0 < \eta \le
10^{-5}$. There is $q_0$, depending on $f_0$, $K_0$, $e_1$, $\eta$ and $L$, such that for every $q \ge q_0$, all
integers $P_1, P_2 \ge 1$ with $(\log_q P_1, \log_q P_2) \in \mathcal R_L$, all $S_i \subseteq (P_i, 2P_i]$, all
coefficients and heights $t_1, t_2$, every $K \le K_0$, and all $Q_1, \dots, Q_K \ge q^{e_1}$ and $|h_1|, \dots,
|h_K| \le q^\eta$ with $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/(P_1 P_2)$,
\[
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} |W_1(\chi)|\, |W_2(\chi)| \prod_{j=1}^K |S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)|
  \le q^{L - 1/50000} \Bigl(\frac{V_1}{P_1}\Bigr)^{1/2} \Bigl(\frac{V_2}{P_2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

By Cauchy--Schwarz, $|W_i(\chi_0)| \le P_i (V_i/P_i)^{1/2}$, and $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/(P_1P_2)$, so the
trivial character's term is at most a constant times $q^L (V_1/P_1)^{1/2}(V_2/P_2)^{1/2}$; the bound saves the power
$q^{-1/50000}$ against it. The proof has three parts, as for one Dirichlet polynomial: the prime sums are bounded
through the zeros of each character's $L$-function, the characters are sorted by those zeros, and in each bin the sum
over the characters is bounded. The difference is in the third part. In each bin, Cauchy--Schwarz is taken between
$W_1$ and $W_2$, and each mean square is bounded by the better of orthogonality and the Hal\'asz--Montgomery
inequality in mean square. The number of characters in a bin enters only the second bound, multiplied by a bound for
a kernel; so a density estimate is needed only where the bins are small, and there the classical one suffices.

\subsection{Prime sums in a zero-free rectangle}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:prime-sum}
Assume Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. For every integer $J \ge 0$ there are $B, C \ge 0$ such that the following holds
for every $q \ge 1$, every
$\chi \in \mathcal X^*$, every $1/2 \le \theta \le 11/12$, $0 < \delta \le 1/24$, $H \ge 0$, $Q \ge 4$ and
$y \in \mathbb R$. If $L(s, \chi) \ne 0$ for $\operatorname{Re} s > \theta$, $|\operatorname{Im} s - y| < H + 1$,
then
\begin{multline*}
  |S_Q(\chi, y)| \le M_\delta(\theta)\, \ell_{q, y}\, B\,
  \frac{Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{11/12 + \delta}(1 + H)^{-J}}{\log Q} \\
  + C\bigl(\sqrt{2Q} + 1\bigr)\bigl(\log_2 (2Q) + 1\bigr),
\end{multline*}
where $\ell_{q, y} = \log(q + 2) + \log(|y| + 4) + 1$, and $M_\delta(\theta) = C_{\log}(\theta, \theta + \delta) +
C_{\log}(11/12, 11/12 + \delta)$ depends only on $\theta$ and $\delta$; $C_{\log}$ is the constant of the bound for
$L'/L$ in \S S1.
\end{lemma}

The argument is the classical one for primes in progressions \cite[Chapter~5]{iwaniec-kowalski}, with a smooth weight in
place of a sharp cut-off:
Perron's formula for $-L'/L$ against the Mellin transform of the weight, a bound for $L'/L$ in zero-free discs by a
lemma of Landau's type, and a contour that runs along $\operatorname{Re} s = \theta + \delta$ at the heights near $y$
and along $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta$ elsewhere. The only use of Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} is to move the
line of integration to $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta$ and to bound $L'/L$ on that line and to its right.
\S S1 gives the proof.

\subsection{The bins}\label{sec:bins}

Let $I = 500000$ and $\beta_i = 1/2 + 5i/(12I)$ for $0 \le i \le I$, so that $\beta_0 = 1/2$, $\beta_I = 11/12$ and
the bins have width $1/1200000$. For a height bound $T \ge 2$ the \emph{bin} of $\chi \in \mathcal X^*$ is the largest
$i < I$ such that $L(s, \chi)$ has a zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > \beta_i$ and $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$, and
$0$ if there is none. If $\chi$ is in the bin $i$ then:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L(s, \chi)$ has no zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > \beta_{i+1}$ and $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$: for
  $i + 1 < I$ by the choice of $i$, and for $i + 1 = I$ by Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. So
  Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum} applies with $\theta = \beta_{i+1}$ to every height $|y| \le T - H - 1$.
\item For $i \ge 1$, $\chi$ is counted by $N^*(\beta_i, T; q)$, so a density curve $g$ bounds the number of characters
  in the bin by $C(qT)^{g(\beta_i)}(1 + \log qT)^{10}$. For $i = 0$ the bound $\varphi(q) \le qT$ is the same, with
  $g(1/2) = 1$ and $\max(C, 1)$ in place of $C$, which the bound for every $i$ then uses.
\end{itemize}
The bins depend on $T$, and so on all the zeros up to the height $T$, but not on the heights $h_j$; the density curve is
therefore used only for the zeros up to the height $T \le 3q^\eta$, a small power of $q$. The proof takes
$\delta = 10^{-6}$, $H = q^\eta$ and $T = 2H + 1 \le 3q^\eta$, so that every height $|h_j| \le q^\eta$ has
$|h_j| + H + 1 \le T$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum} with $J = \lceil 5(1 + L)/(12\eta) \rceil$. As
$Q_j \ge q^{e_1} \ge 4$ for large $q$ and $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/(P_1P_2) \le 2^{K_0} q^L$, each $Q_j \le
q^{1 + L}$ for large $q$, so that $Q_j^{11/12 + \delta}(1 + H)^{-J} \le Q_j^{1/2 + \delta}$. The hypothesis $Q_j \ge
q^{e_1}$ gives $\ell_{q, h_j}/\log Q_j \ll 1/e_1$, and $M_\delta$ is bounded on the $I + 1$ values $\beta_i$. So for
large $q$, $|S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)| \le 2 Q_j^{\beta_{i+1} + 2\delta}$ in the bin $i$, and the product of the prime sums is
at most $4^{K_0} (q^L/(P_1P_2))^{\beta_i + \varpi}$ with $\varpi = 1/1200000 + 2\delta < 2.9 \cdot 10^{-6}$.

\subsection{Two bounds for a mean square in a bin}\label{sec:two-bounds}

Let $\mathcal X_i$ be the set of the characters in the bin $i$. Since the prime sums are bounded on $\mathcal X_i$, it
remains to bound $\sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X_i} |W_1(\chi)||W_2(\chi)|$, and by Cauchy--Schwarz over $\mathcal X_i$ this
is at most the geometric mean of the two mean squares $\sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X_i} |W_1(\chi)|^2$ and $\sum_{\chi \in
\mathcal X_i} |W_2(\chi)|^2$. Each has two bounds. Write $W(\chi) = \sum_{m \in S} c_m \chi(m) m^{-ih_0}$ for either
polynomial, with $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$ and $V = \sum_{m \in S} |c_m|^2$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Orthogonality.} For every set $\mathcal X$ of characters mod $q$,
  \[
    \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X} |W(\chi)|^2 \le \sum_\chi |W(\chi)|^2 \le (P + q) V,
  \]
  summed over all characters mod $q$ in the middle. Indeed the middle is $\varphi(q)$ times the sum, over the classes
  mod $q$ prime to $q$, of the squared moduli of the sums over the classes; each class has at most $P/q + 1$ elements
  in $(P, 2P]$, and Cauchy--Schwarz in each class and $\varphi(q) \le q$ give the bound.
\item \emph{The Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality in mean square} \cite{halasz, montgomery69}, Lemma~\ref{lem:hm-sq}
  below: if $\mathcal H$ bounds $|k_{2P}(\psi)|$ for every non-trivial character $\psi$ mod $q$, where
  $k_Y(\psi) = \sum_{n \ge 1} \psi(n) e^{-n/Y}$, then
  \[
    \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X} |W(\chi)|^2 \le e V \bigl(2P + |\mathcal X| \mathcal H\bigr).
  \]
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}[the Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality in mean square]\label{lem:hm-sq}
Let $P \ge 1$, $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$, $c_m \in \mathbb C$, $h_0 \in \mathbb R$, let $\mathcal X$ be a set of
characters mod $q$, and let $\mathcal H \ge 0$ bound $|k_{2P}(\psi)|$ for every non-trivial character $\psi$ mod $q$.
Then $D = \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X} |W(\chi)|^2$ satisfies $D \le e V (2P + |\mathcal X| \mathcal H)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\nu_\chi = \overline{W(\chi)}$, so that $D = \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X} \nu_\chi W(\chi) = \sum_{m \in S} c_m
m^{-ih_0} \Phi(m)$ with $\Phi(m) = \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X} \nu_\chi \chi(m)$. By Cauchy--Schwarz with the weight
$e^{-m/2P}$, which is at least $e^{-1}$ on $(P, 2P]$,
\begin{multline*}
  D^2 \le e V \sum_{m \in S} e^{-m/2P} |\Phi(m)|^2 \le e V \sum_{m \ge 1} e^{-m/2P} |\Phi(m)|^2 \\
  = e V \sum_{\chi, \chi' \in \mathcal X} \nu_\chi \overline{\nu_{\chi'}}\, k_{2P}(\chi \overline{\chi'}).
\end{multline*}
The diagonal terms are at most $\sum_{n \ge 1} e^{-n/2P} \le 2P$ times $|\nu_\chi|^2$, and their sum is at most $2P
\cdot D$. For $\chi \ne \chi'$ the character $\chi\overline{\chi'}$ is non-trivial, so the other terms are at most
$\mathcal H \bigl(\sum_\chi |\nu_\chi|\bigr)^2 \le \mathcal H |\mathcal X| D$, by Cauchy--Schwarz. So $D^2 \le e V(2P +
|\mathcal X|\mathcal H) D$, and the claim follows, trivially if $D = 0$.
\end{proof}

The mean square is the point of the lemma. The inequality in its usual form bounds the first moment,
$(\sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X} |W(\chi)|)^2 \le e V (2P |\mathcal X| + |\mathcal X|^2 \mathcal H)$, which follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:hm-sq} by Cauchy--Schwarz over $\mathcal X$; it bounds the mean square only through $\sum |W|^2 \le
(\sum |W|)^2$, which loses a factor $|\mathcal X|$. In Lemma~\ref{lem:hm-sq} the size of $\mathcal X$ multiplies only
$\mathcal H$. Orthogonality does not involve $|\mathcal X|$ at all. So in a bin of two variables the
number of characters enters only through the second bound, and only multiplied by $\mathcal H$.

For the kernels, the formalisation proves the following bound from Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. Let $\delta' =
\varepsilon' = 1/1000$. There is $C$ such that for every $q$, every non-trivial character $\psi$ mod $q$ and every
$Y \ge 1$,
\[
  |k_Y(\psi)| \le C Y^{1/12 - \delta'} (2q)^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}.
\]
Its proof (\S S1) writes $k_Y(\psi) = (2\pi i)^{-1} \int_{(2)} L(s, \psi) \Gamma(s) Y^s \, ds$, moves the line to
$\operatorname{Re} s = 1/12 - \delta'$, and bounds $L(s, \psi)$ there by the functional equation and the Lindelöf-type
bound that Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} gives on $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta'$, as the Riemann hypothesis gives
the Lindelöf hypothesis \cite[\S 14.2]{titchmarsh}. At $Y = 2P$ with $P = q^m$ the exponent of $q$ in this bound is
\[
  b(m) = \frac5{12} + \frac{m}{12} + \delta'(1 - m) + 2\varepsilon',
\]
which is $1/2 + 2\varepsilon'$ at $m = 1$. Partial summation and the Pólya--Vinogradov inequality
\cite[Chapter~23]{davenport} give a bound of the same size without Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}: since the weight
$e^{-n/Y}$ decreases from below $1$ to $0$, $|k_Y(\psi)| \le \max_N |\sum_{n \le N} \psi(n)| \ll_\varepsilon
q^{1/2 + \varepsilon}$ for every non-trivial $\psi$ mod $q$ and every $Y > 0$ (Section~\ref{sec:region}).

\subsection{The zone condition}\label{sec:zone}

Write $P_i = q^{m_i}$ and $\rho = L - m_1 - m_2$, and let $\beta = \beta_i$. Up to constants, powers of $1 + \log qT$
and the factor $q^\eta$, the bin $i$ has at most $q^{g(\beta)}$ characters, the product of the prime sums is at most
$q^{\rho(\beta + \varpi)}$, and $\mathcal H \le q^{b(m)}$ at $P = q^m$. A number $M$ is an \emph{exponent} of the mean
square of a polynomial of length $q^m$ over the bin if
\[
  M \ge \max(m, 1) \qquad \text{or} \qquad M \ge \max\bigl(m, g(\beta) + b(m)\bigr),
\]
the first by orthogonality, since $P + q \le 2q^{\max(m, 1)}$, and the second by Lemma~\ref{lem:hm-sq}, since $2P \le
2q^m$ and $|\mathcal X_i|\mathcal H$ is at most a constant times $q^{g(\beta) + b(m) + \eta}$. With exponents $M_1$ and
$M_2$ of the two mean squares, and $V_i^{1/2} = q^{m_i/2}(V_i/P_i)^{1/2}$, the bin contributes at most $q^E
(V_1/P_1)^{1/2}(V_2/P_2)^{1/2}$, up to the same factors, with
\[
  E = \rho(\beta + \varpi) + \frac{m_1 + M_1}2 + \frac{m_2 + M_2}2.
\]

\begin{definition}\label{def:zone}
A set $\mathcal R$ of pairs $(m_1, m_2)$ satisfies the \emph{zone condition} for a curve $g$ at $L$ if for every $1/2
\le \beta \le 11/12$, every $0 \le \varpi \le 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$ and every $(m_1, m_2) \in \mathcal R$ there are
exponents $M_1$ and $M_2$ of the two mean squares, with $\delta' = \varepsilon' = 1/1000$, such that $E \le L -
1/25000$.
\end{definition}

Each variable chooses its bound independently, but the two share the bin, and so the value $g(\beta)$.

\begin{lemma}[one bin]\label{lem:bin}
Let $g \le 1$ be a function and $\mathcal R$ a set satisfying the zone condition for $g$ at $L$. Given $C_0, C_d, C_K
\ge 0$, there is $C'$ such that the following holds. Let $q > 1$, $(m_1, m_2) \in \mathcal R$ with $P_i = q^{m_i}$
integers, $1 \le T \le 3q^\eta$ with $\eta \ge 0$, $1/2 \le \beta \le 11/12$ and $0 \le \varpi \le 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$.
Let $\mathcal X$ be a set of at most $C_d (qT)^{g(\beta)} (1 + \log qT)^{10}$ characters on which $\prod_{j=1}^K
|S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)| \le C_0 (q^L/(P_1P_2))^{\beta + \varpi}$, for some $K$, $Q_j$ and $h_j$, and let $\mathcal H_i \le
C_K (2P_i)^{1/12 - \delta'} (2q)^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}$ bound $|k_{2P_i}(\psi)|$ for every non-trivial
character $\psi$ mod $q$. Then for all $S_i \subseteq (P_i, 2P_i]$, coefficients and heights,
\begin{multline*}
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X} |W_1(\chi)|\, |W_2(\chi)| \prod_{j=1}^K |S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)| \\
  \le C' (1 + \log qT)^{10} q^{L - 1/25000 + \eta} \Bigl(\frac{V_1}{P_1}\Bigr)^{1/2} \Bigl(\frac{V_2}{P_2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\end{multline*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The left side is at most $C_0 (q^L/(P_1P_2))^{\beta + \varpi}$ times $\sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X} |W_1||W_2|$, which is
at most the geometric mean of the two mean squares. Take the exponents $M_1$ and $M_2$ of the zone condition at
$(\beta, m_1, m_2, \varpi)$. If $M_i$ is an exponent by orthogonality, the mean square is at most $(P_i + q)V_i \le 2
q^{M_i + \eta} V_i$. If it is one by Lemma~\ref{lem:hm-sq}, then $(qT)^{g(\beta)} \le 3 q^{g(\beta) + \eta}$ since $g
\le 1$ and $T \le 3q^\eta$, and $2^{1/12 - \delta'} 2^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'} \le 4$, so that $|\mathcal
X|\mathcal H_i \le 12 C_d C_K (1 + \log qT)^{10} q^{g(\beta) + b(m_i) + \eta}$, and the mean square is at most $e(2 + 12
C_d C_K)(1 + \log qT)^{10} q^{M_i + \eta} V_i$. In both cases its square root is at most $(e(2 + 12C_dC_K))^{1/2}(1 +
\log qT)^5 q^{(m_i + M_i + \eta)/2} (V_i/P_i)^{1/2}$, and the total exponent of $q$ is $E + \eta \le L - 1/25000 +
\eta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:type2-gen}
Assume Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. Let $g$ be a density curve with $g \le 1$ and $g(1/2) = 1$, and let $\mathcal R$
satisfy the zone condition for $g$ at $L$. Then the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} holds with $\mathcal R$ in
place of $\mathcal R_L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the bins of Section~\ref{sec:bins} and the kernel bound of Section~\ref{sec:two-bounds}, each bin $i$ satisfies the
hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:bin} at $\beta = \beta_i$ and $\varpi = 1/1200000 + 2\delta$, with constants that do not
depend on $q$, so each contributes at most $C' (1 + \log qT)^{10} q^{L - 1/25000 + \eta}(V_1/P_1)^{1/2}(V_2/P_2)^{1/2}$.
There are $I$ bins, and for large $q$, $I C' (1 + \log qT)^{10} \le q^{1/100000}$, so the total is at most
$q^{L - 1/25000 + \eta + 1/100000}(V_1/P_1)^{1/2}(V_2/P_2)^{1/2}$. Since $\eta \le 10^{-5}$,
$L - 1/25000 + \eta + 1/100000 \le L - 1/50000$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The region}\label{sec:region}

With $s_i = 1 - m_i$ and $\Delta_i = M_i - m_i \ge 0$, and since $L = \rho + m_1 + m_2$, the inequality $E \le L -
1/25000$ reads
\[
  \Delta_1 + \Delta_2 + \mu \le 2\rho(1 - \beta - \varpi), \qquad \mu = \frac1{12500}.
\]
The least exponents give $\Delta_i = \max(0, s_i)$ by orthogonality, and $\Delta_i = \max(0, g(\beta) - T_* + H_1
s_i)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:hm-sq}, with
\[
  T_* = \frac12 - 2\varepsilon' = \frac{249}{500}, \qquad H_1 = \frac{11}{12} + \delta'.
\]
So the zone condition asks $D(s_1, \beta) + D(s_2, \beta) + \mu \le 2\rho(1 - \beta - \varpi)$ with $D(s, \beta) =
\max\bigl(0, \min(s, g(\beta) - T_* + H_1 s)\bigr)$, and it involves $L$ only through $\rho$. Two consequences.
\begin{itemize}
\item For two variables of length at least $q$, $D = 0$ and the condition is $\rho(1 - \beta - \varpi) \ge 1/25000$,
  which $\rho \ge 1/2000$ gives at every $\beta \le 11/12$, whatever the curve: no density estimate enters.
\item For a variable shorter than $q$, orthogonality costs $s_i$, and the Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality does better
  exactly when $g(\beta) < T_* + (1/12 - \delta')s_i$; for a variable of length about $q$ that is $g(\beta) <
  249/500$. Huxley's exponent gives $(1 - \beta) \cdot 3/(3\beta - 1) \le 249/500$ exactly for $\beta \ge 0.77837$.
\end{itemize}
So the density estimate matters only for $\beta$ above about $0.778$, and the curve $g_{\mathrm H}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:inputs} takes the trivial count below $\beta^\dagger = 7789/10000$ and Huxley's estimate above,
where $g_{\mathrm H} \le (7014/3125)(1 - \beta^\dagger) = 0.49625\ldots < 249/500$. The threshold is the one for which
the certificate's tests of Section~\ref{sec:decomp} are written: Huxley's estimate alone would allow $\beta^\dagger =
0.7784$ and $\lambda = 0.443192$, a region about $0.2\%$ wider, which the paper does not use (\S S6).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:region}
For every real $L$, the region $\mathcal R_L$ satisfies the zone condition for $g_{\mathrm H}$ at $L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every case below increases with $\varpi$, since $\rho \ge 0$, so take $\varpi = 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$.

\emph{For $\beta \le \beta^\dagger$}, take both exponents by orthogonality, $\Delta_i = \max(0, s_i)$. Each of the four
choices of the maxima, $\sum_{i \in I} s_i + \mu \le 2\rho(1 - \beta - \varpi)$ for $I \subseteq \{1, 2\}$, is
hardest at $\beta = \beta^\dagger$, where $2(1 - \beta^\dagger - \varpi) = \lambda$. There the four cases are the
region's inequalities $s_1 + \mu \le \lambda\rho$, $s_2 + \mu \le \lambda\rho$, $s_1 + s_2 + \mu \le \lambda\rho$, and
$\mu \le \lambda\rho$, which follows from $\rho \ge 1/2000$. This holds for every curve, and the cases at
$\beta^\dagger$ hold with no slack: that is how $\lambda$ is chosen.

\emph{For $\beta \ge \beta^\dagger$}, take both exponents by Lemma~\ref{lem:hm-sq}. On each of the $17$ pieces
$[\beta_-, \beta_+]$ of $g_{\mathrm H}$ from $\beta^\dagger$ to $11/12$, $g_{\mathrm H}(\beta) = A(1 - \beta)$ with $A$
constant, and each of the four choices of the maxima, $\Delta_i \in \{0,\ A(1 - \beta) - T_* + H_1 s_i\}$, makes the
case affine in $\beta$. So it suffices to check the four cases at the two ends of each piece. At an end, a case is a
linear inequality in $(s_1, s_2, \rho)$, with nonnegative coefficients on $s_1$ and $s_2$ and the coefficient $-2(1 -
\beta - \varpi) < 0$ on $\rho$. The region is a polyhedron in $(s_1, s_2, \rho)$ given by its six inequalities, with
$L$ eliminated; its recession directions increase $\rho$ and decrease $s_1$ and $s_2$, along which every case gets
easier, so it suffices to check the cases at the polyhedron's vertices. The $17 \times 2 \times 4 = 136$ cases hold at
the vertices, in exact rational arithmetic (\S S5); in the formalisation each case is a linear combination of the six
inequalities with nonnegative coefficients, for every $L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:curve}, $g_{\mathrm H}$ is a density curve under Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, with
$g_{\mathrm H} \le 1$ and $g_{\mathrm H}(1/2) = 1$. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:type2-gen}, under
Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, with the region $\mathcal R_L$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:region}.
\end{proof}

For one short variable the region is exact: below $\beta^\dagger$ the curve $g_{\mathrm H} = 1$ leaves only
orthogonality, so the case at $\beta \to \beta^\dagger$ from below is necessary, and it is the region's inequality.
The bound $m_i \ge 2/3$ is inactive for $L$ up to about $2.42$, since the other inequalities give $s_i \le 1/3$ there.
Beyond, it caps $s_i$ at $1/3$, so that the vertices of the region have $\rho \le (2/3 + \mu)/\lambda \approx 1.51$, and
the case it is needed for there is the one at $\beta = 11/12$, where it keeps $\Delta_i = 0$ in the
Hal\'asz--Montgomery case: $(101/50)/12 - T_* + H_1/3 < 0$.

At $L = 43/20$ the region is the quadrilateral with the vertices $(m_1, m_2) \approx (0.881234, 1)$ and $(1,
0.881234)$, where $\rho = 9370/34863 \approx 0.268766$, and $(1.149641, 0.999859)$ and $(0.999859, 1.149641)$, where
$\rho = 1/2000$. In exponents of $x = q^L$, the two variables lie in $[0.40987, 0.53472]$, within $0.0697$ of each
other, and the rest has exponent at most $0.125008$. The sieve's comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:type1} are built so
that each sum they compare has a first variable, a second, and a rest of primes, in these ranges.

\subsection*{What the estimate uses}

The kernel bound enters only through $b(m)$ in the Hal\'asz--Montgomery case. With the Pólya--Vinogradov bound in
its place, the case reads $\Delta_i = \max(0, g(\beta) - (1/2 - \varepsilon) + s_i)$, and the $136$ cases of the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:region} still hold at the vertices for every $\varepsilon \le 0.0037$, by the same check
(\file{tools/linnik/gen\_zone.py --pv}; not formalised). So Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} is needed for the prime sums, through Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum}, and not for
the kernels.

No density estimate better than Huxley's would enlarge the region by much. Below $\beta^\dagger$ the curve is not used.
A smaller exponent at $\beta^\dagger$ and above would let the threshold move down, to the point where $g(\beta) =
249/500$, and with it $\lambda = 2(1 - \beta^\dagger - \varpi)$ up: Chen, Gupta and Li's exponent $4/(1 + \sigma)$
\cite{cgl} is larger than Huxley's there, and even the density hypothesis, $A = 2$, would give the threshold
$0.751$ and $\lambda \approx 0.498$. The limit of the method lies elsewhere: in the number of the sieve's terms that
fall outside the region (Section~\ref{sec:comparison}).

In one variable the bound is different in kind. There Cauchy--Schwarz is taken over the bin, so the bin's size enters
orthogonality as $|\mathcal X_i|^{1/2} \approx q^{g(\beta)/2}$, and for a polynomial of length about $q$ the condition
becomes $L \ge 1 + A/2$ for the largest density exponent $A$ of the curve: $11/5$ with Huxley's $12/5$, $13/6$ with
Chen, Gupta and Li's $7/3$. In two variables the bin's size never meets orthogonality, and the region holds for every
$L$. What the second variable costs is that both lengths must be near $q$, with a rest of primes of length at least
$q^{1/2000}$.

\section{Type I estimates and Harman's sieve in two variables}\label{sec:type1}

Let $2 < L \le 5/2$; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} takes $L = 43/20$. Let $x = q^L$ and let $a$ be prime to $q$. The sets of
the sieve are
\[
  \mathcal A = \{n \in (x, 2x] : n \equiv a \ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\}, \qquad
  \mathcal B = \{n \in (x, 2x] : (n, q) = 1\},
\]
whose integers are those of $(N_1, N_2]$ with $N_1 = \lfloor x \rfloor$ and $N_2 = \lfloor 2x \rfloor$.
For a set $\mathcal C$ of integers, a positive integer $r$ and $z \ge 1$, $\mathcal C_r$ is the set of the multiples of
$r$ in $\mathcal C$, and $S(\mathcal C_r, z)$ the number of $n \in \mathcal C_r$ with no prime factor below $z$. When
every prime factor of $r$ is at least $z$, as in every use below, this is the number of multiples $rm$ of $r$ in
$\mathcal C$ whose cofactor $m$ has no prime factor below $z$; the cofactor $m = 1$ counts. With
$z_{\mathrm{top}} = \lceil x^{(D/2 + 1)/D} \rceil$, $D = 2^{40}$, an integer of $(N_1, N_2]$ with no prime factor below
$z_{\mathrm{top}}$ is prime for large $q$, so $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ counts primes in $\mathcal A$. The sieve
compares $\mathcal A$ with $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ through the weight
\[
  \vartheta(n) = \mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \frac{\mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]}{\varphi(q)}
  = \frac1{\varphi(q)} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \overline\chi(a) \chi(n),
\]
which is orthogonality: the trivial character cancels, and the other characters carry the difference. This section
states the tools of Harman's sieve \cite{harman83}, \cite[Chapter~3]{harman} in the form the proof uses, with every
exponent an exponent of $q$; Section~\ref{sec:decomp} converts them to exponents of $x$. Lemma~\ref{lem:type1} is the
classical Type~I count, and Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIB} a bilinear form of Type~I information, proved by orthogonality,
Cauchy--Schwarz and Parseval's identity, with no bound for Kloosterman sums; Lemma~\ref{lem:leaves} applies it at the
leaves of Buchstab's tree. Lemmas~\ref{lem:family-two} to~\ref{lem:term-two} carry Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} to sums over
integers whose primes are cut out by conditions, and Propositions~\ref{prop:harman-two} and~\ref{prop:harman-fill} are
Harman's comparison with them. The proofs that are standard, or long, are in the supplement (\S S2 and \S S3).

Here and below, a \emph{comparison} of tuples of primes is a condition $c + \sum_i e_i \log y_i > 0$ (or $\ge 0$) on
their entries, with a constant term $c$ and coefficients $e_i \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$; an index $i$ with $e_i = \pm 1$ is a
\emph{pivot} of it. The constant $C_0$ bounds the sums of $1/p$ over the ranges $[y, y^2)$, and
$\zeta_k = e^{2C_0} (2C_0^k/k!) \exp(e^{2C_0} \cdot 2C_0^{k + 1}/(k + 1)!)$, which tends to $0$ as the even level $k$
grows (\S S3).

\subsection{Type~I}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:type1}
Let $0 \le N \le N'$ be integers and let $M > 0$ be prime to $q$. The number of $n \in (N, N']$ with $n \equiv a \pmod
q$ and $M \mid n$ is $(N' - N)/(Mq)$ within $1$, and the number of $n \in (N, N']$ prime to $q$ with $M \mid n$ is
$\varphi(q)(N' - N)/(Mq)$ within $\varphi(q)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $(M, q) = 1$, the Chinese remainder theorem gives $c$ with $c \equiv 0 \pmod M$ and $c \equiv a \pmod q$, and
the two conditions $n \equiv a \pmod q$ and $M \mid n$ together say $n \equiv c \pmod{Mq}$. For $r \ge 1$ the number of
$n \in (N, N']$ with $n \equiv c \pmod r$ is $\lfloor (N' - c)/r \rfloor - \lfloor (N - c)/r \rfloor$, which
differs from $(N' - N)/r$ by less than $1$. With $r = Mq$ this is the first count. The integers prime to $q$ form the
$\varphi(q)$ residue classes $b \bmod q$ with $(b, q) = 1$, and adding the first count over these classes gives the
second.
\end{proof}

So $|\mathcal A_M| - |\mathcal B_M|/\varphi(q)$ is at most $2$ in absolute value for every $M$ prime to $q$. It serves
at the leaves of Buchstab's tree as long as the number of counts, about $x/q$ times a power of $q$ below $1$, keeps their
errors small against the size $x/q$ of the main terms; so these counts serve moduli up to about $x/q$. Beyond $x/q$
the sieve uses Type~I information in a bilinear form, for moduli that are products $d_1 d_2$ of two parts.

\subsection{Type~I beyond \texorpdfstring{$x/q$}{x/q}}

Write $e_q(t) = e^{2\pi i t/q}$. For a weight $w$ on a finite set $S$ of integers, let
\begin{gather*}
  \hat w(h) = \sum_{k \in S} w(k)\, e_q(hk) \quad (h \bmod q), \\
  E_w = \sum_{k \in S} w(k) \mathbf 1[(k, q) = 1] - \frac{\varphi(q)}{q} \sum_{k \in S} w(k).
\end{gather*}

\begin{lemma}[a bilinear Type~I estimate]\label{lem:typeIB}
Let $\mathcal D_1$ and $\mathcal D_2$ be finite sets of positive integers prime to $q$, with coefficients $\alpha(d_1)$
and $\beta(d_2)$, and let $w$ be a weight on a finite set $S$ of integers. Let $H \ge 0$ and $D \ge 1$ be integers with
$\mathcal D_2 \subseteq [1, D]$ and $2H < q$, and suppose that every residue class mod $q$ contains the inverses of at
most $m$ elements of $\mathcal D_1$, that every integer $|t| d$ with $0 < |t| \le H$ and $d \in \mathcal D_2$ has at
most $F$ divisors, and that $W \ge 0$ and $|\hat w(h)| \le W$ at every $h \ne 0$ that is not the residue of an integer
$t$ with $0 < |t| \le H$. Then
\begin{multline*}
  \Bigl|\sum_{d_1, d_2} \alpha(d_1) \beta(d_2) \sum_{k \in S} w(k)\, \vartheta(d_1 d_2 k)\Bigr| \\
  \le \Bigl(\sum |\alpha|^2\Bigr)^{1/2} \Bigl(mF\Bigl(\Bigl\lfloor \frac{2HD}q \Bigr\rfloor + 1\Bigr) \sum |\beta|^2
  \cdot \frac1q \sum_h |\hat w(h)|^2\Bigr)^{1/2} \\
  + W \sum |\alpha| \sum |\beta| + \frac{|E_w|}{\varphi(q)} \Bigl|\sum \alpha \sum \beta\Bigr|.
\end{multline*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $d$ prime to $q$, with $\bar d$ its inverse mod $q$, $\mathbf 1[dk \equiv a] = q^{-1} \sum_h e_q(h(k - a\bar d))$, and
$(dk, q) = 1$ exactly when $(k, q) = 1$. The frequency $h = 0$ contributes $q^{-1} \sum_k w(k)$, and with the second
part of $\vartheta$ it leaves $-E_w/\varphi(q)$, so that
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_{d_1, d_2} \alpha(d_1)\beta(d_2) \sum_k w(k)\, \vartheta(d_1 d_2 k)
  = \frac1q \sum_{h \ne 0} \hat w(h) B(h) - \frac{E_w}{\varphi(q)} \sum \alpha \sum \beta, \\
  B(h) = \sum_{d_1, d_2} \alpha(d_1) \beta(d_2)\, e_q(-ha \bar d_1 \bar d_2).
\end{gather*}
Since $2H < q$, the residues of the integers $t$ with $0 < |t| \le H$ are distinct. At the other frequencies
$|\hat w(h)| \le W$ and $|B(h)| \le \sum |\alpha| \sum |\beta|$, and there are fewer than $q$ of them, which gives the
second term. At the frequencies $t$, Cauchy--Schwarz over $d_1$ gives
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_t \hat w(t) B(t)\Bigr|^2 \le \sum |\alpha|^2 \cdot
  \sum_{d_1} \Bigl|\sum_{t, d_2} \hat w(t) \beta(d_2)\, e_q(-a t \bar d_2 \cdot \bar d_1)\Bigr|^2,
\]
and as $d_1$ runs over $\mathcal D_1$, $\bar d_1$ runs over the residues mod $q$, each at most $m$ times. Parseval's
identity, $\sum_{s \bmod q} |\sum_j c_j e_q(s f_j)|^2 = q \sum_{f_j \equiv f_{j'}} c_j \bar c_{j'}$, with $j = (t, d_2)$,
$c_j = \hat w(t)\beta(d_2)$ and $f_j = -at\bar d_2$, bounds the inner sum by $mq$ times the sum of
$|\hat w(t)\beta(d_2)\hat w(t')\beta(d_2')|$ over the pairs with $t \bar d_2 \equiv t' \bar d_2'$, that is
$t d_2' \equiv t' d_2 \pmod q$. Bound each product by $\tfrac12(|\hat w(t)|^2 |\beta(d_2')|^2 + |\hat w(t')|^2
|\beta(d_2)|^2)$. For given $(t, d_2')$ the products $t' d_2$ of the pairs $(t', d_2)$ with $t' d_2 \equiv t d_2'$ are
integers of $[-HD, HD]$ in one residue class mod $q$, so they take at most $\lfloor 2HD/q \rfloor + 1$ values, and each
value is $t' d_2$ for at most $F$ pairs, the divisors of $|t'| d_2$. So the inner sum is at most
$mqF(\lfloor 2HD/q \rfloor + 1) \sum_t |\hat w(t)|^2 \sum |\beta|^2$, and $\sum_t |\hat w(t)|^2 \le \sum_h |\hat
w(h)|^2$. Dividing by $q$ gives the first term.
\end{proof}

No bound for Kloosterman sums enters: the inverses $\bar d_1$ are averaged by Parseval's identity, and the inverses
$\bar d_2$ survive only on the diagonal $t d_2' \equiv t' d_2$, which the size of the products confines to
$\lfloor 2HD/q \rfloor + 1$ integers. The lemma is applied with $w$ a smooth majorant or minorant of the cofactor's
range, a spline: for an interval $(A, B]$, a step $\ell \ge 1$ and a power $r \ge 1$, $w(k)$ is $\ell^{-r}$ times the
number of $(i, j_1, \dots, j_r)$ with $A < i \le B$, $0 \le j_s < \ell$ and $i + j_1 + \cdots + j_r = k$. Then $0 \le w
\le 1$, $w = 1$ on $(A + r(\ell - 1), B]$, $w = 0$ off $(A, B + r(\ell - 1)]$, $\sum_h |\hat w(h)|^2 \le q(\lfloor n/q
\rfloor + 1) n$ with $n = B - A + r(\ell - 1)$, $|E_w| \le d(q)$, and, since $\hat w(h)$ is the sum over the interval
times the $r$-th power of a geometric sum of length $\ell$, $|\hat w(h)| \le (B - A)(q/(2\ell(H + 1)))^r$ at the
residues $h$ of the integers $t$ with $H < |t| \le q/2$ (\S S3). In the application the coefficients are bounded, and
the pairs $(d_1, d_2)$ are taken in short blocks, on each of which $d_1 \approx P_1$, $d_2 \approx P_2$ and the cofactor
runs over an interval of length about $n = x/(P_1 P_2)$, with a spline whose step $\ell$ is below $n$ by a small power
of $q$. The cut is $H = \lceil qY/\ell \rceil$ for a small power $Y$ of $q$: then the second term is $(2Y)^{-r}$ times
the block's size, small for large $r$ whatever the size of $d_2$, and the factor $\lfloor 2HD/q \rfloor + 1$ of the
first term, with $D = P_2$, is about $1 + Y P_2/\ell$, that is $1 + Y q^{s} d_1 d_2^2/x$ for a step $\ell = n q^{-s}$.
Measured against $x/q$, the size of a block's main term, the first term is then about
$(F(P_1 + q)(n + q)(1 + Y q^s d_1 d_2^2/x)/x)^{1/2}$: that is $(q^2/x)^{1/2}$ times the factors $(P_1/q + 1)^{1/2}$ of
Parseval's identity over the inverses $\bar d_1$ and $(n/q + 1)^{1/2}$ of the cofactor's residues, and those of the
diagonal, $F$ and the number of its integers.
At the leaves below, where the cofactor is short, $n \le 2q^{1 + \delta'}$, it is small when $d_1 \le x/q^{1 + \xi}$ and
$d_1 d_2^2 \le x q^c$ with $\max(c, 0) + \delta' < \xi$: so $d_1 d_2$ may exceed $x/q$, and $d_1 d_2^2$ may exceed $x$;
only $d_1$, the part that Parseval's identity averages, must stay below $x/q$.

The sieve meets these sums at the leaves of Buchstab's tree, the integers that are multiples of a product $d$ of primes
and have no other prime factor below a small power $w$ of $q$. Sifting by the primes below $w$ with Brun's weights and
splitting the primes of $d$ into two groups gives sums of the kind of Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIB}, which yields the following
lemma.

\begin{lemma}[the leaves by the bilinear estimate]\label{lem:leaves}
Let $L > 2$; let $\xi$, $c$, $\delta'$ and $\kappa$ be reals with $\kappa > 0$, $0 \le \delta' \le \min(\kappa/4, 1/8)$,
$\xi \le L - 2$, $-L \le c$ and $\max(c, 0) \le \xi - \delta' - \kappa$; and let $\varepsilon_w > 0$, $\Lambda_0 \ge 0$,
integers $K_1$, $K_2$ and $J_0$, and an even $k \ge 2$ with $2C_0^2 \le (k + 1)(k + 2)$ and $(2k + 4)\varepsilon_w \le
1/16$. There are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$,
with $w = \lceil q^{\varepsilon_w} \rceil$. Let $\mathbf y_1$ and $\mathbf y_2$ run over sets of strictly decreasing
$K_1$- and $K_2$-tuples of primes in $[w, N_2]$, cut out by at most $J_0$ comparisons, each with a pivot and with its
form at most $\Lambda_0 \log q$, and suppose that wherever the comparisons hold, the products $d_1 = \prod \mathbf y_1$
and $d_2 = \prod \mathbf y_2$ satisfy
\[
  d_1 w^{2k + 4} \le q^{L - 1 - \xi}, \qquad d_1 w^{2k + 4} d_2^2 \le q^{L + c},
  \qquad q^{L - 1 - \delta'} \le d_1 d_2.
\]
Then, with $d = d_1 d_2$,
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{\mathbf y_1, \mathbf y_2} \Bigl(S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr)\Bigr|
  \le \frac{\zeta_k (N_2 - N_1)}{\varphi(q) \log w} \sum_{\mathbf y_1, \mathbf y_2} \frac1d + \frac{q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

The proof is in \S S3. The first term is the sieve's: Brun's weights lose the factor $\zeta_k$ of the main term. The
factor $w^{2k + 4}$ bounds the moduli of Brun's weights, which join $d_1$, the part that Parseval's identity averages.
In exponents of $x$, with $d_1 = x^{a_1}$, $d_2 = x^{a_2}$ and $\tau = 1/L$, and up to the margins, the leaves of the
lemma are those with
\[
  a_1 < 1 - \tau, \qquad \max(a_1, \tau) + a_1 + 2a_2 < 2 - \tau,
\]
that is $2a_1 + 2a_2 < 2 - \tau$ for $a_1 \ge \tau$ and $a_1 + 2a_2 < 2 - 2\tau$ for $a_1 \le \tau$: the second condition
is that of $\max(c, 0) < \xi$, with $\xi = L - 1 - \max(L a_1, 1)$ the room that $d_1$ leaves below $x/q$ and $c = L(a_1 +
2a_2 - 1)$.

\subsection{Type~II in two variables}

Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} bounds products of two Dirichlet polynomials and prime sums. The sieve needs sums over
$\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ of integers $n = m_1 m_2 p_1 \cdots p_K$, with the two variables $m_1$ and $m_2$ in the
region of the theorem and the primes cut out by comparisons. Throughout the subsection a region $\mathcal R$ is fixed on
which Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} holds at $L$: its conclusion, for the lengths $P_1$ and $P_2$ with
$(\log_q P_1, \log_q P_2) \in \mathcal R$, is the hypothesis of what follows.

Fix a smooth function $f_0 \ge 0$ supported in $[1/2, 2]$, integers $K_0 \ge 1$ and $J_0 \ge 0$, and reals $e_1 > 0$ and
$E_0, \Lambda_0 \ge 0$. A \emph{family in two variables} consists of
\begin{itemize}
\item integers $P_1, P_2 \ge 1$ with $(\log_q P_1, \log_q P_2) \in \mathcal R$, sets $S_1 \subseteq (P_1, 2P_1]$ and
  $S_2 \subseteq (P_2, 2P_2]$ of integers, and coefficients $a_{m_1}$ and $b_{m_2}$ with $|a_{m_1}| \le A_0$ and
  $|b_{m_2}| \le B_0$;
\item between $1$ and $K_0$ primes $p_i \le 2Q_i$ with weights $f_0(p_i/Q_i)$, at scales $Q_i \ge q^{e_1}$ with $Q_1
  \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/(P_1 P_2)$;
\item at most $J_0$ comparisons, conditions on $(m_1, m_2, p)$ that hold when $\ell_j > 0$ and fail when $\ell_j < 0$,
  where $\ell_j = u_j(m_1) + v_j(m_2) + \sum_i e_{ji} \log p_i$ with real functions $u_j$ and $v_j$,
  $|e_{ji}| \le E_0$, and $\ell_j \le \Lambda_0 \log q$ for all $m_1 \in S_1$, $m_2 \in S_2$ and primes $p_i \le 2Q_i$;
  each has a pivot, an index $i$ with $e_{ji} = \pm 1$.
\end{itemize}
These comparisons are more general than those of tuples of primes: their coefficients are real, and their constant terms
are functions of the two variables. The family's sum over $\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ is the sum of $a_{m_1}
b_{m_2} \prod_i f_0(p_i/Q_i)\, \vartheta(m_1 m_2 p_1 \cdots p_K)$ over the $(m_1, m_2, p)$ that satisfy the comparisons.
The integers $m_1 m_2 p_1 \cdots p_K$ need not lie in $(x, 2x]$; where they must, two comparisons say so.

\begin{lemma}[families in two variables]\label{lem:family-two}
% Lean: SieveFamilyTwo.typeII_sharp_two (from SieveFamilyGen.sharp_gen and SieveFamilyTwo.box_two)
Given $f_0$, $K_0$, $J_0$, $e_1$, $E_0$ and $\Lambda_0$, there are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that for every $q \ge
q_0$, every $a$ prime to $q$ and every family in two variables, its sum over $\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ is at
most $A_0 B_0 q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ in absolute value.
\end{lemma}

The proof is in \S S2. By orthogonality the family's sum is a sum over the non-trivial characters. Replacing each
comparison by a smooth cut-off of its form makes the family smooth, and Fourier inversion separates the variables: at
each frequency the phase is a phase on $m_1$, plus one on $m_2$, plus one on each prime, so the twisted sum over the
pairs $(m_1, m_2)$ is the product $W_1(\chi) W_2(\chi)$ of two Dirichlet polynomials, and the twisted sums over the
primes are prime sums $S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} bounds the smooth family at each frequency of size
at most a small power of $q$, and the decay of the cut-offs' Fourier transforms handles the larger ones. The difference
between the sharp and the smooth comparisons lies near the boundaries $\ell_j = 0$; the proof bounds it by families of
the same kind, again through Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, and by a count along the pivot.

\begin{lemma}[families in two variables with sharp prime ranges]\label{lem:family-sharp-two}
% Lean: SieveUnwTwo.typeII_unw_two (from SieveUnwGen.unw_gen and SieveFamilyTwo.box_two)
Given integers $K_0 \ge 1$ and $J_0 \ge 0$, and reals $e_1 > 0$ and $E_0, \Lambda_1 \ge 0$, there are $\delta > 0$ and
$q_0$ such that the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let $m_1$ and $m_2$ run over sets
$S_1 \subseteq (P_1, P_1']$ and $S_2 \subseteq (P_2, P_2']$ of integers, for integers $P_1, P_2 \ge 1$ and $P_1', P_2'
\le 2q^L$ such that $\mathcal R$ contains the rectangle $[\log_q P_1, \log_q P_1'] \times [\log_q P_2, \log_q P_2']$,
with coefficients $|a_{m_1}| \le A_0$ and $|b_{m_2}| \le B_0$, and let $1 \le K \le K_0$ primes $p_1, \dots, p_K$ run
over all the primes up to $N_2$. Let $J \le J_0$ comparisons as in a family in two variables, with forms $\ell_j =
u_j(m_1) + v_j(m_2) + \sum_i e_{ji} \log p_i$, $|e_{ji}| \le E_0$, $u_j(m_1) + v_j(m_2) \le \Lambda_1
\log q$ on $S_1 \times S_2$, and pivots, cut out the $(m_1, m_2, p)$, and suppose that wherever they all hold, for $m_1
\in S_1$, $m_2 \in S_2$ and any primes $p_i$, every $p_i \ge 2q^{e_1}$ and $m_1 m_2 p_1 \cdots p_K \le 2q^L$. Then the
sum of $a_{m_1} b_{m_2}\, \vartheta(m_1 m_2 p_1 \cdots p_K)$ over the $(m_1, m_2, p)$ that satisfy the comparisons is at
most $A_0 B_0 q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ in absolute value.
\end{lemma}

The proof is in \S S2. The pairs $(m_1, m_2)$ are cut into the pairs of dyadic blocks $(P_1 2^{b_1}, P_1 2^{b_1 + 1}]
\times (P_2 2^{b_2}, P_2 2^{b_2 + 1}]$, at most $(3 + 4 \log q)^2$ of them since $L \le 5/2$, and each prime variable by
the smooth partition of unity $\sum_t f_0(y/2^t) = 1$ of the function $f_0(y) = F(y) - F(y/2)$ of \S S2 into at
most $3 + 4 \log q$ dyadic scales $Q_i = 2^{t_i}$. A piece
with some $Q_i < q^{e_1}$, or with $Q_1 \cdots Q_K > 2^{K_0 + 1} q^L/(P_1 2^{b_1} P_2 2^{b_2})$, vanishes, since the
comparisons force $p_i \ge 2q^{e_1}$ and $m_1 m_2 p_1 \cdots p_K \le 2q^L$. Every other piece is a family in two
variables at the lower ends $(P_1 2^{b_1}, P_2 2^{b_2})$ of its block, whose exponents lie in the rectangle when the
block is not empty, and Lemma~\ref{lem:family-two}, with $K_0 + 1$ in place of $K_0$ and $\Lambda_1 + K_0 E_0 (L + 3)$
in place of $\Lambda_0$, bounds it; the number of pieces, a power of $\log q$, is absorbed by a power of $q$. The region
is asked on the whole rectangle of the two ranges' exponents; Lemma~\ref{lem:chains-two} asks it only on the part that
the product $m_1 m_2$ allows.

In both lemmas every comparison has a prime as a pivot, and its form separates into a function of $m_1$, one of $m_2$
and the logarithms of the primes. A condition on $m_1$ alone, or on $m_2$ alone, can go into the coefficients. A
comparison between $m_1$ and $m_2$ alone has no pivot, and its cut-off does not separate into a phase on each variable;
the next lemma keeps the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} under such conditions, at the cost of a power of $\log q$.

\begin{lemma}[conditions between the two variables]\label{lem:cross}
Let $X_1$ and $X_2$ be finite sets, $\Xi$ a function on $X_1 \times X_2$, $F_\chi$ and $G_\chi$ functions on $X_1$ and on
$X_2$ for each $\chi$ in a finite set, $W \ge 0$ a weight on that set, and $C \ge 0$ such that
\[
  \sum_\chi \Bigl|\sum_{i \in X_1} \sum_{j \in X_2} f_i g_j \Xi(i, j) F_\chi(i) G_\chi(j)\Bigr| W(\chi)
  \le C \|f\|_2 \|g\|_2
\]
for all $f$ and $g$. Let $u_1, \dots, u_k$ be real functions on $X_1$ and $v_1, \dots, v_k$ real functions on $X_2$,
and let $|X_2| < 2^N$. Then the same holds with $(N + 1)^k C$ in place of $C$ and $\Xi(i, j) \prod_l \mathbf 1[u_l(i)
\le v_l(j)]$ in place of $\Xi(i, j)$, or with $<$ in place of $\le$ in any of the conditions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to take one condition, $k = 1$, and to apply it $k$ times, each time with the kernel that the conditions
before it have made. Replacing $u(i)$ and $v(j)$ by their ranks
among the values of $v$ on $X_2$ (the number of values below $u(i)$, or at most $u(i)$ for a strict condition, and below
$v(j)$), the condition becomes $u(i) \le v(j)$ with integers $0 \le u, v < 2^N$. Induct on $N$; for $N = 0$ the
condition always holds. For $N \ge 1$ put $d = 2^{N - 1}$ and split $f = f_0 + f_1$ by $u < d$ or $u \ge d$, and $g =
g_0 + g_1$ by $v < d$ or $v \ge d$. Then $\mathbf 1[u \le v]$ is $1$ on the pairs $(f_0, g_1)$, $0$ on the pairs
$(f_1, g_0)$, and $\mathbf 1[u \bmod d \le v \bmod d]$ on the pairs $(f_0, g_0)$ and $(f_1, g_1)$. The first part is at
most $C \|f_0\| \|g_1\|$ by the hypothesis, and each of the other two at most $NC$ times the product of the norms, by
the induction applied to the remainders mod $d$. With $\|f_0\| \|g_1\| \le \|f\| \|g\|$ and $\|f_0\| \|g_0\| + \|f_1\|
\|g_1\| \le \|f\| \|g\|$ by Cauchy--Schwarz, the total is at most $(N + 1) C \|f\| \|g\|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[chains in two variables]\label{lem:chains-two}
Fix $e_0 > 0$, integers $K$ and $J_0$, and $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$. There are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that the following
holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let $\mathcal S_1$ and $\mathcal S_2$ be disjoint subsets of
$\{1, \dots, K\}$ whose union is not all of it; let $\mathbf z = (z_1, \dots, z_K)$ run over the $K$-tuples of primes up
to $N_2$ that satisfy at most $J_0$ comparisons, of any kind, with constant terms at most $\Lambda_1 \log q$; and let
$P_1, P_1', P_2, P_2', M_{\mathrm{lo}}, M_{\mathrm{hi}}$ be integers with $P_1, P_2, M_{\mathrm{lo}} \ge 1$ and
$M_{\mathrm{hi}} \le 2q^L$. Suppose that every tuple of primes that satisfies the comparisons has $z_t \ge q^{e_0}$ for
every $t$, $P_1 < \prod_{\mathcal S_1} z_t \le P_1'$, $P_2 < \prod_{\mathcal S_2} z_t \le P_2'$,
$M_{\mathrm{lo}} < \prod_{\mathcal S_1 \cup \mathcal S_2} z_t \le M_{\mathrm{hi}}$ and $\prod_t z_t \le 2q^L$; and that
$(m_1, m_2) \in \mathcal R$ for every $m_1 \in [\log_q P_1, \log_q P_1']$ and $m_2 \in [\log_q P_2, \log_q P_2']$ with
$M_{\mathrm{lo}} \le 4q^{m_1 + m_2}$ and $q^{m_1 + m_2} \le M_{\mathrm{hi}}$. Then
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{\mathbf z} \vartheta(z_1 \cdots z_K)\Bigr| \le \frac{q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

The two variables are $m_1 = \prod_{\mathcal S_1} z_t$ and $m_2 = \prod_{\mathcal S_2} z_t$, and the rest, the product
of the other primes, at least one, supplies the prime sums of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. The tuples need neither be
ordered nor have distinct entries. The band $(M_{\mathrm{lo}}, M_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ is the range of $m_1 m_2$, the integer
divided by the rest; the region is asked only on the part of the rectangle of the two lengths that the band allows,
since $\mathcal R$ reads the rest's exponent as $L - m_1 - m_2$.

\begin{proof}
Write every tuple as its part on $\mathcal S_1 \cup \mathcal S_2$ and its part on the rest $\mathcal S_r$, the other
indices. A comparison with a nonzero coefficient at an index of $\mathcal S_r$ has a prime of the rest as a pivot; the
others, the band's two among them, are conditions between the two variables of the kind of Lemma~\ref{lem:cross}, each
of the form $u(\mathbf z_1) \le v(\mathbf z_2)$ or $<$ with $u = -c - \sum_{\mathcal S_1} e_t \log z_t$ and $v =
\sum_{\mathcal S_2} e_t \log z_t$. Group the parts on $\mathcal S_1$ and on $\mathcal S_2$ by their products, $m_1$ and
$m_2$: a product has at most $(K + 1)^K$ tuples, since their entries are $1$ or prime factors of the product, so the
coefficients of the two Dirichlet polynomials are at most that in size, and they lie in dyadic blocks $(P_\iota 2^b,
P_\iota 2^{b + 1}]$, at most $(3 + 4\log q)^2$ pairs of them. On a pair of blocks with a pair $(m_1, m_2)$ in the band,
the blocks' lower ends satisfy the band's condition up to the factor $4$, so the region holds there; a pair of blocks
with no such pair contributes nothing. On each pair of blocks the family is then a family of the kind of
Lemma~\ref{lem:family-two} with the two polynomials over the blocks, the primes of the rest and the pivoted comparisons,
and with the cross conditions in the kernel; at each frequency of the separation of variables (\S S2) the bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} holds with the cross conditions by Lemma~\ref{lem:cross}, at the cost of a factor $(N + 1)^{J_0
+ 2}$ with $N = \lfloor \log_2((K + 1)^K \lfloor 2q^L \rfloor) \rfloor + 1$, a power of $\log q$. Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp-two}'s
argument (\S S2) then bounds each pair of blocks by $q^{L - \delta_1}/\varphi(q)$ times a power of $\log q$, and
$\delta = \delta_1/2$ will do for large $q$.
\end{proof}

The sieve meets these sums as the terms of its decomposition, sums of $S(\mathcal A_r, z)$ over chains of primes. The
next lemma writes such a term as chains in two variables: the integers counted for a chain $r = r_1 \cdots r_k$ are $n =
rm$ with $m$ free of prime factors below the last prime, and $m$ is a product of at most $\lceil (L + 1)/e_0 \rceil$
primes.

\begin{lemma}[a term in two variables]\label{lem:term-two}
Fix $e_0 > 0$, integers $k \ge 1$ and $J_0$, and $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$. There are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that the
following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let $\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal S_r$ be disjoint
subsets of $\{1, \dots, k\}$ with $\mathcal S_r \ne \emptyset$; let $(r_1, \dots, r_k)$ run over the $k$-tuples of
primes up to $N_2$ that satisfy at most $J_0$ comparisons with constant terms at most $\Lambda_1 \log q$; and let $A,
B, R_-, R_+$ be integers with $A, B, R_+ \ge 1$ and $B R_+ \le N_1$. Suppose that every tuple of primes that satisfies
the comparisons is strictly decreasing, has $r_i \ge q^{e_0}$ for every $i$, $A < \prod_{\mathcal S} r_i \le B$ and
$R_- < \prod_{\mathcal S_r} r_i \le R_+$; and that $(m_1, m_2) \in \mathcal R$ for every $m_1 \in [\log_q A, \log_q B]$
and every $m_2$ with $\lfloor N_1/R_+ \rfloor \le 4q^{m_1 + m_2}$ and $q^{m_1 + m_2} \le \lfloor N_2/(R_- + 1)
\rfloor$. Then, with $r = r_1 \cdots r_k$,
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{(r_1, \dots, r_k)} \Bigl(S(\mathcal A_r, r_k) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_r, r_k)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr)\Bigr|
  \le \frac{q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The cofactor $m = n/r$ of an integer counted for the chain has no prime factor below $r_k \ge q^{e_0}$ and is at most
$2x \le q^{L + 1}$, so it is the product of a unique non-increasing tuple $y_1 \ge \dots \ge y_s \ge r_k$ of primes with
$s \le s_1 = \lceil (L + 1)/e_0 \rceil$. For each $s$, the pairs $(\mathbf r, \mathbf y)$ are the $(k + s)$-tuples of
primes that satisfy the given comparisons on $\mathbf r$, the comparisons $y_b \le y_a$ for $a \le b$ and $y_a \ge r_k$,
and $N_1 < \prod \mathbf r \prod \mathbf y \le N_2$, at most $J_0 + s_1^2 + s_1 + 2$ comparisons in all, with constant
terms at most $(\Lambda_1 + L + 1)\log q$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:chains-two} to them with $\mathcal S_1 = \mathcal S$,
$\mathcal S_2$ the other primes of the chain and all the primes $y_a$, so that the rest is $\prod_{\mathcal S_r} r_i$,
with $P_1 = A$, $P_1' = B$, $P_2 = \lfloor N_1/(B R_+) \rfloor$, $P_2' = \lfloor N_2/((A + 1)(R_- + 1)) \rfloor$, and
the band $M_{\mathrm{lo}} = \lfloor N_1/R_+ \rfloor$, $M_{\mathrm{hi}} = \lfloor N_2/(R_- + 1) \rfloor$, the range of
$m_1 m_2 = n/\prod_{\mathcal S_r} r_i$. The region's hypothesis is the one assumed. There are at most $s_1 + 1$ values
of $s$, and $\delta = \delta_1/2$ will do for large $q$.
\end{proof}

So a term is evaluated outright when a set $\mathcal S$ of its primes, the first variable, and a disjoint nonempty set
$\mathcal S_r$, the rest, have products in ranges on which the region holds for every second variable, the integer
divided by the two.

\subsection{Harman's comparison in two variables}

\begin{proposition}[Harman's comparison in two variables]\label{prop:harman-two}
Fix integers $j \ge 1$ and $J_0$, $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$, an exponent $a_0 > 0$, exponents $l_i \le h_i$ for $1 \le i \le j$,
disjoint sets $\mathcal S_0, \mathcal S_r \subseteq \{1, \dots, j\}$ with $\mathcal S_r \ne \emptyset$, a set
$\mathcal S_1 \subseteq \{1, \dots, j\}$, a side $\iota \in \{1, 2\}$ and reals $t$, $u$, $v$, $\rho_1$, $\rho_2$,
$\varepsilon > 0$ and $\eta > 0$ with
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $0 < u$, $u + a_0 < v$, $u - \sum_{\mathcal S_0} l_i \le t$ and $\sum_{\mathcal S_0} h_i < v$;
\item[(b)] $0 \le \rho_1 \le \sum_{\mathcal S_r} l_i$, $\sum_{\mathcal S_r} h_i \le \rho_2$ and $v + \rho_2 < L$;
\item[(c)] $(m_1, m_2) \in \mathcal R$ whenever $u \le m_1 \le v$ and $L - \rho_2 - \varepsilon \le m_1 + m_2 \le L -
  \rho_1 + \varepsilon$;
\item[(d)] with $D_1 = \sum_{\mathcal S_1} h_i + \mathbf 1[\iota = 1]\, t$ and $D_2 = \sum_{i \notin \mathcal S_1} h_i +
  \mathbf 1[\iota = 2]\, t$,
  \[
    D_1 < L - 1, \qquad \max(D_1, 1) + D_1 + 2D_2 < 2L - 1.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
There are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let
$(r_1, \dots, r_j)$ run over the $j$-tuples of primes up to $N_2$ that satisfy at most $J_0$ comparisons, with constant
terms at most $\Lambda_1 \log q$, which force $r_1 > \dots > r_j \ge z_0 = \lceil q^{a_0} \rceil$ and $q^{l_i} \le r_i \le
q^{h_i}$ for every $i$. Then, with $r = r_1 \cdots r_j$,
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{(r_1, \dots, r_j)} \Bigl(S(\mathcal A_r, z_0) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_r, z_0)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr)\Bigr|
  \le \frac{\eta\, q^L}{\varphi(q) \log q} + \frac{q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
\end{proposition}

The proof (\S S3) iterates Buchstab's identity \cite{buchstab} from $z_0$ down to a small power $w$ of $q$, stopping a
branch as soon as the product $r_{\mathcal S_0} = \prod_{\mathcal S_0} r_i$ times its new primes exceeds $\lceil q^u \rceil$. A stop's
first variable, $r_{\mathcal S_0}$ times its new primes, then lies in $(q^u, q^v]$, since its last prime is below $z_0$ and $u + a_0 <
v$; its rest is $\prod_{\mathcal S_r} r_i$, in $[q^{\rho_1}, q^{\rho_2}]$; and Lemma~\ref{lem:term-two}, with the
region's hypothesis from (c), evaluates the stops. The tuples with $r_{\mathcal S_0}$ already above $\lceil q^u \rceil$ are in the
first variable's range, since $r_{\mathcal S_0} \le q^{\sum h_i} < q^v$, and one step of Buchstab's identity and
Lemma~\ref{lem:term-two} evaluate them. The leaves are sifted only by the primes below $w$, and their new primes have a
product below $4q^t$; split into $d_1$, the primes of $\mathcal S_1$ and, if $\iota = 1$, the new primes, and $d_2$, the
rest, they satisfy $d_1 \le 4q^{D_1}$ and $d_2 \le 4q^{D_2}$, and (d) is the condition of Lemma~\ref{lem:leaves} with
margins. The leaves with $d$ at most $q^{L - 1 - 4c'}$, for a small $c' > 0$, are counted by Lemma~\ref{lem:type1}.
Brun's sieve \cite{brun}, in the form of the fundamental lemma of sieve theory \cite{friedlander-iwaniec},
\cite[Chapter~6]{iwaniec-kowalski}, gives the main terms of the leaves an error that the elementary bound
$\prod_{p < w}(1 - 1/p) \le 1/\log w$, Mertens' theorem for the sum over the leaves and the choice of the sieve's
level make at most $\eta q^L/(\varphi(q) \log q)$.

The rest of a stop is a set of the term's own primes. A term whose primes are all too large for a rest, or that has no
prime at all, as the first term of the sieve, takes its rest from its own tree: from the small primes that Buchstab's
identity brings out.

\begin{proposition}[Harman's comparison with the rest among the tree's primes]\label{prop:harman-fill}
Fix integers $j \ge 0$ and $J_0$, $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$, an exponent $a_0 > 0$, exponents $l_i \le h_i$ for $1 \le i \le
j$, a set $\mathcal S_0 \subseteq \{1, \dots, j\}$, a set $\mathcal S_1 \subseteq \{1, \dots, j\}$, a side
$\iota \in \{1, 2\}$ and reals $t \ge 0$, $u$, $v$, $\tau$, $\rho_2$, $\varepsilon > 0$ and $\eta > 0$ with
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $0 < u$, $u + a_0 < v$, $u - \sum_{\mathcal S_0} l_i \le t$ and $\sum_{\mathcal S_0} h_i < v$;
\item[(b)] $\tau > 0$, $\tau + a_0 < \rho_2$ and $v + \rho_2 < L$;
\item[(c)] $(m_1, m_2) \in \mathcal R$ whenever $u \le m_1 \le v$ and $L - \rho_2 - \varepsilon \le m_1 + m_2 \le L -
  \tau + \varepsilon$;
\item[(d)] with $D_1 = \sum_{\mathcal S_1} h_i + \mathbf 1[\iota = 1](t + \rho_2)$ and $D_2 = \sum_{i \notin \mathcal
  S_1} h_i + \mathbf 1[\iota = 2](t + \rho_2)$, $D_1 < L - 1$ and $\max(D_1, 1) + D_1 + 2D_2 < 2L - 1$.
\end{enumerate}
Then the conclusion of Proposition~\ref{prop:harman-two} holds. For $j = 0$ the sum is the single term
$S(\mathcal A, z_0) - S(\mathcal B, z_0)/\varphi(q)$.
\end{proposition}

The proof (\S S3) has two trees. The first is that of Proposition~\ref{prop:harman-two}: it brings the product of
$\mathcal S_0$ and of the largest small primes into the first variable's range. Each of its stops $S(\mathcal A_W, p)$
is not evaluated but sifted further, by Buchstab's tree below its last prime $p$, stopped as soon as that second tree's
own product reaches $\lceil q^\tau \rceil$; this product, in $[\lceil q^\tau \rceil, \lceil q^\tau \rceil \lceil
q^{a_0} \rceil)$, inside $[q^\tau, q^{\rho_2}]$ for large $q$ by (b), is the rest, and Lemma~\ref{lem:term-two}
evaluates the second stops. A tuple whose first variable is already in its range has the second tree alone, from
$z_0$. The rest's range no longer depends on the term, so (c) asks the region for every rest in $[\tau, \rho_2]$, and the
leaves' new primes, of both trees, have a product below $4q^{t + \rho_2}$, which is (d). The leaves pass through two
trees, which costs one more product $\prod (1 + 1/p)$ over the primes of the second in the sum over them, and so one
more factor $1/\varepsilon_w$; the level $k$ of Brun's sieve is chosen with $(2k + 4)^3 \zeta_k$ small in place of $(2k +
4)^2 \zeta_k$.

\section{The decomposition}\label{sec:decomp}

This section bounds $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ from below by a decomposition chosen term by term. Buchstab's
identity writes $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ as a signed sum of terms, each a sum of $S(\mathcal A_r, z)$ over the
products $r$ of primes whose exponents lie in a \emph{box} of ranges. A term is evaluated, that is compared with its
version in $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$, by the results of Section~\ref{sec:type1}; or decomposed further; or, if it has the
sign $+$, dropped, which costs its version in $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$; or, if it has the sign $-$, bounded by a reversal
(\S\ref{sec:reversal}). A \emph{tree} records these choices, and its \emph{cost}, an integer computed exactly, bounds
what they lose. Theorem~\ref{thm:tree} turns a tree of cost below $\mathcal K = 2^{60}$ into Linnik's theorem with
exponent $L$, and Section~\ref{sec:certificate} finds one at $L = 43/20$. Throughout, $2 < L \le 5/2$, $x = q^L$,
$\mathcal R$ is the region of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, $\lambda = 27637/62500$ is its constant, and
\[
  X = \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x},
\]
the size of the main term $S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})/\varphi(q)$.

\subsection{Boxes and their terms}

Exponents are measured in units of $1/D$ of exponents of $x$, $D = 2^{40}$: an integer $e$ stands for the exponent
$e/D$ of $x$, which is the exponent $Le/D$ of $q$. A \emph{cell} is a pair of integers $0 \le l \le h$, and a prime $p$
lies in it if $x^{l/D} \le p < x^{h/D}$. A \emph{box} $\mathfrak b$ of $k \ge 0$ primes is a list of $k$ cells $(l_1,
h_1), \dots, (l_k, h_k)$, and its \emph{family} $\mathcal F(\mathfrak b)$ is the set of the tuples $(p_1, \dots, p_k)$ of
primes up to $N_2$ with $p_1 > p_2 > \dots > p_k$ and $p_i$ in the $i$-th cell. The cells may overlap; the order of the
primes is a condition on the tuples, not a consequence of the cells. The box $\emptyset$ of no prime has the one tuple
$()$, of product $1$. For a set $\mathcal S$ of indices let $l(\mathcal S) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal S} l_i$ and
$h(\mathcal S) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal S} h_i$, so that $x^{l(\mathcal S)/D} \le \prod_{i \in \mathcal S} p_i <
x^{h(\mathcal S)/D}$ on the family when $\mathcal S \ne \emptyset$; $l(\mathfrak b)$ and $h(\mathfrak b)$ are the sums
over all the indices. $\mathfrak b[i \mapsto c]$ is $\mathfrak b$ with its $i$-th cell replaced by the cell $c$, and
$\mathfrak b \oplus c$ is $\mathfrak b$ with one more prime $p_{k + 1}$, in the cell $c$.

A \emph{floor} is an integer $f \ge 0$. It stands for the level $z_f = \lceil x^{f/D} \rceil$, and an integer is at least
$z_f$ exactly when it is at least $x^{f/D}$. Write $P^-(n)$ for the least prime factor of $n$, with $P^-(1) = \infty$.
For $\mathcal C = \mathcal A$ or $\mathcal B$, the \emph{term} of a box of $k \ge 1$ primes and its \emph{terms at the
floor} $f$ are
\[
  T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b) = \sum_{\mathcal F(\mathfrak b)} S(\mathcal C_r, p_k), \qquad
  T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b; f) = \sum_{\mathcal F(\mathfrak b)} S(\mathcal C_r, z_f), \qquad r = p_1 \cdots p_k,
\]
and $T(\mathcal C, \emptyset; f) = S(\mathcal C, z_f)$. Since every prime factor of $r$ is at least $p_k$, the term
counts the $n = rm \in \mathcal C$ whose cofactor $m$ has $P^-(m) \ge p_k$; the cofactor $m = 1$ counts, and $p_k$ may
divide $m$. The difference between $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ is
\[
  \Delta(\mathfrak b) = T(\mathcal A, \mathfrak b) - \frac{T(\mathcal B, \mathfrak b)}{\varphi(q)}
  = \sum_{\mathcal F(\mathfrak b)} \sum_{\substack{N_1 < n \le N_2 \\ r \mid n,\ P^-(n) \ge p_k}} \vartheta(n),
\]
and $\Delta(\mathfrak b; f)$ is the same with $P^-(n) \ge z_f$.

Every estimate below is uniform in the class. A quantity $E(q, a)$ is $o(X)$ if for every $\eta > 0$ there is $q_0$
with $|E(q, a)| \le \eta X$ for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. The term of $\mathfrak b$ is
\emph{evaluated} if $\Delta(\mathfrak b) = o(X)$, and its terms at the floor $f$ are if $\Delta(\mathfrak b; f) = o(X)$.
For a sign $\varepsilon \in \{+1, -1\}$ and a real $C \ge 0$, the term of $\mathfrak b$ \emph{with the sign
$\varepsilon$ is bounded at $C$} if for every $\eta > 0$ there is $q_0$ with
\[
  \varepsilon\, \Delta(\mathfrak b) \ge -(C + \eta) X
\]
for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. With the sign $+$ this is the lower bound $T(\mathcal A, \mathfrak b)
\ge T(\mathcal B, \mathfrak b)/\varphi(q) - (C + o(1))X$ that a term entering the sieve with the sign $+$ needs, and with
the sign $-$ the upper bound that a term entering with the sign $-$ needs.

\emph{The root.} Let $f_* \le D/2 + 1$ be a floor. The primes of $[z_{f_*}, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ are those of the cell
$(f_*, D/2 + 1)$, and Buchstab's identity over them (Lemma~\ref{lem:box-identities}(d)) is
\[
  S(\mathcal C, z_{\mathrm{top}}) = S(\mathcal C, z_{f_*}) - T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b_0), \qquad
  \mathfrak b_0 = \bigl((f_*, D/2 + 1)\bigr).
\]
So the box $\mathfrak b_0$ of the first prime enters the sieve with the sign $-$, and each further step of Buchstab's
identity flips the sign: below the root a box of $k$ primes has the sign $(-1)^k$.

\subsection{Buchstab's identity on boxes, and trees}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:box-identities}
% Lean: SieveAd.famI_cut, SieveAd.famI_buch, SieveAd.famI_vanish_box, SieveAd.Ttop_eq, SieveDecomp.buchstab_pt
Let $\mathfrak b$ be a box of $k \ge 1$ primes and $\mathcal C = \mathcal A$ or $\mathcal B$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For $1 \le i \le k$ and $l_i \le e \le h_i$,
  $T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b) = T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b[i \mapsto (l_i, e)]) + T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b[i \mapsto (e, h_i)])$.
\item[(b)] For a floor $f \le l_k$, $T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b) = T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b; f) - T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b
  \oplus (f, h_k))$.
\item[(c)] If $h(\mathfrak b) + 1 \le D$ and $l(\mathfrak b) + l_k \ge D + 1$, then $T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b) = 0$ for
  large $q$.
\item[(d)] For a floor $f_* \le D/2 + 1$ and large $q$, $S(\mathcal C, z_{\mathrm{top}}) = S(\mathcal C, z_{f_*}) -
  T(\mathcal C, ((f_*, D/2 + 1)))$.
\end{enumerate}
So (a) and (b) hold for $\Delta$ in place of $T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) The ranges $[x^{l_i/D}, x^{e/D})$ and $[x^{e/D}, x^{h_i/D})$ partition that of the $i$-th prime. (b) Let $r = p_1
\cdots p_k$ for a tuple of the family, and $n$ a multiple of $r$ with $P^-(n) \ge z_f$. Either $P^-(n) \ge p_k$, or $p =
P^-(n)$ has $z_f \le p < p_k$; then $p \nmid r$, so $rp \mid n$ with $P^-(n) = p$, and $p$ is determined by $n$. So
\[
  \mathbf 1[r \mid n,\ P^-(n) \ge z_f] = \mathbf 1[r \mid n,\ P^-(n) \ge p_k]
  + \sum_{z_f \le p < p_k} \mathbf 1[rp \mid n,\ P^-(n) \ge p],
\]
and the tuples $(p_1, \dots, p_k, p)$ with $z_f \le p < p_k$ are the family of $\mathfrak b \oplus (f, h_k)$, since $p \ge
z_f$ means $p \ge x^{f/D}$, and $p < p_k$ implies $p < x^{h_k/D}$. (c) For large $q$, $x^{1/D} \ge 3$. Then $r <
x^{h(\mathfrak b)/D} \le x^{1 - 1/D} < N_1$, and a multiple $rm$ with $m > 1$ and $P^-(m) \ge p_k$ has $m \ge p_k$, so
$rm \ge x^{(l(\mathfrak b) + l_k)/D} \ge x^{1 + 1/D} > N_2$. (d) is the argument of (b) with $r = 1$ and $z_{\mathrm{top}}$
in place of $p_k$: an integer is below $z_{\mathrm{top}} = \lceil x^{(D/2 + 1)/D} \rceil$ exactly when it is below
$x^{(D/2 + 1)/D}$, and $z_{\mathrm{top}} \le N_2$.
\end{proof}

The cell of the new prime in (b) is $(f, h_k)$, not $(f, l_k)$: the new prime is below $p_k$, which ranges up to
$x^{h_k/D}$, and the order of the tuples keeps it there. So the cells of a box overlap after a step of Buchstab's
identity, and the identities are exact for the ordered tuples, while the bounds for dropped terms below sum over all the
tuples of the cells' primes, in any order.

\begin{definition}\label{def:tree}
% Lean: SieveAd.Tree, SieveAd.ok, SieveAd.cost, SieveAd.vanishOK, SieveAd.dropOK, SieveAd.dropBOK, SieveAd.splitOK; node (R): SieveAd.flipOK, SieveAd.flipCost, SieveAd.flipCostD, SieveAd.altFD
Let $\mathfrak b$ be a box of $k \ge 1$ primes and $\varepsilon$ a sign. A \emph{tree} on $\mathfrak b$ with the sign
$\varepsilon$ is one of the following nodes, with the trees below it. It \emph{passes} if the node's condition holds and
the trees below it pass. Its \emph{cost} is an integer, which stands for its multiple of $1/\mathcal K$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(V)] \emph{Empty}: $h(\mathfrak b) + 1 \le D$ and $l(\mathfrak b) + l_k \ge D + 1$. Cost $0$.
\item[(E)] \emph{Evaluated}: $\mathfrak b$ passes the test (I) of Definition~\ref{def:tests}. Cost $0$.
\item[(D)] \emph{Dropped}: $\varepsilon = +$, $0 < l_i < h_i$ for every $i$, and $h(\mathfrak b) \le D$. Cost
  $c_D(\mathfrak b)$ of (\ref{eq:cD}).
\item[(P)] \emph{Dropped for its products}: $\varepsilon = +$, $k \ge 2$, $0 < l_i < h_i$ for every $i$, $l(\mathfrak b)
  + l_k \ge D + 1$ and $h(\mathfrak b) - h_k < D$. Cost $c_P(\mathfrak b)$ of (\ref{eq:cD}).
\item[(S)] \emph{Cut} at an index $i$ and an integer $l_i < e < h_i$: a tree on $\mathfrak b[i \mapsto (l_i, e)]$ and
  one on $\mathfrak b[i \mapsto (e, h_i)]$, both with the sign $\varepsilon$. Cost the sum of theirs.
\item[(B)] \emph{Buchstab's identity} at a floor $f$: $0 < f \le l_k$, $\mathfrak b$ passes the test (J2) or (J3) of
  Definition~\ref{def:tests} at $f$, and a tree on $\mathfrak b \oplus (f, h_k)$ with the sign $-\varepsilon$. Cost its
  cost.
\item[(R)] \emph{Reversed}: $\varepsilon = -$, and a reversal of $\mathfrak b$ (Definition~\ref{def:reversal}). Cost
  $c_R$ of (\ref{eq:cR}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The cost of a tree is the sum of the costs of its dropped boxes and of its reversals: the empty and the evaluated boxes
cost nothing, and the errors $o(X)$ of the tests are not counted. A node (B) is Lemma~\ref{lem:box-identities}(b), with
its first term evaluated by the test, and a node (V) is (c). Only boxes of the sign $+$ are dropped, since dropping a
term uses $T(\mathcal A, \mathfrak b) \ge 0$; a box of the sign $-$ is evaluated, decomposed, cut or reversed.
Proposition~\ref{prop:tree-bound} shows that a tree that passes bounds the term of its box, with its sign, at its cost
over $\mathcal K$.

\subsection{The tests}

The tests are the conditions on a box's exponents under which Lemma~\ref{lem:term-two} and
Propositions~\ref{prop:harman-two} and~\ref{prop:harman-fill} apply to its terms. In the exponents $m_1$ of the first
variable and $\rho = L - m_1 - m_2$ of the rest, exponents of $q$, the region $\mathcal R$ is cut out by six linear
inequalities,
\begin{gather*}
  \rho \ge \tfrac1{2000}, \qquad m_1 \ge \tfrac23, \qquad m_1 + \rho \le L - \tfrac23, \\
  m_1 + \lambda \rho \ge 1 + \tfrac1{12500}, \qquad m_1 + (1 - \lambda)\rho \le L - 1 - \tfrac1{12500}, \\
  (1 - \lambda)\rho \le L - 2 - \tfrac1{12500},
\end{gather*}
which say that $\rho \ge 1/2000$, that $m_1, m_2 \ge 2/3$, and that $s_1$, $s_2$ and $s_1 + s_2$ are at most $\lambda\rho
- 1/12500$. So $\mathcal R$ holds on a rectangle of pairs $(m_1, \rho)$ when each inequality holds at the corner where it
is tightest. For integers $\rho_- \le \rho_+$, the \emph{window} $\mathcal W(\rho_-, \rho_+)$ is the set of the integers
$w$ such that $(m_1, L - m_1 - \rho) \in \mathcal R$ for $m_1 = Lw/D$ and every $\rho \in [L(\rho_- - 1)/D, L(\rho_+ +
1)/D]$: the first variables that the region allows with every rest of exponent within one unit of $[\rho_-, \rho_+]$. It
is empty unless the two inequalities in $\rho$ alone hold at $\rho = L(\rho_- - 1)/D$ and at $\rho = L(\rho_+ + 1)/D$,
and then it is the set of the integers of $[u, v]$, with
\begin{align*}
  u &= \max\Bigl(\Bigl\lceil \frac{2D}{3L} \Bigr\rceil,\ \Bigl\lceil \frac DL \Bigl(1 + \frac1{12500}\Bigr) -
  \lambda(\rho_- - 1) \Bigr\rceil\Bigr), \\
  v &= \min\Bigl(\Bigl\lfloor \frac DL \Bigl(L - \frac23\Bigr) \Bigr\rfloor - \rho_+ - 1,\\
  &\hphantom{{}= \min\Bigl(} \Bigl\lfloor \frac DL \Bigl(L - 1 - \frac1{12500}\Bigr) - (1 - \lambda)(\rho_+ + 1)
  \Bigr\rfloor\Bigr).
\end{align*}

\begin{definition}\label{def:tests}
% Lean: SieveAd.chkI2, SieveAd.okI2, SieveAd.chkJ2, SieveAd.okJ2, SieveAd.chkJ3, SieveAd.okJ3, SieveAd.uOf, SieveAd.vOf, SieveAd.fillLo, SieveAd.fillTests; the region: SieveTwoZone7D.Zone7d
Let $\mathfrak b$ be a box of $k \le 13$ primes. For a set $\mathcal S_1$ of its indices, a side $\iota \in \{1, 2\}$ and
an integer $g \ge 0$, the \emph{growth}, the \emph{leaves' condition} is
\[
  (\mathrm L) \qquad a_1 < 1 - \frac1L, \qquad a_1 + a_2 < 1 - \frac1{2L}, \qquad a_1 + 2a_2 < 2 - \frac2L,
\]
where $a_1 = (h(\mathcal S_1) + \mathbf 1[\iota = 1]\, g)/D$ and $a_2 = (h(\mathfrak b) - h(\mathcal S_1) + \mathbf
1[\iota = 2]\, g)/D$. The box passes
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(I)] the \emph{test of the estimate in two variables}, for disjoint sets $\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal S_r$ of
  indices, if $1 \le l_i \le h_i \le D$ for every $i$, and $(m_1, L - m_1 - \rho) \in \mathcal R$ for every $m_1 \in
  [L(l(\mathcal S) - 1)/D, Lh(\mathcal S)/D]$ and every $\rho \in [L(l(\mathcal S_r) - 1)/D, L(h(\mathcal S_r) + 1)/D]$;
\item[(J2)] the \emph{test of the comparison with the rest among the box's primes} at a floor $f$, for disjoint sets
  $\mathcal S_0$ and $\mathcal S_r$, a set $\mathcal S_1$ and a side $\iota$, if $0 < f \le l_i \le h_i \le D$ for every
  $i$, the window $\mathcal W(l(\mathcal S_r), h(\mathcal S_r)) = [u, v]$ has $u + f < v$ and $h(\mathcal S_0) < v$, and
  $(\mathrm L)$ holds with $g = \max(u - l(\mathcal S_0), 0)$;
\item[(J3)] the \emph{test of the comparison with the rest among the tree's primes} at a floor $f$, for sets $\mathcal
  S_0$ and $\mathcal S_1$ and a side $\iota$, if $0 < f \le l_i \le h_i \le D$ for every $i$, the window $\mathcal W(r_1,
  r_2) = [u, v]$ has $u + f < v$ and $h(\mathcal S_0) < v$, and $(\mathrm L)$ holds with $g = \max(u - l(\mathcal S_0),
  0) + r_2$; here $r_1$ is the least integer with $L(r_1 - 1)/D \ge 1/2000$, and $r_2 = r_1 + f + 1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

In (I) the first variable is the product of the primes of $\mathcal S$, the rest that of $\mathcal S_r$, and the second
variable all the rest of $n$: the other primes of the box and the cofactor. In (J2) and (J3) the first variable is the
product of the primes of $\mathcal S_0$ and of the primes below the floor that Harman's comparison brings out, until it
enters the window $[u, v]$. In (J2) the rest is the product of the primes of $\mathcal S_r$, and the window is the
largest that the region allows for it. In (J3) the rest is a product of the comparison's own primes below the floor,
from about $x^{r_1/D}$, where $r_1/D$ is about the least exponent that the region admits, up to about $x^{(r_1 + f)/D}$;
the window is the largest that the region allows for all such rests, and the leaves grow by the rest as well, which is
why $g$ has $r_2$ added. So (J3) needs no rest among the box's primes, and applies to the box of no prime, where (J2)
does not. The growth $g$ bounds the leaves' new primes, which go to the side $\iota$, and $(\mathrm L)$ implies the
leaves' rule of Lemma~\ref{lem:leaves} in exponents of $x$, $a_1 < 1 - 1/L$ and $\max(a_1, 1/L) + a_1 + 2a_2 < 2 - 1/L$.
The one-unit margins absorb the integer parts of the ranges. For rational $L$ each condition, multiplied through by its
denominators, is an inequality between integers, and the formalisation checks it so.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tests}
% Lean: SieveAd.evI_okI2, SieveAd.evI_two, SieveAd.chain2_exp, SieveAd.evJ_okJ2, SieveAd.evJ_two, SieveAd.evJ_fill, SieveAd.fillTests_sound; (c), with the comparisons SieveDecomp.Atom: SieveAd.evJ_fill_core, SieveAd.evJ_two_core, SieveAd.evI_twoE, SieveAd.evJ_twoE, SieveAd.evJ_fillE
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The term of a box of $k \ge 1$ primes that passes (I) is evaluated.
\item[(b)] The terms at the floor $f$ of a box that passes (J2) or (J3) at $f$ are evaluated.
\item[(c)] For every $J$, (b) holds uniformly when the family is cut by at most $J$ further comparisons, each of a
  prime with a threshold, of two primes, or of the product of some of the primes with a threshold, the thresholds in
  $[1, q^{L + 1}]$: $q_0$ does not depend on the comparisons. This holds also for boxes of more than $13$ primes that
  satisfy the conditions of (J2) or (J3). So does (a), for at most $40 - 3k$ such comparisons.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The family $\mathcal F(\mathfrak b)$ is the set of the $k$-tuples of primes up to $N_2$ that satisfy at most $3k \le 39$
comparisons, $2k$ for the cells and $k - 1$ for the order, each with a pivot and with constant terms at most $L \log q$.
Take $J_0 = 40$, or $J_0 = 3k + J$ for (c), and $\Lambda_1 = L + 1$.

(a) Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:term-two} with $e_0 = L/D$, since every prime is at least $x^{1/D}$, the sets $\mathcal S$ and
$\mathcal S_r$, and $A = \lceil x^{l(\mathcal S)/D} \rceil - 1$, $B = \lfloor x^{h(\mathcal S)/D} \rfloor$, $R_- =
\lceil x^{l(\mathcal S_r)/D} \rceil - 1$ and $R_+ = \lfloor x^{h(\mathcal S_r)/D} \rfloor$, so that $A < \prod_{\mathcal
S} p_i \le B$ and $R_- < \prod_{\mathcal S_r} p_i \le R_+$ on the family. The inequality $\rho \ge 1/2000$ at $L(l(\mathcal
S_r) - 1)/D$ makes $\mathcal S_r$ nonempty, and $m_1 + \rho \le L - 2/3$ at the corner $(Lh(\mathcal S)/D, L(h(\mathcal
S_r) + 1)/D)$ gives $h(\mathcal S) + h(\mathcal S_r) + 1 < D$, so $BR_+ \le x^{1 - 1/D} \le N_1$ for large $q$. For large
$q$, $\log_q A \ge L(l(\mathcal S) - 1)/D$, and an $m_2$ with $\lfloor N_1/R_+ \rfloor \le 4q^{m_1 + m_2}$ and $q^{m_1 +
m_2} \le \lfloor N_2/(R_- + 1) \rfloor$ has $L - m_1 - m_2$ in $[L(l(\mathcal S_r) - 1)/D, L(h(\mathcal S_r) + 1)/D]$,
since the factors $4$ and $2$ are below $x^{1/D}$. So the region's hypothesis of the lemma is (I), and its bound
$q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ is $o(X)$.

(b) For (J2), apply Proposition~\ref{prop:harman-two} with $j = k$, the exponents $Ll_i/D$ and $Lh_i/D$, $a_0 = Lf/D$,
the sets $\mathcal S_0$, $\mathcal S_r$ and $\mathcal S_1$, the side $\iota$, $Lu/D$, $Lv/D$ and $Lg/D$ in place of $u$,
$v$ and $t$, $\rho_1 = Ll(\mathcal S_r)/D$, $\rho_2 = Lh(\mathcal S_r)/D$, $\varepsilon = L/D$, and $\eta/(2L)$ in place
of $\eta$. The family's comparisons force $p_1 > \dots > p_k \ge z_f$, since a prime at least $x^{l_i/D} \ge x^{f/D}$ is
at least $z_f$. Hypothesis (a) of the proposition is $u > 0$, which $m_1 \ge 2/3$ gives, $u + f < v$, $g \ge u -
l(\mathcal S_0)$ and $h(\mathcal S_0) < v$;
(b) holds since $m_1 + \rho \le L - 2/3$ at the corner $(Lv/D, \rho_2 + \varepsilon)$; (c) asks the region for $m_1 \in
[Lu/D, Lv/D]$ and the rests in $[\rho_1 - \varepsilon, \rho_2 + \varepsilon]$, which is the definition of the window; and
(d) is $(\mathrm L)$: with $D_1 = La_1$ and $D_2 = La_2$, the first inequality of $(\mathrm L)$ is $D_1 < L - 1$, and
$\max(D_1, 1) + D_1 + 2D_2 < 2L - 1$ is the second if $D_1 \ge 1$ and the third if $D_1 \le 1$. The proposition's bound
is $\eta q^L/(2L\varphi(q) \log q) + q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q) = \eta X/2 + q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$, at most $\eta X$
for large $q$. For (J3), apply Proposition~\ref{prop:harman-fill} in the same way, with $\tau = Lr_1/D$ and $\rho_2 =
Lr_2/D$: $\tau + a_0 < \rho_2$ since $r_1 + f < r_2$, (c) asks the region for the rests in $[\tau - \varepsilon, \rho_2
+ \varepsilon]$, which is again the window's definition, and (d) has the growth $t + \rho_2$, which is $Lg/D$. For $k =
0$ the proposition's sum is $\Delta(\emptyset; f)$.

(c) Lemma~\ref{lem:term-two} and the propositions hold for every family of comparisons with the stated bounds, with $\delta$
and $q_0$ that depend only on $J_0$, $\Lambda_1$ and the exponents, and the propositions for chains of any length; the
family of (a) with $m$ further comparisons has $3k + m \le 40 = J_0$ of them.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dropped terms}

A term with the sign $+$ may be dropped: $T(\mathcal A, \mathfrak b) \ge 0$, so $\Delta(\mathfrak b) \ge -T(\mathcal B,
\mathfrak b)/\varphi(q)$, and the cost of the drop is a bound for $T(\mathcal B, \mathfrak b)/\varphi(q)$ in units of
$X$. The cofactors of its integers are rough numbers. Let $\Phi(y, z)$ be the number of integers in $(y, 2y]$ with no
prime factor below $z$, and let
\[
  \omega^*(u) = \begin{cases}
    0, & u < 1, \\
    1/u, & 1 \le u < 2, \\
    (1 + \log(u - 1))/u, & 2 \le u \le 3, \\
    0.565073, & u > 3.
  \end{cases}
\]
On $[1, 3]$ this is Buchstab's function $\omega$, whose maximum on $[2, 3]$ is $0.567143\ldots$, the root $t$ of
$t e^t = 1$, at $u = 1 + 1/t$, where $(u - 1)\log(u - 1) = 1$ \cite{cheer-goldston}; above $3$ it is the constant that
the induction of the next lemma needs.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rough}
% Lean: SieveRough.psi_le, SieveRough.Psi_le_buchstab, SieveRough.super_M, SieveOmegaB.omB_le_w1
For every $U$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, for all large integers $z$ and all $y \ge 1$ with $u = \log 2y/\log z \le U$,
$\Phi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon)\, y/\log z$.
\end{lemma}

This is an upper bound of the kind that Buchstab's asymptotic for rough numbers gives \cite{buchstab},
\cite[Chapter~III.6]{tenenbaum}, with an explicit constant above $3$. The proof (\S S4) is an induction on $u$ with
Buchstab's inequality, the prime number theorem and a delay inequality for $\omega^*$. Above $3$ that inequality holds
exactly when the constant $c_*$ of $\omega^*$ there is at least $(1 + \log 2 + 0.5671433)/4 = 0.5650726\ldots$, and
$0.565073$ is that bound rounded up.

\emph{The bound $\Omega$.} For an interval $[u_-, u_+)$, $\Omega[u_-, u_+)$ is $\mathcal K$ times the following upper
bound for $\omega^*$ on it, rounded up to an integer; the cases are needed since $\omega^*$ is not monotone.
\begin{itemize}
\item $0$ if $u_+ \le 1$;
\item $0.565073$ if $u_- \ge 3$;
\item $1/\max(u_-, 1)$ if $u_+ \le 2$;
\item otherwise the largest of $1/\max(u_-, 1)$, if $u_- < 2$; of a bound for $\omega$ on the part of the interval in
  $[2, 3]$; and of $0.565073$, if $u_+ > 3$.
\end{itemize}
On $[2, 3]$, $\omega$ increases up to $2.76$ and decreases from $2.77$ on (\S S4), so the bound there is $\omega(u_+)$ if
$u_+ \le 2.76$, $\omega(u_-)$ if $u_- \ge 2.77$, and $0.5671433$ otherwise. At a rational $u \in [2, 3]$, $\omega(u) = (1
+ \log(u - 1))/u$ is bounded by $\log(u - 1) \le \ell_j + (\xi - 1/\xi)/2$, where $j = \lfloor 16(u - 2) \rfloor$, $\xi =
(u - 1)/(1 + j/16) \ge 1$, and $\ell_j \ge \log(1 + j/16)$ is one of seventeen rationals of a table, each checked against
the exponential's series; the second term is $\log \xi \le (\xi - 1/\xi)/2$, the trapezoid rule again, which here errs
by less than $4 \cdot 10^{-5}$.

\emph{The costs.} For a cell $(l, l + w)$ with $l > 0$ let
\begin{gather*}
  t(l, w) = \frac{w(2l + w)}{2l(l + w)} \ge \log\Bigl(1 + \frac wl\Bigr) = \int_{l/D}^{(l + w)/D} \frac{d\alpha}\alpha,
  \\
  s(l, w) = \frac{Dw}{l(l + w)} = \int_{l/D}^{(l + w)/D} \frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^2},
\end{gather*}
the first by the trapezoid rule, since $1/\alpha$ is convex (it is $\xi \le \sinh \xi$ at $\xi = \log(1 + w/l)$), and let
$\tilde t$ and $\tilde s$ be their values rounded up to multiples of $2^{-64}$. With $w_i = h_i - l_i$, the costs of a
box of $k$ primes, dropped and dropped for its products, are
% Lean: SieveAd.dropCost, SieveAd.dropBCost, SieveAd.trF, SieveAd.exF, SieveCert.omKw, SieveCert.omF, SieveCert.lnT
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cD}
\begin{gathered}
  c_D(\mathfrak b) = \Bigl\lceil \Omega\Bigl[\frac{D - h(\mathfrak b)}{h_k}, \frac{D + 1 - l(\mathfrak b)}{l_k}\Bigr)\,
  \tilde s(l_k, w_k) \prod_{i < k} \tilde t(l_i, w_i) \Bigr\rceil, \\
  c_P(\mathfrak b) = \Bigl\lceil \frac{\mathcal K D}{D - h(\mathfrak b) + h_k} \prod_{i < k} \tilde t(l_i, w_i)
  \Bigr\rceil.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first discretises $\int \omega^*((1 - \alpha_1 - \dots - \alpha_k)/\alpha_k)\, d\alpha_1 \cdots
d\alpha_k/(\alpha_1 \cdots \alpha_{k - 1} \alpha_k^2)$ over the box: $\Omega$ bounds $\omega^*$ at the arguments $u =
\log 2y/\log p_k$, $y = x/r$, which lie in its interval once $\log 2/\log x < 1/D$, and the rest are the integrals of
$d\alpha/\alpha$ over the cells but the last and of $d\alpha/\alpha^2$ over the last.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:drop}
% Lean: SieveAd.drop_bound, SieveAd.dropCost_ge, SieveRough.shareBox, SieveDrop.famI_gB_le, SieveCert.omegaC_le_w, SieveRough.evLe_box_tr, SieveRough.evLe_box_ex, SieveAd.dropB_bound, SieveAd.dropBCost_ge, SieveAd.shareBd
Let $\mathfrak b$ be a box of $k \ge 1$ primes with $0 < l_i < h_i$ for every $i$, and $\varepsilon > 0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $h(\mathfrak b) \le D$, then for large $q$ the \emph{share} of the box, $\sum_{\mathcal F(\mathfrak b)} \Phi(x/r,
  p_k)$, is at most $(c_D(\mathfrak b)/\mathcal K + \varepsilon)\, x/\log x$, and $T(\mathcal B, \mathfrak b)/\varphi(q)
  \le (c_D(\mathfrak b)/\mathcal K + \varepsilon) X$.
\item If $k \ge 2$, $l(\mathfrak b) + l_k \ge D + 1$ and $h(\mathfrak b) - h_k < D$, then for large $q$, $T(\mathcal B,
  \mathfrak b)/\varphi(q) \le (c_P(\mathfrak b)/\mathcal K + \varepsilon) X$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a cell $(l, l + w)$, by the prime number theorem and partial summation, for large $x$,
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_{x^{l/D} \le p < x^{(l + w)/D}} \frac1p \le t(l, w) + \varepsilon_1, \\
  \log x \sum_{x^{l/D} \le p < x^{(l + w)/D}} \frac1{p \log p} \le s(l, w) + \varepsilon_1.
\end{gather*}

(1) An $n \in \mathcal B$ counted for a tuple is $n = rm$ with $m \in (x/r, 2x/r]$ and $P^-(m) \ge p_k$, and $n \mapsto m$
is injective; so $T(\mathcal B, \mathfrak b)$ is at most the share. Drop the order of the primes, and sum over the
tuples of the cells' primes. Since $r < x^{h(\mathfrak b)/D} \le x$, $y = x/r > 1$, and $u = \log 2y/\log p_k$ lies in
$[(D - h(\mathfrak b))/h_k, (D + 1 - l(\mathfrak b))/l_k)$ once $\log 2/\log x < 1/D$. Lemma~\ref{lem:rough}, with
$U = 2D/l_k$, gives $\Phi(y, p_k) \le (\Omega/\mathcal K + \varepsilon_1)\, x/(r \log p_k)$, with $\Omega$ the bound in
(\ref{eq:cD}). Summing over the cells, the share is at most
\[
  \Bigl(\frac\Omega{\mathcal K} + \varepsilon_1\Bigr) \frac{x}{\log x}\, (s(l_k, w_k) + \varepsilon_1) \prod_{i < k}
  (t(l_i, w_i) + \varepsilon_1),
\]
and $\tilde t \ge t$, $\tilde s \ge s$; take $\varepsilon_1$ small.

(2) For large $q$, $x^{1/D} \ge 3$, so $rp_k \ge x^{(l(\mathfrak b) + l_k)/D} \ge x^{1 + 1/D} > N_2$, and a counted $n =
rm$ has $m = 1$: the box counts only the products $n = r \in (N_1, N_2]$. Given $p_1, \dots, p_{k-1}$, with product $r'
< x^\sigma$, $\sigma = (h(\mathfrak b) - h_k)/D < 1$, the last prime $p_k$ is a prime of $(x/r', 2x/r']$, and $x/r' \ge
x^{1 - \sigma}$; so there are at most $(1 + \varepsilon_1)(x/r')/\log(x/r') \le (1 + \varepsilon_1)\, x/((1 -
\sigma)\, r' \log x)$ of them for large $q$. Summing $1/r'$ over the cells gives at most $\prod_{i < k} (t(l_i, w_i) +
\varepsilon_1)$, and $1/(1 - \sigma) = D/(D - h(\mathfrak b) + h_k)$.
\end{proof}

A box with $h(\mathfrak b) \le D$ has no integer $n = r$, and one with $l(\mathfrak b) + l_k \ge D + 1$ has no integer
with a cofactor $m > 1$; a box with neither can be neither dropped nor declared empty. Such a box is evaluated,
decomposed, or cut until its parts are of one kind or the other.

\subsection{Reversal through the largest prime}\label{sec:reversal}

A term with the sign $-$ needs an upper bound, and Buchstab's identity gives one only when its box passes a test at a
floor. Role reversal \cite{bhp}, \cite{harman} counts a term's integers from the other end: a term of $p_1 > p_2 > \dots
> p_k$ counts the $n = p_1 R$ with $R = p_2 \cdots p_k m$, and summing over $R$ instead, the quotient $n/R$ is the
prime $p_1$. When $p_1$ is large, a number of its size with no prime factor below the square root of its range is prime,
so the term is a term of a sieve on the numbers $n/R$, which Buchstab's identity decomposes further.

\emph{Role reversal of three primes.} For a box of three primes with $h(\mathfrak b) \le D$, $h_2 \le l_1$, $l_3 \le l_1$
and $D + 1 - l(\mathfrak b) \le 3l_3$, the cofactor $m$ of a counted $n = p_1 p_2 p_3 m$ lies in $(1, p_3^3)$, so it is a
prime or the product of two primes at least $p_3$. Summing over $r' = p_2 p_3 m$, whose primes in their order form five
kinds of tuples, and sifting $n/r'$ at $z_{l_3}$, bounds the term with the sign $-$ by the terms at that level of five
kinds of boxes, under the condition that $x/r'$ lies in the cell of $p_1$, which trees with the tests of
Lemma~\ref{lem:tests}(c) bound; the loss is the numbers $n/r'$ free of primes below $z_{l_3}$ that are not prime, which
Lemma~\ref{lem:rough} and the prime number theorem bound, and the $n$ with a square factor. The formalisation proves
this bound; the certificate of Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate} does not use it, and the paper does not need it. The reversal below keeps the
cofactor as one number, whatever its prime factors, and loses nothing to it.

\emph{The frozen part.} Let $\mathfrak b$ be a box of $k \ge 2$ primes, $(l_1, h_1)$ the cell of its largest prime
$p_1$, and $\mathfrak b'$ the box of the others, with the cells $(l_2, h_2), \dots, (l_k, h_k)$. For integers $e_- <
e_+$, the \emph{frozen part} $\mathcal M(e_-, e_+)$ is the set of the pairs $(\mathbf a, m)$ of a tuple $\mathbf a = (a_2,
\dots, a_k) \in \mathcal F(\mathfrak b')$ and an integer $1 \le m \le N_2$ with $P^-(m) \ge a_k$, such that
\[
  R = a_2 \cdots a_k\, m \in (x^{e_-/D}, x^{e_+/D}];
\]
its \emph{weight} is $\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M) = \sum 1/R$. The \emph{wide part} is $\mathcal M_{\mathrm w} =
\mathcal M(D - h_1, D - l_1 + 1)$, the range of $x/p_1$ for
$p_1$ in its cell, within the factor $2$; the \emph{narrow part} is $\mathcal M_{\mathrm n} = \mathcal M(D - h_1 + 1, D -
l_1)$, and the \emph{ends} are $\mathcal M(D - h_1, D - h_1 + 1)$ and $\mathcal M(D - l_1, D - l_1 + 1)$, whose union with
the narrow part is the wide part. The \emph{level} of the reversal is $\nu = \lceil h_1/2 \rceil$.

For a frozen part $\mathcal M$ and a box $\mathfrak c$ of $j \ge 0$ primes $p'_1 > \dots > p'_j$, the \emph{chain}, with
cells $(l'_i, h'_i)$, the terms over $\mathcal M$ are
\begin{gather*}
  T_{\mathcal M}(\mathcal C, \mathfrak c) = \sum_{(\mathbf a, m) \in \mathcal M} \sum_{\mathcal F(\mathfrak c)}
  \#\{n \in \mathcal C : R p'_1 \cdots p'_j \mid n,\ P^-(n/R) \ge p'_j\} \quad (j \ge 1), \\
  T_{\mathcal M}(\mathcal C, \mathfrak c; f) = \sum_{(\mathbf a, m) \in \mathcal M} \sum_{\mathcal F(\mathfrak c)}
  \#\{n \in \mathcal C : R p'_1 \cdots p'_j \mid n,\ P^-(n/R) \ge z_f\},
\end{gather*}
and $\Delta_{\mathcal M}(\mathfrak c)$, $\Delta_{\mathcal M}(\mathfrak c; f)$ are their differences between $\mathcal A$
and $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$. The condition is on $n/R$: the chain's primes are primes of $n/R$, ordered among themselves
only, and may lie above some primes of $R$. For each $R$ these are the terms of the boxes of the chain on the integers
$n/R$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:box-identities}(a) and~(b) hold for them, $R$ by $R$; and so does, for floors $f \le \nu \le
D$ and large $q$, Buchstab's identity between two levels,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:levels}
  T_{\mathcal M}(\mathcal C, \emptyset; \nu) = T_{\mathcal M}(\mathcal C, \emptyset; f) - T_{\mathcal M}(\mathcal C,
  ((f, \nu))).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sandwich}
% Lean: SieveAd.flip_cover, SieveAd.flip_inject, SieveAd.flip_cover_pt, SieveAd.flip_inject_pt
Let $0 < l_1 \le h_1 \le D$ and $\nu \le l_1$, let $\mathcal C$ be a set of integers of $(N_1, N_2]$, and let $q$ be
large. Then $T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b) \le T_{\mathcal M_{\mathrm w}}(\mathcal C, \emptyset; \nu)$. If moreover $h_2
\le l_1$, then $T_{\mathcal M_{\mathrm n}}(\mathcal C, \emptyset; \nu) \le T(\mathcal C, \mathfrak b)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $n \in \mathcal C$ be counted for a tuple $(p_1, \mathbf a)$ of $\mathfrak b$, so $n = p_1 a_2 \cdots a_k m$ with
$P^-(m) \ge a_k$. Then $(\mathbf a, m)$ has $R = n/p_1 \in (x/p_1, 2x/p_1] \subset (x^{(D - h_1)/D}, x^{(D - l_1 + 1)/D}]$,
as $2 \le x^{1/D}$, and $n/R = p_1$ is a prime at least $x^{l_1/D} \ge x^{\nu/D}$, so $P^-(n/R) \ge z_\nu$. The map $(p_1,
\mathbf a) \mapsto (\mathbf a, m)$ is injective for each $n$, since $p_1 = n/R$; this is the first inequality.
Conversely, let $(\mathbf a, m) \in \mathcal M_{\mathrm n}$ and $n \in \mathcal C$ with $R \mid n$ and $P = n/R$ free of
primes below $z_\nu$. Since $x^{(D - h_1 + 1)/D} < R \le x^{(D - l_1)/D}$, $P \in [x^{l_1/D}, 2x^{(h_1 - 1)/D}) \subset
[x^{l_1/D}, x^{h_1/D})$; so $P > 1$ and $P < x^{2\nu/D} \le z_\nu^2$, and $P$, having no prime factor below $z_\nu$, is
prime. As $P \ge x^{l_1/D} \ge x^{h_2/D} > a_2$, $(P, \mathbf a)$ is a tuple of $\mathfrak b$ for which $n$ counts, with
the cofactor $m$, and $(\mathbf a, m) \mapsto (P, \mathbf a)$ is injective for each $n$.
\end{proof}

The two parts differ only in the $R$ within the factor $x^{1/D}$ of the two ends of the range. So, for either set, the
term of the box lies between the terms at the level $\nu$ of the narrow and of the wide frozen part, and only the ends
are lost. The weight of a frozen part is bounded by the share of a box.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:frozen-weight}
% Lean: SieveAd.froz_weight_le, SieveAd.shareLe_list, SieveAd.shareLe_of_box, SieveAd.card_primes_le_famJR
Let $0 < l_i < h_i$ for every $i$, $h(\mathfrak b) \le D$, $h_1 \le 2l_1$ and $h_2 \le l_1$. For every $\varepsilon > 0$
and large $q$, $\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M_{\mathrm n}) \le (h_1/D)\, c_D(\mathfrak b)/\mathcal K + \varepsilon$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $(\mathbf a, m) \in \mathcal M_{\mathrm n}$, $y = x/R$ lies in $[x^{l_1/D}, x^{(h_1 - 1)/D})$, so by the prime number
theorem there are at least $(1 - \varepsilon_1)\, y/\log y \ge (1 - \varepsilon_1)(D/h_1)\, y/\log x$ primes $P \in (y,
2y]$ for large $q$. Each $n = RP$ lies in $(x, 2x]$ and has $R \mid n$ and $n/R = P$, a prime at least $x^{l_1/D} \ge
x^{\nu/D}$, as $h_1 \le 2l_1$ gives $\nu \le l_1$. By the second inequality of Lemma~\ref{lem:sandwich}, applied to all
the integers of $(N_1, N_2]$, these pairs $((\mathbf a, m), P)$ are at most the pairs of a tuple of $\mathfrak b$ and an
integer it counts, which are the share of the box. By Lemma~\ref{lem:drop}(1), $(1 - \varepsilon_1)(D/h_1)
\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M_{\mathrm n})\, x/\log x \le (c_D(\mathfrak b)/\mathcal K + \varepsilon_1)\, x/\log x$.
\end{proof}

\emph{The tree over the frozen part.} The terms over a frozen part are decomposed by trees as the terms of boxes are.
Their tests read the variables of the tuples that their terms sum over: the chain's primes, the frozen primes $a_i$ and
the cofactor $m$. For a frozen part $\mathcal M(e_-, e_+)$ and a set $\mathcal S$ of these variables, let $\mathcal S'$
be its frozen primes, $\overline{\mathcal S'}$ the frozen primes not in it and $\mathcal S''$ its chain primes; the
product over $\mathcal S$ lies in $[x^{\underline e(\mathcal S)/D}, x^{\overline e(\mathcal S)/D}]$ with
\[
  (\underline e, \overline e)(\mathcal S) = \begin{cases}
    \bigl(l(\mathcal S') + l'(\mathcal S''),\ h(\mathcal S') + h'(\mathcal S'')\bigr), & m \notin \mathcal S, \\
    \bigl(e_- - h(\overline{\mathcal S'}) + l'(\mathcal S''),\ e_+ - l(\overline{\mathcal S'}) + h'(\mathcal S'')\bigr),
    & m \in \mathcal S,
  \end{cases}
\]
since $m \prod_{\mathcal S'} a_i = R/\prod_{\overline{\mathcal S'}} a_i$; here $l'$ and $h'$ are the sums of the chain's
cells. With the \emph{room} $\gamma = 12 + \lfloor D/l_k \rfloor$, a chain $\mathfrak c$ of $j$ primes, $j + k \le 13$,
passes over $\mathcal M$
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(I$_{\mathcal M}$)] for disjoint sets $\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal S_r$ of variables, if all the cells of $\mathfrak
  c$ and of $\mathfrak b'$ have $1 \le l \le h \le D$, and $\mathcal R$ holds on the rectangle of (I) with $l(\mathcal
  S)$, $h(\mathcal S)$, $l(\mathcal S_r)$ and $h(\mathcal S_r)$ replaced by $\underline e(\mathcal S)$, $\overline
  e(\mathcal S)$, $\underline e(\mathcal S_r)$ and $\overline e(\mathcal S_r)$;
\item[(J2$_{\mathcal M}$), (J3$_{\mathcal M}$)] at a floor $f$, for the sets and the side of (J2) or (J3), now of
  variables, if all the cells of $\mathfrak c$ and of $\mathfrak b'$ have $0 < f \le l \le h \le D$, and the other conditions
  of (J2) or (J3) hold with $l(\mathcal S_0)$ and $l(\mathcal S_r)$ replaced by
  $\max(\underline e(\mathcal S_0) - \gamma, 0)$ and $\max(\underline e(\mathcal S_r) - \gamma, 0)$, and $h(\mathcal
  S_0)$, $h(\mathcal S_r)$, $h(\mathcal S_1)$ and $h(\mathfrak b) - h(\mathcal S_1)$ by $\overline e(\mathcal S_0) +
  \gamma$, $\overline e(\mathcal S_r) + \gamma$, $\overline e(\mathcal S_1) + \gamma$ and $\overline e(\overline{\mathcal
  S_1}) + \gamma$, with $\overline{\mathcal S_1}$ the variables not in $\mathcal S_1$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:frozen-tests}
% Lean: SieveAd.frozTests_sound, SieveAd.evIF_froz_two, SieveAd.evJF_froz_fill, SieveAd.evJF_froz_two, SieveAd.evJF_frozP, SieveAd.evJ_famSet, SieveAd.evJ_fam_fill, SieveAd.evJ_fam_two, SieveAd.full2_core, SieveAd.frozW_noninj_le, SieveAd.sum_frozS_eq, SieveAd.rough_div_iff
Let $\mathcal M = \mathcal M_{\mathrm w}$ be the wide part of a box $\mathfrak b$ of $k \ge 2$ primes with $0 < l_1 \le
h_1 \le D$. If a chain $\mathfrak c$ of $j \ge 1$ primes passes (I$_{\mathcal M}$), then $\Delta_{\mathcal M}(\mathfrak c)
= o(X)$; if a chain $\mathfrak c$ of $j \ge 0$ primes passes (J2$_{\mathcal M}$) or (J3$_{\mathcal M}$) at $f$, then
$\Delta_{\mathcal M}(\mathfrak c; f) = o(X)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $m$ as the product of a non-increasing tuple $\mathbf y$ of primes at least $a_k$. Since $m \le 2x <
x^{(D + 1)/D}$ and $a_k \ge x^{l_k/D}$, $\mathbf y$ has at most $\lfloor D/l_k \rfloor$ entries, so the frozen part is a
union of finitely many families of tuples $(\mathbf a, \mathbf y)$ of primes, cut out by comparisons: the cells and the
order of $\mathbf a$, the order of $\mathbf y$ and $y_i \ge a_k$, and the two of $R \in (x^{e_-/D}, x^{e_+/D}]$. The
product of the variables of a set $\mathcal S$ lies in the range above.

The term $\Delta_{\mathcal M}(\mathfrak c)$ counts the $n = R p'_1 \cdots p'_j k'$ with $P^-(k') \ge p'_j$; writing $k'$
as a non-increasing tuple $\mathbf y'$ of primes at least $p'_j$, it is the sum of $\vartheta(n)$ over the tuples
$(\mathbf a, \mathbf y, \mathbf p', \mathbf y')$ of primes with product $n \in (N_1, N_2]$, with $\mathbf p' = (p'_1,
\dots, p'_j)$, again finitely many families cut out by
comparisons, with entries at least $x^{1/D}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:chains-two} applies to each, since it asks neither order
nor distinct entries: the first variable is the product of the variables of $\mathcal S$, the rest that of $\mathcal
S_r$, which exceeds $1$, the second variable all the other primes, and the band is the range of $n$ over the rest. Its
region's hypothesis is (I$_{\mathcal M}$), as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tests}(a).

For the terms at a floor, every prime of $R$ is at least $a_k \ge x^{l_k/D} \ge x^{f/D}$, so for $R \mid n$, $P^-(n/R) \ge
z_f$ exactly when $P^-(n) \ge z_f$. So $\Delta_{\mathcal M}(\mathfrak c; f)$ is the sum over the tuples $\mathbf w =
(\mathbf a, \mathbf y, \mathbf p')$ of the sum of $\vartheta(n)$ over the $n \in (N_1, N_2]$ with $\prod \mathbf w \mid n$
and $P^-(n) \ge z_f$; a tuple $\mathbf w$ has $K \le (k - 1) + \lfloor D/l_k \rfloor + j \le \gamma$ entries. A tuple
with a repeated prime makes $p^2 \mid n$ for a prime $p \ge z_f$; each $n$ has at most $(D + 1)^K$ tuples of $K$
entries, and the $n \in (x, 2x]$ divisible by such a square number $O(x/(z_f q) + \sqrt x)$ in
the class of $a$ and $O(x/z_f)$ in all, which is $o(X)$ in $\mathcal A$ and in $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$, as $q =
x^{1/L}$ with $L > 2$. The tuples of
distinct primes form a family cut out by comparisons, with no order between $\mathbf a$, $\mathbf y$ and $\mathbf p'$.
Sorted in decreasing order, each lies in exactly one box of cells of one unit, $(\lfloor D \log p/\log x \rfloor, \lfloor
D\log p/\log x \rfloor + 1)$ for each prime $p$; there are at most $D^K K!$ such boxes, a number that does not depend on
$q$, and the sum is the sum over them of their terms at the floor $f$, under the family's comparisons carried to the
box's order. A unit box that holds a tuple of the family has, for each set $\mathcal S$ of variables, its lower ends'
sum at least $\underline e(\mathcal S) - K$ and its upper ends' sum at most $\overline e(\mathcal S) + K$, since each of
the at most $K \le \gamma$ primes loses at most one unit. Its cells
have $f \le l \le h \le D$, as every prime is at least $x^{f/D}$ and below $x$. So
Proposition~\ref{prop:harman-two} or~\ref{prop:harman-fill} applies to it as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tests}(b),
with $\rho_1 = \max(\underline e(\mathcal S_r) - \gamma, 0)$ and $\rho_2 = \overline e(\mathcal S_r) + \gamma$, which it allows
since it asks only $\rho_1 \le \sum_{\mathcal S_r} l_i$ and $\sum_{\mathcal S_r} h_i \le \rho_2$, and with $\max(\underline
e(\mathcal S_0) - \gamma, 0)$ and the upper bounds in the growth and in (d), whose conditions only weaken as the box's sums
move inside these bounds; by Lemma~\ref{lem:tests}(c), which allows boxes of any number of primes, it does so
uniformly in the family's comparisons. Summing over the
finitely many boxes gives $o(X)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:frozen-tree}
% Lean: SieveAd.opsK, SieveAd.vanishK, SieveAd.dropK, SieveAd.chainOf; its tests: SieveAd.frozTests, SieveAd.okIF, SieveAd.okJF2, SieveAd.okJF3, SieveAd.loR, SieveAd.hiR, SieveAd.hiRc, SieveAd.coreI2, SieveAd.coreJ2, SieveAd.coreJ3
Let $\mathfrak b$ be a box of $k \ge 2$ primes and $\mathcal M = \mathcal M_{\mathrm w}$ its wide part. A \emph{tree
over the frozen part} on a chain $\mathfrak c$ of $j \ge 1$ primes, $j + k \le 13$, with a sign $\varepsilon$, is a node
(S) or (B) of Definition~\ref{def:tree}, with (J2$_{\mathcal M}$) and (J3$_{\mathcal M}$) in place of (J2) and (J3), on
the chain's cells, and trees over the frozen part below it; or one of
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(V$_{\mathcal M}$)] $h'(\mathfrak c) + 1 \le l_1$ and $l'(\mathfrak c) + l'_j \ge h_1 + 1$. Cost $0$.
\item[(E$_{\mathcal M}$)] $\mathfrak c$ passes (I$_{\mathcal M}$). Cost $0$.
\item[(D$_{\mathcal M}$)] $\varepsilon = +$, $0 < l'_i < h'_i$ for every $i$ and $h'(\mathfrak c) + 1 \le l_1$. Cost
  \[
    c'_D(\mathfrak c) = \Bigl\lceil \Omega\Bigl[\frac{l_1 - 1 - h'(\mathfrak c)}{h'_j}, \frac{h_1 + 1 - l'(\mathfrak
    c)}{l'_j}\Bigr)\, \tilde s(l'_j, h'_j - l'_j) \prod_{i < j} \tilde t(l'_i, h'_i - l'_i) \Bigr\rceil.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
The cost of a tree over the frozen part stands for its multiple of $\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M)/\mathcal K$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:frozen-tree}
% Lean: SieveAd.frozRules, SieveAd.frozSolve_sound, SieveAd.bdF_vanish, SieveAd.bdF_drop, SieveAd.bdF_eval, SieveAd.bdF_split, SieveAd.bdF_buch, SieveAd.dIF_cut, SieveAd.dIF_buch
Let $\mathcal M$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:frozen-tree}, with $0 < l_1 \le h_1 \le D$. If a tree over the frozen
part on $\mathfrak c$ with the sign $\varepsilon$ passes, with cost $c_F$, then for every $\eta > 0$, for large $q$ and every
$a$ prime to $q$,
\[
  \varepsilon\, \Delta_{\mathcal M}(\mathfrak c) \ge -\bigl((c_F/\mathcal K + \eta) \operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M) + \eta\bigr) X.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the tree, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:tree-bound} below, with the identities on $n/R$ for (S) and (B) and
Lemma~\ref{lem:frozen-tests} for (E$_{\mathcal M}$) and the tests of (B). For $R \in (x^{(D - h_1)/D}, x^{(D - l_1 +
1)/D}]$ and $n \in (x, 2x]$, $n/R \in [x^{(l_1 - 1)/D}, x^{(h_1 + 1)/D})$. (V$_{\mathcal M}$): a counted $n/R = p'_1 \cdots
p'_j k'$ would be below $x^{(l_1 - 1)/D}$ if $k' = 1$, and at least $p'_1 \cdots p'_j p'_j \ge x^{(h_1 + 1)/D}$ if $k' > 1$;
so the term is $0$. (D$_{\mathcal M}$): $T_{\mathcal M}(\mathcal A, \mathfrak c) \ge 0$, and as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:drop}(1), $T_{\mathcal M}(\mathcal B, \mathfrak c) \le \sum_{(\mathbf a, m)} \sum_{\mathcal F(\mathfrak c)}
\Phi(x/(Rp'_1 \cdots p'_j), p'_j)$, where $y = x/(R p'_1 \cdots p'_j) > 1$ since $p'_1 \cdots p'_j < x^{(l_1 - 1)/D}$; the
arguments $\log 2y/\log p'_j$ lie in the interval of $\Omega$ in $c'_D$, which takes $R$ as one more variable of range
$[D - h_1, D - l_1 + 1]$, and summing over $R$ gives the factor $\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M)$ in place of a sum over a
cell.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:reversal}
% Lean: SieveAd.flipOK, SieveAd.flipT, SieveAd.lastC, SieveAd.initC, SieveAd.flipTree, SieveAd.flipTreeD, SieveAd.flipCost, SieveAd.flipCostD, SieveAd.bdK
A box $\mathfrak b$ of $k$ primes \emph{may be reversed} if $2 \le k \le 11$, $0 < l_i < h_i$ for every $i$, $h(\mathfrak
b) + 2 \le D$, $h_2 + 1 \le l_1$, $l_1 \ge 3$, $\nu \le l_1$ and $h_1 + \nu + 1 \le D$, where $\nu = \lceil h_1/2 \rceil$.
A \emph{reversal} of such a box is a floor $f$ with $0 < f \le \min(l_k, \nu)$ at which the chain of no prime passes
(J2$_{\mathcal M}$) or (J3$_{\mathcal M}$) over the wide part $\mathcal M_{\mathrm w}$, and a tree over the frozen part on
the chain $((f, \nu))$ with the sign $+$. If that tree's cost is $c_F$, the reversal's cost is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cR}
  c_R = \Bigl\lceil \frac{c_F\,(h_1 c_D(\mathfrak b) + E) + c_e E}{D\mathcal K} \Bigr\rceil, \qquad
  E = (h_1 + 1)\, c_D(\mathfrak b^{\mathrm{lo}}) + (l_1 + 1)\, c_D(\mathfrak b^{\mathrm{hi}}),
\end{equation}
where $\mathfrak b^{\mathrm{lo}} = \mathfrak b[1 \mapsto (h_1 - 1, h_1 + 1)]$, $\mathfrak b^{\mathrm{hi}} = \mathfrak
b[1 \mapsto (l_1 - 1, l_1 + 1)]$, and $c_e = \lceil \Omega[(l_1 - 1)/(\nu + 1), (h_1 + 2)/\nu)\, D/\nu \rceil$, the
cost of the ends.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:reversal}
% Lean: SieveAd.bd_flip_neg, SieveAd.bd_flip_core, SieveAd.bd_flipCost, SieveAd.bd_flipCostD, SieveAd.froz_ends_le, SieveAd.froz_top_le, SieveAd.dJF_level, SieveAd.psi_level; the sign +: SieveAd.bd_flip_pos
Let $\mathfrak b$ be a box that may be reversed, with a reversal of cost $c_R$ whose tree passes. Then the term of
$\mathfrak b$ with the sign $-$ is bounded at $c_R/\mathcal K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $C = c_F/\mathcal K$, $C_e = c_e/\mathcal K$, $w_0 = (h_1/D)\, c_D(\mathfrak b)/\mathcal K$, $w_1 = ((h_1 + 1)/D)\,
c_D(\mathfrak b^{\mathrm{lo}})/\mathcal K$ and $w_2 = ((l_1 + 1)/D)\, c_D(\mathfrak b^{\mathrm{hi}})/\mathcal K$, so that
$c_R/\mathcal K \ge C(w_0 + w_1 + w_2) + C_e(w_1 + w_2)$. Write $\mathcal M_{\mathrm e}$ for the union of the two ends. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:sandwich}, applied to $\mathcal A$ for the upper inequality and to $\mathcal B$ for the lower,
\[
  \Delta(\mathfrak b) \le T_{\mathcal M_{\mathrm w}}(\mathcal A, \emptyset; \nu) - \frac{T_{\mathcal M_{\mathrm
  n}}(\mathcal B, \emptyset; \nu)}{\varphi(q)} = \Delta_{\mathcal M_{\mathrm w}}(\emptyset; \nu) + \frac{T_{\mathcal
  M_{\mathrm e}}(\mathcal B, \emptyset; \nu)}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
By (\ref{eq:levels}), $\Delta_{\mathcal M_{\mathrm w}}(\emptyset; \nu) = \Delta_{\mathcal M_{\mathrm w}}(\emptyset; f) -
\Delta_{\mathcal M_{\mathrm w}}(((f, \nu)))$. The first term is $o(X)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:frozen-tests}, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:frozen-tree} the second is at most $((C + \eta)\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M_{\mathrm w}) + \eta)X$. For
$R$ in the wide part, $y = x/R \in [x^{(l_1 - 1)/D}, x^{h_1/D})$, and the integers of $\mathcal B$ counted for $R$ at
the level $\nu$ are at most $\Phi(y, z_\nu)$; the arguments $\log 2y/\log z_\nu$ lie in $[(l_1 - 1)/(\nu + 1), (h_1 +
2)/\nu)$, and $1/\log z_\nu \le D/(\nu \log x)$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:rough} gives $T_{\mathcal M_{\mathrm e}}(\mathcal B,
\emptyset; \nu)/\varphi(q) \le (C_e + \eta)\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M_{\mathrm e}) X$ for large $q$. Finally
$\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M_{\mathrm w}) = \operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M_{\mathrm n}) + \operatorname{wt}(\mathcal
M_{\mathrm e})$, and the ends $\mathcal M(D - h_1, D - h_1 + 1)$ and $\mathcal M(D - l_1, D - l_1 + 1)$ are the narrow
parts of $\mathfrak b^{\mathrm{lo}}$ and of $\mathfrak b^{\mathrm{hi}}$, whose largest primes have the cells $(h_1 - 1,
h_1 + 1)$ and $(l_1 - 1, l_1 + 1)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:frozen-weight} applies to the three
boxes, since $h(\mathfrak b^{\mathrm{lo}}) = h(\mathfrak b) + 1 \le D$, $h(\mathfrak b^{\mathrm{hi}}) \le h(\mathfrak b)$,
$h_1 + 1 \le 2(h_1 - 1)$ and $l_1 + 1 \le 2(l_1 - 1)$ as $h_1 > l_1 \ge 3$, and $h_2 \le l_1 - 1$; it gives
$\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M_{\mathrm n}) \le w_0 + \eta$ and $\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M_{\mathrm e}) \le w_1 + w_2
+ 2\eta$. Collecting, $\Delta(\mathfrak b) \le (C(w_0 + w_1 + w_2) + C_e(w_1 + w_2) + O(\eta))X$, with an $O$ that
depends only on $C$, $C_e$ and the $w_i$.
\end{proof}

The same argument with the narrow part in place of the wide one, and a tree on $((f, \nu))$ with the sign $-$, bounds the
term with the sign $+$ at $Cw_0 + C_e(w_1 + w_2)$; the formalisation proves it, and the search does not use it.

\emph{When the reversal wins.} The reversal costs about $C h_1/D$ times the box's share $c_D(\mathfrak b)/\mathcal K$,
the ends adding little, as they are one unit wide. Here $C$ is the cost of the tree over the frozen part in units of
$\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M)$, against which the main term of the sieve over the frozen part, the primes $P \in (x/R,
2x/R]$, is about $(D/h_1)\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal M)$: so $Ch_1/D$ is the fraction of that main term which the tree
over the frozen part loses. Where Buchstab's identity on a box of the sign $-$ creates terms of the sign $+$ that can only
be dropped at their full shares, the reversal pays only that fraction of the box's share. It applies only to boxes whose
largest prime is large: the frozen numbers $R$, of size $x/p_1$, are among the variables of the tests over the frozen
part, and $(\mathrm L)$ there asks $a_1 + a_2 < 1 - 1/(2L)$, while $a_1 + a_2 \ge (D - l_1 + 1)/D$; so the tests at a floor
pass only for $l_1 > D/(2L) + 1$. The search of Section~\ref{sec:certificate} takes the reversal when its cost is below
that of the decomposition by Buchstab's identity, and searches the tree over the frozen part only up to the cost $c$ at
which $c_R$ would reach that of the alternative.

\subsection{What a tree gives}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tree-bound}
% Lean: SieveAd.sound, SieveAd.bd_split, SieveAd.bd_buch, SieveAd.bd_mono, SieveAd.rulesMP, SieveAd.solveD_sound, SieveAd.altFD_sound, SieveAd.bd_flipCostD, SieveAd.pr_split, SieveAd.pr_buch
Let $\mathfrak b$ be a box of $k \ge 1$ primes. If a tree on $\mathfrak b$ with the sign $\varepsilon$ passes, with cost
$c$, then the term of $\mathfrak b$ with the sign $\varepsilon$ is bounded at $c/\mathcal K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the tree; each case is a finite conjunction of statements for large $q$, which hold together for large
$q$. (V): by Lemma~\ref{lem:box-identities}(c), $\Delta(\mathfrak b) = 0$ for large $q$. (E): by Lemma~\ref{lem:tests}(a),
$\varepsilon\Delta(\mathfrak b) \ge -|\Delta(\mathfrak b)| \ge -\eta X$. (D) and (P): $\varepsilon = +$ and
$\Delta(\mathfrak b) \ge -T(\mathcal B, \mathfrak b)/\varphi(q)$, which Lemma~\ref{lem:drop} bounds. (S): by
Lemma~\ref{lem:box-identities}(a), $\Delta(\mathfrak b)$ is the sum of the two parts' $\Delta$, each bounded with the sign
$\varepsilon$ at its cost, with $\eta/2$ for each. (B): by Lemma~\ref{lem:box-identities}(b),
\[
  \varepsilon\Delta(\mathfrak b) = \varepsilon\Delta(\mathfrak b; f) + (-\varepsilon)\Delta(\mathfrak b \oplus (f, h_k))
  \ge -\frac\eta2 X - \Bigl(\frac c{\mathcal K} + \frac\eta2\Bigr) X,
\]
the first term by Lemma~\ref{lem:tests}(b) and the second by the induction, the tree below having the sign $-\varepsilon$
and the cost $c$. (R): Proposition~\ref{prop:reversal}.
\end{proof}

The tree is finite, so finitely many applications of Lemmas~\ref{lem:tests} and~\ref{lem:drop} and of
Propositions~\ref{prop:harman-two} and~\ref{prop:harman-fill} enter, each with its own $q_0$; none of them is claimed to be
effective, and the thresholds are enormous ($x^{1/D} \ge 3$, for one, needs $q \ge 3^{D/L}$). The only main term that the
argument computes is that of $S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})$: every other term is compared with its version in
$\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$, dropped at the cost of that version, or bounded through a reversal by such comparisons.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:tree}
% Lean: SieveAd.linnik_pr, SieveAd.linnik_bd, SieveAd.linnik_large_bd, SieveAd.linnik_tree, SieveAd.Ttop_eq, SieveAd.Ttop_gB_ge, SieveMain2.route_of_large
Let $2 < L \le 5/2$, and let $f_* \le D/2 + 1$ be a floor at which the box of no prime passes (J3). If the term of
the box $((f_*, D/2 + 1))$ of one prime with the sign $-$ is bounded at some $C < 1$, in particular if that box has a
tree with the sign $-$ that passes, with cost $c < \mathcal K$ and $C = c/\mathcal K$, then there is $K$ such that for
every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$ there is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with $p \le Kq^L$; and for large $q$
there is one with $q^L < p \le 2q^L$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\eta = (1 - C)/8$, and let $q$ be large and $a$ prime to $q$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:box-identities}(d) for $\mathcal A$ and for $\mathcal B$,
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) - \frac{S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})}{\varphi(q)} = \Delta(\emptyset; f_*) -
  \Delta(\mathfrak b_0), \qquad \mathfrak b_0 = ((f_*, D/2 + 1)).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:tests}(b), $|\Delta(\emptyset; f_*)| \le \eta X$, and by the hypothesis (with
Proposition~\ref{prop:tree-bound} for a tree), $-\Delta(\mathfrak b_0) \ge -(C + \eta)X$. A prime of $(x, 2x]$ exceeds $q$, so it is prime to $q$, and it has
no prime factor below $z_{\mathrm{top}} \le N_1$; so $S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ is at least the number of primes
of $(x, 2x]$, which is at least $(1 - \eta)x/\log x$ by the prime number theorem. So
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) \ge (1 - \eta)X - \eta X - (C + \eta)X > \frac{1 - C}2 X > 0.
\]
An integer counted by $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ is at most $N_2 < z_{\mathrm{top}}^2$ and has no prime factor
below $z_{\mathrm{top}}$, so it is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with $q^L < p \le 2q^L$. This gives $q_0$ such that the
theorem holds for $q \ge q_0$ with $K = 2$. Each of the finitely many pairs $(q, a \bmod q)$ with $q < q_0$ has a prime
$p \equiv a \pmod q$ by Dirichlet's theorem, and $K$ is taken large enough to cover them.
\end{proof}

The theorem holds for every such $L$ with the tests as defined. For rational $L$ the tests are inequalities between
integers, which is how the formalisation states them and how its kernel evaluates them: it proves the theorem for
rational $L$, and at $L = 43/20$ with $f_*$ and the bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate}.

\section{The certificate}\label{sec:certificate}

Theorem~\ref{thm:tree} reduces Theorem~\ref{thm:main} to a finite computation: a floor $f_*$ at which the box of no
prime passes (J3), and a bound below $1$ for the term of the box of the first prime with the sign $-$, which a tree of
cost below $\mathcal K$ gives. At $L =
43/20$ the tree has some $3.4 \cdot 10^6$ nodes, those of the trees over the frozen parts of its reversals included,
and the formalisation never writes it down. Lean's kernel runs a search that follows a plan computed
beforehand; what is checked is that the search, on the first prime's box, returns a cost below $\mathcal K$. This
section describes the search, why its result is a proof whatever the plan, and the numbers at $43/20$.

\subsection{The search}

The search takes a box, a sign and a \emph{limit}, an integer, and returns an integer: a value below the limit is a
cost at which the term is bounded, and a value at least the limit says nothing. At a box it does the first of the
following that applies.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If the box is empty, (V), or a proposed configuration passes the test (I), it returns $0$.
\item If the box may be dropped, (D) or (P), it returns the smaller of the drop's cost and the cost of its decomposition
  by Buchstab's identity, the latter searched with the smaller of the drop's cost and the limit as its limit.
\item Otherwise it decomposes the box at the first proposed floor at which (J2) or (J3) passes, if the box has fewer
  primes than the strategy's \emph{depth}: the new prime's range $[f, h_k)$ is cut at the proposed cuts, and each cell
  is searched with the limit that the cells before it leave; the search of the range stops as soon as the costs reach
  the limit. A box of the sign $-$ that may be reversed is reversed instead if that costs less, and a box that can be
  neither decomposed nor reversed is cut in two at the middle of its newest cell, if that cell is wider than the
  strategy's least width.
\end{enumerate}
A reversal is searched in the same way: its floor is the first proposed floor at which the chain of no prime passes a
test over the frozen part, and the tree over the frozen part on $((f, \nu))$ is searched with the nodes of
Definition~\ref{def:frozen-tree}, and with the limit past which the reversal's cost (\ref{eq:cR}) would not be below
that of the decomposition (the search that the kernel follows searches it with the fixed limit $64\mathcal K$, which
no tree of the plan reaches).

What the search tries is proposed by a \emph{strategy}, which needs no proof: every proposal is tested before it is
used. At $43/20$ the strategy proposes the following.

\begin{itemize}
\item For (I): as the rest, the first prime of the box, from the smallest, whose cell the region admits for a rest and
  for which a set of the other primes has sums that the region admits for the first variable with it; and that set.
\item For (J2): for each prime that the region admits as the rest, the floor $\min(l_{\min}, v - u - 1)$ allowed by its

  $(\mathcal S_1, \iota)$ of the leaves that passes $(\mathrm L)$. The first floor with such a split is proposed before

\item Cuts of the new prime's range $[f, h_k)$: cells of side at most $4398046511/D$, about $0.004$, for the first four

\subsection{Checking a search}

Lean's kernel can decide a proposition about natural numbers, lists and Booleans by evaluating it, with no trust in
compiled code. The search is such a function: its tests are the conditions of Definitions~\ref{def:tests},
\ref{def:frozen-tree} and~\ref{def:reversal} in integers, and its costs are the integers (\ref{eq:cD}), $c'_D$ and
(\ref{eq:cR}), every division rounded up, with $\Omega$ computed from its table. Its soundness is proved once, for every
strategy.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:search}
% Lean: SieveAd.solveF_sound, SieveAd.has_of_kids, SieveAd.solveG_sound, SieveAd.kidsP_sound, SieveAd.solveD_sound, SieveAd.stepD_sound, SieveAd.kidsC_sound, SieveAd.partD_sound, SieveAd.solveMD_sound, SieveAd.altFD_sound, SieveAd.pr_of_partD
Whatever the strategy, or the hints that lead the search (below), if the search returns on a box, a sign and a limit a value
$c$ below the limit, then the term of the box with that sign is bounded at $c/\mathcal K$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the depth of recursion, with the cases of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:tree-bound}. A value
below the limit comes from a node whose condition the search has evaluated to true: an empty or evaluated box, at the cost $0$; a dropped box, at its cost; a decomposition at a floor
whose test has passed, whose cells' values, each below its limit, are the costs of trees by the induction, and add up
by cuts (S), since any point strictly inside a range is a cut; a reversal whose test at its floor has passed and whose
tree over the frozen part has, by the same induction, the cost from which (\ref{eq:cR}) is computed; or a box cut in
two. A branch whose condition fails returns its limit, which says nothing.
\end{proof}

The same induction shows that the nodes that the search took form a tree on the box with that sign that passes, of
cost at most $c$; the formalisation proves the bound alone (\S S5).

So the certificate is a set of propositions about integers, that the search's values on the parts of the first prime's
range, with the sign $-$ and the limit $\mathcal K$, add up to at most $c$; the kernel proves them by evaluation, part by
part, and the formalisation proves the bound of the term that Lemma~\ref{lem:search} draws from them. It proves the
bound, rule by rule, and never writes the tree down.

\subsection{Hints}

A search that tries what its strategy proposes spends most of its time on what it does not keep: trees under boxes
that are then dropped or reversed, the test of every proposed floor where one is used, and the search for a
configuration among the sets of a box's cells. So the search is run once outside the kernel, in exact integer
arithmetic and definition by definition, and the plan it keeps is written as \emph{hints}: for each part of the check,
a natural number, read from its low end in the order in which the search visits the boxes. At a box the hints give a
run of ones closed by a zero, which names the node, and then what the node needs.

A configuration of (I) is the rest's cell, $0$ for the first or $1$ and three bits for its index, and the set $\mathcal

plan joins consecutive cells that one test evaluates, and consecutive dropped cells while the joined cell's cost stays
within $0.2\%$ of the sum of theirs; so the cells of the certificate are unions of the strategy's, and may be wider than

the box and each cut from its code, and evaluates the tests and the costs.

the search chooses, with whatever configuration, floor and cut the hints give, applied only after its condition has
passed, and a cut at any point is a node (S). A hint that names a node whose condition fails makes the value the
limit, which says nothing. So the hints are not trusted: a wrong hint can make the check fail, not make it wrong.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Take $L = 43/20$. Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, under Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, is the Type~II estimate in two variables
on the region $\mathcal R_L$, so the comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:type1} hold at $L$, and with them the tests of
Section~\ref{sec:decomp}. Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate} gives a floor $f_*$ at which the box of no prime passes
(J3), and bounds the term of the root box with the sign $-$ at $c/\mathcal K$, with $c = 1093510193343867034 <
\mathcal K = 2^{60}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:tree} gives Theorem~\ref{thm:main} under the input.
Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} enters through Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum} and the top bin in Section~\ref{sec:type2},
through the kernel bound of Section~\ref{sec:two-bounds} (where the Pólya--Vinogradov inequality would do instead),
through the piece of $g_{\mathrm H}$ above $11/12$, which only makes $g_{\mathrm H}$ a density curve on all of
$[1/2, 1]$, and, in the formalisation, through the prime number theorem, which it derives from the case of $\zeta$
in the form $\psi(t) - t = O(t^{11/12 + \varepsilon})$ and uses for the main term of Theorem~\ref{thm:tree} and for the
sums over primes of Sections~\ref{sec:type1} and~\ref{sec:decomp}.
\end{proof}

The proof does not compute $K$: the threshold $q_0$ comes from limits in $q$ in each step, and the small moduli
from Dirichlet's theorem. As in the classical proofs, every constant is effective in principle; unlike them, the
argument needs no step on an exceptional zero, since Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} leaves none. But its thresholds are
enormous: the cells of exponents are multiples of $2^{-40}$, and the steps of Section~\ref{sec:decomp} ask, among
other things, $x^{2^{-40}} \ge 3$, that is $q \ge 3^{2^{40}/L}$.

that join the parts are proved one by one. The proof's import cone contains no module of the library's formalisation of

and Li's Theorem~1.2 and Corollary~1.4 give $7/3 + \varepsilon$; the figures computed by the repository's scripts (the

\section{What the input gives without the sieve, and the method's limits}\label{sec:comparison}

This section claims nothing new. It places Theorem~\ref{thm:main} against what the same input gives by the classical
argument, which has no sieve, and against the exponents known for special moduli; the table of the introduction sums
it up.

\subsection*{The classical deduction}

Suppose that for some $A \ge 2$ there is a uniform density estimate
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le C (qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)} (\log qT)^B \qquad (1/2 \le \sigma \le 1,\ T \ge 2).
\]
With Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} it gives Linnik's theorem with every exponent $L > A$. The library's proof writes the
smoothed sum $\sum_{n \equiv a} \Lambda(n) e^{-n/x}$ through the characters, bounds each non-trivial character's sum by
a contour shift to the edge of its largest zero-free rectangle, at one of the levels $\theta_j = \min(1/2 + j\delta/2,
11/12)$, for a small $\delta > 0$ fixed in terms of $L - A$, and counts the characters at each level by the density
estimate at the height $T + 1$, with $T = 2 \log x + 2$: the characters with zeros of real part about $\sigma$
contribute about $(q^A/x)^{1 - \sigma}$ together, and none has a zero of real part above $11/12$. With it the library
proves:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L = 12 + \varepsilon$ from Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} alone: at most $\varphi(q) \le qT$ characters are
  counted for every $\sigma$, and at most one above $11/12$, which is the estimate with $A = 12$;
\item $L = 36/13 + \varepsilon$ with Ingham's estimate, whose exponent $3/(2 - \sigma)$ is $36/13$ at
  $\sigma = 11/12$;
\item $L = 12/5 + \varepsilon$ with Huxley's estimate (with Ingham's below $\sigma = 3/4$), which bounds the density
  exponent by $12/5$ on the whole range.
\end{itemize}
Appendix~\ref{app:claims} names the Lean theorem of each. With Chen, Gupta and Li's estimate the exponent is $7/3 +
\varepsilon$, by the same deduction from their Theorem~1.2 \cite{cgl} (exponent $A = 7/3$), which the library does not
formalise as they state it. They do not state a Linnik exponent, but their Corollary~1.4 \cite{cgl}, on primes
in progressions in short intervals under a zero-free region, contains this one: for $h \le x$ it gives
$\psi(x + h; q, a) - \psi(x; q, a) \sim h/\varphi(q)$ whenever
$h/\varphi(q) \ge x^{1/2 + \varepsilon} q^{1/6} + x^{17/30 + \varepsilon}$, and with $h = x$ and
Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} as the zero-free region this holds at $x = q^L$ for every $L > 7/3$ and all large $q$.

\subsection*{Special moduli}

For special moduli smaller exponents are known. For the powers of a fixed prime, Banks and Shparlinski
\cite{banks-shparlinski} have $2.1115$ (the value as cited in \cite[footnote~1]{cgl}). R.~Li \cite{li-harman} adapts
his Harman-sieve calculation to primes in progressions to large moduli $q$ whose prime factors are all below $q^C$, for
an absolute constant $C > 0$ whose existence his theorem asserts without giving it, when the $L$-functions of the
primitive characters to the divisors of $q$ have no zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > 1 - (\log q)^{-3/4}$ and
$|\operatorname{Im} s| \le \exp(\varepsilon (\log q)^{3/4})$, with the $\varepsilon > 0$ of his Theorem~9; for the
powers of a fixed prime he deduces the exponent $1/0.4752 < 2.1044$ (his Theorem~11), improving Banks and Shparlinski's.
He presents these theorems as modifications of that calculation, without separate proofs. Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}
implies the zero-free hypothesis of his Theorem~9 for every large $q$, so if that theorem holds as stated, it gives with
Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} the exponent $1/0.4752$ for every large modulus whose prime factors are all below $q^C$.

\subsection*{What the half-plane is used for}

The classical deduction does not need the value $11/12$ of Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}: it works with any zero-free
half-plane $\operatorname{Re} s > 1 - \eta_0$, for a fixed $\eta_0 > 0$. The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} uses the
half-plane for the prime sums of Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum}, whose contour runs to its edge, for the top bin of
Section~\ref{sec:bins}, for the kernel bound, where the Pólya--Vinogradov inequality would do
(Section~\ref{sec:region}), and for the prime number theorem. With a narrower half-plane the bins would run up to $1 -
\eta_0$, and the zone condition for two variables of length $q$ at the top bin would need $\rho \ge 1/(25000\eta_0)$
in place of $1/2000$; the region, and so the certificate, would change, and this is not pursued. What the proof uses is
that there are no zeros near $\operatorname{Re} s = 1$, and in particular no exceptional zero.

\subsection*{Where the gain comes from, and its limits}

The deduction needs the zeros of every real part to contribute less than the main term, so its exponent is the
largest density exponent on $[1/2, 11/12]$, which is $12/5$ with Huxley's estimate, at $\sigma = 3/4$. The sieve needs
the zeros only in sums of two variables near $q$ times a product of primes, where Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} holds for
every $L$ and uses the density estimate only above $0.7789$, and it recovers the primes from those sums at the cost that
the certificate bounds. In exponents of $x$ the region shrinks as $L$ grows smaller, and more of the sieve's terms
fall outside it: the cost is $0.94847$ at $43/20$, and the plan of the same search passes at $2.145$ with cells of side
$0.0035$ (cost $0.975$, about twice the build). On twenty of the first prime's cells, about a quarter of the cost at
$43/20$ is the cells' width; each $0.005$ of exponent costs between $5\%$ and $9\%$ of it; and the rules that the
certificate lacks are worth a few per cent each in the exact search. By these estimates $2.14$ needs several of them
and cells of side about $0.003$ together, and $2.12$ is beyond them (\S S6). Going much further needs Type~I
information on longer products, or a Type~II estimate on a wider region, which a better density estimate above
$0.7789$ would give only a little of (Section~\ref{sec:region}).
