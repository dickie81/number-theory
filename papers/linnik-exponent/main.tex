\title{The least prime in an arithmetic progression: the exponent $11161/5000$ from a half-plane zero-free region and a formalised sieve}
\author{Ritchie Comley}
\date{Draft}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove that there is a constant $K$ such that, for every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$, there is a prime
$p \equiv a \pmod q$ with $p \le K q^{11161/5000}$. The proof is a sieve argument. Its Type~II estimate uses two
recent results: that no Dirichlet $L$-function vanishes in $\operatorname{Re} s > 11/12$, from OpenAI's release, and
Chen, Gupta and Li's zero-density estimate, used at its exponent $4/(1 + \sigma)$ on narrow steps. Neither result is
refereed: the first is in preprints that have not been reviewed, and the second is an arXiv preprint. The theorem
depends on both. The theorem, these two results and the classical results the proof uses are proved in Lean~4, and
the proof depends on no axioms beyond Lean's standard three.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is $K$ such that for every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$ there is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with
$p \le K q^{11161/5000}$.
\end{theorem}

Linnik \cite{linnik44} proved that there are absolute constants $K$ and $L$ such that every reduced residue class
mod $q$ contains a prime $p \le K q^L$. The admissible exponent $L$ has been lowered many times. Heath-Brown
\cite{heath-brown92} reached $L = 5.5$ and Xylouris $L = 5$ \cite{xylouris11}, and then $L < 5$ \cite{xylouris18}.
Under the generalised Riemann hypothesis every $L > 2$ is admissible \cite{lls}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has
$L = 11161/5000 = 2.2322$. Its Lean proof has no hypothesis, but it rests on two recent results that are not yet
refereed, OpenAI's zero-free half-plane (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane}) and Chen, Gupta and Li's zero-density
estimate (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:density}), which Section~\ref{sec:inputs} states.

\subsection*{The zeros near $\operatorname{Re} s = 1$}

The difficulty in Linnik's theorem is the zeros of the $L$-functions mod $q$ near the line $\operatorname{Re} s = 1$.
By the explicit formula, a zero $\rho = \beta + i\gamma$ of $L(s, \chi)$ contributes about $x^{\beta - 1}$, relative to
the main term, to the count of primes $p \le x$ in a class mod $q$. A zero-density estimate with exponent $A$, a
bound of about $(qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)}$ for the number of zeros with real part at least $\sigma$, makes the zeros of
real part about $\sigma$ contribute about $(q^A/x)^{1 - \sigma}$ together. Classically a zero may have
$1 - \beta$ as small as $c/\log q$, and at $x = q^L$ the contribution $(q^A/x)^{1 - \beta}$ is then a constant, not a
small quantity. The record proofs control these zeros with zero-free regions, density estimates without logarithmic
factors and the Deuring--Heilbronn phenomenon, and the constants of all three enter the exponent.

The zero-free half-plane of OpenAI's release \cite{openai}, Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane}, removes the zeros with
$\beta > 11/12$. Every remaining zero then contributes at most a power $x^{-1/12}$, and the problem becomes one of
density: a density exponent $A$ for $1/2 \le \sigma \le 11/12$ gives Linnik's theorem with every exponent $L > A$.
Huxley's density estimate \cite{huxley75} gives $A = 12/5$. Chen, Gupta and Li \cite{cgl}, whose method is
Guth and Maynard's \cite{guth-maynard}, prove $A = 7/3$; their Theorem~1.2, for a divisor $q_1$ of $q$, has at
$q_1 = q$ the exponent $4/(1 + \sigma)$, which is $7/3$ at $\sigma = 5/7$ and smaller beyond. Their
Corollary~1.4, on primes in short intervals in arithmetic progressions under a zero-free region that
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane} supplies, gives the exponent $7/3 + \varepsilon$.
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} gives the details.

\subsection*{Below the density exponent}

This paper goes below $7/3$ with a sieve. Harman's sieve \cite{harman83, harman} bounds from below the number of
primes in the set $\mathcal A$ of integers $n \in (x, 2x]$ with $n \equiv a \pmod q$ by comparing it with the set
$\mathcal B$ of the integers $n \in (x, 2x]$ prime to $q$, weighted by $1/\varphi(q)$. It needs two kinds of
information. Type~I information is the comparison of $\mathcal A$ with $\mathcal B$ on the multiples of each small
integer, which counting in residue classes gives. Type~II information is the comparison on bilinear sums
$\sum_{mn} a_m b_n$, with $m$ in a range $(x^{u_1}, x^{u_2})$, bounded coefficients $a_m$, and $b_n$ supported on
products of primes, which needs the zeros. A comparison over all such sums is not available at
$x = q^{11161/5000}$. What is available is a window $[u_1, u_2]$ of exponents on which it holds, and the sieve recovers
the primes from the sums it can compare,
losing a fraction of them that a finite computation bounds.

The paper's contributions are three.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A Type~II estimate over the zeros' real parts} (Section~\ref{sec:type2}). The characters mod $q$ are
  sorted into bins by the largest real part of their $L$-function's zeros near a given height. In each bin the
  bilinear sum is bounded by the zeros, which make the Dirichlet polynomial of the primes small, and by the number
  of characters in the bin, which a density estimate bounds; the best of orthogonality and the
  Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality is taken in each bin. Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:half-plane} and~\ref{hyp:density},
  with the classical density estimates of Ingham and Huxley, give the window
  $[u_1, u_2] = [40981/100000,\ 47725/100000]$ at $L = 11161/5000$.
\item \emph{A decomposition by Buchstab's identity, with a refinement} (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}). The
  identity is applied to depth four. The terms in which the product of some of the prime variables has its
  exponent in the window, or in its reflection $[1 - u_2, 1 - u_1]$, are Type~II sums and are evaluated, and the
  others are dropped. The refinement keeps, in a dropped term of depth two, the part whose integers have exactly two
  prime factors in the cofactor and are, in some grouping of their four prime factors, a Type~II sum.
\item \emph{A certificate} (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}). The fraction of the primes the decomposition loses is
  bounded above by a sum over the cells of a grid of the exponents, computed exactly in integers. On a grid of
  $300$ cells a side the sum is below $0.99873$, which is less than $1$, so a positive proportion of the primes
  remains.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{The exponent}

The exponent $11161/5000$ uses Chen, Gupta and Li's Proposition~3.1 at every point of $[5/7, 1307/1680]$: on each of
$107$ steps of length $1/1680$ the exponent is just above $4/(1 + \sigma)$ at the step's left end. From $\sigma = 7/9$
on, Huxley's exponent $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ is below $4/(1 + \sigma)$, and on the three steps of that length that follow the
step containing $7/9$, up to $131/168$, the curve takes Huxley's: just above $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ at the step's left end. It also uses Ingham's
estimate on its last step, $[99/140, 5/7]$, at $\varepsilon = 1/400000$, where the exponent is $7/3 + 1/100000$. The
steps are narrow because the ends of the window are set by the places where a step's exponent most exceeds the curve it
bounds: at the steps around $7/9$, where $4/(1 + \sigma)$ meets Huxley's $3/(3\sigma - 1)$, and at $\sigma = 5/7$,
where the exponent is largest. Using Huxley's exponent on those three steps, instead of Chen, Gupta and Li's, lowers
the certificate's total enough to close at $11161/5000$ in place of $893/400 = 2.2325$, a gain of $0.0003$.

The rest is not new. Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:half-plane} and~\ref{hyp:density} are OpenAI's and Chen, Gupta and Li's,
and the density estimates of Ingham \cite{ingham} and Huxley \cite{huxley72, huxley75}, the Hal\'asz--Montgomery
inequality \cite{halasz, montgomery69} and Harman's sieve are classical. Section~\ref{sec:inputs} states the inputs
and Section~\ref{sec:type1} the form of the sieve that is used.

\section{Notation and the two inputs}\label{sec:inputs}

Throughout, $q \ge 1$ is the modulus and $a$ is prime to $q$. The Dirichlet characters mod $q$ are $\chi$, the trivial
one is $\chi_0$, and $L(s, \chi)$ is the $L$-function of $\chi$, continued to the whole plane (in Lean, Mathlib's
\lean{DirichletCharacter.LFunction}). For $1/2 \le \sigma \le 1$ and $T \ge 2$ let
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) = \#\{\chi \bmod q : L(s, \chi) = 0 \text{ for some } s \in \mathcal R(\sigma, T)\},
\]
where $\mathcal R(\sigma, T) = \{s : \operatorname{Re} s \ge \sigma,\ |\operatorname{Im} s| \le T\}$: the number of
characters mod $q$ whose $L$-function has a zero in the rectangle. It counts characters, not zeros, so a bound
for the number of zeros bounds it. The proof is a sieve on the integers up to $x = q^L$, with $L = 11161/5000$ in the
end, and $q$ is taken large.

\begin{hypothesis}[the zero-free half-plane]\label{hyp:half-plane}
For every $q \ge 1$, every Dirichlet character $\chi$ mod $q$ and every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re} s > 11/12$,
$L(s, \chi) \ne 0$, except at $s = 1$ for the trivial character.
\end{hypothesis}

This is OpenAI's result \cite{openai}: their companion preprint proves it, and their main preprint proves the
stronger $\operatorname{Re} s > 7/8$. These preprints have not been refereed, and the theorem of this paper depends on
the result. It is proved in Lean as \lean{Eis.LFunction\_ne\_zero\_of\_re\_gt}, following
the companion preprint. \file{formalisations/openai-half-plane.md} gives the attribution and the differences
between the Lean statement and the source's.

\begin{hypothesis}[zero-density estimates from $5/7$ to $1307/1680$]\label{hyp:density}
For $0 \le k < 107$ let $\sigma_k = 5/7 + k/1680$. There are exponents $A_k$ with
$4/(1 + \sigma_k) < A_k < 4/(1 + \sigma_k) + 1/10000$, listed in the header of \file{lib/pnt/LinnikCGLSteps.lean}
($A_0 = 2.3334$ and $A_{106} = 2.25057$), such that for each $k$ there is $C$ with the following property: for every
$q \ge 1$, every $\sigma_k \le \sigma \le \sigma_k + 1/1680$ and every $T \ge 2$, at most
$C (qT)^{A_k (1 - \sigma)} (1 + \log qT)^{10}$ characters mod $q$ have an $L$-function with a zero in
$\operatorname{Re} s \ge \sigma$, $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$.
\end{hypothesis}

The exponent $A_k$ is that of Chen, Gupta and Li's Theorem~1.2 \cite{cgl} at $q_1 = q$, taken at the step's left end
$\sigma_k$, up to $10^{-4}$. Their estimate counts zeros, which gives the count of characters stated here. The
library does not prove their Theorem~1.2. It proves their Proposition~3.1, the large-values estimate, and derives each
step from it, in \file{lib/pnt/LinnikCGLSteps.lean} (Section~\ref{sec:lean} names the theorems), and
\file{formalisations/} documents it. Their paper \cite{cgl} is an arXiv preprint.

\subsection*{The density curve}

The Type~II estimate of Section~\ref{sec:type2} needs a bound for $N^*(\sigma, T; q)$ at every $\sigma$ in
$[1/2, 1]$. It takes it in the form of a curve.

\begin{definition}\label{def:curve}
A function $g : \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ is a \emph{density curve} if there is $C \ge 0$ such that for every
$q \ge 1$, every $1/2 \le \sigma \le 1$ and every $T \ge 2$,
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le C\, (qT)^{g(\sigma)} \bigl(1 + \log qT\bigr)^{10}.
\]
\end{definition}

The curve of this paper is the function $g_7$ defined as follows (in Lean, \lean{SieveTypeIIU22322D.g7d}); each of
its pieces is a known density estimate, and the steps make each piece a constant multiple of $1 - \sigma$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $g_7(\sigma) = 1$ for $\sigma < 4/7$.
\item On $[4/7, 5/7]$, divided into twenty steps $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2)$ of length $1/140$,
  $g_7(\sigma) = \bigl(3/(2 - \sigma_2) + \varepsilon\bigr)(1 - \sigma)$: Ingham's exponent at the step's right end,
  with $\varepsilon = 1/2000$ except on the last step $[99/140, 5/7)$, where $\varepsilon = 1/400000$ and the exponent
  is $7/3 + 1/100000$.
\item On $[5/7, 1307/1680)$, divided into $107$ steps $[\sigma_k, \sigma_{k+1})$ of length $1/1680$,
  $g_7(\sigma) = A_k(1 - \sigma)$ with $A_k$ the exponent of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:density}.
\item On $[1307/1680, 131/168)$, divided into three steps $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2)$ of length $1/1680$,
  $g_7(\sigma) = A'(1 - \sigma)$ with $A' = 2.24915$, $2.24614$ and $2.24315$: Huxley's exponent $3/(3\sigma_1 - 1)$ at the
  step's left end plus at most $2 \cdot 10^{-4}$.
\item On $[131/168, 5/6]$, divided into fifteen steps $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2)$ of length $1/280$,
  $g_7(\sigma) = \bigl(3/(3\sigma_1 - 1) + 1/2000\bigr)(1 - \sigma)$: Huxley's exponent at the step's left end.
\item $g_7(\sigma) = (101/50)(1 - \sigma)$ for $\sigma \ge 5/6$.
\end{itemize}
The exponents are written in Lean as fractions; the first step's is $3/(2 - 81/140) + 1/2000 = 840199/398000$. On
$[4/7, 5/7]$ the exponent rises from about $2.111$ to $7/3 + 1/100000$. On $[5/7, 1307/1680]$ it falls from $2.3334$ to
$2.25057$, on $[1307/1680, 131/168]$ it falls on to $2.24315$, and on $[131/168, 5/6]$, where Huxley's steps take over at $2.2405$, it falls to about $2.015$. On each step $A(1 - \sigma) \le A(1 - \sigma_1) \le 1$, so $g_7 \le 1$ everywhere, and
$g_7(1/2) = 1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve}
Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:density}, $g_7$ is a density curve.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A finite union of ranges on each of which the bound holds, with its own constant, is a range on which it holds with
the sum of the constants, so it suffices to prove the bound on each piece.
\begin{itemize}
\item For $\sigma < 4/7$: $N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le \varphi(q) \le qT$.
\item On each step of $[4/7, 5/7]$: Ingham's estimate \cite{ingham} in the form over the characters mod $q$, which
  bounds $N^*(\sigma, T; q)$ by $C(qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)}(\log qT)^{10}$ on $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2]$ for every $A$
  above $3/(2 - \sigma_2)$ (\file{formalisations/ingham-density.md}; for the last step, Section~\ref{sec:lean}). Ingham proved his estimate for the zeros of
  $\zeta$; the form over the characters is Theorem~12.1 of Montgomery's book \cite{montgomery-topics}.
\item On each step of $[5/7, 1307/1680]$: Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:density}.
\item On each of the three steps of $[1307/1680, 131/168]$: Huxley's estimate \cite{huxley75} over the characters mod $q$,
  with the exponents $A'$ above, which exceed $3/(3\sigma_1 - 1)$ at the step's left end $\sigma_1$ by less than
  $2 \cdot 10^{-4}$ (\file{formalisations/huxley-density.md}; the library's step of that argument at new parameters, in
  \file{lib/pnt/LinnikHuxJunction.lean}).
\item On each step of $[131/168, 5/6]$: Huxley's estimate \cite{huxley75} over the characters mod $q$, with the
  exponent $3/(3\sigma_1 - 1) + 1/2000$ on $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2]$ (\file{formalisations/huxley-density.md}).
\item For $\sigma \ge 5/6$: the exponent $101/50$, by the same large-values argument at fixed parameters on
  $[5/6, 11/12]$, and by Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane} above $11/12$, where at most the trivial character is
  counted (\file{formalisations/huxley-density.md}).
\end{itemize}
Each of these bounds is a theorem of the library, with a factor $(\log qT)^{10}$ at most $(1 + \log qT)^{10}$; the
claims table of Section~\ref{sec:lean} names them.
\end{proof}

The only piece that is not classical is the one on $[5/7, 1307/1680]$, where Chen, Gupta and Li's estimate, in the
step form of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:density}, replaces Huxley's exponent, which is $12/5$ at $\sigma = 3/4$. Their
exponent is $7/3$ at $\sigma = 5/7$ and falls below it inside the range; the curve $g_7$ follows it step by step,
to within $10^{-4}$ of $4/(1 + \sigma)$ at each step's left end. Huxley's exponent $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ is the smaller of
the two from $\sigma = 7/9$ on, and $7/9$ lies in the step $[653/840, 1307/1680]$, which is the last of the $107$; so
from $1307/1680$ on, on the three steps up to $131/168$ and on the steps from $131/168$ on, $g_7$ uses Huxley's. Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane} enters the curve only
above $11/12$, and enters the Type~II estimate through the largest real part of the zeros, which it bounds by $11/12$.

\section{The Type II estimate}\label{sec:type2}

Fix a smooth real function $f_0$ supported in $[1/2, 2]$. For a character $\chi$, a scale $Q \ge 4$ and a
height $h \in \mathbb R$, the smoothed prime sum is
\[
  S_Q(\chi, h) = \sum_{p \le 2Q} \chi(p) \Bigl(\frac{p}{Q}\Bigr)^{-ih} f_0\Bigl(\frac{p}{Q}\Bigr),
\]
and for a finite set $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$ of integers, coefficients $a_m \in \mathbb C$ and a height $h_0$,
\[
  W(\chi) = \sum_{m \in S} a_m \chi(m) m^{-ih_0}.
\]
The sieve of Section~\ref{sec:type1} needs, for the integers with a factor $m$ in a range $(x^{u_1}, x^{u_2})$ and
the rest a product of primes, that the non-trivial characters contribute less than the main term. In the Dirichlet
polynomials above this is the following estimate. Write $\mathcal X^*$ for the non-trivial characters mod $q$.

\begin{theorem}[the Type~II estimate]\label{thm:type2}
Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:density}, and let $L = 11161/5000$, $u_1 = 40981/100000$ and $u_2 = 47725/100000$. Let
$K_0 \ge 0$ be an integer, $e_1 > 0$ and $0 < \eta \le 10^{-5}$. There is $q_0$ such that for every $q \ge q_0$, every
$P$ with $q^{u_1 L} \le P \le q^{u_2 L}$, every $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$, all $a_m$ and $h_0$, every $K \le K_0$, and all
$Q_1, \dots, Q_K \ge q^{e_1}$ and $|h_1|, \dots, |h_K| \le q^\eta$ with $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/P$,
\[
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} |W(\chi)| \prod_{j=1}^K |S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)|
  \le q^{L - 1/50000} \Bigl(\frac1P \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

At $x = q^L$ the condition on $P$ is $x^{u_1} \le P \le x^{u_2}$. The bound saves the power $q^{-1/50000}$ over what
the sieve needs, which is the size $q^L (\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$ of the trivial character's term up to a constant.
The proof has three parts: the prime sums are bounded through the zeros of each character's $L$-function, the
characters are sorted by those zeros, and in each class the sum over the characters is bounded in two ways.

\subsection{Prime sums in a zero-free rectangle}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:prime-sum}
For every integer $K \ge 0$ there are $B, C \ge 0$ such that the following holds for every $q \ge 1$, every
$\chi \in \mathcal X^*$, every $1/2 \le \theta \le 11/12$, $0 < \delta \le 1/24$, $H \ge 0$, $Q \ge 4$ and
$y \in \mathbb R$. If $L(s, \chi) \ne 0$ for $\operatorname{Re} s > \theta$, $|\operatorname{Im} s - y| < H + 1$,
then
\begin{multline*}
  |S_Q(\chi, y)| \le M_\delta(\theta)\, A\, B\,
  \frac{Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{11/12 + \delta}(1 + H)^{-K}}{\log Q} \\
  + C\bigl(\sqrt{2Q} + 1\bigr)\bigl(\log_2 (2Q) + 1\bigr),
\end{multline*}
where $A = \log(q + 2) + \log(|y| + 4) + 1$ and $M_\delta(\theta)$ depends only on $\theta$ and $\delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The weight $\psi_Q(u) = f_0(u)(1 + \log u/\log Q)^{-1}$ turns the prime sum into $1/\log Q$ times the sum of
$\chi(n)\Lambda(n)(n/Q)^{-iy}\psi_Q(n/Q)$, up to the proper prime powers, which give the last term. By Mellin
inversion that sum is an integral of $-L'/L(s, \chi)$ against the Mellin transform of the weight, which decays
faster than any power of $|\operatorname{Im} s - y|$ uniformly in $Q$. The line of integration moves from
$\operatorname{Re} s = 2$ to $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta$, which Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane} allows,
and, for $|\operatorname{Im} s - y| \le H$, across the zero-free rectangle to $\operatorname{Re} s = \theta + \delta$.
On both lines $|L'/L(s, \chi)| \ll A$, by the bound for the logarithmic derivative in a zero-free disc.
\end{proof}

The argument is the classical one for primes in progressions, with a smooth weight in place of a sharp cut-off; the
only use of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane} is the line $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta$.

\subsection{The bins}

Let $N = 500000$ and $\beta_i = 1/2 + i/(12N/5)$ for $0 \le i \le N$, so that $\beta_0 = 1/2$, $\beta_N = 11/12$ and
the bins have width $1/1200000$. For a height bound $T \ge 2$ the \emph{bin} of $\chi \in \mathcal X^*$ is the largest
$i < N$ such that $L(s, \chi)$ has a zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > \beta_i$ and $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$, and
$0$ if there is none. If $\chi$ is in the bin $i$ then:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L(s, \chi)$ has no zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > \beta_{i+1}$ and $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$: for
  $i + 1 < N$ by the choice of $i$, and for $i + 1 = N$ by Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane}. So
  Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum} applies with $\theta = \beta_{i+1}$ to every height $|y| \le T - H - 1$.
\item For $i \ge 1$, $\chi$ is counted by $N^*(\beta_i, T; q)$, so a density curve $g$ bounds the number of characters
  in the bin by $C(qT)^{g(\beta_i)}(1 + \log qT)^{10}$. For $i = 0$ the bound $\varphi(q) \le qT$ is the same with
  $g(1/2) = 1$.
\end{itemize}
The bins depend on $T$ but not on the heights $h_j$. The proof takes $H = q^\eta$ and $T = 2H + 1$, so that every
height $|h_j| \le q^\eta$ has $|h_j| + H + 1 \le T$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum} with $K$ large in terms of $\eta$,
so that $(1 + H)^{-K}$ cancels the term $Q^{11/12 + \delta}$. For large $q$ it gives
$|S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)| \le 2 Q_j^{\beta_{i+1} + 2\delta}$ in the bin $i$.

\subsection{Two bounds in a bin}

Let $R$ be the set of characters in a bin. Since the prime sums are bounded on $R$, it remains to bound
$\sum_{\chi \in R} |W(\chi)|$. There are two bounds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Orthogonality.} For $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$ and every $t$,
  \[
    \sum_\chi \Bigl|\sum_{m \in S} c_m \chi(m) m^{-it}\Bigr|^2 \le (P + q) \sum_{m \in S} |c_m|^2,
  \]
  summed over all characters mod $q$, since each residue class mod $q$ has at most $P/q + 1$ elements in
  $(P, 2P]$. With Cauchy--Schwarz over $R$,
  \[
    \sum_{\chi \in R} |W(\chi)| \le |R|^{1/2} \Bigl((P + q) \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
  \]
\item \emph{The Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality} \cite{halasz, montgomery69}, in the form over the characters mod
  $q$ at one height (\file{formalisations/halasz-montgomery.md}):
  \[
    \Bigl(\sum_{\chi \in R} |W(\chi)|\Bigr)^2 \le e \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2 \cdot \bigl(2P |R| + |R|^2 B\bigr),
  \]
  where $B$ bounds the off-diagonal kernels $K_{2P}(\chi\chi'^{-1}, 0)$, $\chi \ne \chi'$ in $R$. These are smoothed
  sums of a non-trivial character over $n \ll P$, and $|K_{2P}(\psi, 0)| \le C(2P)^{1/12 - \delta'}
  (2q)^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}$: the kernel's integral moves to $\operatorname{Re} w = 1/12 - \delta'$, where
  the functional equation and the bound on $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta$ that
  Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane} gives bound the $L$-function.
\end{enumerate}
The first bound is the better one when the bin is large, the second when it is small, which is the case near the top
of the curve. The proof takes the better of the two in each bin.

\subsection{The window condition}

Write $P = q^m$ and $|R| \le q^{g(\beta) + o(1)}$. The product of the prime sums is at most
$q^{(L - m)(\beta + w) + o(1)}$, where $\beta$ is the bin's lower end and $w \le 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$ accounts for the
width of the bins and for the $2\delta$ above. Measured against $q^L (\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$, the two bounds give the
exponents
\[
  E_1 = (L - m)(\beta + w) + \frac{g(\beta)}2 + \frac m2 + \frac{\max(1, m)}2
\]
by orthogonality, and by the Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality the larger of
\begin{align*}
  E_2 &= (L - m)(\beta + w) + \frac{g(\beta)}2 + m, \\
  E_3 &= (L - m)(\beta + w) + \frac{g(\beta)}2 + \frac m2 + \frac{g(\beta) + b(m)}2,
\end{align*}
with $b(m) = 5/12 + m/12 + \delta'(1 - m) + 2\varepsilon'$ the exponent of the kernel bound.

\begin{definition}\label{def:window}
A window $[m_1, m_2]$ is \emph{admissible} for a curve $g$ at $L$ if for every $1/2 \le \beta \le 11/12$, every
$m_1 \le m \le m_2$ and every $0 \le w \le 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$, either $E_1 \le L - 1/25000$, or $m \le 1$ and
$\max(E_2, E_3) \le L - 1/25000$, with $\delta' = \varepsilon' = 1/1000$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:type2-gen}
Let $g$ be a density curve with $g \le 1$ and $g(1/2) = 1$, and let $[m_1, m_2]$ be admissible for $g$ at $L$. Then the
conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} holds with $q^{m_1} \le P \le q^{m_2}$ in place of
$q^{u_1 L} \le P \le q^{u_2 L}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sum the better of the two bounds over the bins. Each bin contributes at most
$q^{L - 1/25000 + \eta}(\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$ times constants and a power of $\log qT$, the factor $q^\eta$
coming from $T \le 3q^\eta$ in the count of the bin. There are $N$ bins, so for large $q$ the total is at most
$q^{L - 1/25000 + \eta + 1/100000}(\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$. Since $\eta \le 10^{-5}$,
$L - 1/25000 + \eta + 1/100000 \le L - 1/50000$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:window}
The window $[u_1 L, u_2 L]$, with $u_1 = 40981/100000$ and $u_2 = 47725/100000$, is admissible for $g_7$ at
$L = 11161/5000$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On each piece of $g_7$, $g_7(\beta) = A(1 - \beta)$ with $A$ constant, or $g_7 = 1$. There $E_1$, $E_2$ and $E_3$ are
polynomials of degree at most one in each of $\beta$, $m$ and $w$ separately, so on a box
$[\beta_{\mathrm{lo}}, \beta_{\mathrm{hi}}] \times [m_{\mathrm{lo}}, m_{\mathrm{hi}}] \times [0, 4 \cdot 10^{-6}]$ each
attains its maximum at a corner. Split the window at $m = 1$. On every piece below $5/6$ the inequality
$E_1 \le L - 1/25000$ holds at the corners. On the top piece $[5/6, 11/12]$ it holds at the corners with $m \ge 1$,
and $\max(E_2, E_3) \le L - 1/25000$ holds at the corners with $m \le 1$. Each corner is an inequality between
rational numbers, checked exactly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:curve}, $g_7$ is a density curve under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:density}, with $g_7 \le 1$ and
$g_7(1/2) = 1$. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:type2-gen} with the window of Proposition~\ref{prop:window}.
\end{proof}

The Hal\'asz--Montgomery branch is used only on the top piece of the curve, at the corners with $m \le 1$, where the
bins are small.

\section{Type I estimates and Harman's sieve}\label{sec:type1}

Let $x = q^L$ and let $a$ be prime to $q$. The sets of the sieve are
\[
  \mathcal A = \{n \in (x, 2x] : n \equiv a \ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\}, \qquad
  \mathcal B = \{n \in (x, 2x] : (n, q) = 1\}.
\]
For a set $\mathcal C$ of integers, an integer $r$ and $z \ge 1$, $\mathcal C_r$ is the set of the multiples of $r$
in $\mathcal C$, and $S(\mathcal C_r, z)$ the number of $n \in \mathcal C_r$ with no prime factor of $n/r$ below $z$.
So $S(\mathcal A, (2x)^{1/2})$ is the number of primes in $\mathcal A$. The sieve compares $\mathcal A$ with
$\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ through the weight
\[
  \mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \frac{\mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]}{\varphi(q)}
  = \frac1{\varphi(q)} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \overline\chi(a) \chi(n),
\]
which is orthogonality: the trivial character cancels, and the other characters carry the difference. This section
states the three tools of Harman's sieve \cite{harman83, harman} in the form the proof uses. None of them is new;
\file{formalisations/harman-sieve.md} gives their sources and the differences between the Lean statements and the
classical ones.

\subsection{Type~I}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:type1}
Let $N_1 \le N_2$ and let $M > 0$ be prime to $q$. The number of $n \in (N_1, N_2]$ with $n \equiv a \pmod q$ and
$M \mid n$ is $(N_2 - N_1)/(Mq)$ within $1$, and the number of $n \in (N_1, N_2]$ prime to $q$ with $M \mid n$ is
$\varphi(q)(N_2 - N_1)/(Mq)$ within $\varphi(q)$.
\end{lemma}

So $|\mathcal A_M| - |\mathcal B_M|/\varphi(q)$ is at most $2$ in absolute value for every $M$ prime to $q$. This is
all the Type~I information the sieve needs: it is used for the moduli up to $q^{b_0}$ with $b_0 < L - 1$, where the
error $2$ is small against the size $x/(Mq)$ of the count.

\subsection{Type~II}

Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} bounds Dirichlet polynomials. The sieve needs sums over $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ of
integers $n = m p_1 \cdots p_K$ whose primes are cut out by comparisons. Fix a smooth function $f_0 \ge 0$ supported
in $[1/2, 2]$, an integer $K_0$ and $e_1 > 0$. A \emph{family} consists of
\begin{itemize}
\item a scale $P$ with $x^{u_1} \le P \le x^{u_2}$, a set $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$ and coefficients $|a_m| \le A_0$;
\item $K \le K_0$ primes $p_i \le 2Q_i$ with weights $f_0(p_i/Q_i)$, at scales $Q_i \ge q^{e_1}$ with
  $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} x/P$;
\item at most $J_0$ comparisons $\ell_j(m, p) > 0$, where
  $\ell_j(m, p) = \varphi_j(m) + \sum_i e_{ji} \log p_i$ with $|e_{ji}| \le E_0$ and $\ell_j \le \Lambda_0 \log q$, each
  with a \emph{pivot}, a prime $p_i$ with $e_{ji} = \pm 1$.
\end{itemize}
Its sum over $\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ is the sum of $a_m \prod_i f_0(p_i/Q_i)$ times the weight above at
$n = m p_1 \cdots p_K$, over the $(m, p)$ that satisfy the comparisons.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:family}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. Given $f_0$, $K_0$, $e_1$, $J_0$, $E_0$, $\Lambda_0$ and $A_0$, there
are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that for every $q \ge q_0$ and every family, its sum over
$\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ is at most $A_0 q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ in absolute value.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Replace each sharp comparison $\mathbf 1[\ell_j > 0]$ by a smooth cut-off $G(\ell_j)$ that differs from it only for
$|\ell_j| \le \theta$, with $\theta = x^{-\varepsilon_2}$ a small negative power. By Fourier inversion,
$\prod_j G(\ell_j) = \int_{\mathbb R^J} \prod_j \hat G(\xi_j) e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \ell_j} \, d\xi$. The part of
the phase that depends on $m$ goes into the coefficients $a_m$, and the parts that depend on the $p_i$ are twists
$(p_i/Q_i)^{-ih_i}$ with $h_i$ a combination of the $\xi_j$. On the box $|\xi_j| \le T_0$, with
$T_0 = x^{2\varepsilon_2}$, the heights are at most $q^\eta$, so after the orthogonality above each character's sum is
$W(\chi) \prod_i S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i)$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} bounds the sum over $\chi \in \mathcal X^*$. Outside
the box the trivial bound and the decay of $\hat G$ suffice. Where a sharp comparison and its smooth cut-off differ,
the pivot prime lies in a range of relative length about $\theta$, and the count of such integers is small.
\end{proof}

If instead a product of some of the primes of $n$ lies in $(x^{1 - u_2}, x^{1 - u_1})$, the product of the other
factors of $n$ lies in the window's range and plays the role of $m$. So the lemma also covers the reflected range,
and this is how the sieve uses the window and its reflection.

\subsection{Harman's comparison}

Write $u_1, u_2$ for the window's ends as fractions of $L$, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, so that the window in
terms of $q$ is $[u_1 L, u_2 L]$.

\begin{proposition}[Harman's comparison, in the form used]\label{prop:harman}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. Fix $j$, $J_0$, $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$, $\eta > 0$, and exponents
$a_0 > 0$ and $b_0$ with $a_0 + b_0 < (1 - u_1)L$ and $(1 - u_2)L < b_0 < L - 1$. There are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such
that the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let $r = r_1 \cdots r_j$ run over the
products of the $j$-tuples of primes up to $2x$ that satisfy at most $J_0$ comparisons (with constants at most
$\Lambda_1 \log q$) which force every $r_i \ge z_0 = \lceil q^{a_0} \rceil$ and $r \le q^{b_0}$. Then
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_r \Bigl(S(\mathcal A_r, z_0) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_r, z_0)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr)\Bigr|
  \le \frac{\eta\, x}{\varphi(q) \log q} + \frac{q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
For each $r$, iterate Buchstab's identity \cite{buchstab}
\[
  S(\mathcal C_r, z) = S(\mathcal C_r, w) - \sum_{w \le p < z} S(\mathcal C_{rp}, p)
\]
from $z_0$ down to $w = \lceil q^{\varepsilon_w} \rceil$, stopping a branch as soon as its product $W$ of primes
exceeds a threshold $X'$, chosen so that $W$ lies in $(X', X' z_0)$ and the cofactor $k$ of every multiple $Wk$ in
$(x, 2x]$ lies in the window's range. A stopped term is then a family in which $k$ is the Type~II variable, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:family} bounds it. At a leaf, a product $d \le q^{b_0}$, the fundamental lemma of the sieve
\cite{brun} applied to $\mathcal A_d$ and $\mathcal B_d$, with Lemma~\ref{lem:type1} for the counts of
multiples, makes the two sifted counts agree up to $c(k)$ times the number of $w$-rough multiples of $d$, where the
explicit constant $c(k)$ of Brun's sieve at the level $k$ tends to $0$ as $k$ grows. A suitable even level $k$ puts
the sum over the leaves below $\eta x/(\varphi(q) \log q)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rough numbers}

The last tool bounds from above the number $\Psi(y, z)$ of integers in $(y, 2y]$ with no prime factor below $z$. Let
$\omega^*(u) = 0$ for $u < 1$, $\omega^*(u) = 1/u$ for $1 \le u < 2$ and $\omega^*(u) = 0.5672$ for $u \ge 2$, a crude
upper bound for Buchstab's function $\omega$: the two agree on $[1, 2)$, and $\omega$ stays below $0.5672$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rough}
For every $U$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, for all large $z$ and all $y$ with $u = \log 2y/\log z \le U$,
$\Psi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon)\, y/\log z$.
\end{lemma}

It is proved from Buchstab's inequality for $\Psi$, partial summation over the primes and the prime number theorem
with an error $o(t/\log t)$. The certificate of Section~\ref{sec:certificate} uses $\omega^*$ to bound the share of the
integers in each term it drops.

\section{The decomposition and its certificate}\label{sec:certificate}

This section bounds $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$, with $z_{\mathrm{top}} = (2x)^{1/2}$, from below. The
decomposition writes it by Buchstab's identity as a signed sum of sifted terms. Each term is evaluated, that is
compared with its $\mathcal B$-version by Lemma~\ref{lem:family} or Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}, or, if it has
sign $+$, dropped, which costs its $\mathcal B$-version. The certificate is an upper bound for the total cost,
computed exactly in integers.

\subsection{Exponents and the grid}

The exponent of a prime $p$ is $\alpha = \log p/\log x$. Fix a denominator $D = 2^{40}$ and round the window's ends
inward: $U_1 = \lceil u_1 D \rceil$ and $U_2 = \lfloor u_2 D \rfloor$, so that $u_1 < U_1/D < U_2/D < u_2$. Let
$v = (U_2 - U_1)/D$. The decomposition sifts the primes of exponent at least $v$, so $z_0 = x^v$. The grid has $n$
cells a side, of side $h = \lceil (D/2 - (U_2 - U_1))/n \rceil / D$; the cell $k$ is the range
$[e_k, e_k + h]$ of exponents with $e_k = v + kh$, for $0 \le k < n$, and the cells cover $[v, 1/2]$. Here $n = 300$.

The set of \emph{evaluable} exponents is
\[
  \mathcal U = (U_1/D,\ U_2/D) \cup (1 - U_2/D,\ 1 - U_1/D).
\]
A box, a product of cells, is \emph{surely evaluable} if for some subset of its coordinates the sum of the exponents
lies in $\mathcal U$ throughout the box. If the exponents of the primes $p_1, \dots, p_k$ of a term lie in such a box,
then a product of some of them lies in $(x^{U_1/D}, x^{U_2/D})$, or in the reflected range, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:family} the term's sums over $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ agree up to $q^{L - \delta}/
\varphi(q)$. A box is tested exactly: the subset sums of a box range over intervals with rational ends.

\subsection{The decomposition}

Write $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots$ for the exponents of the primes $p_1 > p_2 > \cdots$ that Buchstab's identity
introduces, each at least $v$. Let $X'$ be the threshold of Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}'s proof, a power of $x$
chosen so that a product $W > X'$ of primes below $x^{1/2}$ with $W \le x^{1 - U_1/D}$ has every cofactor $k$ with
$Wk \in (x, 2x]$ in the window's range.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Depth one.}
  $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})
  = S(\mathcal A, z_0) - \sum_{z_0 \le p_1 < z_{\mathrm{top}}} S(\mathcal A_{p_1}, p_1)$.
  The first term is evaluated by Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}. A term whose $\alpha_1$ lies in a cell inside the
  window is evaluated by Lemma~\ref{lem:family}; the others are decomposed again by Buchstab's identity, which gives
  $S(\mathcal A_{p_1}, z_0)$, evaluated by Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}, and the terms
  $S(\mathcal A_{p_1 p_2}, p_2)$ of depth two with $z_0 \le p_2 < p_1$, with sign $+$.
\item \emph{Depth two.} Let $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ lie in the cell $(i, j)$. If the box of the cell is surely
  evaluable, the term is evaluated. Otherwise the cell is a \emph{candidate} if it can meet the region
  $\alpha_2 < \alpha_1$, $2\alpha_1 < 1$, $\alpha_1 + 2\alpha_2 \le 1$ where the term is not empty. A non-candidate
  cell that is not surely evaluable is dropped. A candidate cell is either dropped or decomposed, by the rule below;
  if decomposed, its terms with $p_1 p_2 > X'$ are evaluated with the cofactor as the Type~II variable, and those with
  $p_1 p_2 \le X'$ are decomposed again, which gives terms of depth three with sign $-$.
\item \emph{Depth three.} A term of depth three cannot be dropped. If its box is surely evaluable, it is evaluated;
  otherwise it is split at $X'$ in the same way, and the part with $p_1 p_2 p_3 \le X'$ gives terms of depth four with
  sign $+$.
\item \emph{Depth four.} A term of depth four whose box is surely evaluable is evaluated, and the others are dropped.
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{sharp rule} allows a candidate cell $(i, j)$ to be decomposed only if
$\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 < 1 - U_1/D$ on its box, and $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 + \alpha_3 < 1 - U_1/D$ on the boxes of its
non-evaluable cells of depth three: then the splits at $X'$ produce only cofactors in the window's range.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:decomp}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. For every $\varepsilon > 0$, for all large $q$ and every $a$ prime to
$q$,
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) \ge \frac{1}{\varphi(q)} \Bigl(S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})
  - D(\mathcal B)\Bigr) - \varepsilon \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x},
\]
where $D(\mathcal B)$ is the sum of the $\mathcal B$-versions of the dropped terms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identities of the decomposition are exact. A dropped term has sign $+$ and its $\mathcal A$-count is at least
$0$, so dropping it lowers the right side by its $\mathcal B$-version. Every evaluated term, and every term
$S(\cdot, z_0)$, has its $\mathcal A$- and $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$-counts within an error $\mathrm{Err}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:family} and Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}, and there are at most $1 + n(1 + n(n(n + 2) + 2))$ of
them. For large $q$ the total error is below $\varepsilon x/(\varphi(q) \log x)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The cost of a dropped term}

A dropped term of depth two over the cell $(i, j)$ counts the $n = p_1 p_2 m \in (x, 2x]$ whose cofactor $m$ has no
prime factor below $p_2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:rough} and the prime number theorem its $\mathcal B$-version is, for large
$q$, at most $x/(\varphi(q) \log x)$ times
\[
  \iint_{\text{cell}} \omega^*\Bigl(\frac{1 - \alpha_1 - \alpha_2}{\alpha_2}\Bigr)
  \frac{d\alpha_1\, d\alpha_2}{\alpha_1 \alpha_2^2},
\]
plus $\varepsilon$, and similarly at depth four with
$d\alpha_1 \cdots d\alpha_4/(\alpha_1 \alpha_2 \alpha_3 \alpha_4^2)$ and the argument
$(1 - \alpha_1 - \cdots - \alpha_4)/\alpha_4$. The certificate bounds each integral by its integrand's maximum on
the cell times the cell's volume, with $\omega^*$ bounded on the cell's range of arguments, and works in units
of $1/K$ with $K = 2^{60}$, every quotient rounded up.
\begin{itemize}
\item A dropped cell of depth two costs this bound, \texttt{bound2}.
\item A decomposed cell $(i, j)$ costs the bounds of its dropped cells of depth four, summed over its non-evaluable
  cells of depth three: \texttt{refcost}. The cell is decomposed when the sharp rule allows it and this sum is at
  most its own \texttt{bound2}.
\item The total is the sum of the costs over the candidate cells, $\mathtt{total} = \sum_{i, j} \mathtt{cost}(i, j)$.
\end{itemize}
The non-candidate cells that are not surely evaluable cost nothing in the total: on them the term is empty, or the
argument $(1 - \alpha_1 - \alpha_2)/\alpha_2$ is below $1$, where $\omega^* = 0$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:drop}
If $\mathtt{total}/K \le c$, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and all large $q$,
$D(\mathcal B) \le (c + \varepsilon)\, x/\log x$.
\end{lemma}

On the grid of this paper the certificate as it stands totals about $1.10833K$ (a computation by
\file{tools/linnik/route_limits.py}, not checked in Lean).
Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp} with Lemma~\ref{lem:drop} then gives nothing. The refinement removes about a tenth of the total.

\subsection{The refinement: keeping two-prime parts}

A dropped term $S(\mathcal A_{p_1 p_2}, p_2)$ counts, among others, the $n = p_1 p_2 q_1 q_2$ with primes
$p_2 < q_1 < q_2$. Such an $n$ is a product of four primes, and if some of their exponents sum into $\mathcal U$ it
belongs to a Type~II sum. The refinement keeps these parts instead of dropping them.

Let the cell $(i, j)$ be a candidate that is not decomposed and lie in the region $\alpha_2 < \alpha_1 < 1/2$
(\texttt{reg2}). For a cell $k$ with $e_k \ge e_j + h$ (so $q_1 > p_2$ on the box), with $2(e_k + h) + e_i + e_j + 2h
\le 1$ (so $q_1 < q_2$ on the box), and with the box $(i, j, k)$ surely evaluable (\texttt{keep}), the \emph{kept part}
below $k$ counts the $n = p_1 p_2 q_1 q_2 \in (x, 2x]$ with $(p_1, p_2)$ in the cell $(i, j)$, $q_1$ a prime of the
cell $k$, $q_2 > q_1$ a prime, and $q_1, q_2 \ne p_1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kept}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The kept parts below a cell are disjoint sets of the dropped term's integers, so their $\mathcal A$-count is at
  most the term's.
\item Each kept part is the sum of three families of products of four primes in decreasing order, according to the
  position of $q_2$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:family} evaluates each of them.
\item For every $\delta > 0$ and all large $q$, the $\mathcal B$-version of the kept part below $k$ is at least
  $(\mathtt{keepK}(i, j, k)/K - \delta)\, x/(\varphi(q) \log x)$, where
  $\mathtt{keepK}(i, j, k) = \lfloor K h^3 / ((e_i + h)(e_j + h)(e_k + h)(1 - e_i - e_j - e_k)) \rfloor$ is a lower
  bound for its density $d\alpha_1\, d\alpha_2\, d\beta/(\alpha_1 \alpha_2 \beta \gamma)$ over the box, $\gamma$ the
  exponent of $q_2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

In the decomposition, a dropped candidate cell of \texttt{reg2} now uses $\mathcal A \ge$ (its kept parts) in place of
$\mathcal A \ge 0$, and the kept parts' $\mathcal A$- and $\mathcal B$-counts agree within the leaves' error. So the
kept parts' $\mathcal B$-count comes off the cost: the refined cost $\mathtt{cost2}(i, j)$ of such a cell is its
\texttt{bound2} less the sum $\mathtt{sub2}(i, j)$ of the \texttt{keepK} over the cells $k$ that \texttt{keep} passes,
truncated at $0$, and every other cell costs as before. The decisions which cells are candidates and which are
decomposed are unchanged.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:decomp-kept}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, and let $\mathtt{total2} = \sum_{i, j} \mathtt{cost2}(i, j)$. If
$\mathtt{total2}/K \le c$, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$, for all large $q$ and every $a$ prime to $q$,
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) \ge \frac{1}{\varphi(q)} S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})
  - (c + \varepsilon)\frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\subsection{The certificate}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:certificate}
On the grid of $n = 300$ cells a side with $U_1 = \lceil (40981/100000) D \rceil$ and
$U_2 = \lfloor (47725/100000) D \rfloor$, $\mathtt{total2} < K$; in fact $\mathtt{total2} < 0.99873 K$.
\end{proposition}

The total is a finite sum of rational expressions in natural numbers, rounded to integers, so the proposition is a
computation. Lean's kernel checks it by evaluation (\lean{decide +kernel}), split into 561 parts in 143 files: ranges
of consecutive rows of the grid, and the heaviest $119$ rows in pieces of columns. Each part proves that its sum is at
most a bound $\lceil 1.001 \times \text{part} \rceil$, computed exactly, and the bounds sum to
$1152600486568670170 < 2^{60} = K$, that is to below $0.99973 K$. The parts make $3{,}515{,}675$ tests of boxes of
depth four. The definitions
the kernel evaluates are arithmetic on natural numbers, lists and booleans, and do not depend on Mathlib.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:proof}

The argument of Sections~\ref{sec:type1} and~\ref{sec:certificate} does not use the particular values of $L$, $u_1$,
$u_2$ and $n$. It gives the following theorem, of which Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a case.

\begin{theorem}[the route theorem]\label{thm:route}
Let $2 < L \le 5/2$ and $0 < u_1 < u_2 < 1$, and let a grid of $n$ cells a side, with ends $U_1$ and $U_2$ over
$D = 2^{40}$, satisfy $u_1 D < U_1$, $U_2 < u_2 D$ and $L U_2 > D$. Assume the conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} for $L$ on the window $[u_1 L, u_2 L]$, and $\mathtt{total2} < K$ on the grid. Then there is
$K'$ such that for every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$ there is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with
$p \le K' q^L$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $c = \mathtt{total2}/K < 1$ and $0 < \varepsilon < (1 - c)/4$. By the prime number theorem, for large $q$ the
primes in $(x, 2x]$ that do not divide $q$ number at least $(1 - \varepsilon) x/\log x$, and each of them is counted
by $S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})$. Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp-kept} gives, for large $q$ and every $a$ prime to
$q$,
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) \ge (1 - \varepsilon - c - \varepsilon) \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x}
  > \frac{1 - c}{2} \cdot \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x} > 0.
\]
An integer of $\mathcal A$ counted by $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ has no prime factor below $(2x)^{1/2}$ and is at
most $2x$, so it is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with $x < p \le 2q^L$. So there is $q_0$ such that the theorem holds
for $q \ge q_0$ with $K' = 2$. Each of the finitely many pairs $(q, a)$ with $q < q_0$ has a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$
by Dirichlet's theorem, and $K'$ is taken large enough to cover them.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Take $L = 11161/5000$, $u_1 = 40981/100000$, $u_2 = 47725/100000$ and the grid of Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate}. The
grid fits the window, since its ends are rounded inward and $L U_2 > D$; Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, under
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:density}, is the Type~II estimate, and Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate} is $\mathtt{total2} < K$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:route} gives Theorem~\ref{thm:main} under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:density}, and Chen, Gupta and Li's
Proposition~3.1 proves the hypothesis. Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane} enters through Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum}, the top bin
and the kernel bound in Section~\ref{sec:type2}, and through the piece of $g_7$ above $11/12$.
\end{proof}

The proof does not compute $K$: the threshold $q_0$ comes from limits in $q$ in each step, and the small moduli
from Dirichlet's theorem. The Lean proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:route} is \lean{SieveMain2.linnik\_route\_lt2}, and the
case of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is \lean{Linnik22322R.linnik\_22322r}, which has no hypothesis: the steps of
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:density} are theorems of the library.

\subsection*{The statements}

The Lean statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
theorem linnik_22322r : ∃ K : ℝ, ∀ (q : ℕ) [NeZero q] (a : ℕ),
    a.Coprime q → ∃ p : ℕ, p.Prime ∧ p ≡ a [MOD q] ∧
      (p : ℝ) ≤ K * (q : ℝ) ^ (11161 / 5000 : ℝ)
\end{verbatim}
}
where \lean{[NeZero q]} says $q \ge 1$ and the power is the real power. The statements of the two hypotheses and of the
density estimates count the characters $\chi$ mod $q$, as elements of Mathlib's \lean{DirichletCharacter ℂ q}, for
which \lean{LFunction χ s = 0} at some $s$ in the rectangle. Mathlib's $L$-function of a character mod $q$ is the
analytic continuation of $\sum_n \chi(n) n^{-s}$, with the character taken mod $q$ and not reduced to a primitive
one. So $L(s, \chi)$ is the $L$-function of the primitive character inducing $\chi$ times the Euler factors at the
primes dividing $q$, whose zeros lie on $\operatorname{Re} s = 0$ and do not affect the counts for $\sigma \ge 1/2$.
\file{formalisations/} records, for each result from the literature, the differences between the Lean statement and
the published one.

\subsection*{What the kernel checks}

The library's own sources contain no \lean{sorry}, no declared axiom and no \lean{native\_decide}, which
\texttt{scripts/check\_hygiene.py} checks. The certificate of Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate} is checked by kernel
evaluation, \lean{decide +kernel}, and not by compiled code. The library uses 19 files of PrimeNumberTheoremAnd and
59 files of the \texttt{zeta23} folder of Anthropic's \texttt{formal-math}, each ported to the library's toolchain by
a patch that the repository records.

\subsection*{Building}

The repository's README gives the commands: \texttt{lake exe cache get} for Mathlib's prebuilt files,
\texttt{scripts/build.sh} for the build, and \texttt{python3 scripts/check\_axioms.py} for the axioms. The build's
default target is \texttt{lib/check/Check.lean} and everything it imports. Lean's kernel checks of the certificate
need several GiB of memory each, and \texttt{scripts/build.sh} limits how many run at once. On every change to the
library the repository's continuous integration rebuilds it and runs the axiom check.

\section{What the inputs give without the sieve}\label{sec:comparison}

This section claims nothing new. It places Theorem~\ref{thm:main} against what the same inputs give by the
classical argument, which has no sieve.

\subsection*{The classical deduction}

Suppose that for some $A \ge 2$ there is a uniform density estimate
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le C (qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)} (\log qT)^B \qquad (1/2 \le \sigma \le 1,\ T \ge 2).
\]
With Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane}, the explicit formula for the primes in a class mod $q$, summed over the zeros
of the $L$-functions mod $q$ up to a height, then gives Linnik's theorem with every exponent $L > A$: the zeros of
real part about $\sigma$ contribute about $(q^A/x)^{1 - \sigma}$, and none has real part above $11/12$. The library
proves this deduction as \lean{Linnik.linnik\_of\_charDensity}, and with it:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L = 12 + \varepsilon$ from Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane} alone: at most $\varphi(q) \le qT$ characters are
  counted for every $\sigma$, and at most one above $11/12$, which is the estimate with $A = 12$;
\item $L = 36/13 + \varepsilon$ with Ingham's estimate, whose exponent $3/(2 - \sigma)$ is $36/13$ at
  $\sigma = 11/12$;
\item $L = 12/5 + \varepsilon$ with Huxley's estimate, which bounds the density exponent by $12/5$ on the whole
  range.
\end{itemize}
Section~\ref{sec:lean}'s table names the Lean theorem of each.
With Chen, Gupta and Li's estimate as well, the exponent is $7/3 + \varepsilon$: this is their
Corollary~1.4 \cite{cgl}, under a zero-free region that Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:half-plane} supplies. In the terms of
this paper, the curve $g_7$ of Section~\ref{sec:inputs} satisfies $g_7(\sigma) \le (7/3 + 1/2000)(1 - \sigma)$ on
$[1/2, 1]$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:curve} and the deduction give every exponent above $7/3 + 1/2000$; the library
does not state this case as a theorem.

\subsection*{Where the gain comes from}

The deduction needs the zeros of every real part to contribute less than the main term, so its exponent is the
largest density exponent on $[1/2, 11/12]$, which is $7/3$, at $\sigma = 5/7$. The sieve needs the zeros
only in bilinear sums with one variable in the window, where Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} trades the density exponent
against the length of the variable, and recovers the primes from those sums at the cost that the certificate bounds.
The refinement of Section~\ref{sec:certificate} lowers that cost by evaluating part of the terms of depth two that the
certificate would otherwise drop. The density curve follows the decrease of Chen, Gupta and Li's exponent below $7/3$ after $\sigma = 5/7$ step by step
(Section~\ref{sec:inputs}).

\subsection*{Below $11161/5000$}

The method as it stands does not go much further. On the grid of $300$ cells a side the certificate without the
refinement totals about $1.10833K$ at $11161/5000$, above $K$ (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}); this is a computation, not
a proof. Going lower would need more of
the dropped terms evaluated, a sharper bound for the rough numbers than $\omega^*$, or a better density exponent.

On the last, one remark, which is a heuristic and not a theorem. The curve $g_7$ is set by Chen, Gupta and Li's
estimate on $[5/7, 1307/1680]$, and this paper uses no density estimate for all moduli that is better than theirs. A
rough model of the method, made outside the library, not checked in Lean and accurate to about $5 \cdot 10^{-4}$,
suggests that even a hypothetical saving in a term of their bound for prime moduli, as large as
square-root cancellation, would lower the exponent only to about $2.2168$. A larger improvement would then need new
ideas in the $q$-aspect. Nothing in the library depends on this.
