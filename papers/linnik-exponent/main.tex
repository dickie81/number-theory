\title{The least prime in an arithmetic progression: the exponent $89/40$ from a half-plane zero-free region, a zero-density estimate and a formalised sieve}
\author{Ritchie Comley}
\date{Draft, 25 October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove, in Lean~4, that there is a constant $K$ such that, for every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$, there is a
prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with $p \le K q^{89/40}$. The Lean theorem has no hypotheses and depends on no axioms beyond
Lean's standard three, so it is unconditional: its truth rests on Lean's kernel, Mathlib and the pinned toolchain (with
the vendored upstream ports and the provenance of the build cache), not on any preprint. Its proof uses two recent
results that have not been refereed, OpenAI's zero-free half-plane $\operatorname{Re} s > 11/12$ for every Dirichlet
$L$-function and Chen, Gupta and Li's zero-density estimate. The library proves both in Lean, as it does the classical
results the proof uses: the half-plane along the route of OpenAI's companion preprint, and for the density estimate it
formalises Chen, Gupta and Li's Proposition~3.1 and derives from it their exponent $4/(1 + \sigma)$ on $107$ narrow
steps; the rest of their paper is not formalised. From the two results the classical deduction gives every exponent
above $7/3$. The proof here is a sieve argument whose Type~II estimate uses them, and it goes below $7/3$. The exponent
$89/40$ is below the unconditional exponents of Heath-Brown and Xylouris; their proofs use refereed results only, and
this one uses the two unrefereed results, through their formalisations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is $K$ such that for every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$ there is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with
$p \le K q^{89/40}$.
\end{theorem}

Linnik \cite{linnik44} proved that there are absolute constants $K$ and $L$ such that every reduced residue class
mod $q$ contains a prime $p \le K q^L$. The admissible exponent $L$ has been lowered many times. Heath-Brown
\cite{heath-brown92} reached $L = 5.5$, Xylouris $L = 5.18$ \cite{xylouris-acta} and then $L = 5$ \cite{xylouris11},
and again $L = 5$ in a short version, which notes that a slightly smaller value follows \cite{xylouris18}.
Under the generalised Riemann hypothesis every $L > 2$ is admissible \cite{lls, lls-corr}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has
$L = 89/40 = 2.225$, below the exponents of Heath-Brown and Xylouris, but not on the same footing, since their proofs
use refereed results only.

\subsection*{Status}

The Lean statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has no hypotheses, so the theorem is unconditional. Its proof uses two
recent results that are not yet refereed, OpenAI's zero-free half-plane (Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}) and Chen, Gupta and Li's zero-density
estimate (Input~\ref{inp:density}), which Section~\ref{sec:inputs} states. They are called inputs because the argument
of the paper is written under them. The library formalises both in Lean, so the Lean theorem does not assume them: the
half-plane from OpenAI's companion preprint, and the density estimate from Chen, Gupta and Li's Proposition~3.1, with
the exponents of Input~\ref{inp:density} derived from it. The rest of their paper, in particular their Theorem~1.2, is
not formalised. The truth of the Lean theorem therefore rests on Lean's kernel, Mathlib and the pinned toolchain
(with the vendored upstream ports and the provenance of the build cache), not on the preprints. Whether the Lean
statements of the two inputs faithfully capture the preprints' theorems matters for attributing and interpreting them, not
for the truth of the Lean theorem, and the kernel does not check it. Section~\ref{sec:inputs} says what is known of independent checking, and Section~\ref{sec:lean} what the
library does and does not formalise.

\subsection*{The zeros near $\operatorname{Re} s = 1$}

The difficulty in Linnik's theorem is the zeros of the $L$-functions mod $q$ near the line $\operatorname{Re} s = 1$.
By the explicit formula, a zero $\rho = \beta + i\gamma$ of $L(s, \chi)$ contributes about $x^{\beta - 1}$, relative to
the main term, to the count of primes $p \le x$ in a class mod $q$. A zero-density estimate with exponent $A$, a
bound of about $(qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)}$ for the number of zeros with real part at least $\sigma$, makes the zeros of
real part about $\sigma$ contribute about $(q^A/x)^{1 - \sigma}$ together. Classically a zero may have
$1 - \beta$ as small as $c/\log q$, and at $x = q^L$ the contribution $(q^A/x)^{1 - \beta}$ is then a constant, not a
small quantity. The record proofs control these zeros with zero-free regions, density estimates without logarithmic
factors and the Deuring--Heilbronn phenomenon, and the constants of all three enter the exponent.

The zero-free half-plane of OpenAI's release \cite{openai}, Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, removes the zeros with
$\beta > 11/12$. Every remaining zero then contributes at most a power $x^{-1/12}$, and the problem becomes one of
density: a density exponent $A$ for $1/2 \le \sigma \le 11/12$ gives Linnik's theorem with every exponent $L > A$.
Huxley's density estimate \cite{huxley75} gives $A = 12/5$. Chen, Gupta and Li \cite{cgl}, whose method is
Guth and Maynard's \cite{guth-maynard}, prove $A = 7/3$; their Theorem~1.2, for a divisor $q_1$ of $q$, has at
$q_1 = q$ the exponent $4/(1 + \sigma)$, which is $7/3$ at $\sigma = 5/7$ and smaller beyond. With
Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} the classical deduction of Section~\ref{sec:comparison} turns the density exponent $7/3$
into Linnik's theorem with every exponent above $7/3$. Chen, Gupta and Li do not state a Linnik exponent, but their
Corollary~1.4, on primes in progressions in short intervals under a zero-free region, contains this one: applied to the
whole interval $(x, 2x]$, with Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} as the zero-free region, it gives every exponent above $7/3$.
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} gives the details.

\subsection*{Below the density exponent}

This paper goes below $7/3$ with a sieve. Harman's sieve has been used on Linnik's problem before, for special
moduli. R.~Li \cite{li-harman} adapts the calculation behind his theorem on primes in short intervals to primes in
progressions to moduli $q$ whose prime factors are below $q^C$ and for which the $L$-functions of the primitive
characters to the divisors of $q$ have no zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > 1 - (\log q)^{-3/4}$ and
$|\operatorname{Im} s| \le \exp(\varepsilon (\log q)^{3/4})$ (his Theorem~9), and deduces Linnik's theorem with the
exponent $1/0.4752 < 2.1044$ when $q$ is a power of a fixed prime (his Theorem~11), improving the exponent $2.1115$ of
Banks and Shparlinski \cite{banks-shparlinski} for those moduli. Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} of Section~\ref{sec:inputs}
implies the zero-free hypothesis of his Theorem~9 for every large $q$, so with it that theorem gives the exponent
$1/0.4752$ for every large modulus whose prime factors are all below $q^C$, not only for powers of a fixed prime.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} holds for every modulus. Harman's sieve \cite{harman83, harman} bounds from below the number of
primes in the set $\mathcal A$ of integers $n \in (x, 2x]$ with $n \equiv a \pmod q$ by comparing it with the set
$\mathcal B$ of the integers $n \in (x, 2x]$ prime to $q$, weighted by $1/\varphi(q)$. It needs two kinds of
information. Type~I information is the comparison of $\mathcal A$ with $\mathcal B$ on the multiples of each small
integer, which counting in residue classes gives. Type~II information is the comparison on bilinear sums
$\sum_{mn} a_m b_n$, with $m$ in a range $(x^{u_1}, x^{u_2})$, bounded coefficients $a_m$, and $b_n$ supported on
products of primes, which needs the zeros. A comparison over all such sums is not available at
$x = q^{89/40}$. What is available is a window $[u_1, u_2]$ of exponents on which it holds, and the sieve recovers
the primes from the sums it can compare,
losing a fraction of them that a finite computation bounds.

The paper's contributions are three.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A Type~II estimate over the zeros' real parts} (Section~\ref{sec:type2}). The characters mod $q$ are
  sorted into bins by the largest real part of their $L$-function's zeros near a given height. In each bin the
  bilinear sum is bounded by the zeros, which make the Dirichlet polynomial of the primes small, and by the number
  of characters in the bin, which a density estimate bounds; the best of orthogonality and the
  Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality is taken in each bin. Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density},
  with the classical density estimates of Ingham and Huxley, give the window
  $[u_1, u_2] = [41479/100000,\ 47555/100000]$ at $L = 89/40$.
\item \emph{A decomposition by Buchstab's identity, with a refinement} (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}). The
  identity is applied to depth six, the primes of the two deepest levels on a coarser grid. The terms in which the product of some of the prime variables has its
  exponent in the window, or in its reflection $[1 - u_2, 1 - u_1]$, are Type~II sums and are evaluated, and the
  others are dropped. The refinement keeps, in a dropped term of depth two, the part whose integers have exactly two
  prime factors in the cofactor and are, in some grouping of their four prime factors, a Type~II sum.
\item \emph{A certificate} (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}). The fraction of the primes the decomposition loses is
  bounded above by a sum over the cells of a grid of the exponents, computed exactly in integers. On a grid of
  $340$ cells a side (and $85$ for the deepest primes) the sum is below $0.99602$, which is less than $1$, so a positive proportion of the primes
  remains.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{The exponent}

The exponent $89/40$ uses Chen, Gupta and Li's Proposition~3.1 at every point of $[5/7, 1307/1680]$: on each of
$107$ steps of length $1/1680$ the exponent is just above $4/(1 + \sigma)$ at the step's left end. From $\sigma = 7/9$
on, Huxley's exponent $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ is below $4/(1 + \sigma)$, and on the three steps of that length that follow the
step containing $7/9$, up to $131/168$, the curve takes Huxley's: just above $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ at the step's left end. It also uses Ingham's
estimate on its last step, $[99/140, 5/7]$, with a slack of $1/100000$ in place of the $1/2000$ of the other steps, so
that its exponent is $7/3 + 1/100000$. The
steps are narrow because the ends of the window are set by the places where a step's exponent most exceeds the curve it
bounds: at the steps around $7/9$, where $4/(1 + \sigma)$ meets Huxley's $3/(3\sigma - 1)$, and at $\sigma = 5/7$,
where the exponent is largest. Using Huxley's exponent on those three steps, instead of Chen, Gupta and Li's, lowers
the certificate's total, and with the two deeper levels of the decomposition this is what lets the certificate close at $89/40$.

The rest is not new. Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density} are OpenAI's and Chen, Gupta and Li's,
and the density estimates of Ingham \cite{ingham} and Huxley \cite{huxley72, huxley75}, the Hal\'asz--Montgomery
inequality \cite{halasz, montgomery69} and Harman's sieve are classical. Section~\ref{sec:inputs} states the inputs
and Section~\ref{sec:type1} the form of the sieve that is used.

\subsection*{The formalisation}

Every step of the proof is proved in Lean~4 with the Mathlib library, and so is each result it uses from the
literature, except that for Chen, Gupta and Li's paper only its Proposition~3.1, with the results it rests on, and the steps derived from it are
formalised. The Lean theorem \lean{Linnik22250R.linnik\_22250r} is Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and its axioms are Lean's
standard three. The certificate's sum is checked by Lean's kernel in 4715 parts. Section~\ref{sec:lean} names the Lean
theorem behind each claim of the paper, and the directory \file{formalisations/} of the repository documents each
result from the literature: its source, its Lean statement and the differences between them. The library's proofs of
Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density} are the library's own formalisations. For the second, one ingredient
of the Lean proof, Heath-Brown's Theorem~10.1 with characters, which Chen, Gupta and Li cite, follows Kerr and Jutila
because the cited source was not read; some lemmas of their paper were formalised from an outline of their proofs only; and
the formalisation found six places where the text of their paper needs a small repair, none affecting its results.
\file{formalisations/chen-gupta-li-density.md} records all three.

The proofs of the paper follow the Lean proofs step by step. Where the Lean proof fixes a constant whose value the
argument does not need, the paper says only that the constant exists. Section~\ref{sec:lean} names the Lean theorem
behind each claim.

\section{Notation and the two inputs}\label{sec:inputs}

Throughout, $q \ge 1$ is the modulus and $a$ is prime to $q$. The Dirichlet characters mod $q$ are $\chi$, the trivial
one is $\chi_0$, and $L(s, \chi)$ is the $L$-function of $\chi$, continued to the whole plane (in Lean, Mathlib's
\lean{DirichletCharacter.LFunction}). For $1/2 \le \sigma \le 1$ and $T \ge 2$ let
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) = \#\{\chi \bmod q : L(s, \chi) = 0 \text{ for some } s \in \mathcal R(\sigma, T)\},
\]
where $\mathcal R(\sigma, T) = \{s : \operatorname{Re} s \ge \sigma,\ |\operatorname{Im} s| \le T\}$: the number of
characters mod $q$ whose $L$-function has a zero in the rectangle. It counts characters, not zeros, so a bound
for the number of zeros bounds it. The proof is a sieve on the integers up to $x = q^L$, with $L = 89/40$ in the
end, and $q$ is taken large.

The paper states two inputs, written \emph{Input} and not \emph{hypothesis} because the library proves them in Lean.
A statement below that holds `under' an input is conditional in the text of the paper. In Lean the corresponding
theorems either take the statement of the input as an explicit hypothesis (as \lean{SieveTypeIIHM.typeII\_gen\_hm} takes
a density curve's counts) or use the library's proof of the input directly; the final theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, has no hypotheses.

\begin{inputthm}[the zero-free half-plane]\label{inp:half-plane}
For every $q \ge 1$, every Dirichlet character $\chi$ mod $q$ and every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re} s > 11/12$,
$L(s, \chi) \ne 0$, except at $s = 1$ for the trivial character.
\end{inputthm}

This is OpenAI's result \cite{openai}: their companion preprint proves it, and their main preprint proves the
stronger $\operatorname{Re} s > 7/8$. These preprints have not been refereed. The theorem of this paper uses the result,
which is proved in Lean as \lean{Eis.LFunction\_ne\_zero\_of\_re\_gt}, following the companion preprint.

Independent checking of the result is partial, and little of it is independent of OpenAI. OpenAI's release has its own
Lean formalisation of the main preprint's result. Its catalogue \texttt{lean/formalization.yaml} \cite{openai-lean},
read on 25 October 2026, lists that preprint among the papers with a formalised main result, and names the theorem
\lean{OAI.DirichletCharacter.\allowbreak LFunction\_ne\_zero\_of\_seven\_eighths\_lt\_re}. Its statement is for every modulus
$q \ge 1$, every character mod $q$ and every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re} s > 7/8$, against Mathlib's $L$-function,
with the same exception at $s = 1$ for the trivial character. Since $7/8 < 11/12$, it implies Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}
as stated here. The same catalogue gives the scope as partial progress and the review status as unchecked, so it is
OpenAI's own record and not an independent one. The author read that release's statements, scanned its Lean and
rebuilt it on 7 October 2026, which showed its two main theorems to depend on Lean's standard three axioms only, but
did not read its proofs in full. This library's proof of Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} is a new formalisation of the
companion preprint's route, on a different Lean and Mathlib; it imports nothing from the release and was not compared
with it. It is a machine-checked proof of the companion preprint's theorem, but the route is OpenAI's and the
formalisation is the author's, so it is not a review of the mathematics that is independent of OpenAI and of the
author. Third-party reports of replaying OpenAI's Lean proof of the $7/8$ result exist (including, in at least one report, for
the Dirichlet statement); these check the proof, not the mathematics, and the author has not rerun them. The check
recorded by the tracker AIProofWatch \cite{aiproofwatch} on 25 October 2026 concerns the specialisation to the Riemann zeta
function at $7/8$, not Dirichlet $L$-functions in general. So the author knows of no review of the mathematics for
Dirichlet $L$-functions in general that is independent of OpenAI and of the author. The release's Lean was not confirmed to be
correct, beyond the statement above and the rebuild.
\file{formalisations/openai-half-plane.md} gives the attribution and the differences between the Lean statement and
the source's.

\begin{inputthm}[zero-density estimates from $5/7$ to $1307/1680$]\label{inp:density}
For $0 \le k < 107$ let $\sigma_k = 5/7 + k/1680$. There are exponents $A_k$ with
$4/(1 + \sigma_k) < A_k < 4/(1 + \sigma_k) + 1/10000$, listed in the header of \file{lib/pnt/LinnikCGLSteps.lean}
($A_0 = 2.3334$ and $A_{106} = 2.25057$), such that for each $k$ there is $C$ with the following property: for every
$q \ge 1$, every $\sigma_k \le \sigma \le \sigma_k + 1/1680$ and every $T \ge 2$, at most
$C (qT)^{A_k (1 - \sigma)} (1 + \log qT)^{10}$ characters mod $q$ have an $L$-function with a zero in
$\operatorname{Re} s \ge \sigma$, $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$.
\end{inputthm}

The exponent $A_k$ is that of Chen, Gupta and Li's Theorem~1.2 \cite{cgl} at $q_1 = q$, taken at the step's left end
$\sigma_k$, up to $10^{-4}$. Their estimate counts zeros, which gives the count of characters stated here. The
library does not prove their Theorem~1.2. It formalises their Proposition~3.1, the large-values estimate, and derives each
step from it, in \file{lib/pnt/LinnikCGLSteps.lean} (Section~\ref{sec:lean} names the theorems); other parts of their paper are not
formalised. \file{formalisations/chen-gupta-li-density.md} documents what is, and lists six small repairs that the
formalisation needed in the text of their paper, none affecting its results. Their paper \cite{cgl} is an arXiv
preprint and has not been refereed.

\subsection*{The density curve}

The Type~II estimate of Section~\ref{sec:type2} needs a bound for $N^*(\sigma, T; q)$ at every $\sigma$ in
$[1/2, 1]$. It takes it in the form of a curve.

\begin{definition}\label{def:curve}
A function $g : \mathbb R \to \mathbb R$ is a \emph{density curve} if there is $C \ge 0$ such that for every
$q \ge 1$, every $1/2 \le \sigma \le 1$ and every $T \ge 2$,
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le C\, (qT)^{g(\sigma)} \bigl(1 + \log qT\bigr)^{10}.
\]
\end{definition}

The curve of this paper is the function $g_7$ defined as follows (in Lean, \lean{SieveTypeIIU22250D.g7d}); each of
its pieces is a known density estimate, and the steps make each piece a constant multiple of $1 - \sigma$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $g_7(\sigma) = 1$ for $\sigma < 4/7$.
\item On $[4/7, 5/7]$, divided into twenty steps $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2)$ of length $1/140$,
  $g_7(\sigma) = \bigl(3/(2 - \sigma_2) + \delta\bigr)(1 - \sigma)$: Ingham's exponent at the step's right end plus a
  slack $\delta$, with $\delta = 1/2000$ except on the last step $[99/140, 5/7)$, where $\delta = 1/100000$ and the
  exponent is $7/3 + 1/100000$. (In Lean the slack is $4\varepsilon$ for the $\varepsilon$ of
  \lean{LinnikInghamC.charDensity\_ingham\_c}, whose exponent is $2 + 4c + 4\varepsilon$: $\varepsilon = 1/8000$ on
  the first nineteen steps and $\varepsilon = 1/400000$ on the last.)
\item On $[5/7, 1307/1680)$, divided into $107$ steps $[\sigma_k, \sigma_{k+1})$ of length $1/1680$,
  $g_7(\sigma) = A_k(1 - \sigma)$ with $A_k$ the exponent of Input~\ref{inp:density}.
\item On $[1307/1680, 131/168)$, divided into three steps $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2)$ of length $1/1680$,
  $g_7(\sigma) = A'(1 - \sigma)$ with $A' = 2.24915$, $2.24614$ and $2.24315$: Huxley's exponent $3/(3\sigma_1 - 1)$ at the
  step's left end plus at most $2 \cdot 10^{-4}$.
\item On $[131/168, 5/6]$, divided into fifteen steps $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2)$ of length $1/280$,
  $g_7(\sigma) = \bigl(3/(3\sigma_1 - 1) + 1/2000\bigr)(1 - \sigma)$: Huxley's exponent at the step's left end.
\item $g_7(\sigma) = (101/50)(1 - \sigma)$ for $\sigma \ge 5/6$.
\end{itemize}
The exponents are written in Lean as fractions; the first step's is $3/(2 - 81/140) + 1/2000 = 840199/398000$. On
$[4/7, 5/7]$ the exponent rises from about $2.111$ to $7/3 + 1/100000$. On $[5/7, 1307/1680]$ it falls from $2.3334$ to
$2.25057$, on $[1307/1680, 131/168]$ it falls on to $2.24315$, and on $[131/168, 5/6]$, where Huxley's steps take over at $2.2405$, it falls to about $2.015$. On each step $A(1 - \sigma) \le A(1 - \sigma_1) \le 1$, so $g_7 \le 1$ everywhere, and
$g_7(1/2) = 1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:curve}
Under Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density}, $g_7$ is a density curve.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A finite union of ranges on each of which the bound holds, with its own constant, is a range on which it holds with
the sum of the constants, so it suffices to prove the bound on each piece.
\begin{itemize}
\item For $\sigma < 4/7$: $N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le \varphi(q) \le qT$.
\item On each step of $[4/7, 5/7]$: Ingham's estimate \cite{ingham} in the form over the characters mod $q$, which
  bounds $N^*(\sigma, T; q)$ by $C(qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)}(\log qT)^{10}$ on $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2]$ for every $A$
  above $3/(2 - \sigma_2)$ (\file{formalisations/ingham-density.md}; for the last step, Section~\ref{sec:lean}). Ingham proved his estimate for the zeros of
  $\zeta$; the form over the characters is Theorem~12.1 of Montgomery's book \cite{montgomery-topics}.
\item On each step of $[5/7, 1307/1680]$: Input~\ref{inp:density}.
\item On each of the three steps of $[1307/1680, 131/168]$: Huxley's estimate \cite{huxley75} over the characters mod $q$,
  with the exponents $A'$ above, which exceed $3/(3\sigma_1 - 1)$ at the step's left end $\sigma_1$ by less than
  $2 \cdot 10^{-4}$ (\file{formalisations/huxley-density.md}; the library's step of that argument at new parameters, in
  \file{lib/pnt/LinnikHuxJunction.lean}).
\item On each step of $[131/168, 5/6]$: Huxley's estimate \cite{huxley75} over the characters mod $q$, with the
  exponent $3/(3\sigma_1 - 1) + 1/2000$ on $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2]$ (\file{formalisations/huxley-density.md}).
\item For $\sigma \ge 5/6$: the exponent $101/50$, by the same large-values argument at fixed parameters on
  $[5/6, 11/12]$, and by Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} above $11/12$, where at most the trivial character is
  counted (\file{formalisations/huxley-density.md}).
\end{itemize}
Each of these bounds is a theorem of the library, with a factor $(\log qT)^{10}$ at most $(1 + \log qT)^{10}$; the
claims table of Section~\ref{sec:lean} names them.
\end{proof}

The only piece that is not classical is the one on $[5/7, 1307/1680]$, where Chen, Gupta and Li's estimate, in the
step form of Input~\ref{inp:density}, replaces Huxley's exponent, which is $12/5$ at $\sigma = 3/4$. Their
exponent is $7/3$ at $\sigma = 5/7$ and falls below it inside the range; the curve $g_7$ follows it step by step,
to within $10^{-4}$ of $4/(1 + \sigma)$ at each step's left end. Huxley's exponent $3/(3\sigma - 1)$ is the smaller of
the two from $\sigma = 7/9$ on, and $7/9$ lies in the step $[653/840, 1307/1680]$, which is the last of the $107$; so
from $1307/1680$ on, on the three steps up to $131/168$ and on the steps from $131/168$ on, $g_7$ uses Huxley's. Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} enters the curve only
above $11/12$, and enters the Type~II estimate through the largest real part of the zeros, which it bounds by $11/12$.

\section{The Type II estimate}\label{sec:type2}

Fix a smooth real function $f_0$ supported in $[1/2, 2]$. For a character $\chi$, a scale $Q \ge 4$ and a
height $h \in \mathbb R$, the smoothed prime sum is
\[
  S_Q(\chi, h) = \sum_{p \le 2Q} \chi(p) \Bigl(\frac{p}{Q}\Bigr)^{-ih} f_0\Bigl(\frac{p}{Q}\Bigr),
\]
and for a finite set $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$ of integers, coefficients $a_m \in \mathbb C$ and a height $h_0$,
\[
  W(\chi) = \sum_{m \in S} a_m \chi(m) m^{-ih_0}.
\]
The sieve of Section~\ref{sec:type1} needs, for the integers with a factor $m$ in a range $(x^{u_1}, x^{u_2})$ and
the rest a product of primes, that the non-trivial characters contribute less than the main term. In the Dirichlet
polynomials above this is the following estimate. Write $\mathcal X^*$ for the non-trivial characters mod $q$.

\begin{theorem}[the Type~II estimate]\label{thm:type2}
Assume Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density}, and let $L = 89/40$, $u_1 = 41479/100000$ and
$u_2 = 47555/100000$. Let
$K_0 \ge 0$ be an integer, $e_1 > 0$ and $0 < \eta \le 10^{-5}$. There is $q_0$ such that for every $q \ge q_0$, every
$P$ with $q^{u_1 L} \le P \le q^{u_2 L}$, every $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$, all $a_m$ and $h_0$, every $K \le K_0$, and all
$Q_1, \dots, Q_K \ge q^{e_1}$ and $|h_1|, \dots, |h_K| \le q^\eta$ with $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} q^L/P$,
\[
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} |W(\chi)| \prod_{j=1}^K |S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)|
  \le q^{L - 1/50000} \Bigl(\frac1P \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

At $x = q^L$ the condition on $P$ is $x^{u_1} \le P \le x^{u_2}$. The bound saves the power $q^{-1/50000}$ over what
the sieve needs, which is the size $q^L (\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$ of the trivial character's term up to a constant.
The proof has three parts: the prime sums are bounded through the zeros of each character's $L$-function, the
characters are sorted by those zeros, and in each class the sum over the characters is bounded in two ways.

\subsection{Prime sums in a zero-free rectangle}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:prime-sum}
Assume Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. For every integer $K \ge 0$ there are $B, C \ge 0$ such that the following holds
for every $q \ge 1$, every
$\chi \in \mathcal X^*$, every $1/2 \le \theta \le 11/12$, $0 < \delta \le 1/24$, $H \ge 0$, $Q \ge 4$ and
$y \in \mathbb R$. If $L(s, \chi) \ne 0$ for $\operatorname{Re} s > \theta$, $|\operatorname{Im} s - y| < H + 1$,
then
\begin{multline*}
  |S_Q(\chi, y)| \le M_\delta(\theta)\, A\, B\,
  \frac{Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{11/12 + \delta}(1 + H)^{-K}}{\log Q} \\
  + C\bigl(\sqrt{2Q} + 1\bigr)\bigl(\log_2 (2Q) + 1\bigr),
\end{multline*}
where $A = \log(q + 2) + \log(|y| + 4) + 1$ and $M_\delta(\theta)$ depends only on $\theta$ and $\delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Throughout, $Q \ge 4$, so that $\log Q > 1$, and $L$ stands for $L(\cdot, \chi)$, which is entire since $\chi$ is not
trivial. Put $\sigma_1 = 11/12 + \delta$ and $E = (1 + H)^{-K}$.

\emph{The weight.} Let $\psi_Q(u) = f_0(u)(1 + \log u/\log Q)^{-1}$ for $u > 0$, and $w(u) = u^{-iy} \psi_Q(u)$. If
$f_0(n/Q) \ne 0$ then $n \ge Q/2 \ge 2$ and $1 + \log(n/Q)/\log Q = \log n/\log Q$, so that
$\Lambda(n) \psi_Q(n/Q) = \log Q \cdot (\Lambda(n)/\log n) f_0(n/Q)$. Since $\Lambda(p)/\log p = 1$,
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w(n/Q) = \log Q \, \bigl(S_Q(\chi, y) + R\bigr), \\
  R = \sum_{\substack{p^k \le 2Q \\ k \ge 2}} \frac{\chi(p^k)}{k} \Bigl(\frac{p^k}{Q}\Bigr)^{-iy} f_0\Bigl(\frac{p^k}{Q}\Bigr).
\end{gather*}
Each term of $R$ is at most $C = \max |f_0|$ in absolute value, and a proper prime power $p^k \le 2Q$ has
$p \le \sqrt{2Q}$ and $k \le \log_2(2Q)$, so $|R| \le C(\sqrt{2Q} + 1)(\log_2(2Q) + 1)$. It remains to prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sw-bound}
  \Bigl|\sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w(n/Q)\Bigr| \le M_\delta(\theta) \, A \, B \bigl(Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{\sigma_1} E\bigr).
\end{equation}

\emph{The Mellin transform.} Write $\mathcal M \psi(s) = \int_0^\infty u^{s - 1} \psi(u) \, du$. The substitution
$u = e^{-v}$ gives, with $a = 1/\log Q \in (0, 1/\log 4]$,
\[
  \mathcal M \psi_Q(\sigma + it) = \int_{\mathbb R} e^{-itv} g(v) \, dv, \qquad
  g(v) = e^{-\sigma v} f_0(e^{-v}) (1 - av)^{-1}.
\]
The function $g$ vanishes for $|v| > \log 2$, and $av \le 1/2$ for $v \le \log 2$, so $g$ is smooth. By Leibniz's rule,
with $|(d/dv)^m (1 - av)^{-1}| = m! \, a^m |1 - av|^{-1 - m} \le m! \, 2^{m + 1}$ on $|v| \le \log 2$, the $j$-th
derivative of $g$ is bounded by a constant $G_j$ that depends only on $f_0$ and $j$, uniformly in $0 \le \sigma \le 2$
and $Q \ge 4$. Integration by parts $j$ times gives $|t|^j |\mathcal M \psi_Q(\sigma + it)| \le 2 \log 2 \cdot G_j$, and
with $j = 0$ and $j = K + 3$,
\[
  (1 + |t|)^{K + 3} |\mathcal M \psi_Q(\sigma + it)| \le B \qquad (0 \le \sigma \le 2,\ t \in \mathbb R,\ Q \ge 4),
\]
where $B = 2^{K + 4} \log 2 \, (G_0 + G_{K + 3})$ depends only on $f_0$ and $K$. Since $\psi_Q$ is continuous on
$(0, \infty)$ and vanishes near $0$ and near $\infty$, $\mathcal M \psi_Q$ is entire. Finally
$\mathcal M w(s) = \mathcal M \psi_Q(s - iy)$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mellin-decay}
  (1 + |t - y|)^{K + 3} |\mathcal M w(\sigma + it)| \le B \qquad (0 \le \sigma \le 2,\ t \in \mathbb R).
\end{equation}

\emph{Perron's formula.} For $\operatorname{Re} s > 1$, $-L'/L(s) = \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) n^{-s}$. Mellin
inversion on the line $\operatorname{Re} s = 2$ at each point $n/Q$, with the exchange of sum and integral justified by
\eqref{eq:mellin-decay}, gives
\[
  \sum_n \chi(n) \Lambda(n) w(n/Q) = \frac1{2\pi} \int_{\mathbb R} F(2 + it) \, dt, \qquad
  F(s) = -\frac{L'}{L}(s) \, \mathcal M w(s) \, Q^s.
\]

\emph{The logarithmic derivative.} For $1/2 \le \theta_0 < \theta' < 3/2$ there is $\kappa(\theta_0, \theta')$ with the
following property, for every $q$, every non-trivial $\chi$ mod $q$ and every $t \in \mathbb R$: if $L$ has no zero in
the open disc $D_t$ of centre $3/2 + it$ and radius $\rho = 3/2 - \theta_0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logderiv}
  \Bigl|\frac{L'}{L}(\sigma + it)\Bigr| \le \kappa(\theta_0, \theta') \bigl(\log(q + 2) + \log(|t| + 4)\bigr)
  \qquad (\sigma \ge \theta').
\end{equation}
For $\sigma \ge 3/2$ this holds as soon as $\kappa \ge -\zeta'/\zeta(3/2)$, by the Dirichlet series of $L'/L$. For
$\theta' \le \sigma < 3/2$ it follows from a lemma of Landau's type: if $f$ is analytic near the closed unit disc,
$f(0) = 1$, $|f| \le B_0$ on $|z| \le R$, $f$ has no zero in $|z| < 1$ and $0 < r' < r < R' < R < 1$, then
\[
  |f'/f(z)| \le \Bigl(\frac{16 r^2}{(r - r')^3} + \frac1{(R^2/R' - R') \log(R/R')}\Bigr) \log B_0 \qquad (|z| \le r').
\]
(The library uses the form of this lemma in PrimeNumberTheoremAnd, \lean{FinalBound}, which combines a Blaschke
product, the Borel--Carath\'eodory inequality and Jensen's formula, and allows zeros.) Apply it to
$f(z) = L(3/2 + it + \rho z)/L(3/2 + it)$ at $z = (\sigma - 3/2)/\rho$, with $r' = (3/2 - \theta')/\rho$ and with $r$,
$R'$ and $R$ a quarter, a half and three quarters of the way from $r'$ to $1$; then $f'/f(z) = \rho \, L'/L(\sigma + it)$.
The bound $B_0$ comes from two estimates. First, $|L(s)| \le 3(q + 1)(|\operatorname{Im} s| + 2)$ for
$1/2 \le \operatorname{Re} s \le 3$, which follows by partial summation from $|L(s)| \le (q + 1)|s|/\operatorname{Re} s$
for $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$. Second, $|L(3/2 + it)|^{-1} \le \zeta(3/2)$, from $1/L(s) = \sum \mu(n) \chi(n) n^{-s}$.
Together they give $|f| \le 3(q + 1)(|t| + 4) \zeta(3/2)$ on $|z| \le R$, and $\log B_0 \ll \log(q + 2) + \log(|t| + 4)$.
The constant $\kappa(\theta_0, \theta')$ is explicit and of order $(\theta' - \theta_0)^{-3}$.

The disc $D_t$ lies in $\operatorname{Re} s > \theta_0$ and, since $\rho \le 1$, in $|\operatorname{Im} s - t| < 1$. So
\eqref{eq:logderiv} applies in two cases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] with $\theta_0 = 11/12$ and every $t$, by Input~\ref{inp:half-plane};
\item[(ii)] with $\theta_0 = \theta$ and every $t$ with $|t - y| \le H$, by the hypothesis of the lemma.
\end{enumerate}
Since $|t| + 4 \le (|y| + 4)(1 + |t - y|)$ and $\log(1 + u) \le u$, we have
$\log(q + 2) + \log(|t| + 4) \le A(1 + |t - y|)$. With \eqref{eq:mellin-decay} this gives, in either case,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-bound}
  |F(\sigma + it)| \le \kappa(\theta_0, \theta') \, A \, B \, Q^\sigma (1 + |t - y|)^{-K - 2} \qquad (\theta' \le \sigma \le 2).
\end{equation}

\emph{The contour.} By Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, $F$ is holomorphic on $\sigma_1 \le \operatorname{Re} s \le 2$, and by
\eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (i), with $\theta' = \sigma_1$, it is $O((1 + |t - y|)^{-2})$ there uniformly. So Cauchy's
theorem on the rectangles $[\sigma_1, 2] \times [-T, T]$, with $T \to \infty$, moves the line of integration from $2$ to
$\sigma_1$. Split the line $\operatorname{Re} s = \sigma_1$ at the heights $y \pm H$. By the hypothesis of the lemma, $F$
is holomorphic on the rectangle $\theta + \delta \le \operatorname{Re} s \le \sigma_1$, $|\operatorname{Im} s - y| \le H$,
so by Cauchy's theorem the integral over $|t - y| \le H$ on the line $\sigma_1$ equals the integral over the same
heights on the line $\theta + \delta$ plus the integrals over the two horizontal sides at the heights $y \pm H$.

\emph{The pieces.} Put $\kappa_1 = \kappa(11/12, \sigma_1)$ and $\kappa_2 = \kappa(\theta, \theta + \delta)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item On the line $\sigma_1$ with $|t - y| \ge H$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (i) and
  $(1 + u)^{-K - 2} \le (1 + H)^{-K} (1 + u^2)^{-1}$ for $u \ge H$, $|F| \le \kappa_1 A B Q^{\sigma_1} E/(1 + (t - y)^2)$.
  Each of the two tails contributes at most $\pi \kappa_1 A B Q^{\sigma_1} E$.
\item On the line $\theta + \delta$ with $|t - y| \le H$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (ii),
  $|F| \le \kappa_2 A B Q^{\theta + \delta}/(1 + (t - y)^2)$, so this part contributes at most
  $\pi \kappa_2 A B Q^{\theta + \delta}$.
\item On each horizontal side, of length $11/12 - \theta \le 1$: by \eqref{eq:F-bound} in case (ii), with
  $Q^\sigma \le Q^{\sigma_1}$, $|F| \le \kappa_2 A B Q^{\sigma_1} (1 + H)^{-K - 2} \le \kappa_2 A B Q^{\sigma_1} E$.
\end{itemize}
Adding the pieces and dividing by $2\pi$, the sum in \eqref{eq:Sw-bound} is at most
\[
  \kappa_1 A B Q^{\sigma_1} E + \tfrac12 \kappa_2 A B Q^{\theta + \delta} + \tfrac1\pi \kappa_2 A B Q^{\sigma_1} E
  \le (\kappa_1 + \kappa_2) \, A \, B \bigl(Q^{\theta + \delta} + Q^{\sigma_1} E\bigr),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:Sw-bound} with $M_\delta(\theta) = \kappa(\theta, \theta + \delta) + \kappa(11/12, 11/12 + \delta)$.
\end{proof}

The argument is the classical one for primes in progressions, with a smooth weight in place of a sharp cut-off; the
only use of Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} is to move the line of integration to $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta$ and
to bound $L'/L$ on that line and to its right.

\subsection{The bins}

Let $N = 500000$ and $\beta_i = 1/2 + i/(12N/5)$ for $0 \le i \le N$, so that $\beta_0 = 1/2$, $\beta_N = 11/12$ and
the bins have width $1/1200000$. For a height bound $T \ge 2$ the \emph{bin} of $\chi \in \mathcal X^*$ is the largest
$i < N$ such that $L(s, \chi)$ has a zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > \beta_i$ and $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$, and
$0$ if there is none. If $\chi$ is in the bin $i$ then:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L(s, \chi)$ has no zero with $\operatorname{Re} s > \beta_{i+1}$ and $|\operatorname{Im} s| \le T$: for
  $i + 1 < N$ by the choice of $i$, and for $i + 1 = N$ by Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. So
  Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum} applies with $\theta = \beta_{i+1}$ to every height $|y| \le T - H - 1$.
\item For $i \ge 1$, $\chi$ is counted by $N^*(\beta_i, T; q)$, so a density curve $g$ bounds the number of characters
  in the bin by $C(qT)^{g(\beta_i)}(1 + \log qT)^{10}$. For $i = 0$ the bound $\varphi(q) \le qT$ is the same with
  $g(1/2) = 1$.
\end{itemize}
The bins depend on $T$ but not on the heights $h_j$. The proof takes $H = q^\eta$ and $T = 2H + 1$, so that every
height $|h_j| \le q^\eta$ has $|h_j| + H + 1 \le T$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum} with $K$ large in terms of $\eta$,
so that $(1 + H)^{-K}$ cancels the term $Q^{11/12 + \delta}$. For large $q$ it gives
$|S_{Q_j}(\chi, h_j)| \le 2 Q_j^{\beta_{i+1} + 2\delta}$ in the bin $i$.

\subsection{Two bounds in a bin}

Let $R$ be the set of characters in a bin. Since the prime sums are bounded on $R$, it remains to bound
$\sum_{\chi \in R} |W(\chi)|$. There are two bounds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Orthogonality.} For $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$ and every $t$,
  \[
    \sum_\chi \Bigl|\sum_{m \in S} c_m \chi(m) m^{-it}\Bigr|^2 \le (P + q) \sum_{m \in S} |c_m|^2,
  \]
  summed over all characters mod $q$, since each residue class mod $q$ has at most $P/q + 1$ elements in
  $(P, 2P]$. With Cauchy--Schwarz over $R$,
  \[
    \sum_{\chi \in R} |W(\chi)| \le |R|^{1/2} \Bigl((P + q) \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
  \]
\item \emph{The Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality} \cite{halasz, montgomery69}, in the form over the characters mod
  $q$ at one height (\file{formalisations/halasz-montgomery.md}):
  \[
    \Bigl(\sum_{\chi \in R} |W(\chi)|\Bigr)^2 \le e \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2 \cdot \bigl(2P |R| + |R|^2 B\bigr),
  \]
  where $B$ bounds the off-diagonal kernels $K_{2P}(\chi\chi'^{-1}, 0)$, $\chi \ne \chi'$ in $R$. These are smoothed
  sums of a non-trivial character over $n \ll P$, and $|K_{2P}(\psi, 0)| \le C(2P)^{1/12 - \delta'}
  (2q)^{5/12 + \delta' + 2\varepsilon'}$: the kernel's integral moves to $\operatorname{Re} w = 1/12 - \delta'$, where
  the functional equation and the bound on $\operatorname{Re} s = 11/12 + \delta$ that
  Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} gives bound the $L$-function.
\end{enumerate}
The first bound is the better one when the bin is large, the second when it is small, which is the case near the top
of the curve. The proof takes the better of the two in each bin.

\subsection{The window condition}

Write $P = q^m$ and $|R| \le q^{g(\beta) + o(1)}$. The product of the prime sums is at most
$q^{(L - m)(\beta + w) + o(1)}$, where $\beta$ is the bin's lower end and $w \le 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$ accounts for the
width of the bins and for the $2\delta$ above. Measured against $q^L (\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$, the two bounds give the
exponents
\[
  E_1 = (L - m)(\beta + w) + \frac{g(\beta)}2 + \frac m2 + \frac{\max(1, m)}2
\]
by orthogonality, and by the Hal\'asz--Montgomery inequality the larger of
\begin{align*}
  E_2 &= (L - m)(\beta + w) + \frac{g(\beta)}2 + m, \\
  E_3 &= (L - m)(\beta + w) + \frac{g(\beta)}2 + \frac m2 + \frac{g(\beta) + b(m)}2,
\end{align*}
with $b(m) = 5/12 + m/12 + \delta'(1 - m) + 2\varepsilon'$ the exponent of the kernel bound.

\begin{definition}\label{def:window}
A window $[m_1, m_2]$ is \emph{admissible} for a curve $g$ at $L$ if for every $1/2 \le \beta \le 11/12$, every
$m_1 \le m \le m_2$ and every $0 \le w \le 4 \cdot 10^{-6}$, either $E_1 \le L - 1/25000$, or $m \le 1$ and
$\max(E_2, E_3) \le L - 1/25000$, with $\delta' = \varepsilon' = 1/1000$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:type2-gen}
Assume Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}. Let $g$ be a density curve with $g \le 1$ and $g(1/2) = 1$, and let $[m_1, m_2]$ be
admissible for $g$ at $L$. Then the
conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} holds with $q^{m_1} \le P \le q^{m_2}$ in place of
$q^{u_1 L} \le P \le q^{u_2 L}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sum the better of the two bounds over the bins. Each bin contributes at most
$q^{L - 1/25000 + \eta}(\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$ times constants and a power of $\log qT$, the factor $q^\eta$
coming from $T \le 3q^\eta$ in the count of the bin. There are $N$ bins, so for large $q$ the total is at most
$q^{L - 1/25000 + \eta + 1/100000}(\sum |a_m|^2/P)^{1/2}$. Since $\eta \le 10^{-5}$,
$L - 1/25000 + \eta + 1/100000 \le L - 1/50000$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:window}
The window $[u_1 L, u_2 L]$, with $u_1 = 41479/100000$ and $u_2 = 47555/100000$, is admissible for $g_7$ at
$L = 89/40$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On each piece of $g_7$, $g_7(\beta) = A(1 - \beta)$ with $A$ constant, or $g_7 = 1$. There $E_1$, $E_2$ and $E_3$ are
polynomials of degree at most one in each of $\beta$, $m$ and $w$ separately, so on a box
$[\beta_{\mathrm{lo}}, \beta_{\mathrm{hi}}] \times [m_{\mathrm{lo}}, m_{\mathrm{hi}}] \times [0, 4 \cdot 10^{-6}]$ each
attains its maximum at a corner. Split the window at $m = 1$. On every piece below $5/6$ the inequality
$E_1 \le L - 1/25000$ holds at the corners. On the top piece $[5/6, 11/12]$ it holds at the corners with $m \ge 1$,
and $\max(E_2, E_3) \le L - 1/25000$ holds at the corners with $m \le 1$. Each corner is an inequality between
rational numbers, checked exactly.
\end{proof}

The window has little room. At each end the tightest of the corner inequalities is on the first step of
$[5/7, 1307/1680)$, whose exponent is $2.3334$, at $\beta = 1201/1680$: it holds with a slack of about
$1.16 \cdot 10^{-5}$ below $L - 1/25000$ at $m = u_1 L$, and of about $1.27 \cdot 10^{-5}$ at $m = u_2 L$. All of them
are exact rational inequalities, so there is no numerical tolerance, but a perturbation of the curve or of $L$ of that
size would move an end of the window out.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:curve}, $g_7$ is a density curve under the two inputs, with $g_7 \le 1$ and $g_7(1/2) = 1$.
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:type2-gen}, under Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, with the window of
Proposition~\ref{prop:window}.
\end{proof}

The Hal\'asz--Montgomery branch is used only on the top piece of the curve, at the corners with $m \le 1$, where the
bins are small.

\section{Type I estimates and Harman's sieve}\label{sec:type1}

Let $x = q^L$ and let $a$ be prime to $q$. The sets of the sieve are
\[
  \mathcal A = \{n \in (x, 2x] : n \equiv a \ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\}, \qquad
  \mathcal B = \{n \in (x, 2x] : (n, q) = 1\}.
\]
For a set $\mathcal C$ of integers, an integer $r$ and $z \ge 1$, $\mathcal C_r$ is the set of the multiples of $r$
in $\mathcal C$, and $S(\mathcal C_r, z)$ the number of $n \in \mathcal C_r$ with no prime factor of $n/r$ below $z$.
So $S(\mathcal A, (2x)^{1/2})$ is the number of primes in $\mathcal A$. The sieve compares $\mathcal A$ with
$\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ through the weight
\[
  \mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \frac{\mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]}{\varphi(q)}
  = \frac1{\varphi(q)} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \overline\chi(a) \chi(n),
\]
which is orthogonality: the trivial character cancels, and the other characters carry the difference. This section
states the three tools of Harman's sieve \cite{harman83, harman} in the form the proof uses. None of them is new;
\file{formalisations/harman-sieve.md} gives their sources and the differences between the Lean statements and the
classical ones.

\subsection{Type~I}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:type1}
Let $N_1 \le N_2$ be integers and let $M > 0$ be prime to $q$. The number of $n \in (N_1, N_2]$ with $n \equiv a \pmod q$ and
$M \mid n$ is $(N_2 - N_1)/(Mq)$ within $1$, and the number of $n \in (N_1, N_2]$ prime to $q$ with $M \mid n$ is
$\varphi(q)(N_2 - N_1)/(Mq)$ within $\varphi(q)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $(M, q) = 1$, the Chinese remainder theorem gives $c$ with $c \equiv 0 \pmod M$ and $c \equiv a \pmod q$, and
the two conditions $n \equiv a \pmod q$ and $M \mid n$ together say $n \equiv c \pmod{Mq}$. For $r \ge 1$ the number of
$n \in (N_1, N_2]$ with $n \equiv c \pmod r$ is $\lfloor (N_2 - c)/r \rfloor - \lfloor (N_1 - c)/r \rfloor$, which
differs from $(N_2 - N_1)/r$ by less than $1$. With $r = Mq$ this is the first count. The integers prime to $q$ form the
$\varphi(q)$ residue classes $b \bmod q$ with $(b, q) = 1$, and adding the first count over these classes gives the
second.
\end{proof}

So $|\mathcal A_M| - |\mathcal B_M|/\varphi(q)$ is at most $2$ in absolute value for every $M$ prime to $q$. This is
all the Type~I information the sieve needs: it is used for the moduli up to $q^{b_0}$ with $b_0 < L - 1$, where the
error $2$ is small against the size $x/(Mq)$ of the count.

\subsection{Type~II}

Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} bounds Dirichlet polynomials. The sieve needs sums over $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ of
integers $n = m p_1 \cdots p_K$ whose primes are cut out by comparisons. Fix a smooth function $f_0 \ge 0$ supported
in $[1/2, 2]$, an integer $K_0$ and $e_1 > 0$. A \emph{family} consists of
\begin{itemize}
\item a scale $P$ with $x^{u_1} \le P \le x^{u_2}$, a set $S \subseteq (P, 2P]$ and coefficients $|a_m| \le A_0$;
\item between $1$ and $K_0$ primes $p_i \le 2Q_i$ with weights $f_0(p_i/Q_i)$, at scales $Q_i \ge q^{e_1}$ with
  $Q_1 \cdots Q_K \le 2^{K_0} x/P$;
\item at most $J_0$ comparisons, conditions on $(m, p)$ that hold when $\ell_j(m, p) > 0$ and fail when
  $\ell_j(m, p) < 0$, where $\ell_j(m, p) = \varphi_j(m) + \sum_i e_{ji} \log p_i$ with $|e_{ji}| \le E_0$ and
  $\ell_j \le \Lambda_0 \log q$ for every $m \in S$ and all primes $p_i \le 2Q_i$, each comparison with a
  \emph{pivot}, a prime $p_i$ with $e_{ji} = \pm 1$.
\end{itemize}
Its sum over $\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ is the sum of $a_m \prod_i f_0(p_i/Q_i)$ times the weight above at
$n = m p_1 \cdots p_K$, over the $(m, p)$ that satisfy the comparisons. The integers $n$ need not lie in $(x, 2x]$; where
they must, two comparisons say so.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:family}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. Given $f_0$, $K_0$, $e_1$, $J_0$, $E_0$, $\Lambda_0$ and $A_0$, there
are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that for every $q \ge q_0$ and every family, its sum over
$\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ is at most $A_0 q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ in absolute value.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let the family have the scale $P$, the set $S$, the coefficients $a_m$, the primes $p_1, \dots, p_K$ at the scales
$Q_i$, and the comparisons $C_1, \dots, C_J$ with forms $\ell_j$ and pivots $\pi(j)$. Let $\mathcal T$ be the set of the
$t = (m, p_1, \dots, p_K)$ with $m \in S$ and each $p_i$ a prime at most $2Q_i$; for $t \in \mathcal T$ put
$w(t) = a_m \prod_i f_0(p_i/Q_i)$ and $n(t) = m p_1 \cdots p_K$, and for a function $v$ on $\mathcal T$ put
\[
  D(v) = \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} v(t) \, \Delta(n(t)), \qquad
  \Delta(n) = \mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \frac{\mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
The sum of the lemma is $D(w \prod_j I_j)$, with $I_j(t) = \mathbf 1[C_j \text{ holds at } t]$. Let $F_0 = \max f_0$ and
$\eta_0 = 10^{-5}$.

\emph{Orthogonality.} For $a$ prime to $q$,
$\Delta(n) = \varphi(q)^{-1} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \overline\chi(a) \chi(n)$, so for every $v$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orth}
  |D(v)| \le \frac1{\varphi(q)} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \Bigl|\sum_{t \in \mathcal T} v(t) \chi(n(t))\Bigr|.
\end{equation}

\emph{A smooth family.} Let $g_1, \dots, g_J$ be continuous and integrable, with integrable Fourier transforms
$\hat g_j(\xi) = \int g_j(u) e^{-2\pi i u \xi} \, du$, and let $T_0 \ge 1$ with
$2\pi \sum_j |e_{ji}| \, T_0 \le q^{\eta_0}$ for every $i$. We show that for $q$ larger than the $q_0$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} (with $f_0$, $K_0$, $e_1$ and $\eta_0$),
\begin{multline}\label{eq:smooth-family}
  \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal X^*} \Bigl|\sum_{t \in \mathcal T} w(t) \prod_j g_j(\ell_j(t)) \, \chi(n(t))\Bigr|
  \le q^{L - 1/50000} N \prod_j \|\hat g_j\|_1 \\
  + \varphi(q) \, \mathfrak B \sum_j \int_{|\xi| > T_0} |\hat g_j| \prod_{l \ne j} \|\hat g_l\|_1,
\end{multline}
where
\[
  N = \Bigl(\frac1P \sum_{m \in S} |a_m|^2\Bigr)^{1/2} \le A_0, \qquad
  \mathfrak B = \sum_m |a_m| \prod_i \sum_{p \le 2Q_i} f_0(p/Q_i).
\]
By Fourier inversion, and since the phase factors,
\begin{gather*}
  \prod_j g_j(\ell_j) = \int_{\mathbb R^J} \prod_j \hat g_j(\xi_j) \, e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \ell_j} \, d\xi, \\
  e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \ell_j(t)} = e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \varphi_j(m)} \prod_i p_i^{2\pi i H_i}, \qquad
  H_i = \sum_j \xi_j e_{ji}.
\end{gather*}
So the inner sum in \eqref{eq:smooth-family} is
$\int \prod_j \hat g_j(\xi_j) \, W_\xi(\chi) \prod_i Q_i^{2\pi i H_i} S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i) \, d\xi$, where
$W_\xi(\chi) = \sum_m a_m e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \varphi_j(m)} \chi(m)$ and $h_i = -2\pi H_i$. Take absolute values,
sum over $\chi$, and bound the integrand for each $\xi$. If $|\xi_j| \le T_0$ for every $j$, then $|h_i| \le q^{\eta_0}$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, with the coefficients $a_m e^{2\pi i \sum_j \xi_j \varphi_j(m)}$, which have the moduli
$|a_m|$, and the heights $h_i$, gives
$\sum_\chi |W_\xi(\chi)| \prod_i |S_{Q_i}(\chi, h_i)| \le q^{L - 1/50000} N$. Otherwise some $|\xi_j| > T_0$, and the
trivial bound $\varphi(q) \mathfrak B$ holds. Integrating gives \eqref{eq:smooth-family}.

\emph{The cut-off.} Let $g$ be smooth, with $g = 0$ on $(-\infty, 0]$, $g = 1$ on $[1, \infty)$ and $0 \le g \le 1$, and
let $c_k = \int |g^{(k)}|$. For $0 < \theta \le 2\Lambda$ put $G_{\theta, \Lambda}(u) = g(u/\theta) - g((u - 2\Lambda)/\theta)$.
It is smooth, $0 \le G_{\theta, \Lambda} \le 1$, it vanishes outside $[0, 2\Lambda + \theta]$, and
$G_{\theta, \Lambda}(u) = g(u/\theta)$ for $u \le 2\Lambda$. So if $u \le \Lambda$ and a condition holds when $u > 0$ and
fails when $u < 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
  \bigl|\mathbf 1[\text{condition}] - G_{\theta, \Lambda}(u)\bigr| \le G_{\theta, \theta}(u + \theta),
\end{equation}
since the left side vanishes unless $0 \le u < \theta$, where $G_{\theta, \theta}(u + \theta) = 1$. Integration by parts
gives $(2\pi |\xi|)^k |\hat G_{\theta, \Lambda}(\xi)| \le \|G_{\theta, \Lambda}^{(k)}\|_1 \le 2\theta^{1 - k} c_k$, and
$|\hat G_{\theta, \Lambda}| \le 2\Lambda + \theta$. Hence
\begin{gather*}
  \|\hat G_{\theta, \Lambda}\|_1 \le \pi(2\Lambda + \theta) + \frac{c_2}{2\pi\theta}, \\
  \int_{|\xi| > T_0} |\hat G_{\theta, \Lambda}| \le \frac{4\pi c_k}{(2\pi)^k} \, \theta^{1 - k} T_0^{2 - k}
  \qquad (k \ge 2,\ T_0 \ge 1).
\end{gather*}

\emph{Sharp against smooth.} Let $\Lambda = \Lambda_0 \log q + 1$, so that $\ell_j \le \Lambda$ on $\mathcal T$, and put
$G_j = G_{\theta, \Lambda}(\ell_j)$ and $M_j = G_{\theta, \theta}(\ell_j + \theta)$. All these values lie in $[0, 1]$, so
by \eqref{eq:cutoff} $|\prod_j I_j - \prod_j G_j| \le \sum_j |I_j - G_j| \le \sum_j M_j$. Since
$|\Delta(n)| \le \Delta(n) + 2 \cdot \mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]/\varphi(q)$ and $f_0 \ge 0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-smooth}
  \Bigl|D\Bigl(w \prod_j I_j\Bigr)\Bigr| \le \Bigl|D\Bigl(w \prod_j G_j\Bigr)\Bigr|
  + \sum_j \Bigl(|D(|w| M_j)| + \frac2{\varphi(q)} \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} |w(t)| M_j(t)\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The first term and each $D(|w| M_j)$ are smooth families: the first with $g_j = G_{\theta, \Lambda}$, the second with
the coefficients $|a_m|$ and the single function $G_{\theta, \theta}$ at the form $\ell_j + \theta$. Both are bounded by
\eqref{eq:orth} and \eqref{eq:smooth-family}. The trivial count in the near-boundary region is not enough for them,
since it does not see the condition $n \equiv a$. The last sum is counted trivially, by the pivot. Fix $m$ and the primes
other than $p_{\pi(j)}$; then $\ell_j = c + \sigma \log p_{\pi(j)}$ with $\sigma = \pm 1$, and $M_j \ne 0$ forces
$-\theta \le \ell_j \le 2\theta$, which confines $p_{\pi(j)}$ to an interval $[Y, e^{3\theta} Y]$. At most
$(e^{3\theta} - 1) 2Q_{\pi(j)} + 1$ integers up to $2Q_{\pi(j)}$ lie in it, so
\[
  \sum_{t \in \mathcal T} |w(t)| M_j(t)
  \le F_0 \bigl((e^{3\theta} - 1) 2Q_{\pi(j)} + 1\bigr) \sum_m |a_m| \prod_{i \ne \pi(j)} \sum_{p \le 2Q_i} f_0(p/Q_i).
\]

\emph{The parameters.} Put $\varepsilon_2 = 1/(400000(J_0 + 1))$, $\theta = q^{-\varepsilon_2}$, $T_0 = q^{1/200000}$,
$k = 1200003$ and $\delta = \min(\varepsilon_2, e_1)/2$. For large $q$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $2\pi J_0 E_0 T_0 \le q^{\eta_0}$, so \eqref{eq:smooth-family} applies, as $\sum_j |e_{ji}| \le J_0 E_0$;
\item $\pi(2\Lambda + 1) \le q^{\varepsilon_2}$, so $\|\hat G_{\theta, \Lambda}\|_1$ and $\|\hat G_{\theta, \theta}\|_1$
  are at most $C q^{\varepsilon_2}$ with $C = 1 + c_2/(2\pi)$, and a product of at most $J_0$ of them is at most
  $C^{J_0 + 1} q^{1/400000}$;
\item $\theta^{1 - k} T_0^{2 - k} = q^{\varepsilon_2(k - 1) - (k - 2)/200000} \le q^{-3}$, which bounds the tails.
\end{itemize}
Moreover $\mathfrak B \le P A_0 (3F_0)^K \prod_i Q_i \le A_0 (3F_0 + 1)^{K_0} 2^{K_0} q^L$, since at most $3Q$ integers
lie in $[1, 2Q]$ for $Q \ge 1$ and $P \prod_i Q_i \le 2^{K_0} x$. Finally $e^{3\theta} - 1 \le 6\theta$ for
$\theta \le 1/3$ and $Q_{\pi(j)} \ge q^{e_1}$ give
$(12\theta Q_{\pi(j)} + 1) \prod_{i \ne \pi(j)} Q_i \le (12\theta + q^{-e_1}) \prod_i Q_i$. Putting these into
\eqref{eq:sharp-smooth}, with \eqref{eq:orth} and \eqref{eq:smooth-family}, and using $\varphi(q) \le q$, the sum of the
lemma is at most
\[
  \frac{A_0 q^L}{\varphi(q)} \bigl(a_1 q^{-7/400000} + a_2 q^{-1} + a_3(12 q^{-\varepsilon_2} + q^{-e_1})\bigr),
\]
where $a_1$, $a_2$ and $a_3$ depend only on $K_0$, $J_0$ and $F_0$. The three terms come from the smooth families, from
the tails and from the pivot counts. Each power of $q$ in the bracket is at most $q^{-2\delta}$, so for large $q$ the sum
is at most $A_0 q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$. Here $\delta$ depends only on $J_0$ and $e_1$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}
was used once for the smoothed family and once for each near-boundary family.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:family-sharp}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. Given $K_0$, $J_0$, $e_1 > 0$, $E_0 \ge 0$ and $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$, there
are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such that the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let $m$ run
over a set $S \subseteq (P_1, P_2]$ of integers with $q^{u_1 L} \le P_1$ and $P_2 \le q^{u_2 L}$, with coefficients
$|a_m| \le 1$, and let $1 \le K \le K_0$ primes $p_1, \dots, p_K$ run over all the primes up to $2x$. Let $J \le J_0$
comparisons, with forms $\ell_j = \varphi_j(m) + \sum_i e_{ji} \log p_i$, $|e_{ji}| \le E_0$,
$\varphi_j(m) \le \Lambda_1 \log q$ for $m \in S$, and pivots, cut out the $(m, p)$, and suppose that wherever they all
hold, every $p_i \ge 2q^{e_1}$ and $m p_1 \cdots p_K \le 2x$. Then the sum of
$a_m(\mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]/\varphi(q))$ at $n = m p_1 \cdots p_K$ over the $(m, p)$ that satisfy
the comparisons is at most $q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ in absolute value.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Split $(P_1, P_2]$ into the dyadic blocks $(2^b P_1, 2^{b + 1} P_1]$, and each prime variable by a smooth partition of
unity: take for the $f_0$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:family} a smooth function supported in $[1/2, 2]$, with values in $[0, 1]$
and $\sum_{t \ge 0} f_0(y/2^t) = 1$ for $1 \le y \le 2^M$, $M = \lfloor \log_2 (2x) \rfloor + 1$. This writes the sum as a sum of at most
$(3 + 4 \log q)^{K_0 + 1}$ pieces. In each, $m$ runs over a block, which lies in $(P, 2P]$ with $P$ in the window's
range, and the primes carry the weights $f_0(p_i/2^{t_i})$ at the scales $Q_i = 2^{t_i}$; on its box every form is at
most $(\Lambda_1 + K_0 E_0 (L + 3)) \log q$. A piece with some $2^{t_i} < q^{e_1}$ vanishes, since its weights force
$p_i < 2q^{e_1}$; and so does a piece with $\prod_i 2^{t_i} > 2^{K_0 + 1} x/P$, since its weights force
$m \prod_i p_i > 2x$. Every other piece is a family, with $K_0 + 1$ in place of $K_0$, $A_0 = 1$ and
$\Lambda_0 = \Lambda_1 + K_0 E_0 (L + 3)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:family} bounds it by $q^{L - \delta'}/\varphi(q)$. For
large $q$ the number of pieces is at most $q^{\delta'/2}$, and $\delta = \delta'/2$ will do.
\end{proof}

If instead a product of some of the primes of $n$ lies in $(x^{1 - u_2}, x^{1 - u_1})$, the product of the other
factors of $n$ lies in the window's range and plays the role of $m$. So Lemmas~\ref{lem:family}
and~\ref{lem:family-sharp} also cover the reflected range, and this is how the sieve uses the window and its
reflection.

\subsection{Harman's comparison}

Write $u_1, u_2$ for the window's ends as fractions of $L$, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, so that the window in
terms of $q$ is $[u_1 L, u_2 L]$.

\begin{proposition}[Harman's comparison, in the form used]\label{prop:harman}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. Fix $j$, $J_0$, $\Lambda_1 \ge 0$, $\eta > 0$, and exponents
$a_0 > 0$ and $b_0$ with $a_0 + b_0 < (1 - u_1)L$ and $(1 - u_2)L < b_0 < L - 1$. There are $\delta > 0$ and $q_0$ such
that the following holds for every $q \ge q_0$ and every $a$ prime to $q$. Let $(r_1, \dots, r_j)$ run over the
$j$-tuples of primes up to $2x$, counted with multiplicity, that satisfy at most $J_0$ comparisons, with coefficients
at most $1$ in absolute value, pivots and constants at most $\Lambda_1 \log q$, which force every
$r_i \ge z_0 = \lceil q^{a_0} \rceil$ and $r = r_1 \cdots r_j \le q^{b_0}$. Then
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_r \Bigl(S(\mathcal A_r, z_0) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_r, z_0)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr)\Bigr|
  \le \frac{\eta\, x}{\varphi(q) \log q} + \frac{q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\mathcal P[y, z)$ for the set of primes in $[y, z)$, and say that $n$ is \emph{$z$-rough} if it has no prime
factor below $z$. For $\mathcal C = \mathcal A$ or $\mathcal B$ and $d$ whose prime factors are all at least $z$,
$S(\mathcal C_d, z)$ is the number of $z$-rough multiples of $d$ in $\mathcal C$. Throughout, $x = q^L$, and the
integers of $(x, 2x]$ are those of $(N_1, N_2]$ with $N_1 = \lfloor x \rfloor$ and $N_2 = \lfloor 2x \rfloor$. A tuple
$(r_1, \dots, r_j)$ is counted with multiplicity, and $r = r_1 \cdots r_j$.

\emph{The constants.} They are chosen before $q$ and depend only on $L$, $u_1$, $u_2$, $j$, $J_0$, $\Lambda_1$, $a_0$,
$b_0$ and $\eta$. Let $c = (L - 1 - b_0)/4 > 0$. Let $C_0 \ge 1$ be an absolute constant with
$\sum_{p \in \mathcal P[y, y^2)} 1/p \le C_0$ for every $y \ge 1$; it exists by the prime number theorem. Put
$K_1 = (\log((L + 1)/a_0) + 1)^j$ and
\[
  \zeta_k = e^{2C_0} \cdot \frac{2 C_0^k}{k!} \cdot \exp\Bigl(e^{2C_0} \frac{2 C_0^{k + 1}}{(k + 1)!}\Bigr).
\]
Since $(2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k \to 0$, there is an even $k \ge 2$ with $2C_0^2 \le (k + 1)(k + 2)$, $c < (2k + 4) a_0$,
$\zeta_k \le 1/2$ and $(2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k \le \eta c^2/(8e(K_1 + 1) a_0)$. Put $\varepsilon_w = c/(2k + 4)$, so
that $0 < \varepsilon_w < a_0$ and $(2k + 7) \varepsilon_w \le 2c$, and $S_0 = \lceil L/\varepsilon_w \rceil$. At $q$,
put $w = \lceil q^{\varepsilon_w} \rceil$, $z_0 = \lceil q^{a_0} \rceil$ and $X' = \lfloor q^{b_0} \rfloor$. Then
$w \le z_0$, and by hypothesis every tuple in the sum has $r_i \ge z_0$ and $r \le X'$.

\emph{The tree identity.} Let $r$ have all its prime factors at least $z_0$, and let $r \le X'$. For a set $P$ of primes
write $d_P = r \prod_{p \in P} p$, and let $\rho_z(n) = 1$ if $n$ is $z$-rough and $\rho_z(n) = 0$ otherwise. Then for
every $n$,
\begin{multline}\label{eq:tree}
  \mathbf 1[r \mid n] \, \rho_{z_0}(n)
  = \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P[w, z_0)} (-1)^{|P|} \, \mathbf 1[d_P \le X'] \, \mathbf 1[d_P \mid n] \, \rho_w(n) \\
  + \sum_{p \in \mathcal P[w, z_0)} \ \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P(p, z_0)} (-1)^{|P| + 1} \,
    \mathbf 1[d_P \le X' < p \, d_P] \, \mathbf 1[p \, d_P \mid n] \, \rho_p(n),
\end{multline}
where $\mathcal P(p, z_0)$ is the set of primes in $(p, z_0)$. This is what iterating Buchstab's identity
$S(\mathcal C_m, z) = S(\mathcal C_m, w) - \sum_{w \le p < z} S(\mathcal C_{mp}, p)$ from the root $r$ at $z_0$ gives,
when a child $mp$ is stopped as soon as $mp > X'$: the first sum collects the \emph{leaves} $S(\mathcal C_d, w)$,
$d = d_P \le X'$, and the second the \emph{stops} $S(\mathcal C_W, p)$, $W = p \, d_P$. It is proved directly,
for each $n$. If $r \nmid n$ or $n$ is not $w$-rough, every term vanishes. Otherwise let $Q$ be the set of the primes of
$[w, z_0)$ that divide $n$; the left side is $\mathbf 1[Q = \emptyset]$. Since the primes of $r$ are at least $z_0$, a
leaf $P$ counts $n$ exactly when $P \subseteq Q$, and a stop $(p, P)$ exactly when $p = \min Q$ and
$P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p\}$. If $Q = \emptyset$, only the leaf $P = \emptyset$ counts $n$, with
$\mathbf 1[r \le X'] = 1$. If $Q \ne \emptyset$, let $p_0 = \min Q$; grouping $P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p_0\}$ with
$P \cup \{p_0\}$, and writing $R = d_P$, the right side is
\[
  \sum_{P \subseteq Q \setminus \{p_0\}} (-1)^{|P|}
  \bigl(\mathbf 1[R \le X'] - \mathbf 1[p_0 R \le X'] - \mathbf 1[R \le X' < p_0 R]\bigr) = 0.
\]
Multiplying \eqref{eq:tree} by $\mathbf 1[n \equiv a] - \mathbf 1[(n, q) = 1]/\varphi(q)$ and summing over $n$ and over
the tuples, the sum of the proposition is the sum over the leaves of
$(-1)^{|P|}(S(\mathcal A_d, w) - S(\mathcal B_d, w)/\varphi(q))$ plus the sum over the stops of
$(-1)^{|P| + 1}(S(\mathcal A_W, p) - S(\mathcal B_W, p)/\varphi(q))$.

\emph{The stops.} A stop has $X' < W \le p X' < z_0 X'$, and $W$ is $p$-rough, since the primes of $r$ are at least
$z_0 > p$ and those of $P$ exceed $p$. So the $p$-rough multiples of $W$ in $(N_1, N_2]$ are the $n = Wk$ whose cofactor
$k$ is $p$-rough, and every such $k$ lies in $(P_1, P_2]$ with $P_1 = \lfloor N_1/(z_0 X') \rfloor$ and
$P_2 = \lfloor N_2/(X' + 1) \rfloor$. For large $q$, $q^{u_1 L} \le P_1$, since $5q^{u_1 L + a_0 + b_0} \le q^L$ by
$a_0 + b_0 < (1 - u_1)L$, and $P_2 \le q^{u_2 L}$, since $2q^{L - b_0} \le q^{u_2 L}$ by $(1 - u_2)L < b_0$. So the
cofactor $k$ lies in the window's range, and $W$ in its reflection. A stop has $|P| \le S_0$, since
$\prod P \ge w^{|P|} \ge q^{\varepsilon_w |P|}$ and $\prod P \le X' \le q^L$.

For each $s \le S_0$, the stops with $|P| = s$ form one sum of the kind that Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp} bounds, with
$m = k \in (P_1, P_2]$ and $a_m = 1$, whose primes are $r_1, \dots, r_j$, the elements $y_1 > \dots > y_s$ of $P$ and
$p$, and whose comparisons are the given ones on the $r_i$ and
\begin{gather*}
  y_1 > \dots > y_s > p, \quad y_1 < z_0, \quad p \ge w, \quad r y_1 \cdots y_s \le X' < r y_1 \cdots y_s \, p, \\
  p \le \min\{\ell \text{ prime} : \ell \mid k\}, \qquad N_1 < k r y_1 \cdots y_s p \le N_2 .
\end{gather*}
Each is of the form $\varphi(k) + \sum_i e_i \log x_i > 0$ or $\ge 0$ with coefficients in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ and a pivot.
There are at most $J_0 + s + 7$ of them, and $\varphi(k) \le (\Lambda_1 + L + 1) \log q$ for $k \le P_2$. Wherever they
all hold, every prime is at least $w \ge 2q^{\varepsilon_w/2}$ and $k r y_1 \cdots y_s p \le 2x$. So
Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp}, with $K_0 = j + S_0 + 1$, $J_0 + S_0 + 7$ comparisons, $e_1 = \varepsilon_w/2$, $E_0 = 1$
and $\Lambda_1 + L + 1$ in place of $\Lambda_1$, bounds each of these $S_0 + 1$ sums by $q^{L - \delta_T}/\varphi(q)$,
with $\delta_T > 0$ depending only on the constants above. For large $q$ the stops therefore total at most
$q^{L - \delta_1}/\varphi(q)$, with $\delta_1 = \delta_T/2$.

\emph{A leaf.} Let $d$ have all its prime factors at least $w$. We show
\begin{multline}\label{eq:leaf}
  \Bigl|S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr| \\
  \le \frac{4 \zeta_k}{\varphi(q)} \, \#\{n \in (N_1, N_2] : d \mid n,\ n \text{ is } w\text{-rough}\}
  + 48 \, w^{2k + 7}.
\end{multline}
If $(d, q) > 1$ both counts vanish, so let $(d, q) = 1$. Sieve by the primes below $w$, in the ranges
$T_i = \mathcal P[w^{2^{-i - 1}}, w^{2^{-i}})$ for $0 \le i < J = 2 \lfloor \log_2 w \rfloor + 2$, which cover them, at the
even levels $k_i = k + 2i$. Brun's sieve, in the following form, is used. Let $\mathcal C$ be a finite set of integers,
$X \ge 0$, and $g$ a function on the primes of $\bigcup T_i$ with $0 \le g \le 1$. Suppose that for every choice of
$s_i \subseteq T_i$, with $D = \prod_i \prod_{p \in s_i} p$, the count $\#\{n \in \mathcal C : D \mid n\}$ is
$X \prod_i \prod_{p \in s_i} g(p)$ within $R$. Then $\#\{n \in \mathcal C : (n, \prod_i \prod_{T_i} p) = 1\}$ is
$X \prod_i \prod_{p \in T_i} (1 - g(p))$ within
\[
  X \sum_i e_{k_i}(T_i) \prod_{l \ne i} F_{k_l}(T_l)
  + (J + 1) \, R \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m},
\]
where $e_m(T) = \sum_{s \subseteq T,\ |s| = m} \prod_{p \in s} g(p)$ and $F_k(T) = \sum_{m \le k} (-1)^m e_m(T)$. It
follows from Bonferroni's inequalities $F_{k_i - 1}(T_i) \le \prod_{p \in T_i}(1 - \mathbf 1[p \mid n]) \le F_{k_i}(T_i)$,
taken for each $n$ with $g = \mathbf 1[\cdot \mid n]$ (the levels are even), and the elementary inequality
$\prod_i \mathrm{hi}_i - \sum_i (\mathrm{hi}_i - \mathrm{lo}_i) \prod_{l \ne i} \mathrm{hi}_l \le \prod_i \rho_i$ for
$0 \le \mathrm{lo}_i \le \rho_i \le \mathrm{hi}_i$.

Take $g(p) = 1/p$ for $p \nmid q$ and $g(p) = 0$ for $p \mid q$. For $\mathcal A_d$, take
$\mathcal C = \{n \in (N_1, N_2] : n \equiv a,\ d \mid n\}$, $X = (N_2 - N_1)/(dq)$ and $R = 1$: if no prime of $D$
divides $q$, Lemma~\ref{lem:type1} with $M = dD$ gives the count within $1$, and otherwise the count and the model both
vanish. For $\mathcal B_d$, take $\mathcal C = \{n \in (N_1, N_2] : (n, q) = 1,\ d \mid n\}$, $X\varphi(q)$ and
$R = \varphi(q)$, and divide by $\varphi(q)$. Both main terms are then $XV$ with $V = \prod_{p < w,\ p \nmid q}(1 - 1/p)$,
so that
\begin{gather*}
  \Bigl|S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr| \le 2(XG + \mathrm{rem}), \\
  G = \sum_i e_{k_i}(T_i) \prod_{l \ne i} F_{k_l}(T_l), \qquad
  \mathrm{rem} = (J + 1) \prod_i \sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m}.
\end{gather*}
Each $T_i$ lies in some $\mathcal P[y, y^2)$, so $\sum_{p \in T_i} g(p) \le C_0$ and, since $1 - t \ge e^{-2t}$ for
$0 \le t \le 1/2$, $V_i = \prod_{p \in T_i}(1 - g(p)) \ge e^{-2C_0}$. With $e_m \le C_0^m/m!$,
$F_{k_l} \le V_l + e_{k_l + 1} \le V_l (1 + e^{2C_0} C_0^{k_l + 1}/(k_l + 1)!)$ and
$\prod_{l \ne i} V_l \le e^{2C_0} V$, this gives
$G \le V e^{2C_0} \sum_i \frac{C_0^{k_i}}{k_i!} \prod_l \bigl(1 + e^{2C_0} \frac{C_0^{k_l + 1}}{(k_l + 1)!}\bigr)$. Since
$2C_0^2 \le (k + 1)(k + 2)$, the terms of $\sum_i C_0^{k + 2i}/(k + 2i)!$ decrease at least geometrically with ratio
$1/2$, so the sum is at most $2C_0^k/k!$, and with $\prod (1 + t_l) \le \exp \sum t_l$ we get $G \le \zeta_k V$. The
lower bound for $\mathcal B_d$ gives $XV \le S(\mathcal B_d, w)/\varphi(q) + XV/2 + \mathrm{rem}$, since
$\zeta_k \le 1/2$, so $XV \le 2(S(\mathcal B_d, w)/\varphi(q) + \mathrm{rem})$, and then
\[
  \Bigl|S(\mathcal A_d, w) - \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)}\Bigr|
  \le 4\zeta_k \frac{S(\mathcal B_d, w)}{\varphi(q)} + 4\,\mathrm{rem}.
\]
Finally $S(\mathcal B_d, w)$ is at most the count in \eqref{eq:leaf}, and
$\sum_{m \le k_i} \binom{|T_i|}{m} \le 2 \max(1, |T_i|)^{k_i}$ with $|T_i| \le w^{2^{-i}}$,
$\sum_i 2^{-i}(k + 2i) \le 2k + 4$, $J + 1 \le 3w$ and $2^J \le 4w^2$ give $\mathrm{rem} \le 12 w^{2k + 7}$.

\emph{The leaves together.} The count in \eqref{eq:leaf} is at most the number of $w$-rough integers in
$(x/d, 2x/d]$. For $d \le X' \le x$ and large $q$, Lemma~\ref{lem:rough} with $U = (L + 1)/\varepsilon_w$ and
$\varepsilon = 1$, and $\omega^* \le 1$, bound it by $2x/(d \log w) \le 2x/(d \varepsilon_w \log q)$. With $1 \le X'/d$
for the remainder, the leaves total at most
\[
  \Bigl(\frac{8 \zeta_k x}{\varphi(q) \varepsilon_w \log q} + 48 \, w^{2k + 7} X'\Bigr)
  \sum_{(r_1, \dots, r_j)} \sum_{P \subseteq \mathcal P[w, z_0)} \frac1{r \prod P},
\]
and the double sum is $\sum 1/r \cdot \prod_{w \le p < z_0}(1 + 1/p)$. Each $r_i$ is a prime in $[q^{a_0}, 2q^L]$, so
for large $q$, by the prime number theorem, $\sum 1/r \le K_1$ and $\prod_{w \le p < z_0}(1 + 1/p) \le e a_0/\varepsilon_w$.
The main part is then at most
\[
  \frac{8 e K_1 a_0 \zeta_k}{\varepsilon_w^2} \cdot \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log q}
  = \frac{8 e K_1 a_0 (2k + 4)^2 \zeta_k}{c^2} \cdot \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log q}
  \le \frac{\eta \, x}{\varphi(q) \log q},
\]
by the choice of $k$. Since $w \le 2q^{\varepsilon_w}$ and $(2k + 7)\varepsilon_w \le 2c$, the remainder part is at most
$48 \cdot 2^{2k + 7} K_1 e a_0 \varepsilon_w^{-1} q^{b_0 + 2c} \le q^{b_0 + 3c} = q^{L - 1 - c} \le q^{L - c}/\varphi(q)$
for large $q$, using $\varphi(q) \le q$.

\emph{Conclusion.} The sum of the proposition is at most
$\eta x/(\varphi(q) \log q) + (q^{L - c} + q^{L - \delta_1})/\varphi(q)$, which for large $q$ is at most
$\eta x/(\varphi(q) \log q) + q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$ with $\delta = \min(c, \delta_1)/2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rough numbers}

The last tool bounds from above the number $\Psi(y, z)$ of integers in $(y, 2y]$ with no prime factor below $z$. Let
$\omega^*(u) = 0$ for $u < 1$, $\omega^*(u) = 1/u$ for $1 \le u < 2$ and $\omega^*(u) = 0.5672$ for $u \ge 2$, a crude
upper bound for Buchstab's function $\omega$: the two agree on $[1, 2)$, and $\omega$ stays below $0.5672$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rough}
For every $U$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, for all large $z$ and all $y \ge 1$ with $u = \log 2y/\log z \le U$,
$\Psi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon)\, y/\log z$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $c = 0.5672$, $u = \log 2y/\log z$, and say that an integer is $z$-rough if it has no prime factor below $z$.
Three facts are used.

\emph{(a) Prime sums.} By the prime number theorem with an error $o(t/\log t)$, for every $\varepsilon_3 > 0$,
$|\theta(t) - t| \le \varepsilon_3 t/\log t$ for large $t$. With partial summation this gives, for large $a \le b$,
\[
  \sum_{a < p \le b} \frac1{p \log p} \le \frac1{\log a} - \frac1{\log b} + \frac{4\varepsilon_3}{\log^2 a},
\]
and, if $2 \log b \le \Lambda$,
\[
  \sum_{a < p \le b} \frac1{p(\Lambda - \log p)}
  \le \frac{1 + \varepsilon_3/\log a}{\Lambda} \log \frac{\log b \, (\Lambda - \log a)}{\log a \, (\Lambda - \log b)}
  + \frac{6\varepsilon_3}{\Lambda \log^2 a};
\]
and, for large $y$, there are at most $(1 + \varepsilon_3) y/\log 2y$ primes in $(y, 2y]$.

\emph{(b) Buchstab's inequality.} For $y \ge 2$,
\[
  \Psi(y, z) \le \#\{p : y < p \le 2y\} + \sum_{z \le p \le \sqrt{2y}} \Psi(y/p, p),
\]
since a $z$-rough integer $m \in (y, 2y]$ that is not prime has its least prime factor $p$ in $[z, \sqrt{2y}]$, and
$m/p$ is a $p$-rough integer in $(y/p, 2y/p]$; the map $m \mapsto (p, m/p)$ is injective. Also $\Psi(y, z) = 0$ if
$y \ge 1$ and $2y < z$, since every integer of $(y, 2y]$ is then at least $2$ and below $z$.

\emph{(c) A delay inequality.} Let $M(u) = \log(u - 1)$ for $2 \le u \le 3$ and $M(u) = \log 2 + c(u - 3)$ for $u > 3$,
so that $M(u) = \int_1^{u - 1} \omega^*(t) \, dt$. Then $1 + M(u) \le cu$ for $u \ge 2$. For $u \le 3$, the inequality
$\log t \le t - 1$ at $t = c(u - 1)$ gives $1 + \log(u - 1) \le c(u - 1) - \log c \le cu$, since $-\log c \le c$; that
is, $c e^c \ge 1$, which holds because $e^c \ge \sum_{m \le 5} c^m/m! > 1.7632 > 1/c$. For $u > 3$ it follows from
$1 + \log 2 < 1.7016 = 3c$. So $\omega^*$ satisfies $u \omega^*(u) \ge 1 + \int_1^{u - 1} \omega^*$ for $u \ge 2$, the
inequality that the induction below needs; Buchstab's function itself is not used.

We prove by induction on the integer $k \ge 0$ the statement $\mathrm C(k)$: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is
$z_0$ such that $\Psi(y, z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$ for all $z \ge z_0$ and all $y \ge 1$ with
$u \le k$. $\mathrm C(0)$ holds trivially, since $u > 0$, and the lemma is $\mathrm C(\lceil U \rceil)$. Assume
$\mathrm C(k)$, let $0 < \varepsilon \le 1$ and $\varepsilon_3 = \varepsilon/100$, let $z$ be large in terms of
$\varepsilon$ and of the $z_0$ of $\mathrm C(k)$ for $\varepsilon/4$, and let $y \ge 1$ with
$\Lambda = \log 2y = u \log z \le (k + 1) \log z$. If $2y < z$, then $\Psi(y, z) = 0$. Otherwise $u \ge 1$, $y \ge z/2$
is large, and (a) and (b) give
\[
  \Psi(y, z) \le \Bigl(1 + \frac\varepsilon4\Bigr) \frac y\Lambda + \sum_{z \le p \le \sqrt{2y}} \Psi(y/p, p).
\]
If $u < 2$, then $\sqrt{2y} < z$, the sum is empty, and
$\Psi(y, z) \le (1 + \varepsilon/4) y/(u \log z) \le (\omega^*(u) + \varepsilon) y/\log z$. Let $u \ge 2$, so that
$\omega^*(u) = c$. A prime $p \in [z, \sqrt{2y}]$ has $y/p \ge 1$ and
$\log(2y/p) = \Lambda - \log p \le k \log p$, since $\Lambda \le (k + 1) \log p$. So $\mathrm C(k)$ gives
$\Psi(y/p, p) \le (\omega^*(u_p) + \varepsilon/4) \, y/(p \log p)$ with $u_p = (\Lambda - \log p)/\log p$. The term
$p = z$, if $z$ is prime, contributes at most $2y/(z \log z)$, since $\omega^* \le 1$. By (a), the parts
$\varepsilon/4$ of the other terms contribute at most $(\varepsilon/4)(1/\log z + 4\varepsilon_3/\log^2 z) \, y$. For
the parts $\omega^*(u_p)$: if $p \le (2y)^{1/3}$ then $u_p \ge 2$ and $\omega^*(u_p) = c$; if
$(2y)^{1/3} < p \le (2y)^{1/2}$ then $1 \le u_p < 2$ and $\omega^*(u_p)/(p \log p) = 1/(p(\Lambda - \log p))$. By (a),
applied on $(z, \sqrt{2y}]$ if $u \le 3$, and on $(z, (2y)^{1/3}]$ and $((2y)^{1/3}, \sqrt{2y}]$ if $u > 3$,
\[
  \sum_{z < p \le \sqrt{2y}} \frac{\omega^*(u_p)}{p \log p} \le \frac{M(u) + 12\varepsilon_3}\Lambda
  + \frac{4\varepsilon_3}{\log z}.
\]
Collecting the terms, with $\Lambda = u \log z$,
\[
  \Psi(y, z) \le \frac y{\log z} \Bigl(\frac{1 + M(u)}u + \frac{\varepsilon/4 + 12\varepsilon_3}u + \frac2z
  + 4\varepsilon_3 + \frac\varepsilon4 + \frac{\varepsilon \varepsilon_3}{\log z}\Bigr).
\]
By (c) the first term is at most $c$, and since $u \ge 1$ and $z$ is large the others add up to at most
$0.92\varepsilon$. So $\Psi(y, z) \le (c + \varepsilon) y/\log z$, which proves $\mathrm C(k + 1)$.
\end{proof}

The certificate of Section~\ref{sec:certificate} uses $\omega^*$ to bound the share of the integers in each term it
drops.

\section{The decomposition and its certificate}\label{sec:certificate}

This section bounds $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$, with $z_{\mathrm{top}} = (2x)^{1/2}$, from below. The
decomposition writes it by Buchstab's identity as a signed sum of sifted terms. Each term is evaluated, that is
compared with its $\mathcal B$-version by Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp} or Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}, or, if it has
sign $+$, dropped, which costs its $\mathcal B$-version. The certificate is an upper bound for the total cost,
computed exactly in integers.

\subsection{Exponents and the grid}

The exponent of a prime $p$ is $\alpha = \log p/\log x$. Fix a denominator $D = 2^{40}$ and round the window's ends
inward: $U_1 = \lceil u_1 D \rceil$ and $U_2 = \lfloor u_2 D \rfloor$, so that $u_1 < U_1/D < U_2/D < u_2$. Let
$v = (U_2 - U_1)/D$. The decomposition sifts the primes of exponent at least $v$, so $z_0 = x^v$. The grid has $n$
cells a side, of side $h = \lceil (D/2 - (U_2 - U_1))/n \rceil / D$; the cell $k$ is the range
$[e_k, e_k + h]$ of exponents with $e_k = v + kh$, for $0 \le k < n$, and the cells cover $[v, 1/2]$. Here $n = 340$.
The primes of the two deepest levels of the decomposition, $p_5$ and $p_6$, have their exponents in the cells of a
\emph{coarse grid} with the same window and $n_C = n/4 = 85$ cells a side, of side $h_C = \lceil (D/2 - (U_2 - U_1))/n_C \rceil / D$,
and cells $[e^C_b, e^C_b + h_C]$ with $e^C_b = v + b h_C$.

The set of \emph{evaluable} exponents is
\[
  \mathcal U = (U_1/D,\ U_2/D) \cup (1 - U_2/D,\ 1 - U_1/D).
\]
A box, a product of cells, is \emph{surely evaluable} if for some subset of its coordinates the sum of the exponents
lies in $\mathcal U$ throughout the box. If the exponents of the primes $p_1, \dots, p_k$ of a term lie in such a box,
then a product of some of them lies in $(x^{U_1/D}, x^{U_2/D})$, or in the reflected range, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp} the term's sums over $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ agree up to $q^{L - \delta}/
\varphi(q)$. A box is tested exactly: the subset sums of a box range over intervals with rational ends.

\subsection{The decomposition}

Write $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots$ for the exponents of the primes $p_1 > p_2 > \cdots$ that Buchstab's identity
introduces, each at least $v$. Let $X'$ be the threshold of Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}'s proof, a power of $x$
chosen so that a product $W > X'$ of primes below $x^{1/2}$ with $W \le x^{1 - U_1/D}$ has every cofactor $k$ with
$Wk \in (x, 2x]$ in the window's range.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Depth one.}
  $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})
  = S(\mathcal A, z_0) - \sum_{z_0 \le p_1 < z_{\mathrm{top}}} S(\mathcal A_{p_1}, p_1)$.
  The first term is evaluated by Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}. A term whose $\alpha_1$ lies in a cell inside the
  window is evaluated by Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp}; the others are decomposed again by Buchstab's identity, which gives
  $S(\mathcal A_{p_1}, z_0)$, evaluated by Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}, and the terms
  $S(\mathcal A_{p_1 p_2}, p_2)$ of depth two with $z_0 \le p_2 < p_1$, with sign $+$.
\item \emph{Depth two.} Let $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2)$ lie in the cell $(i, j)$. If the box of the cell is surely
  evaluable, the term is evaluated. Otherwise the cell is a \emph{candidate} if it can meet the region
  $\alpha_2 < \alpha_1$, $2\alpha_1 < 1$, $\alpha_1 + 2\alpha_2 \le 1$ where the term is not empty. A non-candidate
  cell that is not surely evaluable is dropped. A candidate cell is either dropped or decomposed, by the rule below;
  if decomposed, its terms with $p_1 p_2 > X'$ are evaluated with the cofactor as the Type~II variable, and those with
  $p_1 p_2 \le X'$ are decomposed again, which gives terms of depth three with sign $-$.
\item \emph{Depth three.} A term of depth three cannot be dropped. If its box is surely evaluable, it is evaluated;
  otherwise it is split at $X'$ in the same way, and the part with $p_1 p_2 p_3 \le X'$ gives terms of depth four with
  sign $+$.
\item \emph{Depth four.} A term of depth four whose box is surely evaluable is evaluated. Another term of depth four,
  with $(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_4)$ in the cell $(i, j, k, l)$ of the region where it is not empty (a
  \emph{counted} cell), is either dropped, or decomposed once more, which gives terms of depth five with sign $-$,
  $S(\mathcal A_{p_1 \cdots p_5}, p_5)$ with $z_0 \le p_5 < p_4$ and $\alpha_5$ in a cell $b$ of the coarse grid.
\item \emph{Depth five.} A term of depth five cannot be dropped. If its box, the cell $(i, j, k, l)$ of the fine grid and the coarse
  cell $b$, is surely evaluable, it is evaluated; otherwise it is split at $X'$ in the same way, and the part with
  $p_1 \cdots p_5 \le X'$ gives terms of depth six with sign $+$, $S(\mathcal A_{p_1 \cdots p_6}, p_6)$ with
  $\alpha_6$ in a coarse cell $c \le b$.
\item \emph{Depth six.} A term of depth six whose box is surely evaluable is evaluated, and the others are dropped.
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{sharp rule} allows a candidate cell $(i, j)$ to be decomposed only if
$\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 < 1 - U_1/D$ on its box, and $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 + \alpha_3 < 1 - U_1/D$ on the boxes of its
non-evaluable cells of depth three: then the splits at $X'$ produce only cofactors in the window's range. The same rule
allows a counted cell of depth four to be decomposed only if $\alpha_1 + \cdots + \alpha_4 < 1 - U_1/D$ on its box and
$\alpha_1 + \cdots + \alpha_5 < 1 - U_1/D$ on the boxes of its non-evaluable cells of depth five.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:decomp}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}. For every $\varepsilon > 0$, for all large $q$ and every $a$ prime to
$q$,
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) \ge \frac{1}{\varphi(q)} \Bigl(S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})
  - D(\mathcal B)\Bigr) - \varepsilon \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x},
\]
where $D(\mathcal B)$ is the sum of the $\mathcal B$-versions of the dropped terms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identities of the decomposition are exact. A dropped term has sign $+$ and its $\mathcal A$-count is at least
$0$, so dropping it lowers the right side by its $\mathcal B$-version. Every evaluated term, and every term
$S(\cdot, z_0)$, has its $\mathcal A$- and $\mathcal B/\varphi(q)$-counts within an error $\mathrm{Err}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp} and Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}, and there are at most $1 + n(1 + n(2 + n(2 + n(n_C(n_C + 2) + 2))))$ of
them. For large $q$ the total error is below $\varepsilon x/(\varphi(q) \log x)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The cost of a dropped term}

A dropped term of depth two over the cell $(i, j)$ counts the $n = p_1 p_2 m \in (x, 2x]$ whose cofactor $m$ has no
prime factor below $p_2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:rough} and the prime number theorem its $\mathcal B$-version is, for large
$q$, at most $x/\log x$ times
\[
  \iint_{\text{cell}} \omega^*\Bigl(\frac{1 - \alpha_1 - \alpha_2}{\alpha_2}\Bigr)
  \frac{d\alpha_1\, d\alpha_2}{\alpha_1 \alpha_2^2},
\]
plus $\varepsilon$, and similarly at depth four with
$d\alpha_1 \cdots d\alpha_4/(\alpha_1 \alpha_2 \alpha_3 \alpha_4^2)$ and the argument
$(1 - \alpha_1 - \cdots - \alpha_4)/\alpha_4$, and at depth six with
$d\alpha_1 \cdots d\alpha_6/(\alpha_1 \cdots \alpha_5 \alpha_6^2)$ and the argument $(1 - \alpha_1 - \cdots - \alpha_6)/\alpha_6$. The certificate bounds each integral by its integrand's maximum on
the cell times the cell's volume, with $\omega^*$ bounded on the cell's range of arguments, and works in units
of $1/K$ with $K = 2^{60}$, every quotient rounded up.
\begin{itemize}
\item A dropped cell of depth two costs this bound, \texttt{bound2}.
\item A decomposed cell $(i, j)$ costs the sum over its non-evaluable cells of depth three of the costs of its counted
  cells of depth four: \texttt{refcost}. A counted cell of depth four costs its bound \texttt{bound4} if it is dropped,
  and, if it is decomposed, the sum of the bounds \texttt{bound6} of the counted cells of depth six below its
  non-evaluable cells of depth five (\texttt{refcost5}): it is decomposed when the sharp rule allows it and this sum is at
  most its own \texttt{bound4}. The cell $(i, j)$ is decomposed when the sharp rule allows it and its \texttt{refcost} is at
  most its own \texttt{bound2}.
\item The total is the sum of the costs over the candidate cells, $\mathtt{total} = \sum_{i, j} \mathtt{cost}(i, j)$.
\end{itemize}
The non-candidate cells that are not surely evaluable cost nothing in the total: on them the term is empty, or the
argument $(1 - \alpha_1 - \alpha_2)/\alpha_2$ is below $1$, so that the cofactor $m$ is $1$, and these terms together
are $o(x/\log x)$ (the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:drop}).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:drop}
If $\mathtt{total}/K \le c$, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and all large $q$,
$D(\mathcal B) \le (c + \varepsilon)\, x/\log x$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $X = x/\log x$ and, for a cell $k$, $\mathcal P_k$ for the set of the primes $p$ with
$x^{e_k} \le p < x^{e_k + h}$. We bound the $\mathcal B$-version of each dropped term and then add the bounds.

\emph{A dropped term of depth two.} Let $(i, j)$ be a candidate cell that is dropped. Its $\mathcal B$-version counts
the $n \in (x, 2x]$ prime to $q$ of the form $n = p_1 p_2 m$ with $(p_1, p_2)$ in the cell and $m$ without prime
factors below $p_2$. Dropping the conditions $(n, q) = 1$, $p_2 < p_1$, $z_0 \le p_2$ and $p_1 < z_{\mathrm{top}}$, and
mapping $n$ to $m$, it is at most
\[
  \sum_{p_1 \in \mathcal P_i} \sum_{p_2 \in \mathcal P_j} \Psi\Bigl(\frac{x}{p_1 p_2}, p_2\Bigr).
\]
A candidate has $e_i + 2e_j \le 1$, and $e_j \ge v \ge 4h$, so $e_i + e_j + 2h < 1$ and $y = x/(p_1 p_2) \ge 1$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:rough}, with $U = 2/e_j$, applies to $\Psi(y, p_2)$, with
$u = \log 2y/\log p_2 = (1 - \alpha_1 - \alpha_2 + \tau)/\alpha_2$ and $\tau = \log 2/\log x$. Let $\Omega_{ij}$ be the
certificate's upper bound for $\omega^*$ on the cell's range of arguments. Since $\omega^*$ is not monotone, it is
found by cases:
\begin{itemize}
\item $0$ if every argument is below $1$;
\item $0.5672$, rounded up in units of $1/K$, if every argument is at least $2$;
\item $1/u$ at the smallest argument, which is at least $1$, if every argument is below $2$;
\item the larger of the last two otherwise.
\end{itemize}
The certificate's tests are strict inequalities between integers over $D$, so they leave a margin of $1/D$, which
exceeds $\tau$ once $\log x > D \log 2$; so $\Omega_{ij}$ also bounds $\omega^*(u)$. Hence
$\Psi(y, p_2) \le (\Omega_{ij} + \varepsilon') x/(p_1 p_2 \log p_2)$ for large $x$, and the term is at most
\[
  (\Omega_{ij} + \varepsilon') \, x \sum_{p_1 \in \mathcal P_i} \frac1{p_1} \sum_{p_2 \in \mathcal P_j}
  \frac1{p_2 \log p_2}.
\]
By the prime number theorem and partial summation, for large $x$,
\begin{gather*}
  \sum_{p \in \mathcal P_i} \frac1p \le \log\Bigl(1 + \frac h{e_i}\Bigr) + \varepsilon' \le \frac h{e_i} + \varepsilon', \\
  \log x \sum_{p \in \mathcal P_j} \frac1{p \log p} \le \frac1{e_j} - \frac1{e_j + h} + \varepsilon'
  \le \frac h{e_j^2} + \varepsilon'.
\end{gather*}
These are the integrals of $d\alpha/\alpha$ and $d\alpha/\alpha^2$ over the cells, bounded by the integrands at the lower
corners times $h$. Since $\mathtt{bound2}(i, j)/K \ge \Omega_{ij} h^2/(e_i e_j^2)$ by its definition, with every quotient
rounded up, the term is at most $(\mathtt{bound2}(i, j)/K + \delta) X$ for large $q$, for any given $\delta > 0$.

\emph{Depths four and six.} The same argument bounds a dropped term over a counted cell $(i, j, k, l)$ of depth four,
with $\Psi(x/(p_1 \cdots p_4), p_4)$, three sums $\sum 1/p$ and one sum $\log x \sum 1/(p \log p)$, by
$(\mathtt{bound4}/K + \delta) X$; and a dropped term over a counted box of depth six, whose last two primes lie in coarse
cells, by $(\mathtt{bound6}/K + \delta) X$. Here $y \ge 1$ follows from the condition that defines a counted cell, with
$4h \le v$ at depth four and $4h + 2h_C \le v$ at depth six.

\emph{The corner cells.} A cell $(i, j)$ that is neither a candidate nor surely evaluable, with the cell $i$ not inside
the window, is dropped as well. Since $2e_i < 1$, either $e_j \ge e_i + h$, and the term is empty, or
$e_i + 2e_j > 1$. In the second case $p_1 p_2^2 > 2x$ for large $x$, so the cofactor $m$, which has no prime factor
below $p_2$, is $1$, and $n = p_1 p_2 > x$ with $p_1, p_2 < z_{\mathrm{top}}$. Both primes then lie in
$(z_{\mathrm{top}}/4, z_{\mathrm{top}}]$, so there are at most $(12\sqrt x/\log x)^2$ such integers for large $x$. The
corner cells, at most $n^2$ of them, together contribute $O(x/\log^2 x) = o(X)$.

\emph{Assembly.} $D(\mathcal B)$ is the sum of the terms of depth two of the dropped candidate cells, of the dropped terms
of depths four and six below the decomposed candidate cells, and of the terms of the corner cells. By the bounds above,
a candidate cell $(i, j)$ contributes at most $(\mathtt{cost}(i, j)/K + (1 + n^2(1 + n_C^2))\delta) X$, since the cell
has at most $1 + n^2(1 + n_C^2)$ dropped terms below it. Taking $\delta$ small and $q$ large,
$D(\mathcal B) \le (\mathtt{total}/K + \varepsilon/2) X + o(X) \le (c + \varepsilon) X$.
\end{proof}

On the grid of this paper the certificate as it stands totals about $1.11742K$ (a computation by
\file{tools/linnik/route_limits.py}, not checked in Lean).
Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp} with Lemma~\ref{lem:drop} then gives nothing. The refinement removes about a tenth of the total.

\subsection{The refinement: keeping two-prime parts}

A dropped term $S(\mathcal A_{p_1 p_2}, p_2)$ counts, among others, the $n = p_1 p_2 q_1 q_2$ with primes
$p_2 < q_1 < q_2$. Such an $n$ is a product of four primes, and if some of their exponents sum into $\mathcal U$ it
belongs to a Type~II sum. The refinement keeps these parts instead of dropping them.

Let the cell $(i, j)$ be a candidate that is not decomposed and lie in the region $\alpha_2 < \alpha_1 < 1/2$
(\texttt{reg2}). For a cell $k$ with $e_k \ge e_j + h$ (so $q_1 > p_2$ on the box), with $2(e_k + h) + e_i + e_j + 2h
\le 1$ (so $q_1 < q_2$ on the box), and with the box $(i, j, k)$ surely evaluable (\texttt{keep}), the \emph{kept part}
below $k$ counts the $n = p_1 p_2 q_1 q_2 \in (x, 2x]$ with $(p_1, p_2)$ in the cell $(i, j)$, $q_1$ a prime of the
cell $k$, $q_2 > q_1$ a prime, and $q_1, q_2 \ne p_1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:kept}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each pair $(p_1, p_2)$ of the dropped term, the kept parts below the cell count distinct integers, all of
  which the term counts for that pair; so their $\mathcal A$-count is at most the term's.
\item Each kept part is the sum of three families of products of four primes in decreasing order, according to the
  position of $q_2$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp} evaluates each of them.
\item For every $\delta > 0$ and all large $q$, the $\mathcal B$-version of the kept part below $k$ is at least
  $(\mathtt{keepK}(i, j, k)/K - \delta)\, x/\log x$, where
  $\mathtt{keepK}(i, j, k) = \lfloor K h^3 / ((e_i + h)(e_j + h)(e_k + h)(1 - e_i - e_j - e_k)) \rfloor$ is a lower
  bound for its density $d\alpha_1\, d\alpha_2\, d\beta/(\alpha_1 \alpha_2 \beta \gamma)$ over the box, $\gamma$ the
  exponent of $q_2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $X = x/\log x$ and $\mathcal P_c$ for the set of primes of the cell $c$. By the definition of the term
$S(\mathcal A_{p_1 p_2}, p_2)$ of the cell $(i, j)$, its pairs $(p_1, p_2)$ have $p_2 < p_1$, $z_0 \le p_2$ and
$p_1 < z_{\mathrm{top}}$.

(1) Fix a pair $(p_1, p_2)$ of the term. An integer $n = p_1 p_2 q_1 q_2$ of a kept part determines
$q_1 q_2 = n/(p_1 p_2)$, hence the primes $q_1 < q_2$, and $q_1$ determines the cell $k$, since the cells are disjoint.
So, for this pair, the kept parts below $(i, j)$ count each integer at most once. Each such $n$ is counted by the term
for the pair: $n \in (x, 2x]$, $p_1 p_2 \mid n$, and every prime factor of $n$ is one of $p_1$, $p_2$, $q_1$, $q_2$,
which are all at least $p_2$, since $q_1 \ge x^{e_k} \ge x^{e_j + h} > p_2$ by \texttt{keep} and $q_2 > q_1$. Summing over
the pairs, with the nonnegative weight $\mathbf 1[n \equiv a]$, gives (1). The kept $n$ are products of four distinct
primes; the integers of the term with a prime-power cofactor stay in the dropped remainder.

(2) For an integer $n = p_1 p_2 q_1 q_2$ of the kept part below $k$, exactly one of $q_2 < p_1$, $q_1 < p_1 < q_2$ and
$p_1 < q_1$ holds, since $q_1 < q_2$, $q_1, q_2 \ne p_1$ and $p_2 < q_1$. So the kept part is the sum of three sums over
the decreasing $4$-tuples $r_0 > r_1 > r_2 > r_3$ of primes, equal to $(p_1, q_2, q_1, p_2)$, $(q_2, p_1, q_1, p_2)$ and
$(q_2, q_1, p_1, p_2)$ respectively. The conditions are $N_1 < r_0 r_1 r_2 r_3 \le N_2$, that $p_1$ lies in the cell $i$
with $z_0 \le p_1 < z_{\mathrm{top}}$, that $p_2 = r_3$ lies in the cell $j$ with $p_2 \ge z_0$, and that $q_1$ lies in
the cell $k$; $q_2$ is subject to no cell condition. These are at most $18$ comparisons in the sense of
Section~\ref{sec:type1}, with coefficients in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$, pivots and constants $O(\log q)$. Since the box
$(i, j, k)$ is surely evaluable, the product of a set $T$ of the primes $p_1$, $p_2$, $q_1$ lies, on the box, in
$(x^{U_1/D}, x^{U_2/D})$ or in $(x^{1 - U_2/D}, x^{1 - U_1/D})$. In the first case $m = \prod_T$ lies in the window's
range; in the second the complementary product, which contains $q_2$, does, for large $q$, as for the stops in the proof
of Proposition~\ref{prop:harman}, since the grid's window lies strictly inside the route's. Group the tuples by this
$m$, a squarefree integer whose prime factors, read in decreasing order, are the primes of its set. The comparisons
that involve only these primes then become conditions on $m$, absorbed into $a_m \in \{0, 1\}$; the others are pivoted
on the remaining primes. Each of the three sums is then a sum as in Lemma~\ref{lem:family-sharp}, which bounds its
$\mathcal A - \mathcal B/\varphi(q)$ by $q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$.

(3) For $p_1 \in \mathcal P_i$, $p_2 \in \mathcal P_j$ and $q_1 \in \mathcal P_k$, put $y = x/(p_1 p_2 q_1)$ and
$\gamma = 1 - e_i - e_j - e_k$. By \texttt{reg2}, $p_2 < p_1 < x^{1/2}$, so every such pair belongs to the term. By the
second condition of \texttt{keep}, $\gamma \ge e_k + 4h$ and $q_1 < y$, so every prime $q_2 \in (y, 2y]$ has $q_2 > q_1$
and $p_1 p_2 q_1 q_2 \in (x, 2x]$. Exclude $q_2 = p_1$ and the $q_2$ dividing $q$, at most $1 + \omega(q)$ primes with
$\omega(q) \le \log q/\log 2$, and exclude the triples with $q_1 = p_1$ or with a prime dividing $q$. Then the
$\mathcal B$-version of the kept part is at least
\[
  \sum_{p_1, p_2, q_1} \bigl(\pi(2y) - \pi(y)\bigr) - (2 + \omega(q)) \#\{(p_1, p_2, q_1)\}
  - \sum_{\text{excluded triples}} (y + 1).
\]
On the box $x^{\gamma - 3h} < y \le x^\gamma$, so for large $x$ the prime number theorem gives
$\pi(2y) - \pi(y) \ge (1 - \varepsilon) y/\log y \ge (1 - \varepsilon) y/(\gamma \log x)$, and
\[
  \sum_{p_1, p_2, q_1} \frac y{\gamma \log x} = \frac X\gamma \prod_{c \in \{i, j, k\}} \sum_{p \in \mathcal P_c} \frac1p
  \ge \frac X\gamma \Bigl(\prod_c \frac h{e_c + h} - 3d\Bigr),
\]
using $\sum_{p \in \mathcal P_c} 1/p \ge \log(1 + h/e_c) - d \ge h/(e_c + h) - d$ for large $x$, which is the prime
number theorem again, and that each sum is at most $1$. The excluded triples contribute $O(x(1 + \omega(q))/z_0)$, since
$\sum_{p \ge z_0,\ p \mid q} 1/p \le \omega(q)/z_0$, and the triples number $O(x^{e_i + e_j + e_k + 3h})$ with
$e_i + e_j + e_k + 3h < 1$. Both are $o(X)$. Finally
$(1/\gamma) \prod_c h/(e_c + h) \ge \mathtt{keepK}(i, j, k)/K$, because the floor is at most the quotient. Taking
$\varepsilon$ and $d$ small in terms of $\delta$ gives (3). The bound is the integral of the density over the box, read
from below: the number of $q_2$ is about $y/(\gamma \log x)$, and $\int_e^{e + h} d\alpha/\alpha \ge h/(e + h)$.
\end{proof}

In the decomposition, a dropped candidate cell of \texttt{reg2} now uses $\mathcal A \ge$ (its kept parts) in place of
$\mathcal A \ge 0$, and the kept parts' $\mathcal A$- and $\mathcal B$-counts agree within the leaves' error. So the
kept parts' $\mathcal B$-count comes off the cost: the refined cost $\mathtt{cost2}(i, j)$ of such a cell is its
\texttt{bound2} less the sum $\mathtt{sub2}(i, j)$ of the \texttt{keepK} over the cells $k$ that \texttt{keep} passes,
truncated at $0$, and every other cell costs as before. The decisions which cells are candidates and which are
decomposed are unchanged.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:decomp-kept}
Assume the conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, and let $\mathtt{total2} = \sum_{i, j} \mathtt{cost2}(i, j)$. If
$\mathtt{total2}/K \le c$, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$, for all large $q$ and every $a$ prime to $q$,
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) \ge \frac{1}{\varphi(q)} S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})
  - (c + \varepsilon)\frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Follow the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp}, with one change. For a dropped candidate cell $(i, j)$ of
\texttt{reg2}, that proof bounds the $\mathcal A$-count of its term from below by $0$. Instead, let $\kappa_k$ be the
kept part below $k$, for the cells $k$ that \texttt{keep} passes. By Lemma~\ref{lem:kept}(1) and~(2), the term's
$\mathcal A$-count is at least
\[
  \sum_k \mathcal A(\kappa_k) \ge \frac1{\varphi(q)} \sum_k \mathcal B(\kappa_k) - \frac{3n \, q^{L - \delta}}{\varphi(q)}.
\]
So the inequality of Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp} holds with $D(\mathcal B)$ replaced by
$D(\mathcal B) - \sum \mathcal B(\kappa_k)$, the sum over these cells $(i, j)$ and $k$, and with at most $3n^3$ more
errors $q^{L - \delta}/\varphi(q)$, which for large $q$ add less than $\varepsilon x/(4\varphi(q) \log x)$.

It remains to bound $D(\mathcal B) - \sum \mathcal B(\kappa_k)$ by $(c + \varepsilon/2) x/\log x$ for large $q$. By the
proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:drop}, the $\mathcal B$-version of the term of such a cell is at most
$(\mathtt{bound2}(i, j)/K + \delta) x/\log x$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:kept}(3)
$\mathcal B(\kappa_k) \ge (\mathtt{keepK}(i, j, k)/K - \delta) x/\log x$. So the cell contributes at most
\[
  \Bigl(\frac{\mathtt{bound2}(i, j) - \mathtt{sub2}(i, j)}K + (n + 1)\delta\Bigr) \frac x{\log x}
  \le \Bigl(\frac{\mathtt{cost2}(i, j)}K + (n + 1)\delta\Bigr) \frac x{\log x},
\]
since truncating at $0$ can only increase the cost. Every other cell contributes as in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:drop}, and has $\mathtt{cost2} = \mathtt{cost}$. Adding up and taking $\delta$ small gives the bound
$(\mathtt{total2}/K + \varepsilon/4) x/\log x + o(x/\log x) \le (c + \varepsilon/2) x/\log x$. With the inequality of
Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp} for $\varepsilon/4$, this proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The certificate}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:certificate}
On the grid of $n = 340$ cells a side, with $n_C = 85$ cells a side in the coarse grid, and with
$U_1 = \lceil (41479/100000) D \rceil$ and
$U_2 = \lfloor (47555/100000) D \rfloor$, $\mathtt{total2} < K$; in fact $\mathtt{total2}/K = 0.9960153\ldots < 0.99602$.
\end{proposition}

The total is a finite sum of rational expressions in natural numbers, rounded to integers, so the proposition is a
computation. Lean's kernel checks it by evaluation (\lean{decide +kernel}), split into 4715 parts in 351 files: ranges
of consecutive rows of the grid, and the heaviest $187$ rows in pieces of columns. Each part proves that its sum is at
most a bound $\lceil 1.001 \times \text{part} \rceil$, computed exactly, and the bounds sum to
$1149475836901239458 < 2^{60} = K$, that is to below $0.99701 K$. The cells of the parts are tests of boxes of
depths four, five and six: about $7.1$ million, $9.9$ million and $1.6$ million tests, as the evaluator
\file{tools/linnik/mirror2.py} counts them. The definitions
the kernel evaluates are arithmetic on natural numbers, lists and booleans, and do not depend on Mathlib.
The deeper levels lower the total of the certificate without the kept parts from $1.42355K$, at depth four, to $1.11742K$, and
the kept parts then bring it below $K$.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:proof}

The argument of Sections~\ref{sec:type1} and~\ref{sec:certificate} does not use the particular values of $L$, $u_1$,
$u_2$ and $n$. It gives the following theorem, of which Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a case.

\begin{theorem}[the route theorem]\label{thm:route}
Let $2 < L \le 5/2$ and $0 < u_1 < u_2 < 1$, and let a grid of $n$ cells a side, with ends $U_1$ and $U_2$ over
$D = 2^{40}$, satisfy $u_1 D < U_1$, $U_2 < u_2 D$ and $L U_2 > D$. Assume the conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} for $L$ on the window $[u_1 L, u_2 L]$, and $\mathtt{total2} < K$ on the grid. Then there is
$K'$ such that for every $q \ge 1$ and every $a$ prime to $q$ there is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with
$p \le K' q^L$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $c = \mathtt{total2}/K < 1$ and $0 < \varepsilon < (1 - c)/4$. By the prime number theorem, for large $q$ the
primes in $(x, 2x]$ that do not divide $q$ number at least $(1 - \varepsilon) x/\log x$, and each of them is counted
by $S(\mathcal B, z_{\mathrm{top}})$. Proposition~\ref{prop:decomp-kept} gives, for large $q$ and every $a$ prime to
$q$,
\[
  S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}}) \ge (1 - \varepsilon - c - \varepsilon) \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x}
  > \frac{1 - c}{2} \cdot \frac{x}{\varphi(q) \log x} > 0.
\]
An integer of $\mathcal A$ counted by $S(\mathcal A, z_{\mathrm{top}})$ has no prime factor below $(2x)^{1/2}$ and is at
most $2x$, so it is a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$ with $x < p \le 2q^L$. So there is $q_0$ such that the theorem holds
for $q \ge q_0$ with $K' = 2$. Each of the finitely many pairs $(q, a)$ with $q < q_0$ has a prime $p \equiv a \pmod q$
by Dirichlet's theorem, and $K'$ is taken large enough to cover them.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Take $L = 89/40$, $u_1 = 41479/100000$, $u_2 = 47555/100000$ and the grid of Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate}. The
grid fits the window, since its ends are rounded inward and $L U_2 > D$; Theorem~\ref{thm:type2}, under
Inputs~\ref{inp:half-plane} and~\ref{inp:density}, is the Type~II estimate, and Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate} is
$\mathtt{total2} < K$. Theorem~\ref{thm:route} gives Theorem~\ref{thm:main} under the two inputs, and the library proves
both: Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} along the companion preprint's route, and Input~\ref{inp:density} from Chen, Gupta
and Li's Proposition~3.1. Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} enters through Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-sum}, the top bin and the
kernel bound in Section~\ref{sec:type2}, and through the piece of $g_7$ above $11/12$.
\end{proof}

The proof does not compute $K$: the threshold $q_0$ comes from limits in $q$ in each step, and the small moduli
from Dirichlet's theorem. The Lean proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:route} is \lean{SieveMain2.linnik\_route\_lt2}, and the
case of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is \lean{Linnik22250R.linnik\_22250r}, which has no hypotheses: the steps of
Input~\ref{inp:density} and Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} are theorems of the library.

\subsection*{The statements}

The Lean statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
theorem linnik_22250r : ∃ K : ℝ, ∀ (q : ℕ) [NeZero q] (a : ℕ),
    a.Coprime q → ∃ p : ℕ, p.Prime ∧ p ≡ a [MOD q] ∧
      (p : ℝ) ≤ K * (q : ℝ) ^ (89 / 40 : ℝ)
\end{verbatim}
}
where \lean{[NeZero q]} says $q \ge 1$ and the power is the real power. The statements of the two inputs and of the
density estimates count the characters $\chi$ mod $q$, as elements of Mathlib's \lean{DirichletCharacter ℂ q}, for
which \lean{LFunction χ s = 0} at some $s$ in the rectangle. Mathlib's $L$-function of a character mod $q$ is the
analytic continuation of $\sum_n \chi(n) n^{-s}$, with the character taken mod $q$ and not reduced to a primitive
one. So $L(s, \chi)$ is the $L$-function of the primitive character inducing $\chi$ times the Euler factors at the
primes dividing $q$, whose zeros lie on $\operatorname{Re} s = 0$ and do not affect the counts for $\sigma \ge 1/2$.
\file{formalisations/} records, for each result from the literature, the differences between the Lean statement and
the published one.

\section{What the inputs give without the sieve}\label{sec:comparison}

This section claims nothing new. It places Theorem~\ref{thm:main} against what the same inputs give by the
classical argument, which has no sieve. The unconditional exponents of Heath-Brown, Xylouris and others ($5.5$, $5$
and below) are larger, and their proofs use neither a zero-free half-plane nor a zero-density estimate beyond the
published ones; the introduction says how Theorem~\ref{thm:main} stands against them. For special moduli smaller
exponents are known: for the powers of a fixed prime, Banks and Shparlinski's $2.1115$ \cite{banks-shparlinski}, and
$1/0.4752 < 2.1044$ by R.~Li's Harman-sieve argument \cite[Theorem~11]{li-harman}.

\subsection*{The classical deduction}

Suppose that for some $A \ge 2$ there is a uniform density estimate
\[
  N^*(\sigma, T; q) \le C (qT)^{A(1 - \sigma)} (\log qT)^B \qquad (1/2 \le \sigma \le 1,\ T \ge 2).
\]
With Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, the explicit formula for the primes in a class mod $q$, summed over the zeros
of the $L$-functions mod $q$ up to a height, then gives Linnik's theorem with every exponent $L > A$: the zeros of
real part about $\sigma$ contribute about $(q^A/x)^{1 - \sigma}$, and none has real part above $11/12$. The library
proves this deduction as \lean{Linnik.linnik\_of\_charDensity}, and with it:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L = 12 + \varepsilon$ from Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} alone: at most $\varphi(q) \le qT$ characters are
  counted for every $\sigma$, and at most one above $11/12$, which is the estimate with $A = 12$;
\item $L = 36/13 + \varepsilon$ with Ingham's estimate, whose exponent $3/(2 - \sigma)$ is $36/13$ at
  $\sigma = 11/12$;
\item $L = 12/5 + \varepsilon$ with Huxley's estimate, which bounds the density exponent by $12/5$ on the whole
  range.
\end{itemize}
Section~\ref{sec:lean}'s table names the Lean theorem of each.
With Chen, Gupta and Li's estimate as well, the exponent is $7/3 + \varepsilon$, by the same classical deduction from
their Theorem~1.2 \cite{cgl} (exponent $A = 7/3$). They do not state a Linnik exponent, but their Corollary~1.4
\cite{cgl}, on primes in progressions in short intervals under a zero-free region, contains this one: for $h \le x$ it
gives $\psi(x + h; q, a) - \psi(x; q, a) \sim h/\varphi(q)$ whenever
$h/\varphi(q) \ge x^{1/2 + \varepsilon} q^{1/6} + x^{17/30 + \varepsilon}$, and with $h = x$ and
Input~\ref{inp:half-plane} as the zero-free region this holds at $x = q^L$ for every $L > 7/3$ and all large $q$.
In the terms of this paper, the curve $g_7$ of Section~\ref{sec:inputs} satisfies $g_7(\sigma) \le (7/3 + 1/2000)(1 - \sigma)$ on
$[1/2, 1]$ (on the Ingham steps $3/(2 - \sigma_2) \le 7/3$ since $\sigma_2 \le 5/7$, and every slack is at most $1/2000$; the
Chen--Gupta--Li and Huxley steps are below $7/3 + 1/2000$ by the table in the header of \file{lib/pnt/LinnikCGLSteps.lean};
and $g_7 = 1 \le (7/3 + 1/2000)(1 - \sigma)$ for $\sigma \le 4/7$, as $(7/3 + 1/2000)(3/7) > 1$), so Proposition~\ref{prop:curve} and the deduction give every exponent above $7/3 + 1/2000$; the library
does not state this case as a theorem.

\subsection*{Where the gain comes from}

The deduction needs the zeros of every real part to contribute less than the main term, so its exponent is the
largest density exponent on $[1/2, 11/12]$, which is $7/3$, at $\sigma = 5/7$. The sieve needs the zeros
only in bilinear sums with one variable in the window, where Theorem~\ref{thm:type2} trades the density exponent
against the length of the variable, and recovers the primes from those sums at the cost that the certificate bounds.
The refinement of Section~\ref{sec:certificate} lowers that cost by evaluating part of the terms of depth two that the
certificate would otherwise drop. The density curve follows the decrease of Chen, Gupta and Li's exponent below $7/3$ after $\sigma = 5/7$ step by step
(Section~\ref{sec:inputs}).

\subsection*{Below $89/40$}

The method as it stands does not go much further. On the grid of $340$ cells a side the certificate without the
kept parts totals about $1.11742K$ at $89/40$, above $K$, and with them $0.99602K$ (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}); these
are computations, and the second is checked by Lean's kernel. The same computation (\file{tools/linnik/mirror2.py}, not
in Lean) gives $0.99357K$ at $360$ cells a side. A stronger half-plane would not help. With OpenAI's
$\operatorname{Re} s > 7/8$ \cite{openai} in place of Input~\ref{inp:half-plane}, the window condition
(Definition~\ref{def:window}) is needed only for $\beta \le 7/8$, and the exponent of the kernel bound becomes
$b(m) = 3/8 + m/8 + \delta'(1 - m) + 2\varepsilon'$, which is no larger for $m \le 1$; neither change reaches the
corners that set the window's ends, which are on the first step at $5/7$ (Section~\ref{sec:type2}). Going lower would
need more of the dropped terms evaluated, a sharper bound for the rough numbers than $\omega^*$, or a better density
exponent.

On the last, a computation, which is not a theorem. Chen, Gupta and Li note that the worst case for their estimate is
$T = 1$ with no divisor $q_1$ of $q$ of a suitable size, as for prime $q$, that the critical situation is then at
$\sigma = 5/7$, and that improving the term which dominates there would need a joint cancellation which ``seems quite
difficult'' \cite[Remark~12.3]{cgl}. The script \file{tools/linnik/density_payoff.py} measures what lower curves would
give the method. For a curve made from $g_7$, it computes a window admissible for the curve at $L$
(Definition~\ref{def:window}), checked at the corners in exact rationals as in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:window}, and evaluates the certificate's total on it with \file{tools/linnik/mirror2.py} on $340$
cells a side, in exact integers but not in Lean. Starting from $89/40$, it lowers $L$ by $1/1000$ while the total stays
below $0.999K$, then tests once the value $1/2000$ below the last one passed, and reports the lowest $L$ passed. No
theorem of the library gives these curves.

Two places of $g_7$ decide the window. Both ends of the window at $89/40$ are set on the first step of
$[5/7, 1307/1680)$, whose exponent is $2.3334$. And for $m \ge 1$ the window condition on a piece $A(1 - \beta)$
requires $L - m > A/2$, where $g_7 = 1$ counts as $A = 1/(1 - \beta)$, which tends to $7/3$ at $4/7$; so the upper
end stays below $L - 7/6$ however $g_7$ is lowered above $4/7$. With every exponent capped at $30/13$ and $g_7 = 1$
kept below $4/7$, the script reaches only $2.2240$. The other curves therefore also replace the trivial count on
$[11/20, 4/7]$ by one step of Ingham's estimate, of exponent $3/(2 - 4/7) + 10^{-5}$, and set the slack of the steps
on $[4/7, 5/7]$ to $10^{-5}$, both as \lean{LinnikInghamC.charDensity\_ingham\_c} allows; that change alone leaves
$89/40$. With every exponent then capped at $A$, the script reaches:

\toprule
cap $A$ & $2.33$ & $2.325$ & $2.32$ & $2.315$ & $30/13 \approx 2.3077$ \\
lowest $L$ & $2.2240$ & $2.2225$ & $2.2210$ & $2.2195$ & $2.2170$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
With the same change and the exponent of each step inside $[7/10, 4/5]$ capped at Chen, Gupta and Li's bound at
$q_1 = q$ without its first term \cite[Theorem~1.2]{cgl}, the term that gives $4/(1 + \sigma)$, taken at the step's
left end plus $10^{-5}$, the script reaches $2.2100$; on $[7/10, 4/5]$ the largest of the exponents of the other three
terms is $15/(3 + 5\sigma)$, the exponent of Guth and Maynard's estimate for $\zeta$ \cite[Theorem~1.2]{guth-maynard}.
Capping the exponent on $[15/19, 11/12]$ at $2 + 10^{-5}$, as Heath-Brown's estimate
$\sum_{\chi \bmod q} N(\sigma, T, \chi) \ll (qT)^{2 - 2\sigma + \varepsilon}$ for $15/19 \le \sigma \le 1$ \cite{hb79}
would allow, changes none of the figures for $g_7$, for the change below $4/7$ alone, for the cap at $30/13$ or for
this last curve. Nothing in the paper or the library depends on these computations.

Two further computations, on which nothing depends either, measure what the decomposition could still recover with the
same inputs. The script \file{tools/linnik/dropped_mass.py} replays the certificate on the window that
\file{tools/linnik/density_payoff.py} computes for $g_7$ at $L$, and estimates the true mass of the dropped terms by
sampling, in units of $x/\log x$, with Buchstab's $\omega$ in place of $\omega^*$ and each rough cofactor factored into
primes drawn from their asymptotic distribution. If no product of some of an integer's prime factors has its exponent in
$\mathcal U$, the integer lies in no Type~II sum over the window, so a certificate that drops these terms loses it,
whatever parts of them it recovers by such sums. At $89/40$ the dropped terms have true mass $1.084$, which the
certificate bounds by $1.11742$: $0.123$ in the kept parts, $0.075$ with a product of prime factors in $\mathcal U$ that
the certificate does not recover, and $0.886$ with none. The last part, a floor for the loss, is $0.998$ at $L = 2.22$
and $1.132$ at $L = 2.215$, with sampling errors of about $0.002$. Since it counts true masses, neither more recovery by
Type~II sums over the window nor a sharper bound than $\omega^*$ takes the method below about $2.22$ while these terms
are dropped. Of the $0.886$, $0.284$ lies in terms of depth two with $p_1$ below the window and
$p_1 p_2 \ge x^{1 - U_1/D}$, so that every prime factor of their integers is below the window; $0.402$ in terms of depth
two with $p_1$ below the window and $p_1 p_2 < x^{1 - U_1/D}$; $0.192$ in terms of depth two with $p_1$ between the
window and $x^{1/2}$ and $p_1 p_2 \ge x^{1 - U_1/D}$; and $0.008$ in terms of depths four and six. Every candidate
cell that the certificate drops at $89/40$ is one that the sharp rule does not allow to be decomposed. For the third
part, \file{tools/linnik/role_reversal.py} measures two role reversals. Counting $S(\mathcal A_{p_1}, p_1)$ by its
other prime $n/p_1$ loses $0.272$ in place of $0.192$, and more than the certificate at each of the $200$ values of
$\alpha_1$ sampled. Counting a dropped term itself by $n/p_1$, with $p_1$ sifted, gives terms of sign $-$, which must
all be evaluated; even with each value of $n/p_1$ taken separately, that is possible only on integers of total mass
$0.011$.
