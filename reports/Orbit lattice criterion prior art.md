# Prior art stops short of the pivot margin

**Both criteria are new as far as the searches reach: no source states (A), the pivot form, or (B), the shortest-vector form, or a close variant of either; no source measures the Gram–Schmidt pivots of an explicit-formula Gram matrix against the first pivot; and none states RH through a lattice minimum.** The layer underneath is old. The kernel is Matsumoto and Suzuki's H₁(X) = Σ_ρ X^{ρ−1/2}/(ρ(1−ρ)). Their Theorem 1.3 proves that RH holds exactly when H₁(e^t) − H₁(1) is a screw function, a positive-semidefiniteness criterion of which "G_N ⪰ 0 for every N ⟺ RH" is a short discretisation ([Matsumoto–Suzuki](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00888)). Nicolas used its 2×2 case, |K_H(x)| ≤ K_H(1) under RH, in 2012 ([Nicolas](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.0729)); Burnol evaluated d₁ = 2 + γ − log 4π and remarked that RH holds exactly when it equals Σ_ρ 1/|ρ|² ([Burnol 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9810169)); and Lagarias's Theorem 3.1 is the Gram matrix of the same Weil pairing on Li's test functions, with the same first vector 1/s and the same first pivot ([Lagarias](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0404394)). Yoshida's 1992 paper, now read in full, comes closest in apparatus. It uses the same hermitian form, proves RH ⟺ non-degeneracy on the completed window spaces K̂(a) for every a (Theorem 2, p. 321), runs Gram–Schmidt on the high Fourier modes of a window, and certifies positivity at a = (log 2)/2 by Gaussian elimination. But every margin in it is relative to the L² norm on a window, and the orbit vectors 1_{(0,n]} lie outside all of its spaces ([Yoshida 1992](https://projecteuclid.org/ebooks/advanced-studies-in-pure-mathematics/Zeta-Functions-in-Geometry/chapter/On-Hermitian-Forms-attached-to-Zeta-Functions/10.2969/aspm/02110281.pdf)). Because the "if" halves of (A) and (B) follow formally from positivity, the new mathematics is the "only if" half: **under RH every pivot after the first is at least 0.0577 = 1.249·d₁ and grows like log k, so ±w₁ are the only shortest vectors.** Weil positivity does not supply that margin. In Lagarias's basis the same pairing and first vector give a second pivot of 7.4·10⁻⁵, 0.16% of the first, and the analogous inequality fails for curves over finite fields and for a positive proportion of real Dirichlet characters, so it belongs to the multiplicative orbit and to ζ. A paper should present the result as a margin form of Weil positivity on the dilation orbit, credit the kernel, the constant, the positivity layer and the flat model to their sources, and claim neither a route to RH nor the first extremal problem at the zeros. The verdict holds only as far as the reading went: MathSciNet, Broughan's *Equivalents of the Riemann Hypothesis*, Bombieri 2003, Mazhouda 2013 and two withdrawn Zenodo records were not read.

## Positivity is the known layer; the margin and the lattice sit on top

Write Q_N(x) = xᵀG_N x and V(s) = Σ_{n≤N} x_n n^s. Unconditionally Q_N(x) = Σ_ρ V(ρ)V(1−ρ)/(ρ(1−ρ)), and under RH this is Σ_ρ |V(ρ)|²/|ρ|² ([ball-conjecture-tests.md](../docs/ball-conjecture-tests.md), Theorem D). The k-th pivot d_k = min{Q_k(x) : x_k = 1} is, in any realization, the squared distance of w_k from the span of w₁, …, w_{k−1}. Three statements about this one matrix family have different histories, and a paper has to keep them apart.

| Layer | Statement (Lean) | "If" half | "Only if" half | In the literature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Positivity | RH ⟺ G_N ⪰ 0 for every N (`BallWeil.rh_iff_form`, `BallHadamard.rh_iff_posDefH`, `BallPsi.rh_iff_posDef_psi`) | 2×2 minors at (p/q)², then a Landau-type argument | Immediate: under RH, G_N is a Gram matrix in ℓ² of the zeros | Known in substance. It discretises Matsumoto–Suzuki Thm 1.3; Nicolas's bound is its 2×2 case; Lagarias Thm 3.1 is the analogue in Li's basis; Yoshida (p. 302) states the RH ⇒ direction for the Fourier modes of every window |
| Margin (A) | RH ⟺ d_k ≥ d₁ for every k (`PivotSharp.rh_iff_orbitLattice`) | Formal from positivity (`BallWeil.rh_of_pivots`) | New: d_k ≥ 0.0577 for every k ≥ 2 (`PivotSharp.pivot_ge_rh`) | Not found, in this form or a close one |
| Lattice (B) | RH ⟺ Q_N(a) ≥ K_H(1) for every N and every nonzero a ∈ ℤ^N; under RH ±δ₁ are the only minimisers (`PivotSharp.rh_iff_shortest`, `shortest_unique`) | Formal: nonnegativity at the integer vectors already gives RH | From (A) at the last nonzero coordinate, where \|a_k\| ≥ 1 (`PivotSharp.lattice_gap`) | Not found; no criterion for RH through lattice minima of any kind |

### The positivity layer descends from Matsumoto and Suzuki's screw-function theorem

Matsumoto and Suzuki define H₁(X) := Σ_ρ X^{ρ−1/2}/(ρ(1−ρ)) for X ≥ 1 as a variant of Fujii's function, and show that it governs the Goldbach-type sum Σ_{n≤X} n^{−2} Σ_{m+k=n} Λ(m)Λ(k) and Nicolas's lim sup. **Their Theorem 1.3 proves that "g_{H₁}(t) := H₁(e^t) − H₁(1) is a screw function on ℝ if and only if the Riemann hypothesis holds".** A screw function is one for which g(t−u) − g(t) − g(−u) + g(0) is nonnegative definite on every finite set of points ([Matsumoto–Suzuki](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00888)). H₁ is K_H, and K_H is symmetric under r ↦ 1/r. The statement "G_N ⪰ 0 for all N" says that u ↦ K_H(e^u) is positive definite on finite subsets of log ℕ. Clearing denominators extends this to log ℚ₊, continuity extends it to ℝ, and a positive-definite function minus its value at 0 is a screw function. So the matrix statement implies Matsumoto and Suzuki's hypothesis, and under RH the converse is immediate. Their Corollary 3.1 says that an absolutely convergent exponential sum with frequencies closed under conjugation has bounded real part on [0, ∞) only if every frequency is real. It does the work that a Landau-type theorem does in the repository's 2×2-minor argument: one-sided boundedness forces the zeros onto the line. Suzuki's 2023 criteria apply the same idea to the prime function Ψ ([Suzuki 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03682)).

Nicolas works with W(x) = Σ_ρ x^{i Im ρ}/(ρ(1−ρ)), which is K_H(x) under RH. He states |W(x)| ≤ β = K_H(1) and lim sup W(x) = β ([Nicolas](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.0729)). The 2×2 principal minor of G_N at (m, n) is mn(K_H(1)² − K_H(m/n)²), so his bound is exactly the 2×2 layer. Weil's own criterion reaches these test functions through Burnol's remark that Weil proved his formula for step functions, with ĝ(s) = (X^s − 1)/s ([Burnol 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9810169)). The Mellin transform of Σ x_n 1_{(0,n]} is V(s)/s, so G_N is the zero side of Weil's quadratic form on the integer step functions. Reducing Weil positivity to a countable family is also familiar. Bombieri remarks that positivity on his functions g_{n,ε} already suffices ([Bombieri 2000](http://www.bdim.eu/item?id=RLIN_2000_9_11_3_183_0), after Theorem 2), and Lagarias does the same with the Li class. On the approximation side, Báez-Duarte showed that integer dilations suffice ([Báez-Duarte](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0202141)). Finite sections of Weil's form appear even earlier. For the Fourier modes χ_n of a window, Yoshida remarks that "if R.H. holds, the finite dimensional matrix ((⟨χ_n, χ_m⟩) | |n|, |m| ≤ N) must be positive definite for every N and a", which he verified in several cases for k = ℚ; he states only that direction ([Yoshida 1992](https://projecteuclid.org/ebooks/advanced-studies-in-pure-mathematics/Zeta-Functions-in-Geometry/chapter/On-Hermitian-Forms-attached-to-Zeta-Functions/10.2969/aspm/02110281.pdf), p. 302). The proof of his Theorem 2 (pp. 321–322) also contains the argument that makes G_N definite, not merely semidefinite, under RH. A Mellin transform of finite exponential type that vanished at every zero would contradict N(r) ≠ O(r), and the repository's proof rests on the same zero count ([ball-conjecture-tests.md](../docs/ball-conjecture-tests.md), Theorem E). **This layer is not new in substance, and a paper should record it with these citations rather than claim it.** One unread item could state it verbatim. It is a pair of Zenodo records, "Operator-Theoretic and Computational Realizations of the Riemann Hypothesis", which a search summary described as an RH-equivalent Gram matrix of Weil's form; both now return HTTP 410 ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/21143338)).

### The threshold 2 + γ − log 4π already has five names

The threshold d₁ = K_H(1) has five names in the literature, and each carries a citation. Burnol evaluated Σ_ρ 1/(ρ(1−ρ)) = 2 + γ − log 4π from the functional equation and the Hadamard product, and called "RH ⟺ Σ_ρ 1/|ρ|² = 2 + γ − log(4π)" "a little joke reformulation" ([Burnol 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9810169)). Gun, Murty and Rath restate that equivalence as "easy to show" ([Gun–Murty–Rath](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.11201)). The same number is Nicolas's β ([Nicolas](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.0729)). Since 1/(ρ(1−ρ)) = 1/ρ + 1/(1−ρ), it is also twice Li's first coefficient, and equals Lagarias's ‖G₁‖²_W = 2λ₁ ([Lagarias](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0404394)). This report recomputed 2λ₁ = 0.0461914179322421 from the Taylor coefficients of log ξ at 80 digits, in agreement with 2 + γ − log 4π. Finally, it is the constant in the Nyman–Beurling lower bound lim inf d_N² log N ≥ Σ_ρ m_ρ²/|ρ|², and the limit in Bettin, Conrey and Farmer's conditional asymptotic d_N² log N → 2 + γ − log 4π ([Burnol 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0103058); [Bettin–Conrey–Farmer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5191)). In the realization over the zeros, ‖w₁‖² = Σ_ρ 1/|ρ|². So reading the threshold of (A) as ‖w₁‖² is itself Burnol's lemma.

### The new half is a conditional pivot bound of 0.0577

What no source contains is the "only if" half. Under RH, `PivotSharp.pivot_ge_rh` gives **Q_N(x) ≥ (999/1000)(2/45)(13/10)·x_N² ≈ 0.0577·x_N² for every N ≥ 2 and every real x**. So d_k ≥ 1.249·d₁ for every k ≥ 2, with no finite range left ([PivotSharp.lean](../lib/core/PivotSharp.lean)). `BallPivot.pivot_ge` gives d_k ≥ (log k/2 + log 4 + 2)/(22e⁸). Since no prime enters G(k, k−1), the repository also shows d_k ≤ log(k/2π) + 1 − γ + o(1) whenever G_{k−1} is positive definite, so under RH the pivots have exact order log k ([ball-rate.md](../formalisations/ball-rate.md)). The margin is not a spectral gap. λ_min(G_N) ≪ log N/N, under RH λ_min(G_N) ≫ √(log N)/N, and its conjectured asymptotic is 0.0429·log N/N (same source).

For (B), take the last nonzero coordinate a_k of an integer vector. It has a_k² ≥ 1, so the pivot at k gives Q_N(a) ≥ 0.0577 whenever k ≥ 2. A vector supported at 1 has Q_N(a) = a₁²·K_H(1). Every lattice vector other than ±w₁ is therefore longer than w₁ by a factor of at least √1.249 = 1.118. The "if" half of (B) needs no constant at all. `BallPivot.rh_iff_lattice`, with the threshold (log 4 + 2)/(22e⁸), is the bare statement that the orbit spans a lattice. **So (B) is new only through (A)**: its sharp constant and its uniqueness come from the pivot bound plus integrality. Separately, interval certificates prove d_k > d₁ for 2 ≤ k ≤ 10⁴ with no hypothesis ([ball-conjecture.md](../docs/ball-conjecture.md)). These are the counterpart of the finite checks that accompany Li's criterion, such as Maślanka's computation of more than 3000 Li coefficients ([Maślanka](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0402168)).

## Nothing in Weil positivity forces the margin

### Li's basis collapses where the dilation orbit grows

Lagarias defines the Weil scalar product ⟨F, G⟩_W = Σ_ρ F(ρ)·conj(G(1 − ρ̄)) on rational functions with poles in {0, 1}. **His Theorem 3.1 gives, for Li's functions G_n(s) = 1 − (1 − 1/s)^n, ⟨G_n, G_m⟩_W = λ_n + λ_{−m} − λ_{n−m}, with ‖G_n‖²_W = 2 Re λ_n.** He adds that "the Li class L is large enough to characterize the Riemann hypothesis in terms of the semidefiniteness of the Weil scalar product" ([Lagarias](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0404394)). Take the same pairing at F_n(s) = n^s/s, the Mellin transform of 1_{(0,n]}. It gives Σ_ρ m^ρ n^{1−ρ}/(ρ(1−ρ)) = G_N(m, n) unconditionally, and G₁ = 1/s = F₁. **Lagarias's matrix and G_N are therefore Gram matrices of one scalar product on two orbits of one vector.** Li's orbit adds powers of z = 1 − 1/s; the dilation orbit adds powers n^s.

The notes derive what happens to Li's pivots. Under RH, z_ρ lies on the unit circle and G_n = (1/s)(1 + z + ⋯ + z^{n−1}). The Gram–Schmidt pivots of Lagarias's matrix are then the squared norms of the monic orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle for μ = Σ_ρ δ_{z_ρ}/|ρ|². They cannot increase, since zΦ_n is a monic competitor of the same norm. They tend to 0 by Szegő's theorem, because μ has no absolutely continuous part. This report recomputed λ₁, …, λ₈ at 80 digits (λ₂ = 0.0923457352). From them, **Li's pivots are 0.0462, 7.42·10⁻⁵, 1.68·10⁻⁷, 2.07·10⁻¹⁰, and so on down to 5.89·10⁻²³ at k = 8.** The dilation orbit's pivots start at 0.0462 and 0.0794 (d₂ = 1.718·d₁, certified) and grow like log k.

The flat models coincide, which is what makes the comparison fair. Replace the zeros by Lebesgue measure with the same weight 1/(¼ + t²). On the circle, the Cayley map z = 1 − 1/s takes dt/(2π(¼ + t²)) to dθ/2π, whose orthogonal polynomials are the z^n, all of norm 1. On the dilation side the same replacement gives Lubinsky's orthogonal Dirichlet polynomials for the arctangent density, in Dimitrov and Oliveira's form. At p = 2, ψ₁ = 1 and ψ_n(t) = n^{1/2−it} − (n−1)^{1/2−it} are orthonormal for dt/(2π(¼ + t²)) ([Dimitrov–Oliveira](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07887), §2.3; [Lubinsky](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jat.2013.10.001)). They telescope to n^{1/2−it}, so, as the notes derive, the Gram matrix is min(m, n) and every pivot is equal. That matrix is also the Mellin–Plancherel identity ⟨1_{(0,m]}, 1_{(0,n]}⟩ = min(m, n) on L²(0, ∞), the ambient space of the Nyman–Beurling criterion. It is also the covariance of Brownian motion at integer times. That makes it the simplest instance of Burnol's reading of Weil positivity as the covariance of a multiplicatively stationary "arithmetic stochastic process" ([Burnol 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9810169); compare [Morán Ledezma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08519)). The flat model is therefore the equality case for both orbits, with the same measure on the zeros. The zeros push the shift orbit's pivots to 0 and the dilation orbit's to infinity. **The margin is a property of the dilation orbit, not of Weil's form.**

Prediction theory gives the same picture in a few sentences, which a paper can use as motivation. The reading is derived in the notes, not taken from a source. Under RH, consider the stationary process with covariance K_H(e^τ) = Σ_γ e^{iγτ}/(¼ + γ²). Then d_k/k is its one-step prediction error at time log k, given its values at log 1, …, log(k−1). The flat model is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, whose Markov property makes that error exactly d₁/k. So (A) says the arithmetic process is never more predictable at these times than its Ornstein–Uhlenbeck counterpart.

### Every known margin is diagonal, windowed or a sign count

The margins already known for Weil-type criteria are of other kinds. The diagonal of G_N is n·d₁, so ‖w_k‖² ≥ ‖w₁‖² holds trivially, and all of (A) sits in the Schur complements. Li-type margins, by contrast, are diagonal. No source states RH ⟺ λ_n ≥ λ₁ for all n. That statement would follow from Li's criterion if Mazhouda's theorem, that under RH the Li coefficients of Dirichlet L-functions increase with n, is correct and covers ζ ([Mazhouda](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00605-012-0436-3)). That paper was seen only as an abstract snippet. A companion paper by Omar, Ouni and Mazhouda had conjectured the monotonicity from numerics; its journal version was retracted "to prevent other authors from using incorrect mathematical results" ([retraction notice](https://doi.org/10.1112/S1461157018000013)). Bombieri and Lagarias's tolerance version, Re λ_n ≥ −C(ε)e^{εn}, and Voros's asymptotic dichotomy both lower the threshold instead of raising it ([Lagarias](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0404394), Theorem 2.2; [Voros](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0506326)).

For Weil's form itself, the margins that exist live on windows. Yoshida's Lemma 3, examined with the rest of his paper in the next subsection, says that for a₀, µ > 0 there is N such that ⟨φ, φ⟩ ≥ µ‖φ‖²_{L²} for every φ ∈ K_N(a) and every 0 < a ≤ a₀, that is, for every window function on [−a, a] whose Fourier coefficients of index |n| ≤ N vanish ([Yoshida 1992](https://projecteuclid.org/ebooks/advanced-studies-in-pure-mathematics/Zeta-Functions-in-Geometry/chapter/On-Hermitian-Forms-attached-to-Zeta-Functions/10.2969/aspm/02110281.pdf), p. 290). Bombieri's Theorem 12 gives T[F ∗ F̃] ≥ (log(1/|I|) − log log(1/|I|) − O(1))‖F‖² for supports I shorter than log 2 ([Bombieri 2000](http://www.bdim.eu/item?id=RLIN_2000_9_11_3_183_0)). As windows grow, the margin collapses: Connes and Consani find a smallest eigenvalue of 2.389·10⁻⁴⁸ at λ² = 11 ([Connes–Consani 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01715)). The nearest statements about pivots only count signs. Bombieri's Theorem 8 says that the number of negative eigenvalues of his zero-indexed truncations H(Γ; t) equals the number of distinct conjugate pairs of zeros off the line; by Sylvester's law of inertia, that is the number of negative pivots. Suzuki's Theorem 1.8 says that RH holds exactly when every Hankel determinant of the moments of Ψ is nonnegative. (A) is none of these. Its threshold is d₁ rather than 0, it uses no window, and it survives λ_min(G_N) → 0.

### Yoshida's windows run Gram–Schmidt but never measure against a first vector

Yoshida's paper was read in full from a scan the user supplied, and the page numbers below are the journal's. It is the one source that brings the machinery of (A), finite Gram matrices and Gram–Schmidt, to Weil's form ([Yoshida 1992](https://projecteuclid.org/ebooks/advanced-studies-in-pure-mathematics/Zeta-Functions-in-Geometry/chapter/On-Hermitian-Forms-attached-to-Zeta-Functions/10.2969/aspm/02110281.pdf)). His form is the repository's. In the logarithmic variable he sets ⟨φ₁, φ₂⟩ = T_k(φ₁ ∗ φ̃₂), where T_k sums the Mellin transform M(F)(s) = ∫F(x)e^{(s−1/2)x}dx over the zeros of the Dedekind zeta function ζ_k (pp. 281–287). The notes confirm the normalisation numerically: for the L²-normalised indicator of the window at a = (log 2)/2, his closed formula (5.15) gives 0.183383 and the sum over the zeros gives 0.183388.

He restricts the form to test functions supported in a window [−a, a]: the smooth ones, C(a); the restrictions of smooth 2a-periodic functions, K(a); the elements of K(a) whose Fourier coefficients of index |n| ≤ N vanish, K_N(a); and the completion for the form, K̂(a). **His Theorem 2 (p. 321) is that RH for ζ_k holds if and only if the form is non-degenerate on K̂(a) for every a > 0.** Theorem 1 (p. 310) states, for k = ℚ and a = (log 2)/2, that ⟨φ, φ⟩ = T_ℚ(φ ∗ φ̃) ≥ 0 for every φ ∈ K(a), with equality only at φ = 0. Lemma 3 (p. 290) gives, for any a₀, µ > 0, an N with ⟨φ, φ⟩ ≥ µ‖φ‖²_{L²} on K_N(a) for every 0 < a ≤ a₀. His Gram matrices are those of the Fourier modes χ_n = (2a)^{−1/2}e^{πinx/a}, for which his closed forms give |⟨χ_n, χ_m⟩| ≤ κ/|n − m| and ⟨χ_n, χ_n⟩ ∼ [k:ℚ]·log|n| ((5.17)–(5.18), pp. 301–302); the finite-section remark quoted above follows on p. 302. In §7 (pp. 312–319) he applies "the Schmidt orthogonalization process" to the high modes, factors their Gram matrix as C·ᵗC̄ with C lower triangular, and bounds the inverse blocks of C in Lemma 9, (7.19) and (7.23). He uses this only to prove that the form on the finite block W(a) varies continuously with a. In §6 (pp. 306–310) he proves Theorem 1 by certified Gaussian elimination: for odd functions the leading minors of a 10 × 10 Schur-complement matrix are positive, and for even functions interval lower bounds for the final diagonal entries of a 200 × 200 one are positive.

**None of this is (A) or (B).** Every margin in the paper is coercivity relative to ‖φ‖²_{L²}, on small windows (Lemma 2, p. 289) or on high modes, where Lemma 3 has the explicit instances ⟨φ, φ⟩ ≥ 1.52‖φ‖²_{L²} on K₁₀(a) and ≥ 4.08‖φ‖²_{L²} on K₁₉₉(a) at a = (log 2)/2 ((6.8)–(6.9), p. 305). None is measured against a first vector or a first term. The Gram–Schmidt of §7 does imply, though Yoshida never says so, that every pivot of the high modes is at least κ₁², and that the pivots past index M are at least (µ(M) − κ₁^{−1}ν)², which tends to infinity. That is the only place found where each new vector keeps a quantified distance from the span of the earlier ones. But those bounds follow at once from the coercivity assumed in hypothesis (I) of Lemma 9, hold unconditionally, and concern the high modes of one window, whereas G_N has no coercivity at all, since λ_min(G_N) → 0. The p. 302 remark is the sign condition that every pivot of the Fourier modes is positive, not a margin. The pivots of the §6 Schur-complement matrices are the exact analogue of d_k for the frequency-ordered trigonometric basis of one window, since the k-th is the least Weil energy over the functions ω_k + Σ_{j<k} c_j ω_j plus high modes. Yoshida, however, bounds them only through comparison and interval matrices, checks that the lower bounds are positive, and reports no values. Integers enter only as Fourier indices and in the unique-factorisation step of the proof of Proposition 7 (pp. 323–324), so nothing in the paper resembles (B).

The orbit vectors also lie outside every space Yoshida uses. In his variable, 1_{(0,n]} becomes φ_n(x) = e^{x/2}·1_{(−∞, log n]}(x) = √n·φ₁(x − log n), whose Mellin transform is n^s/s, so with T_k taken as the sum over the zeros, G_N is exactly the Gram matrix of his form on φ₁, …, φ_N. But φ_n is not compactly supported, and φ₁ ∗ φ̃₁ = e^{−|x|/2} has Mellin transform 1/(s(1−s)), with poles at 0 and 1, whereas every element of C(a), K(a) and K̂(a) has an entire Mellin transform (p. 283). By the notes' inference, only the hyperplane Σ x_n = 0 of span(w₁, …, w_N) fits a window: its elements are supported in [0, log N] and land in K̂((log N)/2) after translation. The vector w₁, the comparison vector of (A) and the shortest vector of (B), never enters his framework, and he passes between windows by dilations α_t(x) = t^{−1}α(t^{−1}x) (p. 311), never by translates by log n. Two points of contact are worth recording. The "RH ⇒ non-degenerate" half of Theorem 2 is the zero-counting argument noted above for the definiteness of G_N. The diagonal growth ⟨χ_n, χ_n⟩ ∼ log|n|, which drives his high-mode pivots to infinity, parallels d_k ≍ log k; the notes trace both, heuristically, to Re ψ(¼ + it/2) ∼ log|t|.

Reading the paper corrects three second-hand accounts. Connes restates Theorem 1 as W_∞(f) ≥ 0 for every smooth positive-definite f supported in (1/2, 2) whose Fourier transform vanishes at ±i/2 ([Connes 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04022); [Connes–Consani 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.13771)). That is a paraphrase. Yoshida's statement has no hypothesis f̂(±i/2) = 0 and includes the pole term, which for odd φ is negative, −2|∫φ(x)e^{x/2}dx|², by (6.2) on p. 302. No prime contributes, because φ ∗ φ̃ is supported in [−log 2, log 2] and vanishes at its endpoints, so Connes's version is the special case in which the pole terms vanish. The zbMATH review says Yoshida "succeeded in proving the positive definiteness of the deformed Hermitian forms on C(a) and K(a)" ([zbMATH 0817.11041](https://zbmath.org/?q=an:0817.11041)). In fact he proves non-degeneracy, on C(a) for every k and a (Proposition 2, p. 291) and on K(a) only for k = ℚ (Proposition 7, p. 322); he proves positive definiteness only on small windows (Lemma 2, p. 289) and at a = (log 2)/2 for k = ℚ (Theorem 1). Suzuki's transcription of Lemma 3 drops a square: the margin is µ‖φ‖²_{L²}, not µ‖φ‖ ([Suzuki 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03682)). Finally, the paper covers Dedekind zeta functions of number fields, with Theorem 1 and Proposition 7 restricted to ℚ. It treats no Dirichlet, Hecke or Artin L-functions and no function fields, so it has no counterpart to the repository's tests on characters and curves.

### Curves and characters break the analogue, as de Branges's conditions broke for ζ

The repository's tests build K_H for a curve over 𝔽_q from the zeros of its zeta function. Because those zeros repeat vertically, d_k = 0 at k = q^{2g} (Theorem C). Of 148 curves of genus 1 to 3, 98 have some d_k < d₁ with k ≤ 150, and the minimal elliptic curve over 𝔽₄ has d₂/d₁ = 2/9 ([ball-conjecture-tests.md](../docs/ball-conjecture-tests.md), §4). The Frobenius-orbit Gram matrix in the literature fails in the same way. Hallouin and Perret project the graphs Γ^k of the Frobenius powers to the Euclidean part of NS(X × X). Their Lemma 3 computes ⟨p(Γ^k), p(Γ^k)⟩ = 2gq^k and ⟨p(Γ^k), p(Γ^{k+i})⟩ = q^k(q^i + 1 − #X(𝔽_{q^i})). They read Weil's bound as the nonnegativity of the 2×2 Gram determinant of Γ⁰ and Γ¹, and Ihara's as the 3×3 case, and they work in a space of dimension at most 2g ([Hallouin–Perret](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2357); [TAMS version](https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/7813)). Take d₁ = 2g and d₂ = 2gq − (q + 1 − #X(𝔽_q))²/(2g). Then **the margin d₂ ≥ d₁ reads |q + 1 − #X(𝔽_q)| ≤ 2g√(q − 1)**. That is a strict strengthening of Weil's bound, and it is false at every Weil-maximal and Weil-minimal curve, where d₂ = 0. Every pivot past the rank also vanishes. This criterion is derived in the notes and was checked for this report. Connes and Consani call their Toeplitz analysis of the archimedean Weil form "closely related" to this technique ([Connes–Consani 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.13771)).

For Dirichlet L-functions the first pivots decide. The repository proves d₂ ≥ d₁ ⟺ |K₁ + 2c_a + L′/L(1, χ)| ≤ K₁, with c₀ = −¼ log(27/4) and c₁ = ¾ log(3/4). It finds d₂ < d₁ for **5983 of the 29468 primitive characters with q ≤ 400**, and for 637 of the 6086 real ones with q ≤ 10⁴. Through Lamzouri's random model it shows that the proportion of real characters that fail tends to **0.0511 for D > 0 and 0.1609 for D < 0**. That proof is unconditional on paper, and in Lean it is conditional on a zero-free half-plane input ([ball-conjecture-tests.md](../docs/ball-conjecture-tests.md), §5; [d2-density.md](../docs/d2-density.md); [Lamzouri](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1603)). These characters are expected to satisfy GRH, under which their Weil forms are positive. So for them the verbatim analogue of (A) is strictly stronger than GRH.

The nearest precedent is a mirror image. Conrey and Li showed that de Branges's positivity conditions, which would imply GRH, fail for the spaces built from ζ and from L(s, χ₄) ([Conrey–Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9812166)). The orbit margin runs the other way. It is equivalent to RH for ζ, yet its natural analogues fail for curves, where RH is a theorem, and for characters, where GRH is expected. No source found has an example of that kind.

Two consequences follow for a paper. First, the "only if" half cannot come from an argument that transfers to curves or characters. It has to use ζ's own numbers: conductor 1, K_H(1) = 0.046, no zero below height 14.13. The Lean proof does exactly that through explicit constants, with the binding case at k = 2, where the true value is 1.718·d₁ and the proof gives 1.249·d₁. Second, (B) works differently from its curve analogue. For an elliptic curve the lattice minimum comes from integrality: "a positive-definite integral form has no nonzero vector of norm below 1" ([ball-conjecture.md](../docs/ball-conjecture.md)). The notes also derive, by a resultant argument, that the diagonal is a shortest vector of Hallouin and Perret's Frobenius-orbit lattice whenever the Weil polynomial is irreducible. For ζ no integrality reason is known, and (B) rests entirely on the analytic margin.

### Dimitrov and Oliveira solved the dual extremal problem

The extremal reading of (A) is that, among P(s) = Σ_{n≤k} x_n n^s with x_k = 1, the constant 1 minimises Σ_ρ |P(ρ)|²/|ρ|². That problem has one published relative, and it is the dual problem. Dimitrov and Oliveira's Problem 2 fixes B(ρ_j) = 1 at m zeros and minimises the flat norm. Their Theorem 3 gives a unique minimiser with minimum ∼ (1/log n)·Σ_{j≤m} 1/|ρ_j|², unconditionally, and they call the resemblance to Bettin–Conrey–Farmer "amazing" ([Dimitrov–Oliveira](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07887)). The same constant sits at both ends: there it decays like 1/log n, and here it bounds the minimum from below. The normalisation is essential. Szegő-type theory for Dirichlet series fixes the constant term instead. Guo, Ni and Zhou's Theorem 3.1, for example, treats H^p(𝕋^∞) and polynomials without constant term ([Guo–Ni–Zhou](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01993)). That theory says nothing here, because measures carried by a vertical line are singular on 𝕋^∞. With the constant term fixed, the orbit behaves like Nyman–Beurling: numerically, the squared distance of w₁ from the span of w₂, …, w_N falls like N^{−0.87} ([ball-conjecture-tests.md](../docs/ball-conjecture-tests.md), §7). Orthogonal polynomials on the zeros appear once, in Cardon's criterion. It says that RH with simple zeros holds exactly when p_{2n}(z)/p_{2n}(0) → ξ(½ + iz)/ξ(½), where the p_n are ordinary polynomials orthogonal against a step function on the zeros. That is a statement about ratio asymptotics, not norms ([Cardon–de Gaston manuscript](https://mathdept.byu.edu/cardon/papers/Orthogonal_RH.pdf)). A paper of the same title appeared as D. A. Cardon and S. A. Roberts, [J. Approx. Theory 138 (2006) 54–64](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jat.2005.09.016).

### Multiplicative Toeplitz theory never takes the zeros as a symbol

Bombieri calls the dilation τ_a f(x) = a^{−1/2} f(x/a) "the analogue of a normalized Frobenius operator". He avoids τ_a-invariant spaces because minimisers there are not unique ([Bombieri 2000](http://www.bdim.eu/item?id=RLIN_2000_9_11_3_183_0)). The orbit is the sequence of Frobenius translates w_n = √n·τ_n 1_{(0,1]}, and taking them in order is what breaks the invariance (derived in the notes). In matrix terms G_N = D·T·D, where D = diag(√n) and T(m, n) = K_H(m/n) is a multiplicative Toeplitz matrix. In Hilberdink's theory and its successors the symbols are ζ or arithmetic Dirichlet series, never the zeros, and positivity is unconditional ([Hilberdink](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/50981/); [Nikolski–Pushnitski](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01474); [Hilberdink–Pushnitski](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14323)). Nikolski and Pushnitski even show that the natural truncations m, n ≤ N do not give the spectral law that a first Szegő theorem would predict.

The closest single object is Lindqvist and Seip's family M_s = (gcd(m,n)^{2s}/(m^s n^s)); its s = ½ member is Smith's gcd matrix, which has the same D·T·D shape. They prove that λ_min(M_s) tends to ζ(2s)/ζ(s)² for s > 1 and to 0 for ½ < s ≤ 1 ([Lindqvist–Seip](http://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa84/aa8425.pdf)). Since gcd(i, j) = Σ_{d|i, d|j} φ(d), Smith's matrix has pivots φ(k) (derived in the notes). So in both classical relatives, Smith's matrix and Brownian min(m, n), "d_k ≥ d₁" and "e₁ is shortest" hold trivially. In this language d_k = k·det T_k/det T_{k−1}, and (A) is the lower bound det T_k/det T_{k−1} ≥ K_H(1)/k. That is a one-step prediction bound for a multiplicative Toeplitz matrix with a singular symbol, and no theory of such bounds was found.

### Lattices meet RH only through new zeta functions and computation

Weng's zeta functions integrate over moduli of semistable lattices. They satisfy RH in rank 2 (Lagarias–Suzuki) and in further cases, but their zeros are not ζ's ([Lagarias–Suzuki](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0412039); [Weng](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03604)). Bost's theta invariants and the Stuhler–Grayson slope filtration supply vocabulary but no equivalent of RH ([Bost](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04992); [Coulangeon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00331)). Odlyzko and te Riele, and later Best and Trudgian, used lattice reduction on the zeros to disprove Mertens's conjecture ([Odlyzko–te Riele](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/6447/6447D.pdf); [Best–Trudgian](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.3843)). Searches pairing "shortest vector" with RH found only algorithmic and cryptographic papers that assume GRH ([Wesolowski](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01561)). The notes also translate (A) into slopes. The subquotient L_k/L_{k−1} of the orbit flag has slope −½ log d_k, so (A) says no subquotient is more stable than ℤw₁, and under RH every L_N with N ≥ 2 is destabilised by ℤw₁. Nothing in the slope literature checked has a counterpart for RH.

## Broughan's catalogue is the largest unread source

The researchers searched arXiv metadata and selected full texts, the zbMATH Open API (several hyphenated queries returned server errors), Crossref and the general web. MathSciNet was not accessed. The Google Scholar citation trails of Li 1997 and Bombieri–Lagarias 1999 were not followed. The repository's own searches of October and November 2026 likewise found no statement of the conjecture ([ball-conjecture.md](../docs/ball-conjecture.md)). Absence is established only to that extent. The largest single gap is Broughan's catalogue. Crossref lists chapters of its Volume Two on Banach and Hilbert space methods (pp. 23–36), orthogonal polynomials (93–116), Weil's explicit formula, inequality and conjectures (150–192), discrete measures (193–220) and Hermitian forms (221–273) ([Broughan, vol. 2](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108178266)). Volume One has a chapter on Euler's totient function, where Nicolas's β belongs ([vol. 1](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108178228)). None was read. They appeared in 2017, so they cannot contain Matsumoto and Suzuki's theorem. They are, however, exactly where an older criterion phrased through Gram matrices, Hermitian forms or orthogonal polynomials would be catalogued.

Several primary sources are known only second-hand, and two of them sit close to the claims. Wiley blocked Bombieri 2003, which is known only from its review. Mazhouda 2013 is known from an abstract snippet, and a search summary's claim that Omar, Ouni and Mazhouda prove "RH iff the Li coefficients increase" for Hecke L-functions, which would be a diagonal margin stated as an equivalence, is unverified ([MaRDI record](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Publication:471812)). Lubinsky's formulas come through Dimitrov and Oliveira, and his Christoffel-function results from a catalogue snippet. Bombieri–Lagarias 1999, Keiper 1992, Brown 2005 and Li 1997 are known through Lagarias. Vasyunin 1995/96, BBLS 2000 and 2005, and Landreau–Richard 2002 were not read, and the last could contain Gram–Schmidt data for the Nyman–Beurling system. Hilberdink's 2006, 2009 and 2017 papers were read only through his chapter, and only the arXiv version of Hallouin–Perret was read, although the TAMS version's new title stresses Toeplitz matrices and it may add Cholesky structure. Bost's monograph, Grayson's and Stuhler's papers, Weil 1952, Helson's *Dirichlet Series*, Queffélec–Queffélec, Nikolski's "In a shadow of the RH", Iwaniec–Luo–Sarnak, Montgomery–Odlyzko 1984, Conrey–Ghosh–Gonek 1998 and the published Cardon–Roberts paper were not consulted at all; for Cardon only the 2004 manuscript was read. The withdrawn Zenodo records could not be assessed, and no journal version of Dimitrov–Oliveira was found on arXiv or Crossref. Yoshida 1992, which Project Euclid had blocked, is no longer on this list: the user supplied a scan and the researchers read it in full. That settles the questions the second-hand accounts left open. His Lemma 3 bounds the squared norm, his Propositions 2 and 7 prove non-degeneracy, and he reports no pivot or eigenvalue values. Not reproduced are his formula (5.16) and the 10 × 10 and 200 × 200 matrices of §6. The notes also found two misprints in §6 (pp. 305 and 309), neither of which affects his results.

Several statements in this report are derivations from cited inputs rather than quotations: the pivots in Li's basis, the flat-model identification, the curve criterion |q + 1 − #X(𝔽_q)| ≤ 2g√(q − 1), the 2×2-minor reading, the slopes, the prediction reading, the shortest vector for irreducible Weil polynomials, and the placement of the orbit vectors in Yoshida's notation, including the inference that only the hyperplane Σ x_n = 0 fits his windows. The Li-basis pivots and the curve criterion were recomputed for this report; the others rest on the notes' algebra. The curve and character tests are certified or numerical computations of the repository, which its tests page says are not formalised in Lean. Only the density of failing real characters is formalised, conditionally on a half-plane input ([d2-density.md](../docs/d2-density.md)).

As far as these searches reach, a paper can claim five things. The first is, under RH, a uniform and explicit lower bound for the Gram–Schmidt pivots of the Weil Gram matrix of the dilation orbit. It is the first such bound measured against the first pivot, and the first for an explicit-formula Gram matrix that is not coercive; Yoshida's Lemma 9 bounds pivots only implicitly, on high Fourier modes where the form dominates the L² norm. The second is the exact order log k of those pivots, and the third the equivalences RH ⟺ (A) ⟺ (B), with (B) the first statement of RH as a lattice minimum. The fourth is the evidence, from Li's basis, curves and characters, that the margin is specific to the orbit and to ζ, and the fifth a machine-checked proof that uses only the standard axioms ([ball-rate.md](../formalisations/ball-rate.md)). It should not claim novelty for K_H, for d₁ or the identity RH ⟺ Σ1/|ρ|² = d₁, for the flat model or for the positivity layer. Nor should it present (A) as a route to RH, since its "if" half is formal. It should not claim the first extremal problem for Dirichlet polynomials at the zeros, which is Dimitrov and Oliveira's, or the first Gram-matrix or lattice construction connected with RH, which belong to Lagarias, Suzuki, Yoshida and Weng.

For Yoshida, the notes suggest two sentences a paper can use, with [Y92] standing for his paper. The first credits what he proved:

> Yoshida [Y92] localised Weil's criterion to test functions supported in a window [−a, a] of the logarithmic variable. He showed that RH for a Dedekind zeta function is equivalent to the non-degeneracy of Weil's hermitian form on a completed space K̂(a) for every a > 0 [Y92, Thm. 2]. He also showed that on the high Fourier modes of a window the form dominates any prescribed multiple of the squared L²-norm [Y92, Lemma 3]. For a = (log 2)/2 he verified positivity by a certified Gaussian elimination of a finite Gram matrix of trigonometric modes [Y92, Thm. 1, §6].

The second marks the difference:

> Unlike [Y92], whose test functions are compactly supported and whose positivity bounds are relative to the L²-norm, our test vectors are the multiplicative translates 1_{(0,n]} (equivalently, Dirichlet polynomials), which lie in no window. Our criteria bound every Gram–Schmidt pivot below by the first one (resp. identify w_1 as a shortest vector of the integer orbit lattice). No statement of this kind appears in [Y92].

## Conclusion

The literature has studied Weil's form thoroughly as a form: on windows, in Li's basis, and through screw kernels. It has studied the form along a flag only once, when Yoshida orthogonalised the Fourier modes of a window to prove coercivity and continuity, and never with the first pivot as the yardstick. That is where the novelty lies. Positivity of a form does not depend on the basis, but a margin along a flag does. Li's basis shows that the same zeros, the same first vector and the same flat model can produce either collapse or growth. The repository locates the dilation orbit's pivot at heights comparable to k, near the critical sampling rate ([ball-conjecture.md](../docs/ball-conjecture.md)). The margin is a fact about n^s, not about Weil positivity. Its failure for curves and characters makes it a fingerprint of ζ's numbers rather than a structural principle. A version that survives elsewhere would have to subtract the flat model, or depend on the conductor and the low zeros, as the repository's tests already suggest.

For a paper, the equivalences are packaging. The theorem is the RH-conditional bound d_k ≥ 1.249·d₁, with growth of order log k, together with the evidence that it is specific to ζ, and the paper should be organised around that. The mathematical question it opens has not been asked: lower bounds for the ratios det T_k/det T_{k−1} of multiplicative Toeplitz matrices with singular symbols, beyond the first Szegő theorem that Nikolski and Pushnitski address. Before submission, the cheapest check that could still overturn part of the verdict is to read the five relevant chapters of Broughan's Volume Two and Mazhouda's 2013 paper.
