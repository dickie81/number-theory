Gravastar Tower

The integers, added one at a time, each become a vector whose inner products come from the primes. Each dyadic block of integers forms a new shell around the old ones, up to a million integers on twenty shells. Under the Riemann hypothesis the tower never collapses; it only thins. Move the first zero off the critical line and it collapses at a finite level.

How thin the tower gets

What you are watching

  • Each integer is a vector. The integer n becomes a vector wn, with inner products ⟨wm, wn⟩ = √(mn)·K(m/n). Here K(r) = Σρ rρ−½/(ρ(1 − ρ)), summed over the zeros of ζ, and it is computed from the primes through the explicit formula, with no zeros needed. The Riemann hypothesis holds exactly when these vectors exist at every level, that is, when the Gram matrix is positive definite for every N (BallRate.rh_iff_balance, BallKernel.rh_iff_posDef).
  • The shells. The integers from 2k to 2k+1 − 1 sit on shell k. An integer's direction is the shadow of its unit vector in the plane of the first zero (angle 14.1347·log n) and on one axis of the second (21.0220·log n). Each shell carries the same winding, turned, with twice as many integers: the configuration repeats itself in log n. Shell 19 alone holds 524,288 integers, and its winding is a solid thread.
  • New room. The pivot dN/d1 measures how far wN sticks out of the span of the vectors before it, against the length of w1. It grows like log N, and it never falls below 1 (checked to N = 32768): w1 is the shortest vector.
  • The thinnest axis. λN, the smallest eigenvalue of the Gram matrix, says how close the configuration comes to collapsing. Up to N = 2048 your browser computes it as the integers arrive. From there to 32768 it comes from one dense Cholesky factorisation at N = 32768, whose leading blocks factor every smaller Gram matrix. It thins like N·λN ≈ 0.0434·log N + 0.032. On the prime clock, where time τ counts the primes so far, the log disappears: τ·λN levels off toward 0.0429, so each tick thins the tower by the same proportion (the prime number theorem, π(N) ≈ N/log N, absorbs the log). That limit is the constant proved on paper under RH; the fitted slope, 0.0434, runs 1% high over this short range. Under RH it never reaches zero, and √(log N)/N ≪ λN ≪ log N/N is proved in Lean (BallGap, BallUpper). Past 32768 the tower is still drawn, but λN is not computed: the matrix alone would take 137 GB at N = 131072.
  • The ticks are the primes. λN drops sharply each time a prime arrives and barely moves at numbers with many small factors. Up to N = 1024 the largest drops come at 2, 3, 5, 7, 4, 13, 11, 19, 17, 9, …, while 504, 900, 924 and 1000 leave it unchanged to within 0.0005%. The thinnest direction is xn ≈ w(n/N)/ψ(n), with ψ Dedekind's function; it carries ζ's Euler product, which is how it cancels at every zero at once, and a new prime brings it a new Euler factor. On the prime clock the composites are what happens between ticks: about log N of them per tick, each bringing about log N of new room.
  • A zero off the line. Move the first zero to ½ ± δ ± 14.1347i, leaving the others on the line. The tower then collapses at a finite level: N = 1018 for δ = 0.02 and 293 for δ = 0.05. Past that level the integers cannot all be vectors, and adding more never repairs it (BallRate.neg_persists).

Floating-point numerics, uncertified. The kernel is the closed form of scripts/ball_orbit.py in the number-theory repository, where the names above are Lean theorems; the precomputed tables come from the same kernel in double precision.
