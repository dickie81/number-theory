Mode count check: transfer matrices have det 1 and |phi|=1, so the closed tower is lossless and every
mode found is a real r (real axis only; modes = local minima |f|<0.05 on a 0.004 grid, may miss some).
Note on part 1: near r=gamma_n/2 the phase of phi winds fast (pole at distance 1/4 off the line),
forcing an extra real root there, so roots cluster at gamma_n/2 better than random. This is the known
resonance phase-shift effect: it reads off Im(rho) given the zeros, and says nothing about Re(rho).

== 3. Verdict ==
Losslessness makes the tower spectrum real, but that spectrum is a cavity spectrum set by L, K, A_d;
its distance to gamma_n and gamma_n/2 is at the random baseline, like random junctions. The zeros sit at the
POLES of phi (s=rho/2, Re s=1/4), off the unitary line; closing the system does not move them onto it
nor make them eigenvalues. Reality of the tower spectrum says nothing about Re(rho).
