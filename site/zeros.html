The Strip and the Count

What the library's Lean theorems say about the zeros of ζ(s) and of Dirichlet L-functions: where in the critical strip a zero can be, how many can lie to the right of a line Re s = σ, and what that gives for Linnik's exponent. Every constraint drawn in color is a theorem of the library, named beside it, and its numbers are read from the theorem's statement; the gray curves are published or conjectured bounds, drawn for comparison.

The gaps between zeros are bounded

There is an H such that, for large t, every window [t, t + H) contains the ordinate of a zero of ζ. It follows from S(t) = O(log t).

The library also proves the Korobov–Vinogradov zero-free region (LandauKV.zeroFree_KV), whose width shrinks like (log t)−{{kvA}}(log log t)−{{kvB}}, so at large heights the half-plane, of width {{hpWidth}} at every height, says more. The half-plane and the route to it are OpenAI's (2026); the library formalises them (its note). Bohr and Landau's theorem and the bounded gaps use zeta23, a library vendored from anthropics/formal-math: its Riemann–von Mangoldt formula, and the argument principle, Backlund's bound and Stirling's formula behind it.

How many zeros of ζ lie right of σ

A density estimate bounds N(σ, T), the number of zeros with β ≥ σ and |γ| ≤ T, by CTA(1−σ)(log T)B. All zeros up to height T number about T log T, so a bound says something only where A(1−σ) < 1. The library proves {{nzeta}} exponents A; right of {{hp}} the half-plane leaves no zeros at all.

Characters mod q: the Linnik route's density curve

The Linnik paper counts the characters mod q whose L-function has a zero with β ≥ σ, |γ| ≤ T: at most C(qT)g*(σ) (1 + log qT)10. Linnik's exponent

Linnik's theorem: there are K and L such that every reduced residue class mod q contains a prime p ≤ KqL. The zeros above decide L. A zero with real part β costs about xβ−1 of the primes up to x, so the half-plane leaves no zero that costs much, and the density curve says how many zeros there are. The library proves L = {{L}} = {{Ldec}} (Linnik22000E.linnik_22000e), from two inputs that have not been refereed: the half-plane and Chen, Gupta and Li's Proposition 3.1.

From the density exponent to L

The density deduction turns a density exponent A into every L > A (Linnik.linnik_of_charDensity): the diagonal. Harman's sieve goes below it, since Cauchy–Schwarz over the characters halves the density exponent, but not below L = 1 + A/2, where its window closes. The two lines meet at the density hypothesis, A = {{grh}}.

Where the window closes

The sieve needs a Type II estimate for Dirichlet polynomials of length P = xu, with x = qL, for every u in a window [u1, u2] around P = q. At each β, the characters that g*(β) counts allow only part of that range (the paper's {{defWindow}}, SieveCurveGlue.WindowOKHM in the Lean), and the two curves are its ends. The window has to fit at every β at once, so it is pinched where g* peaks, on [{{gmaxLo}}, {{gmaxHi}}]. Move L to open and close it.
